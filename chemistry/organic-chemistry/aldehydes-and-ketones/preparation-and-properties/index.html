Preparation and Properties of Aldehydes and Ketones

Aldehydes and ketones are carbonyl compounds containing the >C=O functional group hybridized in an sp2 planar geometry. They are synthesized primarily via oxidation of alcohols and hydration of alkynes, and undergo characteristic nucleophilic addition reactions.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Synthesis of Aldehydes and Ketones

  • Primary alcohols yield aldehydes when treated with mild oxidizing agents like PCC or PDC in dichloromethane.
  • Secondary alcohols are oxidized to ketones using standard oxidizing agents such as chromic anhydride or Jones reagent.
  • Aromatic aldehydes are selectively prepared via Etard reaction (using CrO2Cl2) or Gattermann-Koch reaction (using CO, HCl, and anhydrous AlCl3/CuCl).
  • Ketones are synthesized via Friedel-Crafts acylation of aromatic rings and reaction of cadmium or organolithium reagents with acyl chlorides.

Nucleophilic Addition Reactions

  • The carbonyl carbon is electrophilic due to resonance polarization toward oxygen, making it susceptible to nucleophilic addition.
  • Addition of HCN yields cyanohydrins, while addition of sodium bisulfite yields crystalline bisulfite addition products used for separation.
  • Reaction with alcohols in the presence of dry HCl gas forms acetals and ketals via hemiacetal intermediates.
  • Reactions with derivatives of ammonia (2,4-DNP, hydroxylamine, hydrazine) proceed via nucleophilic addition followed by dehydration to form imine derivatives.

Oxidation, Reduction, and Special Reactions

  • Aldehydes are easily oxidized to carboxylic acids using Tollens' reagent (ammoniacal silver nitrate) and Fehling's solution.
  • Aldehydes lacking α-hydrogens undergo the Cannizzaro reaction in concentrated alkali to undergo disproportionation.
  • Reduction of carbonyl groups to methylene groups is achieved via Clemmensen reduction (Zn-Hg/HCl) or Wolff-Kishner reduction (NH2NH2/KOH/glycol).
  • Compounds with α-hydrogens undergo Aldol condensation, while those lacking them undergo Cannizzaro reaction.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Formaldehyde reduces Tollens' reagent and Fehling's solution.

Observation: Aromatic aldehydes reduce Tollens' reagent but fail to reduce Fehling's solution. — Fehling's solution is a milder oxidizing agent, and the aromatic ring decreases the aldehyde carbon's susceptibility to oxidation compared to aliphatic aldehydes.

Exception Carbonyl compounds react with Grignard reagents to form alcohols.

Observation: Sterically hindered ketones (like di-tert-butyl ketone) undergo reduction or enolization instead of nucleophilic addition. — Extreme steric hindrance around the carbonyl carbon blocks the approach of the bulky nucleophile.

Exception Aldehydes and ketones undergo nucleophilic addition readily.

Observation: Formaldehyde reacts much faster than higher aliphatic aldehydes and ketones. — Steric hindrance and electron-donating inductive effects of alkyl groups destabilize the tetrahedral intermediate and decrease electrophilicity.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Tollens' vs Fehling's Mnemonic
T-All (Tollens oxidizes ALiphatic and Aromatic), F-Aliph (Fehling oxidizes ALIPHatic only)

Application: Quick identification of aromatic vs aliphatic aldehydes in qualitative analysis questions.

Clemmensen vs Wolff-Kishner Choice
Acidic = Clemmensen (A-C), Basic = Wolff-Kishner (B-W)

Application: Select Zn-Hg/HCl for acid-stable substrates and NH2NH2/KOH for base-stable substrates.

Ozonolysis Cleavage Rule
Erase C=C, insert =O on each carbon fragment

Application: Rapidly determine products of alkene ozonolysis without drawing reaction mechanisms.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Formaldehyde is a gas at room temperature, whereas all other aldehydes and ketones are liquids or solids.
  • Boiling points of aldehydes and ketones are higher than non-polar alkanes of comparable molecular mass due to dipole-dipole interactions.
  • Nucleophilic addition reactivity follows the order: HCHO > RCHO > R2CO.
  • 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine (2,4-DNP) derivatives form yellow, orange, or red crystalline solids used to characterize carbonyl compounds.
  • Haloform reaction is given by methyl ketones and secondary alcohols that can be oxidized to methyl ketones.
  • Cannizzaro reaction requires concentrated alkali (50% NaOH/KOH) and aldehydes lacking α-hydrogen atoms.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the roles of Tollens' reagent and Fehling's solution regarding aromatic aldehydes.
  • Applying Clemmensen reduction to molecules containing acid-labile groups like acetals.
  • Forgetting that ketones do not give positive tests with Tollens' or Fehling's reagents.
  • Assuming steric hindrance affects electronic polarization more than inductive electron-donation.

⚡ Quick Revision: Preparation and Properties of Aldehydes and Ketones

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Preparation Routes

Carbonyl compounds are accessed via oxidation of alcohols, ozonolysis of alkenes, hydration of alkynes, and specialized reductions such as Rosenmund and Stephen reactions.

Reactivity Trends

Electrophilic addition at the carbonyl carbon is governed by both electronic effects (positive charge density) and steric accessibility of the reaction site.

Name Reactions

Key transformations include Aldol condensation, Cannizzaro reaction, Clemmensen reduction, Wolff-Kishner reduction, and Grignard additions.

Identification Tests

Distinction between aldehydes and ketones relies on Tollens' test, Fehling's test, and Schiff's reagent, while methyl ketones are identified via the iodoform test.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Hemiacetal (RCH(OH)OR)
Addition product of one molecule of alcohol to an aldehyde.
Cyanohydrin (RCH(OH)CN)
Addition product of hydrogen cyanide to a carbonyl compound.
Schiff's Reagent (Rosaniline hydrochloride)
Magenta-colored dye decolorized by sulfur dioxide, turned pink/magenta by aldehydes.
Urotropine ((CH2)6N4)
Condensation product of formaldehyde and ammonia used as a urinary antiseptic.
Paraldehyde ((CH3CHO)3)
Trimeric cyclic acetal formed by acid-catalyzed polymerization of acetaldehyde.
Oxime (R2C=NOH)
Condensation product of a carbonyl compound with hydroxylamine.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Reactivity order for nucleophilic addition: HCHO > RCHO > R2CO
  • Formaldehyde gives Cannizzaro reaction because it lacks α-hydrogens.
  • Ketones give iodoform test only if they contain a CH3CO- group.
  • Rosenmund reduction stops at the aldehyde stage using poisoned Pd/BaSO4.
  • Aldol condensation requires at least one α-hydrogen atom.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Aldehydes and ketones feature a sp2 hybridized carbonyl carbon.
  • Nucleophilic addition is the hallmark reaction of the carbonyl group.
  • Aldehydes are easily oxidized, whereas ketones resist mild oxidation.
  • Base-catalyzed condensation reactions build carbon-carbon bonds.

⚖️ Key Differences

Aldol Condensation VS Cannizzaro Reaction

Aldol requires α-hydrogens and dilute base; Cannizzaro requires absence of α-hydrogens and concentrated base.

Which compound undergoes Cannizzaro reaction?

Explanation: Formaldehyde lacks α-hydrogens and undergoes self-oxidation-reduction (Cannizzaro reaction) in concentrated alkali.

Question 5 of 19

The hybridization of the carbonyl carbon in aldehydes and ketones is:

Explanation: The carbonyl carbon forms three sigma bonds and one pi bond, resulting in sp2 hybridization and a planar geometry.

Question 6 of 19

Which compound gives the iodoform test?

Explanation: Propanone is a methyl ketone (CH3-CO-R) and gives a positive iodoform test with I2 and NaOH.

Explanation: Clemmensen reduction reduces aldehydes and ketones to corresponding alkanes using zinc amalgam and HCl.

Explanation: Formaldehyde has no electron-donating alkyl groups and minimal steric hindrance, making it the most reactive.

Explanation: The cyanide ion acts as a nucleophile attacking the electrophilic carbonyl carbon.

Question 10 of 19

Explanation: Secondary alcohols possess one hydrogen on the carbinol carbon and are oxidized to ketones.

Question 11 of 19

Which reagent distinguishes between pentan-2-one and pentan-3-one?

Explanation: Gattermann-Koch reaction uses carbon monoxide, HCl, and anhydrous aluminum chloride to form benzaldehyde from benzene.

Explanation: Reaction of a ketone with hydroxylamine (NH2OH) yields an oxime.

Question 14 of 19

Explanation: Aromatic aldehydes like benzaldehyde fail to reduce Fehling's solution.

Question 15 of 19

Which of the following compounds gives Schiff's test?

Solution: Acetaldehyde is an aliphatic aldehyde and restores the pink color of Schiff's reagent.

Question 3 of 10
The conversion of acetophenone to ethylbenzene is best achieved by:

Solution: Reduction of ketone carbonyl to a methylene group is accomplished using Clemmensen reduction.

Question 4 of 10
Which reagent converts propan-1-ol to propanal?

Solution: PCC is a mild anhydrous reagent that selectively stops the oxidation of primary alcohols at the aldehyde stage.

Question 5 of 10
Which compound will not form a bisulfite addition product?

Solution: Benzophenone is sterically hindered by two bulky phenyl groups, preventing the formation of the bisulfite addition compound.

Question 6 of 10
In the Cannizzaro reaction, the base-catalyzed disproportionation yields:

Solution: Disproportionation of an aldehyde without α-hydrogens gives one molecule of alcohol and one molecule of carboxylate salt.

Question 7 of 10
Hydration of propyne in the presence of HgSO4 and H2SO4 gives:

Solution: Hydration of alkynes (other than ethyne) follows Markovnikov's rule to yield ketones; propyne yields propanone.

Question 8 of 10
Which derivative is formed when carbonyl compounds react with 2,4-DNP?

Solution: Reaction with 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine produces crystalline 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazones.

Question 9 of 10
Which of the following acids on heating undergoes decarboxylation to give a ketone?

Solution: Adipic acid (a 6-carbon dicarboxylic acid) on heating with BaO gives cyclopentanone via ketonization.

Question 10 of 10
What is the product of the reaction between benzaldehyde and formaldehyde in the presence of concentrated NaOH?

Solution: This is a crossed Cannizzaro reaction where the more reactive formaldehyde is oxidized to sodium formate, and benzaldehyde is reduced to benzyl alcohol.
