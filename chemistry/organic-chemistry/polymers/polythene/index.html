Organic Chemistry

Polythene

Polythene is a high-molecular-weight synthetic polymer produced via the addition polymerization of ethene monomers. It serves as a fundamental thermoplastic material widely utilized in industrial and domestic applications.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Preparation and Free Radical Polymerization of Low-Density Polythene

  • Low-Density Polythene (LDPE) is synthesized by heating ethene at 350 K to 570 K under a high pressure of 1000 to 3000 atmospheres.
  • The reaction uses traces of dioxygen or organic peroxides as free radical initiators to trigger chain growth.
  • The resulting polymer structure contains a high degree of long-chain and short-chain branching, preventing close molecular packing.
  • LDPE exhibits a density range of 0.910 to 0.925 g cm-3 and possesses high flexibility combined with low tensile strength.

Coordination Polymerization and High-Density Polythene

  • High-Density Polythene (HDPE) is produced via coordination polymerization of ethene at 333 K to 343 K and 6 to 7 atmospheres pressure.
  • The process employs Ziegler-Natta catalysts, which are heterogeneous mixtures of titanium tetrachloride (TiCl4) and triethylaluminium ((C2H5)3Al).
  • Chains generated are essentially linear with negligible branching, enabling tight crystalline packing of polymer strands.
  • HDPE exhibits a higher density range of 0.941 to 0.965 g cm-3 along with high tensile strength, rigidity, and chemical inertness.

Physicochemical Properties and Applications

  • Polythene is a chemically inert thermoplastic that resists attack by strong acids, bases, and most salt solutions at room temperature.
  • It acts as an excellent electrical insulator due to its non-polar hydrocarbon nature and absence of abstractable functional groups.
  • LDPE is predominantly utilized in the manufacture of squeeze bottles, flexible packaging films, and wire insulation.
  • HDPE finds extensive applications in rigid containers, pipes, buckets, and blow-molded hollow vessels.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkanes are generally unreactive towards electrophiles and nucleophiles under mild conditions.

Observation: Polyethylene chains undergo oxidative degradation when exposed to UV radiation and atmospheric oxygen. — Presence of tertiary carbon radicals at branching points accelerates photo-oxidation and chain scission.

Exception Thermoplastics soften upon heating and melt completely into a liquid state.

Observation: Ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene (UHMWPE) exhibits high melt viscosity and does not flow easily above its melting point. — Extremely high molecular weight and entanglement of long polymer chains prevent fluid mobility.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Polythene is an addition polymer formed from ethene monomers.
  • LDPE preparation requires high pressure and peroxide initiators.
  • HDPE preparation uses Ziegler-Natta coordination catalysts at low pressure.
  • Ziegler-Natta catalyst consists of titanium tetrachloride and triethylaluminium.
  • LDPE has branched chains leading to lower density and flexibility.
  • HDPE has linear chains leading to high crystallinity, strength, and rigidity.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the catalyst systems of LDPE and HDPE; remember HDPE uses organometallic coordination catalysts.
  • Assuming polythene is a condensation polymer; it is formed entirely by addition polymerization without elimination of small molecules.
  • Incorrectly identifying HDPE as being produced under high pressure; it requires low pressure.
  • Failing to account for branching as the primary structural difference determining density.

⚡ Quick Revision: Polythene

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Polymerization Mechanism

Polythene is synthesized through addition reactions converting ethene double bonds into long saturated carbon backbones.

Structural Variations

Control over reaction conditions yields either branched LDPE or linear HDPE with distinct macroscopic properties.

Catalytic Control

Coordination catalysts such as Ziegler-Natta systems direct stereospecific and linear propagation of polymer chains.

Material Properties

High chemical resistance, electrical insulation, and variable rigidity characterize different grades of polythene.

HDPE (High-Density Polythene)

Degree of Polymerization (DP)
Number of monomer units incorporated in a polymer chain.
Thermoplastic (Linear/Branched Polymer)
Polymer that softens on heating and hardens on cooling.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • LDPE: 350-570 K, 1000-3000 atm, peroxide initiator.
  • HDPE: 333-343 K, 6-7 atm, Ziegler-Natta catalyst.
  • Ziegler-Natta components: TiCl4 and (C2H5)3Al.
  • LDPE density: 0.910 - 0.925 g cm-3.
  • HDPE density: 0.941 - 0.965 g cm-3.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Polythene is an addition polymer of ethene.
  • Branching degree dictates density and mechanical stiffness.
  • Coordination catalysts produce linear, highly crystalline chains.
  • Polythene exhibits high chemical inertness and electrical resistance.

⚖️ Key Differences

LDPE Structure VS HDPE Structure

LDPE contains significant chain branching, whereas HDPE consists of linear unbranched chains.

Why is LDPE more flexible than HDPE?
