Bakelite

Bakelite is a thermosetting polymer synthesized via the step-growth condensation polymerization of phenol and formaldehyde in the presence of an acid or base catalyst. It forms a heavily cross-linked three-dimensional network structure that exhibits high thermal stability, chemical inertness, and electrical insulation.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Synthesis and Monomer Chemistry

  • Bakelite is prepared by the condensation reaction of phenol and formaldehyde in the presence of either an acid or a base catalyst.
  • The initial step involves electrophilic aromatic substitution where formaldehyde attacks the ortho and para positions of phenol to form hydroxybenzyl alcohols.
  • Depending on the catalyst and reactant stoichiometry, the reaction proceeds through two distinct pre-polymers: Novolac or Resole.

Novolac and Resole Pre-Polymers

  • Novolac is a linear, thermoplastic pre-polymer formed under acidic conditions using an excess of phenol.
  • Novolac is converted into infusible Bakelite by heating it with hexamethylenetetramine (urotropine), which supplies the methylene bridges.
  • Resole is a branched, thermosetting pre-polymer formed under basic conditions using an excess of formaldehyde that cures directly upon heating.

Structure and Physical Properties

  • Fully cured Bakelite possesses a rigid, highly cross-linked three-dimensional network structure.
  • It is classified as a thermosetting plastic, meaning it cannot be softened or remolded upon heating once set.
  • Exhibits exceptional resistance to heat, electricity, water, and most organic solvents, making it ideal for electrical insulators and switches.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Most condensation polymers are linear or branched thermoplastics (e.g., nylons, polyesters).

Observation: Bakelite forms an infinite, insoluble, infusible 3D network. — Multifunctional ortho and para sites on phenol react with multiple formaldehyde molecules to form extensive covalent cross-links.

Exception Acid-catalyzed phenol-formaldehyde reactions normally yield linear polymers.

Observation: Addition of hexamethylenetetramine to Novolac induces rapid cross-linking. — Hexamethylenetetramine decomposes thermally to release formaldehyde and ammonia, supplying additional methylene bridges for network formation.

Exception Thermoplastics soften and melt upon heating.

Observation: Bakelite chars and decomposes without melting. — Thermal energy is insufficient to break the strong covalent carbon-carbon bonds comprising the 3D cross-linked matrix.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Bakelite is the oldest synthetic polymer known, invented by Leo Baekeland in 1907.
  • It is formed by condensation polymerization accompanied by the elimination of water molecules.
  • The functional groups involved are phenolic hydroxyl groups and active ortho/para positions on the benzene ring.
  • Novolac is a linear polymer with a relatively low molecular mass used in paints and lacquers.
  • Bakelite is widely used for making electrical switches, handles of utensils, and telephone casings due to its low thermal and electrical conductivity.
  • The reaction involves electrophilic substitution at the ortho and para positions of phenol by formaldehyde.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Bakelite with addition polymers like polythene; Bakelite is a condensation polymer.
  • Assuming Novolac is a thermoset; Novolac is thermoplastic until reacted with a cross-linking agent.
  • Forgetting that water is a byproduct of the condensation reaction between phenol and formaldehyde.
  • Mistaking the role of hexamethylenetetramine as a solvent rather than a formaldehyde-releasing cross-linking agent.

⚡ Quick Revision: Bakelite

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Condensation Mechanism

Phenol and formaldehyde react via electrophilic aromatic substitution to yield ortho- and para-hydroxymethylphenol intermediates.

Polymer Architecture

Chain growth leads to methylene bridges (-CH2-) connecting phenolic rings, forming linear or branched chains depending on pH.

Thermosetting Curing

Further heating establishes extensive covalent cross-linking across all spatial dimensions, locking the polymer into an infusible state.

Applications and Stability

The robust 3D matrix imparts high chemical resistance, heat resistance, and excellent electrical insulating properties.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Novolac (Linear PF Pre-polymer)
Linear thermoplastic resin produced under acidic conditions with excess phenol.
Resole (Branched PF Pre-polymer)
Branched thermosetting resin produced under basic conditions with excess formaldehyde.
Hexamethylenetetramine ((CH2)6N4)
Cross-linking agent used to convert Novolac into insoluble Bakelite.
Thermosetting (Heat-setting polymer)
Polymer that becomes permanently hard and insoluble upon heating.
Methylene Bridge (-CH2- linkage)
Group connecting adjacent phenolic rings in the polymer backbone.
Condensation Polymer (Step-growth polymer)
Polymer formed by the elimination of small molecules like water.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Monomers of Bakelite are Phenol and Formaldehyde.
  • Novolac is prepared using an acid catalyst and excess phenol.
  • Resole is prepared using a base catalyst and excess formaldehyde.
  • Bakelite is a thermosetting polymer with a 3D network structure.
  • Hexamethylenetetramine is added to Novolac for curing into Bakelite.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Phenol-formaldehyde resins are classic examples of step-growth condensation polymers.
  • Catalyst choice dictates whether a linear Novolac or branched Resole is formed.
  • Final cross-linking creates a rigid, non-re-moldable 3D network.
  • Bakelite exhibits supreme thermal and electrical resistance.

⚖️ Key Differences

Novolac VS Resole

Novolac is linear and acid-catalyzed; Resole is branched and base-catalyzed.
