Synthetic Polymers

Synthetic polymers are high molecular mass macromolecular compounds synthesized from low molecular mass monomer units via chain-growth or step-growth polymerization mechanisms. Their structural architecture, degree of crystallinity, and thermal behavior dictate their mechanical properties and classification into thermoplastics, thermosets, and elastomers.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Classification and Mechanisms of Polymerization

  • Addition (Chain-Growth) Polymerization proceeds via free radical, cationic, or anionic intermediates using unsaturated monomers like alkenes and dienes.
  • Condensation (Step-Growth) Polymerization involves difunctional or multifunctional monomers eliminating small molecules such as H2O, HCl, or NH3.
  • Ziegler-Natta Catalysts consisting of TiCl4 and Al(C2H5)3 yield high-density, linear polymers with high stereoregularity (isotactic or syndiotactic).

Structure-Property Relationships in Thermoplastics and Thermosets

  • Thermoplastics soften on heating and rigidify on cooling due to weak intermolecular forces between linear or branched chains (e.g., polyethylene, PVC, polystyrene).
  • Thermosetting polymers undergo extensive cross-linking during heating to form a permanent, infusible, three-dimensional network (e.g., bakelite, melamine-formaldehyde).
  • Elastomers possess weak intermolecular forces and random coil structures that permit high elastic stretch under stress, stabilized by minimal cross-linking (vulcanization).

Important Synthetic Polymers and Monomer Precursors

  • Buna-S is a copolymer synthesized from 1,3-butadiene and styrene in a 3:1 ratio via free-radical emulsion polymerization.
  • Nylon-6,6 is a polyamide formed by the condensation reaction of hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid with elimination of water.
  • Dacron (Terylene) is a polyester produced by the transesterification and condensation of ethylene glycol and dimethyl terephthalate.

Number-Average Molecular Mass

Conditions: N_i = Number of polymer molecules of mass M_i
Units: g mol-1
Weight-Average Molecular Mass
M_w = ∑ N_i M_i^2∑ N_i M_i
Conditions: Calculated from light scattering or ultracentrifugation data
Units: g mol-1
Polydispersity Index (PDI)
PDI = M_wM_n
Conditions: For monodisperse polymers, PDI = 1.0; for synthetic polymers, PDI > 1.0
Units: Dimensionless ratio
Degree of Polymerization (DP)
DP = M_nM_0
Conditions: M_n = Number-average molecular mass, M_0 = Molar mass of repeating monomer unit
Units: Dimensionless integer
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Addition polymers generally retain the empirical formula of their starting monomer.

Observation: Fluoropolymers like Teflon (PTFE) and copolymers — Complete elimination of volatile components is absent, but structural rearrangement or halogen redistribution can alter thermal stability profiles.

Exception Condensation polymers always release simple molecules like water or alcohol during formation.

Observation: Polyurethanes formed via addition-elimination without small molecule byproduct release — Reaction proceeds via addition of active hydrogen compounds to isocyanates without condensation byproduct elimination.

Exception Natural rubber is an elastomer with cis-polyisoprene configuration.

Observation: Gutta-percha is a natural polymer of trans-polyisoprene — The trans stereochemistry allows tight chain packing, imparting crystalline, rigid, and brittle properties distinct from elastic cis-polyisoprene.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Monomer Identification Rule for Polyamides and Polyesters
Count carbon atoms in diamine/diacid or diol/diacid precursors

Application: Nylon-x,y means diamine has x carbons and dicarboxylic acid has y carbons. Dacron combines ethylene glycol (2C) and terephthalic acid (8C).

PDI Value Evaluation
PDI = M_w / M_n >= 1

Application: If PDI equals 1, the polymer is strictly monodisperse (synthetic polymers always have PDI > 1 due to varying chain lengths).

Rubber Vulcanization Reagent
Natural Rubber + Sulfur + Heat → Cross-linked Network

Application: Sulfur forms cross-links between double bonds of adjacent polyisoprene chains, restricting sliding and increasing tensile strength.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Polyacrylonitrile (PAN) is used in the manufacture of Orlon fibers and is polymerized from vinyl cyanide.
  • Buna-N is a copolymer of 1,3-butadiene and acrylonitrile, exhibiting high resistance to petrol and lubricating oil.
  • Biodegradable polymers include PHBV (Polyhydroxybutyrate-co-β-hydroxyvalerate) and Nylon-2-nylon-6.
  • Bakelite is a thermosetting polymer prepared by the condensation of phenol and formaldehyde in the presence of an acid or base catalyst.
  • Low-Density Polyethylene (LDPE) is produced by free-radical polymerization at high pressure (1000-2000 atm) and oxygen initiator.
  • High-Density Polyethylene (HDPE) uses Ziegler-Natta catalysts at low pressure (6-7 atm) and room temperature.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the repeat unit of addition polymers with the monomer formula (subscript differences in cyclic vs linear representation).
  • Assuming natural rubber is a trans-polymer instead of the cis-conformation required for high elasticity.
  • Forgetting that condensation polymers require bifunctional or polyfunctional monomers.
  • Mixing up the catalyst systems: Ziegler-Natta uses transition metal halides with alkyl aluminum, not simple free-radical peroxides.

⚡ Quick Revision: Synthetic Polymers

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Polymerization Mechanisms

Chain-growth polymerization involves initiation, propagation, and termination steps via reactive intermediates, whereas step-growth involves independent condensation reactions between functional groups at any stage.

Molecular Mass Distributions

Synthetic polymers are polydisperse; M_n reflects colligative property measurements, while M_w incorporates scattering techniques sensitive to heavier molecular weight fractions.

Stereoregular Polymers

Ziegler-Natta coordination catalysts permit stereospecific polymerization, producing isotactic and syndiotactic polymers with high crystallinity and melting points.

Specialty and Eco-friendly Polymers

Green polymers like biodegradable polyesters and polyamides undergo enzymatic or hydrolytic cleavage in the environment, mitigating persistent plastic pollution.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Monomer (Repeating Unit Precursor)
Low molecular mass reactive molecule that combines to form a polymer.
Homopolymer (Single Monomer Type)
Polymer derived from a single species of monomer.
Copolymer (Multiple Monomer Types)
Polymer derived from two or more different monomer species.
Elastomer (Rubber-like Polymer)
Amorphous polymer with high extensibility and elastic recovery.
Plasticizer (Flexibility Additive)
Substance added to polymers to increase flexibility and lower glass transition temperature.
Tacticity (Stereochemical Arrangement)
Spatial arrangement of pendant groups along the polymer backbone.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Nylon-6,6 monomer precursors: Hexamethylenediamine and Adipic acid
  • Dacron monomer precursors: Ethylene glycol and Dimethyl terephthalate
  • Natural rubber: cis-1,4-polyisoprene
  • Buna-S: Copolymer of 1,3-butadiene and styrene
  • Ziegler-Natta catalyst: TiCl4 + Al(C2H5)3

📌 Quick Recall

  • Addition polymers retain stoichiometry; condensation polymers eliminate small molecules.
  • Thermoplastics soften on heating; thermosets form cross-linked permanent networks.
  • PDI measures molecular weight distribution breadth; monodisperse systems have PDI = 1.
  • Vulcanization introduces sulfur cross-links to improve elastomer tensile strength and elasticity.

⚖️ Key Differences

Addition Polymerization VS Condensation Polymerization

Addition involves opening of multiple bonds without byproduct release; condensation involves reaction between functional groups with small molecule elimination.
