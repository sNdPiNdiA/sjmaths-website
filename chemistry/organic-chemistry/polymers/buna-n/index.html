Buna-N

Buna-N is a synthetic rubber copolymer produced by the addition copolymerization of 1,3-butadiene and acrylonitrile in a 3:1 ratio. It exhibits exceptional resistance to petrol, lubricating oils, and organic solvents.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Monomer Composition and Nomenclature

  • The name Buna-N derives from Bu (butadiene), Na (sodium, historically used as a catalyst), and N (nitrile/acrylonitrile).
  • It is synthesized via the copolymerization of 1,3-butadiene (CH2=CH-CH=CH2) and acrylonitrile (CH2=CH-CN).
  • The standard monomer feed ratio utilizes three parts of 1,3-butadiene to one part of acrylonitrile.

Polymerization Mechanism and Conditions

  • The reaction proceeds via a free radical addition copolymerization mechanism initiated by organic peroxides or sodium dispersion.
  • Industrial production commonly employs emulsion polymerization at around 5°C, yielding high-performance 'cold rubber'.
  • The resulting polymer contains alternating or random arrangements of butadiene and acrylonitrile segments with active double bonds available for vulcanization.

Chemical and Physical Properties

  • Buna-N is an elastomer characterized by high tensile strength and excellent resistance to swelling in oils, gasoline, and hydrocarbon solvents.
  • The polar cyano group (-CN) pendant along the polymer chain imparts superior chemical resistance compared to natural rubber or Buna-S.
  • It maintains structural integrity and elasticity across a wide temperature range, typically from -40°C to 100°C.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception General hydrocarbon elastomers swell extensively in non-polar organic solvents and fuels.

Observation: Buna-N resists swelling in petrol and oils. — The presence of polar cyano (-CN) groups increases intermolecular forces and decreases solubility in non-polar hydrocarbons.

Exception Synthetic rubbers cured with sodium metal generally yield random structures without stereocontrol.

Observation: Modern Buna-N synthesis avoids metallic sodium in favor of emulsion radical catalysts. — Emulsion polymerization provides better molecular weight control and prevents hazardous exothermic side reactions associated with alkali metals.

  • The monomers required for Buna-N are 1,3-butadiene and acrylonitrile.
  • The ratio of 1,3-butadiene to acrylonitrile in Buna-N is 3:1 by weight/moles.
  • Buna-N exhibits exceptional resistance to the action of petrol, lubricating oil, and organic solvents.
  • The polymerization is an example of addition copolymerization.

🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Buna-N monomers with Buna-S (remember Buna-N uses acrylonitrile, Buna-S uses styrene).
  • Assuming Buna-N is a natural polymer; it is entirely synthetic.
  • Forgetting that the 'N' stands for nitrile (-CN), which is the source of its oil resistance.
  • Incorrectly stating the monomer ratio as 1:1 instead of 3:1 (butadiene to acrylonitrile).

⚡ Quick Revision: Buna-N

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Copolymerization Principle

Copolymerization involves the simultaneous polymerization of two or more different monomer species, producing a polymer chain containing multiple repeating units.

Monomer Identification

Buna-N is formed from 1,3-butadiene (CH2=CH-CH=CH2) and acrylonitrile (CH2=CH-CN) via free radical addition.

Structural Significance of Nitrile Group

The polar cyano group restricts chain mobility and enhances resistance to non-polar organic solvents, differentiating NBR from hydrocarbon-only elastomers.

Industrial Applications

Due to its high solvent and oil resistance, Buna-N is extensively utilized in automotive fuel lines, oil seals, gaskets, and protective gloves.
