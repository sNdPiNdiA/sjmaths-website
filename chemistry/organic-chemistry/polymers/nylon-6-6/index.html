Organic Chemistry

Nylon-6,6

Nylon-6,6 is a synthetic semicrystalline polyamide produced via the condensation polymerization of hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid. The repeating unit contains two monomer segments, each contributing six carbon atoms to the polymer backbone.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Monomers and Polymerization Mechanism

  • Nylon-6,6 is synthesized by combining hexamethylenediamine (1,6-diaminohexane) and adipic acid (hexanedioic acid) in a 1:1 stoichiometric ratio.
  • The initial step involves formation of a solid nylon salt, hexamethylenediammonium adipate, by mixing equimolar aqueous solutions of the two monomers.
  • Heating the nylon salt under pressure at approximately 550 K eliminates water via step-growth condensation polymerization to form amide linkages.

Structure and Intermolecular Forces

  • The polymer chains contain alternating amide groups (-CONH-) separated by hydrocarbon segments of specific lengths (-CH2)6 and (-CH2)4.
  • High tensile strength and rigidity are attributed to extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding between carbonyl oxygen atoms and adjacent amide hydrogen atoms.
  • The linear and symmetrical alignment of chains promotes high crystallinity, restricting chain mobility and increasing the melting point to approximately 538 K.

Physical Properties and Applications

  • Nylon-6,6 exhibits exceptional abrasion resistance, high elasticity, and remarkable fatigue resistance under cyclic mechanical stress.
  • It is insoluble in common organic solvents but dissolves in polar solvents like formic acid and phenol due to disruption of hydrogen bonds.
  • Major commercial applications include tire cords, industrial fabrics, parachutes, and molded engineering components such as gears and bearings.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Polyamides are generally stable to mild aqueous acids and bases.

Observation: Strong mineral acids (e.g., concentrated HCl, H2SO4) cause rapid hydrolysis of the amide linkages. — Protonation of the carbonyl oxygen or amide nitrogen weakens the C-N bond, facilitating nucleophilic attack by water.

Exception High crystallinity normally prevents moisture absorption in synthetic polymers.

Observation: Nylon-6,6 absorbs up to 4-8% moisture from the ambient atmosphere. — Amide carbonyl and NH groups act as strong hydrogen-bonding sites for water molecules, which act as plasticizers.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Nylon-6,6 is classified as a synthetic polyamide and a condensation copolymer.
  • The numerical designation '6,6' refers to the carbon atom count in each of the two starting monomers.
  • Hexamethylenediamine contains six carbon atoms and two primary amine functional groups.
  • Adipic acid contains six carbon atoms and two carboxylic acid functional groups.
  • The melting point of dry Nylon-6,6 is around 265°C (538 K).
  • Water acts as a plasticizer for nylon, decreasing its tensile strength while increasing impact resistance.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing Nylon-6,6 with Nylon-6 by assuming both have two monomers.
  • Forgetting that water is eliminated as a byproduct during step-growth polymerization.
  • Mistaking addition polymerization for condensation polymerization in polyamide formation.
  • Believing nylons are polyesters instead of polyamides.

⚡ Quick Revision: Nylon-6,6

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Monomer Composition

Nylon-6,6 is synthesized from hexamethylenediamine [H2N(CH2)6NH2] and adipic acid [HOOC(CH2)4COOH].

Polymerization Mechanism

Reaction proceeds via step-growth condensation with the elimination of water molecules to form amide bonds.

Intermolecular Forces

Strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding between polymer chains imparts high tensile strength, rigidity, and high melting point.

Applications and Properties

Displays high abrasion resistance and chemical resistance, utilized in fibers, textiles, and molded engineering parts.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Polyamide (-[CO-NH]-)
A polymer containing repeating amide linkages along the backbone.
Adipic Acid (HOOC(CH2)4COOH)
A six-carbon dicarboxylic acid used as a monomer.
Hexamethylenediamine (H2N(CH2)6NH2)
A six-carbon diamine used as a comonomer.
Nylon Salt (Amine-Acid Salt)
Stoichiometric crystalline salt formed prior to thermal condensation.
Hydrogen Bonding (Dipole-Dipole Interaction)
Attractive force between polar N-H and C=O groups.
Step-Growth Polymerization (Condensation Polymer)
Polymerization where bifunctional monomers react to form dimers, trimers, and long chains with byproduct elimination.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Monomers: Hexamethylenediamine and Adipic acid
  • Linkage: Amide bond (-CONH-)
  • Polymerization type: Condensation (Step-growth)
  • Melting point: ~538 K
  • Solvent: Formic acid and phenol

📌 Quick Recall

  • Nylon-6,6 is a copolymer of a 6-carbon diamine and a 6-carbon diacid.
  • Hydrogen bonding accounts for its high tensile strength and fiber-forming properties.
  • Water is eliminated during synthesis.
  • It is widely used in textiles, carpets, and engineering plastics.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nylon-6,6 VS Nylon-6

Nylon-6,6 uses two 6-carbon monomers; Nylon-6 uses one 6-carbon lactam monomer.
