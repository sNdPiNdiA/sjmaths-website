Organic Chemistry

Polymers

Polymers are high molecular mass macromolecules formed by the repetitive bonding of numerous small structural units called monomers. They are classified based on their source, structure, mode of polymerization, and intermolecular forces.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Classification and Polymerization Mechanisms

  • Addition polymers (chain-growth) are formed by the successive addition of monomer molecules possessing double or triple bonds without the loss of any small molecules.
  • Condensation polymers (step-growth) involve repetitive condensation reactions between bifunctional or polyfunctional monomers, usually eliminating small molecules like H2O or HCl.
  • Free radical addition polymerization proceeds through initiation, propagation, and termination steps using initiators such as benzoyl peroxide or acetyl peroxide.
  • Ziegler-Natta catalysts (e.g., TiCl4 + (C2H5)3Al) produce high-density linear polymers with stereoregular structures.

Structural Types and Stereoregularity

  • Isotactic polymers feature all substituent groups arranged on the same side of the polymer main chain skeleton.
  • Syndiotactic polymers exhibit an alternating regular arrangement of substituent groups along the polymer chain backbone.
  • Atactic polymers display a random, statistical distribution of substituent groups along the polymer chain axis.
  • Cross-linked polymers (thermrosetting) are formed from bifunctional and trifunctional monomers containing strong covalent bonds forming a three-dimensional network.

Elastomers, Fibers, and Thermoplastics

  • Elastomers are rubber-like solids with weak intermolecular forces that permit high stretching capacity and elasticity.
  • Fibers possess high tensile strength and strong intermolecular forces such as hydrogen bonding holding the chains closely together.
  • Thermoplastics soften upon heating and stiffen upon cooling, allowing them to be repeatedly molded without chemical alteration.
  • Thermosetting plastics undergo permanent chemical changes and extensive cross-linking upon heating, rendering them infusible and insoluble.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Weight-Average Molecular Mass
Mw = ∑(NiMi2) / ∑(NiMi)
Conditions: Calculated from light scattering and ultracentrifugation
Units: g mol-1
Polydispersity Index (PDI)
PDI = Mw / Mn
Conditions: For monodisperse polymers PDI = 1; for natural polymers PDI > 1
Units: Dimensionless
Degree of Polymerization (DP)
DP = M / m
Conditions: M = Molecular mass of polymer, m = molecular mass of monomer
Units: Dimensionless
Number-Average Molecular Mass
Mn = ∑(NiMi) / ∑Ni
Conditions: Calculated from colligative properties or end-group analysis
Units: g mol-1
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Most linear polymers dissolve easily in organic solvents.

Observation: Cross-linked thermosetting polymers (e.g., Bakelite, melamine-formaldehyde) are insoluble. — Extensive three-dimensional covalent bonding prevents individual chain separation and solvation.

Exception Synthetic polymers typically possess a broad distribution of molecular weights (PDI > 1).

Observation: Living anionic polymerization yields monodisperse synthetic polymers where PDI approaches 1. — Absence of chain transfer and termination steps ensures uniform growth of all chains simultaneously.

Exception Natural rubber is an elastomer with weak van der Waals forces and low tensile strength.

Observation: Vulcanization with sulfur introduces cross-links, drastically improving tensile strength and elasticity. — Sulfur bridges restrict the relative sliding of polymer chains past one another.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
PDI Rule of Thumb
PDI = Mw / Mn ≥ 1

Application: Remember that Mw is always greater than or equal to Mn. If PDI = 1, the polymer is strictly monodisperse (typical for proteins).

Monomer Identification Trick
Remove double bond → add -ene to root

Application: For addition polymers like [-CH2-CH(Cl)-]n, remove the brackets, insert a double bond between C1 and C2 to get CH2=CHCl (Vinyl chloride).

Condensation Elimination Trick
Amine + Acid → Amide + H2O

Application: Identify functional groups in condensation monomers: OH + COOH eliminates H2O to form ester linkages (polyesters); NH2 + COOH eliminates H2O to form amide linkages (polyamides).

Addition vs Condensation Polymerization
ParameterAddition PolymerizationCondensation Polymerization

🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing homopolymers with copolymers; ensure all monomer units in a homopolymer are identical.
  • Assuming Mn and Mw are equal for all polymers; Mw is always heavier or equal.
  • Forgetting that natural rubber is cis-isoprene while gutta-percha is trans-isoprene.
  • Confusing Buna-S (styrene) with Buna-N (acrylonitrile).

⚡ Quick Revision: Polymers

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Molecular Mass Averages

Number-average molecular mass depends on the number of molecules, while weight-average molecular mass depends on the weight fraction of molecules. Their ratio defines the polydispersity index.

Chain-Growth vs Step-Growth

Chain-growth proceeds rapidly via reactive intermediates, whereas step-growth proceeds slowly through independent reactions between functional groups.

Stereoisomerism in Polymers

Tacticity (isotactic, syndiotactic, atactic) significantly dictates crystallinity, melting point, and mechanical properties of stereoregular polymers.

Commercial Elastomers and Resins

Synthetic rubbers, fibers, and resins fulfill specialized industrial requirements based on cross-linking density and intermolecular forces.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Homopolymer (Single Monomer Type)
A polymer formed from only one species of monomer units.
Copolymer (Multiple Monomer Types)
A polymer derived from two or more different monomer species.
Tacticity (Spatial Arrangement)
The stereochemical arrangement of chiral centers along the polymer backbone.
Vulcanization (Sulfur Cross-linking)
Process of heating natural rubber with sulfur to improve elasticity and strength.
Biodegradable Polymer (Enzymatically Degradable)
Polymers that decompose by action of microorganisms (e.g., PHBV, Nylon-2-nylon-6).
Plasticizer (Flexibility Additive)
Substances added to polymers to increase flexibility and processability.

  • Buna-S = 1,3-butadiene + Styrene

📌 Quick Recall

  • Polymers are high molecular weight molecules made of repeating monomeric units.
  • Classification includes addition and condensation types based on mechanism.
  • Physical properties depend heavily on intermolecular forces and crystallinity.
  • Molecular weight is expressed via statistical averages (Mn and Mw).

⚖️ Key Differences

Addition Polymerization VS Condensation Polymerization

Addition involves no byproduct elimination and uses unsaturated monomers; condensation eliminates small molecules and uses functional groups.
