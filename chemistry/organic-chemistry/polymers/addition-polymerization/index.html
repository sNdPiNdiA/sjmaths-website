Addition Polymerization

Addition polymerization involves the self-addition of unsaturated monomers containing double or triple bonds to form high molecular weight macromolecules without the elimination of by-products. The reaction proceeds primarily via chain-growth mechanisms involving free radical, cationic, or anionic intermediates.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mechanism of Chain-Growth Polymerization

  • Initiation involves the thermal or photochemical homolytic cleavage of an initiator (e.g., benzoyl peroxide or azobisisobutyronitrile) to generate free radicals.
  • Propagation proceeds via rapid successive addition of monomer units to the active center, maintaining the active species at the chain terminus.
  • Termination occurs irreversibly via radical combination (coupling) or disproportionation between two growing macroradicals.

Ziegler-Natta Catalysis and Stereoregular Polymers

  • Ziegler-Natta catalysts are heterogeneous mixtures composed of transition metal halides (e.g., TiCl4) and organometallic co-catalysts (e.g., Al(C2H5)3).
  • Coordination polymerization allows precise control over stereochemistry, yielding isotactic or syndiotactic polymers instead of atactic forms.
  • High-density polyethylene (HDPE) and isotactic polypropylene are produced under mild pressures using these coordination catalysts.

Important Commercial Addition Polymers

  • Polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE), known as Teflon, is synthesized via free-radical polymerization of tetrafluoroethylene under high pressure with persulfate catalysts.
  • Buna-S is a copolymer derived from 1,3-butadiene and styrene in a 3:1 ratio using sodium or emulsion free-radical catalysis.
  • Neoprene (polychloroprene) is formed by the free-radical addition of 2-chloro-1,3-butadiene, possessing high chemical resistance and tensile strength.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Most monosubstituted alkenes yield atactic polymers during standard free-radical addition.

Observation: Vinylidene chloride and certain fluorinated monomers exhibit predominantly syndiotactic tendencies. — Steric hindrance and dipole-dipole repulsion between bulky substituents direct the incoming monomer approach in a highly ordered alternating fashion.

Exception Allylic monomers undergo normal high-degree chain propagation.

Observation: Allyl acetate and similar allylic compounds undergo degradative chain transfer yielding very low molecular weight oligomers. — The abstraction of allylic hydrogen atoms produces resonance-stabilized radicals that are too stable to add further monomer units.

Exception Addition polymers retain the exact empirical formula of their constituent monomers.

Observation: Polymers formed from conjugated dienes (e.g., isoprene) undergo 1,4-addition alongside 1,2-addition, altering local connectivity. — Allylic resonance stabilization of the intermediate propagating carbanion or radical allows multi-site attack on the diene system.

Monomer to Polymer Conversion Rule
n (CH2=CH-X) → [-CH2-CH(X)-]n

Application: Remove the pi bond, open the terminal valencies with single bonds, and enclose the repeating unit in brackets with subscript n.

Square Root Dependence of Initiator
Rp ∝ [Initiator]1/2

Application: When solving rate problems, quadrupling the initiator concentration doubles the rate of polymerization.

Copolymer Composition Identification
Buna-S = Butadiene + Styrene; Buna-N = Butadiene + Acrylonitrile

Application: Use the suffix letters (S for Styrene, N for Nitrile/Acrylonitrile) to instantly identify the co-monomers in synthetic rubbers.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Teflon (PTFE) is thermally stable and chemically inert due to the high bond dissociation energy of the C-F bond.
  • Natural rubber is a cis-1,4-polyisoprene, whereas gutta-percha is the trans-1,4-stereoisomer.
  • Buna-N is a copolymer of 1,3-butadiene and acrylonitrile, highly resistant to petrol, lubricating oil, and organic solvents.
  • Low-density polyethylene (LDPE) is manufactured at high pressure (1000-2000 atm) and high temperature using trace oxygen or peroxide initiator.
  • Ziegler-Natta catalyst allows the stereospecific polymerization of alkenes yielding high-density linear polymers.
  • Azo-bis-isobutyronitrile (AIBN) decomposes thermally to yield stable nitrogen gas and two carbon-centered radicals.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the repeating unit structure with the monomer structure by retaining the double bond inside the polymer chain brackets.
  • Assuming condensation and addition polymers both release water or small molecules during synthesis.
  • Forgetting that chain transfer agents decrease the average molecular weight without stopping the overall polymerization rate.
  • Misidentifying Buna-S as a homopolymer instead of a copolymer containing styrene.

⚡ Quick Revision: Addition Polymerization

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Free Radical Chain Mechanism

Chain-growth polymerization proceeds via three distinct kinetic steps: initiation via radical generators, rapid monomer addition during propagation, and combination or disproportionation during termination.

Stereoregular Polymers

Coordination catalysts like TiCl4 and Al(C2H5)3 control stereochemistry, producing isotactic and syndiotactic arrangements with enhanced crystallinity and melting points.

Synthetic Rubbers and Elastomers

Copolymers such as Buna-S and Buna-N provide elastomeric properties and chemical resistance through tailored diene and vinyl monomer ratios.

Kinetics of Addition Polymerization

The rate of polymerization is directly proportional to the monomer concentration and the square root of the initiator concentration under steady-state conditions.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Degree of Polymerization (DP (Xn))
Total number of monomeric units incorporated in a polymer chain.
Initiator (R-R / Peroxide)
Compound that thermally or photochemically cleaves to generate free radicals.
Copolymer (Mixed Polymer)
Macromolecule formed from two or more distinct monomer species.
Isotactic (Same Side)
Stereoisomer where all substituent groups lie on the same side of the carbon backbone.
Chain Transfer Agent (CTA)
Substance that reacts with a growing chain to terminate it while initiating a new one.
Atactic (Random)
Polymer configuration where substituent groups are arranged randomly along the chain.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Rp ∝ [M][I]1/2 for radical addition polymerization.
  • Natural rubber is cis-1,4-polyisoprene; gutta-percha is trans-1,4-polyisoprene.
  • Ziegler-Natta catalyst system consists of TiCl4 and Al(C2H5)3.
  • Buna-S contains 1,3-butadiene and styrene; Buna-N contains 1,3-butadiene and acrylonitrile.
  • PTFE (Teflon) is obtained by polymerizing tetrafluoroethylene under high pressure.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Addition polymerization requires unsaturated monomers and proceeds without by-product elimination.
  • Reaction kinetics exhibit half-order dependence on initiator concentration.
  • Stereochemistry is rigidly controlled using coordination catalysts.
  • Commercial polymers like PVC, Teflon, and Buna rubbers are synthesized via this pathway.

⚖️ Key Differences

HDPE VS LDPE

HDPE is linear with high density and crystallinity (Ziegler-Natta catalyst), whereas LDPE is branched with low density and flexibility (high-pressure free radical).
