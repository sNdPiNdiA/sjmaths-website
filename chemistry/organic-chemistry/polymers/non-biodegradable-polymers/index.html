Organic Chemistry

Non-biodegradable polymers

Non-biodegradable polymers are synthetic or modified natural macromolecules that resist degradation by microbial enzymes and environmental weathering. Their carbon-carbon backbones and stable functional linkages prevent enzymatic cleavage, causing extreme persistence in ecosystems.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Synthesis and Structure of Polyolefins

  • Polyethylene (PE) is synthesized via free-radical polymerization of ethylene at high pressure yielding Low-Density Polyethylene (LDPE), or via coordination polymerization using Ziegler-Natta catalysts (TiCl4 + Al(C2H5)3) yielding High-Density Polyethylene (HDPE).
  • Polypropylene (PP) is produced via stereospecific polymerization using Ziegler-Natta catalysts, predominantly yielding isotactic polypropylene with high crystallinity and melting point.
  • Polystyrene (PS) is formed by free-radical vinyl polymerization of styrene, possessing a bulky phenyl pendant group that restricts chain mobility and imparts rigidity and brittleness.
  • The complete absence of polar functional groups in polyolefins makes them completely inert to hydrolytic and enzymatic attack, ensuring long-term environmental persistence.

Halogenated and Heterochain Non-Biodegradable Polymers

  • Polyvinyl chloride (PVC) is manufactured via free-radical suspension or emulsion polymerization of vinyl chloride monomer, yielding a rigid material stabilized commercially by organotin or lead compounds.
  • Polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) is synthesized via addition polymerization of tetrafluoroethylene under pressure in the presence of persulfate catalysts, resulting in extreme thermal and chemical inertness due to the strong C-F bond energy (485 kJ/mol).
  • Polyesters like Polyethylene terephthalate (PET) are condensation polymers formed via transesterification of dimethyl terephthalate with ethylene glycol or direct esterification with terephthalic acid.
  • Polyamides such as Nylon-6,6 possess strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding between adjacent amide linkages, providing high tensile strength and resistance to microbial cleavage.

Degradation Resistance and Environmental Impact

  • Microbial enzymes such as esterases, proteases, and cellulases cannot recognize or cleave the stable aliphatic C-C backbones found in polyolefins and vinyl polymers.
  • Photo-oxidation via solar UV radiation initiates free-radical degradation on polymer surfaces, causing cross-linking or chain scission but failing to mineralize the bulk material rapidly.
  • Accumulation of non-biodegradable plastics leads to microplastic generation through mechanical weathering, posing persistent bioaccumulation risks in aquatic and terrestrial food webs.
  • Thermal recycling, pyrolysis, and chemical depolymerization serve as industrial alternatives to mechanical recycling for reclaiming monomers from waste heterochain polymers.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception All addition polymers from vinyl monomers are strictly non-biodegradable.

Observation: Polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) — Presence of hydroxyl groups on alternate carbon atoms allows specific microbial oxidation and cleavage of the carbon backbone.

Exception Condensation polymers such as polyesters are generally biodegradable.

Observation: Polyethylene terephthalate (PET) — High crystallinity and hydrophobic aromatic benzene rings severely restrict water penetration and enzymatic attack.

Exception Fluoropolymers are completely chemically inert.

Observation: Alkali metals in liquid ammonia — Reactive metals can abstract fluorine atoms from PTFE, causing defluorination and structural breakdown.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Ziegler-Natta Catalyst Components
Transition Metal Halide + Organometallic Co-catalyst

Application: Remember 'Titanium-Aluminum' pair: TiCl4 (or TiCl3) + Al(C2H5)3 for linear HDPE.

Nylon Nomenclature Trick
Nylon-x,y

Application: x = carbons in diamine, y = carbons in dicarboxylic acid. Nylon-6,6 has 6 carbons in hexamethylenediamine and 6 in adipic acid.

PDI Rule for Polymers
PDI = M_w / M_n \ge 1

Application: If PDI = 1, polymer is monodisperse (natural polymers like proteins). If PDI > 1, polymer is polydisperse (synthetic addition polymers).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Ziegler-Natta catalyst consists of titanium tetrachloride and triethylaluminum.
  • PTFE (Teflon) is prepared by polymerizing tetrafluoroethylene under high pressure with persulfate catalyst.
  • Polydispersity index (PDI) for natural polymers is equal to 1, indicating monodispersity.
  • Nylon-6 is synthesized by ring-opening polymerization of caprolactam.
  • PET is a condensation copolymer of ethylene glycol and terephthalic acid.
  • PVC contains approximately 56% chlorine by mass and requires plasticizers like phthalates for flexibility.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing LDPE and HDPE preparation conditions: LDPE requires high pressure and radical initiators, while HDPE uses low pressure and coordination catalysts.
  • Assuming all condensation polymers are biodegradable; aromatic polyesters like PET are highly resistant to enzymatic degradation.
  • Forgetting that natural rubber is an addition polymer of isoprene, whereas gutta-percha is its trans-isomer.
  • Incorrectly calculating PDI by dividing M_n by M_w instead of M_w by M_n.

⚡ Quick Revision: Non-biodegradable polymers

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Chain-Growth vs Step-Growth Polymerization

Chain-growth polymerization proceeds via initiation, propagation, and termination steps involving reactive intermediates like radicals or ions. Step-growth polymerization occurs via condensation reactions between bifunctional monomers with elimination of small molecules like water.

Stereoregularity in Polymers

Tacticity defines the stereochemical arrangement of chiral centers along the polymer backbone. Isotactic polymers have substituents on the same side, syndiotactic on alternating sides, and atactic in random orientations, heavily influencing crystallinity.

Thermal Behavior: Tg and Tm

The glass transition temperature (Tg) marks the onset of segmental motion in amorphous regions, while the melting temperature (Tm) corresponds to the transition of crystalline domains into a viscous liquid.

Environmental Resistance Factors

Resistance to biodegradation depends on high molecular weight, hydrophobic character, absence of hydrolyzable bonds, and high crystallinity which restricts water and enzyme penetration.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Homopolymer (-[M]n-)
Polymer derived from a single monomeric species.
Copolymer (-[M1-M2]n-)
Polymer derived from two or more different monomers.
Elastomer (Cross-linked network)
Polymer with elastic properties exhibiting weak intermolecular forces.
Thermoplastic (Linear/branched)
Polymer that softens on heating and hardens on cooling repeatedly.
Thermosetting (Cross-linked)
Polymer that undergoes permanent chemical cross-heating to form an infusible insoluble network.
Tacticity (Stereochemical arrangement)
Geometric arrangement of pendant groups along the polymer chain skeleton.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Ziegler-Natta catalyst: TiCl4 + Al(C2H5)3
  • Teflon formula: -(CF2-CF2)n-
  • Caprolactam structure for Nylon-6: 7-membered lactam ring
  • Terylene (PET) monomer units: Ethylene glycol and terephthalic acid
  • PDI formula: M_w / M_n

📌 Quick Recall

  • Non-biodegradable polymers possess stable C-C backbones and lack hydrolyzable linkages.
  • Polyethylene exists in LDPE (branched, low density) and HDPE (linear, high density) forms.
  • Halogenated polymers like PVC and PTFE offer high chemical and thermal stability.
  • Condensation polymers like PET and Nylon exhibit high tensile strength due to strong intermolecular forces.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nylon-6 VS Nylon-6,6

Nylon-6 is derived from a single 6-carbon monomer (caprolactam), while Nylon-6,6 is a copolymer from two 6-carbon monomers (hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid).

Which of the following catalysts is used for the polymerization of ethylene to high-density polyethylene (HDPE)?

Explanation: Ziegler-Natta catalyst (mixtures of titanium tetrachloride and triethylaluminum) is used to produce linear high-density polyethylene at low pressures.

Question 2 of 18

Polytetrafluoroethylene (Teflon) is synthesized from which monomer?

Explanation: PTFE is produced by the addition polymerization of tetrafluoroethylene under pressure in the presence of a persulfate catalyst.

Question 3 of 18

What is the polydispersity index (PDI) for a strictly monodisperse synthetic or natural polymer?

Explanation: For a monodisperse polymer, weight-average molecular mass equals number-average molecular mass (M_w = M_n), resulting in a PDI of exactly 1.

Question 4 of 18

Which polymer is formed by the ring-opening polymerization of caprolactam?

Explanation: Nylon-6 is prepared by heating caprolactam with water at high temperatures, initiating ring-opening polymerization.

Question 5 of 18

Terylene (PET) is a condensation polymer of ethylene glycol and:

Explanation: PET (Dacron or Terylene) is formed by the condensation reaction between ethylene glycol and terephthalic acid.

Question 6 of 18

Which structural feature is primarily responsible for the non-biodegradable nature of polyolefins?

Explanation: Microbial enzymes lack the specific catalytic mechanisms required to cleave strong, non-polar aliphatic C-C bonds in polyolefins.

Question 7 of 18

Which of the following is a copolymer?

Explanation: Buna-S is a copolymer synthesized from 1,3-butadiene and styrene.

Question 8 of 18

Low-Density Polyethylene (LDPE) differs from High-Density Polyethylene (HDPE) primarily in having:

Explanation: LDPE is synthesized via free-radical polymerization at high pressures, resulting in a high degree of chain branching which reduces density and crystallinity.

Question 9 of 18

Which monomer is used in the manufacture of Orlon (polyacrylonitrile)?

Explanation: Orlon is polyacrylonitrile, produced by the polymerization of acrylonitrile (vinyl cyanide).

Question 10 of 18

The intermolecular forces present in Nylon-6,6 are primarily:

Explanation: Polyamides like Nylon-6,6 feature strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding between N-H and C=O groups along adjacent chains.

Question 11 of 18

Which of the following is classed as a thermosetting polymer?

Explanation: Bakelite is a cross-linked condensation polymer of phenol and formaldehyde that forms an infusible network upon heating.

Question 12 of 18

Glyptal polymer is obtained by reacting ethylene glycol with:

Explanation: Glyptal is a polyester resin formed by the condensation reaction of ethylene glycol and phthalic acid.

Question 13 of 18

Natural rubber is a polymer of:

Explanation: Natural rubber is cis-polyisoprene, formed by the polymerization of isoprene (2-methyl-1,3-butadiene).

Question 14 of 18

Neoprene is a synthetic elastomer derived from:

Explanation: Neoprene is polychloroprene, produced by the free-radical polymerization of chloroprene (2-chlorobut-1,3-diene).

Question 15 of 18

Which of the following polymers contains fluorine?

Explanation: Teflon (PTFE) is polytetrafluoroethylene, containing repeating -CF2- units.

Question 16 of 18

The number-average molecular mass (M_n) of a polymer is calculated using:

Explanation: Colligative properties such as osmotic pressure depend on the number of particles and are used to determine M_n.

Question 17 of 18

Which property is typical of addition polymers?

Explanation: Addition polymers are formed by the successive addition of unsaturated monomer molecules (alkenes or dienes) without loss of small molecules.

Question 18 of 18

What is the repeating unit of natural rubber?

Explanation: Natural rubber consists of cis-1,4-polyisoprene chains, which grant elasticity due to coiled conformations.
