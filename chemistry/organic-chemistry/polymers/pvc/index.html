Polyvinyl Chloride

Polyvinyl chloride (PVC) is a synthetic thermoplastic polymer produced via the free-radical polymerization of vinyl chloride monomer (VCM). It exhibits high mechanical strength, chemical resistance, and rigidity, making it one of the most versatile commodity plastics.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Monomer Synthesis and Polymerization Mechanism

  • Vinyl chloride is industrially synthesized via the oxychlorination of ethylene using hydrochloric acid and oxygen over a copper catalyst: 2CH2=CH2 + 4HCl + O2 → 2CH2=CHCl + 2H2O.
  • Polymerization proceeds predominantly via a free-radical mechanism, typically initiated by peroxides or azo compounds like AIBN in suspension or emulsion processes.
  • The reaction is exothermic with ΔH ≈ −100 kJ/mol, requiring precise heat transfer control in industrial autoclaves.
  • The resulting polymer has a head-to-tail stereoregularity, predominantly atactic with slight syndiotactic tendencies depending on reaction temperature.

Structure, Properties, and Degradation

  • The presence of polar C-Cl bonds creates strong dipole-dipole interactions between polymer chains, leading to high rigidity and low solvent solubility.
  • Rigid PVC (uPVC) has a glass transition temperature (Tg) around 80°C to 85°C and melts with decomposition near 210°C.
  • Thermal degradation involves dehydrochlorination (loss of HCl), forming conjugated polyene sequences that cause discoloration (yellowing to blackening) and structural embrittlement.
  • Stabilizers such as lead, tin, or calcium-zinc soaps are universally added during processing to scavenge evolved HCl and prevent thermal degradation.

Plasticization and Applications

  • The incorporation of phthalate plasticizers (e.g., DOP, DINP) intercalates between polymer chains, lowering intermolecular forces and reducing Tg to yield flexible PVC.
  • Rigid PVC is utilized in structural applications such as drainage pipes, window frames, and siding due to its weathering and chemical resistance.
  • Flexible PVC finds extensive use in electrical wire insulation, medical tubing, blood bags, and synthetic leather.
  • PVC is inherently flame-retardant due to its high chlorine content (approx. 56.8%), which quenches radical flame reactions upon ignition.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Typical Addition Polymers are Non-Polar and Insoluble in Polar Solvents

Observation: PVC is Polar and Soluble in Specific Polar Solvents — The presence of strongly electronegative chlorine atoms introduces a permanent dipole moment, making it soluble in ketones (like cyclohexanone) and tetrahydrofuran (THF).

Exception Most Thermoplastics Melt Sharply before Decomposition

Observation: PVC Decompantes Near or Below its Melting Point — Cleavage of C-Cl bonds and autocatalytic elimination of HCl occur at temperatures required to melt the rigid crystalline domains, necessitating thermal stabilizers.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Chlorine Content Calculation
% Cl = (Atomic weight of Cl / Molecular weight of repeat unit) × 100

Application: For PVC −[CH2−CHCl]−n, MW = 12.01(2) + 1.008(3) + 35.45 = 62.5 g/mol. % Cl = (35.45 / 62.5) × 100 ≈ 56.8%.

Plasticizer Mnemonic
P-P-P (Polar, Phthalate, Plasticizer)

Application: Remember that flexible PVC requires secondary polar molecules (phthalates) to disrupt chain-chain dipole interactions.

Degradation Warning
PVC + Heat → HCl + Polyene

Application: Always associate thermal processing of PVC with the elimination of hydrogen chloride gas and color development due to conjugated double bonds.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • PVC is prepared by the addition polymerization of vinyl chloride monomer.
  • The empirical formula of polyvinyl chloride is (C2H3Cl)n.
  • PVC has a high chlorine content of approximately 56.8% by mass.
  • Thermal degradation of PVC releases toxic hydrogen chloride (HCl) gas.
  • Plasticizers are added to PVC to lower its glass transition temperature and impart flexibility.
  • Cyclohexanone and tetrahydrofuran (THF) are effective solvents for dissolving PVC.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming PVC is a condensation polymer because of HCl evolution during processing (it is actually an addition polymer formed from an alkene).
  • Confusing the role of plasticizers with cross-linking agents (plasticizers lower intermolecular forces, whereas cross-linking agents tie chains together).
  • Forgetting that pure PVC decomposes before melting without the aid of stabilizers and lubricants.
  • Stating that PVC is completely amorphous (it possesses short-range syndiotactic crystalline domains, approx. 10% crystallinity).

⚡ Quick Revision: Polyvinyl Chloride

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Monomer Preparation

Vinyl chloride is produced industrially by thermal cracking of 1,2-dichloroethane or via the catalytic oxychlorination of ethylene.

Polymerization Process

Free-radical chain-growth polymerization yields high molecular weight atactic polymer chains with head-to-tail isomerism.

Thermal Stability

Instability to heat requires heavy metal stabilizers or organotin compounds to prevent autocatalytic elimination of HCl.

Modification and Use

Addition of plasticizers transitions rigid construction material into flexible consumer grades used in wiring and medical devices.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Vinyl Chloride (CH2=CHCl)
Monomer gas used in PVC synthesis; MW = 62.50 g/mol.
uPVC (Unplasticized PVC)
Rigid form of PVC used for structural construction components.
Plasticizer (Phthalate Esters)
High boiling point organic liquid added to impart flexibility.
Dehydrochlorination (-HCl Elimination)
Thermal degradation pathway yielding conjugated polyenes.
Atactic (Random Stereocenter)
Stereochemical configuration where chlorine atoms are randomly distributed.
Stabilizer (HCl Scavenger)
Additive used to prevent thermal degradation during processing.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Monomer: Vinyl chloride (chloroethene)
  • Polymer repeat unit: −[CH2−CHCl]−
  • Chlorine mass percentage: ~56.8%
  • Primary degradation product: HCl gas
  • Solvent: Tetrahydrofuran (THF)

📌 Quick Recall

  • PVC is a widely used halogenated addition polymer.
  • Polar C-Cl bonds create high stiffness and solvent resistance.
  • Thermal degradation requires stabilization via metal soaps.
  • Plasticizers modify the polymer into flexible application grades.

⚖️ Key Differences

Rigid PVC VS Plasticized PVC

Rigid PVC contains no plasticizers and has high Tg, whereas plasticized PVC contains substantial plasticizers lowering Tg.

Which functional group is present along the backbone of a PVC chain?

Question 14 of 19

What causes the yellowing or blackening of PVC when overheated?

Explanation: Successive elimination of HCl creates extended conjugated double-bond systems (polyenes) that absorb visible light, causing discoloration.

Question 15 of 19

Which of the following represents the correct repeating unit of PVC?

Question 16 of 19

What class of compounds is most commonly used as plasticizers for PVC?

Explanation: Phthalates like dioctyl phthalate (DOP) are traditional plasticizers blended with PVC to impart flexibility.

Question 17 of 19

Why is pure PVC rigid and brittle at room temperature?

Explanation: The polar C-Cl bonds create strong dipole-dipole interactions between chains, restricting movement and causing rigidity.

Question 18 of 19

Which industrial method accounts for the largest volume of PVC production?

Explanation: Suspension polymerization is the predominant industrial method used to produce general-purpose PVC resins.

Question 19 of 19
