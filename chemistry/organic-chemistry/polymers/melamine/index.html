Melamine

Melamine (1,3,5-triazine-2,4,6-triamine) is an organic nitrogenous heterocyclic compound with the chemical formula C3H6N6. It undergoes polycondensation reactions with formaldehyde to form durable thermosetting amino resins.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structure and Synthesis of Melamine

  • Melamine is structurally classified as a s-triazine derivative containing three amino substituents symmetrically arranged around the aromatic-like ring.
  • Commercial synthesis typically proceeds via the thermal decomposition of urea or dicyandiamide at elevated temperatures under catalytic pressure.
  • The molecule exhibits extensive resonance stabilization due to alternating carbon and nitrogen atoms within the planar six-membered ring.

Polymerization and Resin Formation

  • Reaction of melamine with formaldehyde yields mono-, di-, and trimethylolmelamine intermediates through nucleophilic addition at the exocyclic amino groups.
  • Acid-catalyzed condensation polymerization of methylol derivatives eliminates water molecules to form highly cross-linked three-dimensional networks.
  • Cured melamine-formaldehyde (MF) resins display exceptional hardness, scratch resistance, heat tolerance, and chemical inertness.

Chemical Properties and Applications

  • Melamine acts as a weak base (pKb ≈ 9) due to lone pair availability on the ring nitrogen atoms, forming stable salts with strong acids.
  • High nitrogen content (66.6% by mass) makes it a primary component in flame retardants, as thermal degradation releases inert nitrogen gas.
  • Extensively utilized in the manufacture of laminates (Formica), tableware, coatings, and industrial adhesives.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Most aromatic nitrogen heterocycles are basic due to pyridine-type nitrogen.

Observation: Melamine is a significantly weaker base than aliphatic amines. — Electron-withdrawing character of the symmetrical triazine ring delocalizes the lone pairs.

Exception Nitrogen-rich compounds are typically unstable and explosive.

Observation: Melamine exhibits high thermal stability up to its sublimation point. — Strong aromatic resonance energy and intermolecular hydrogen bonding stabilize the lattice.

Exception Standard Kjeldahl protein analysis measures actual protein content.

Observation: Adulteration with melamine falsely elevates total protein readings. — The method measures total nitrogen without distinguishing between peptide bonds and non-protein nitrogen.

  • Molecular formula of melamine is C3H6N6 with a molar mass of 126.12 g mol-1.

🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing melamine with urea; melamine contains a stable s-triazine ring, whereas urea is a simple carbonyl diamide.
  • Assuming melamine-formaldehyde is a thermoplastic; it is a rigid thermosetting polymer.
  • Neglecting the role of pH catalysts in the condensation stage of resin synthesis.
  • Forgetting that melamine sublimes rather than melting directly at atmospheric pressure.

⚡ Quick Revision: Melamine

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Heterocyclic Structure

Melamine features a symmetrical s-triazine ring substituted with three amino groups, providing high rigidity and resonance stability.

Polymerization Mechanism

Reaction with formaldehyde produces methylol derivatives which undergo acid-catalyzed cross-linking to yield durable resins.

Acid-Base Behavior

Ring and exocyclic nitrogens allow protonation, forming stable complexes and salts such as melamine cyanurate.

Industrial Utility

Valued for exceptional surface hardness, thermal stability, and flame-retardant properties in commercial applications.

Melamine VS undefined

What is the molecular weight of melamine (C3H6N6)?

Solution: (3 × 12.01) + (6 × 1.008) + (6 × 14.01) = 36.03 + 6.048 + 84.06 = 126.14 ≈ 126.12 g mol-1.

Question 2 of 10
Melamine is synthesized commercially from which raw material?

Solution: Industrial synthesis of melamine is carried out via the catalytic pyrolysis of urea.

Question 3 of 10
Which structural feature contributes to the high melting/sublimation behavior of melamine?

Solution: Extensive network of intermolecular hydrogen bonds between amino groups and ring nitrogens leads to high sublimation temperatures.

Question 4 of 10
In acid-base reactions, melamine acts as a:

Solution: Melamine accepts protons at the nitrogen lone pairs, functioning as a Bronsted-Lowry base.

Question 5 of 10
What type of reaction mechanism is involved in the formation of methylolmelamine?

Solution: Amino nitrogen attacks the electrophilic carbonyl carbon of formaldehyde via nucleophilic addition.

Question 6 of 10
Which property makes melamine resins suitable for electrical insulators?

Solution: Cured melamine-formaldehyde resins possess excellent electrical insulation and arc resistance properties.

Question 7 of 10
What is the main byproduct of the industrial synthesis of melamine from urea?

Solution: The pyrolysis of urea yields melamine alongside carbon dioxide and ammonia gas.

Question 8 of 10
Melamine cyanurate is formed by combining melamine and cyanuric acid in a ratio of:

Solution: Melamine and cyanuric acid form a 1:1 alternating supramolecular tape network linked by hydrogen bonds.

Question 9 of 10
Why is melamine considered hazardous when ingested in high amounts?

Solution: Melamine complexes with cyanuric acid to form insoluble crystals that precipitate in renal tubules.

Question 10 of 10
What is the geometry of the s-triazine ring in melamine?

Solution: The s-triazine ring is planar with sp2 hybridized carbon and nitrogen atoms.
