Condensation Polymerization

Condensation polymerization involves the stepwise reaction of bifunctional or polyfunctional monomers accompanied by the elimination of small molecules like H2O, HCl, or CH3OH. The growing polymer chain is formed through functional group interactions without requiring an external initiator.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Mechanism and Functional Group Interconversions

  • Monomers must possess at least two reactive functional groups per molecule such as -OH, -COOH, -NH2, or -COX.
  • The reaction proceeds via standard organic mechanisms like esterification, amidation, or transesterification.
  • High conversions (p > 0.99) are required to achieve sufficiently high molecular weights suitable for fiber and engineering applications.
  • Elimination of condensation byproducts (water or alcohol) must be driven to completion by applying a vacuum.

Homopolymers and Copolymers Synthesis

  • Polyamides (Nylons) are synthesized via condensation of diamines with dicarboxylic acids or ring-opening of lactams.
  • Polyesters (e.g., Dacron/Terylene) are produced via transesterification between dimethyl terephthalate and ethylene glycol.
  • Bakelite is a thermosetting condensation polymer formed by the acid- or base-catalyzed reaction of phenol and formaldehyde.
  • Melamine-formaldehyde resin is formed by condensation of melamine with formaldehyde, yielding cross-linked networks.

Molecular Weight Distribution and Kinetics

  • Step-growth kinetics dictates that monomers disappear rapidly in the early stages of the reaction, forming low oligomers.
  • High molecular weight polymers are formed only at very high fractional conversions (p > 0.95) near the reaction end.
  • The theoretical Polydispersity Index (PDI) for step-growth polymers undergoing equal reactivity of functional groups is 2.0.
  • Cross-linking leads to gelation, marked by an abrupt increase in viscosity and loss of solubility when critical conversion pc is reached.
📐 Key Formulas & Equations
Modified Carothers Equation for Non-Stoichiometric Systems
Xn = (1 + r) / (1 + r - 2rp)
Conditions: Where r = NA / NB (≤ 1), ratio of moles of reactants
Units: Xn, r, and p are dimensionless
Number Average Molecular Weight
Mn = Xn × M0
Conditions: Where M0 is the molecular weight of the repeating unit
Units: Mn and M0 in g mol-1
Polydispersity Index (PDI)
PDI = Mw / Mn
Conditions: Applicable to all synthetic polymers
Units: PDI is dimensionless (equal to 1 for monodisperse polymers)
Carothers Equation for Degree of Polymerization
Xn = 1 / (1 - p)
Conditions: Stoichiometric equivalent of bifunctional monomers
Units: Xn (dimensionless number average degree of polymerization), p (fractional monomer conversion)
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Condensation polymers always eliminate small molecules like water or HCl.

Observation: Polyurethanes formed via step-growth addition polymerization — Reaction of diisocyanates with diols involves addition of the -OH proton across the -N=C=O double bond without byproduct elimination.

Exception Step-growth polymers have a theoretical PDI of 2.0.

Observation: Non-stoichiometric reactant feeds or unequal functional group reactivity — Deviations from equimolar proportions truncate chain growth, altering the molecular weight distribution and increasing PDI.

Exception Polyamides are synthesized from dicarboxylic acids and diamines.

Observation: Kevlar synthesis utilizes aromatic diamines and terephthaloyl chloride — Acid chlorides provide high reactivity and eliminate HCl instead of water, driving the reaction rapidly without high-temperature equilibria.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Carothers Conversion Calculation Shortcut
If p = 0.99, Xn = 1 / (1 - 0.99) = 100

Application: Directly calculate degree of polymerization by putting conversion in decimal form into 1/(1-p).

Nylon Nomenclature Mnemonic
Nylon x,y -> x carbons in diamine, y carbons in diacid

Application: Nylon-6,6 has 6 carbons in hexamethylenediamine and 6 carbons in adipic acid.

Functional Group Matching
(-COOH + -OH → Ester) | (-COOH + -NH2 → Amide)

Application: Identify polymer class instantly by observing linkage type: -COO- for polyester and -CONH- for polyamide.

Step-Growth (Condensation) vs Chain-Growth (Addition) Polymerization
ParameterCondensation PolymerizationAddition Polymerization
Monomer RequirementBifunctional or polyfunctional groupsUnsaturated bonds (alkenes, alkynes) or cyclic rings
ByproductSmall molecules eliminated (H2O, HCl)None
Monomer ConsumptionConsumed early in the reactionExists throughout, decreases slowly
Molecular WeightBuilds up slowly at very high conversionHigh molecular weight formed instantly

🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing condensation polymerization with addition polymerization regarding byproduct elimination.
  • Assuming high molecular weight is formed at low monomer conversions in step-growth systems.
  • Forgetting that non-stoichiometric feed ratios limit maximum attainable molecular weight.
  • Incorrectly assigning the PDI value of step-growth polymers as 1.0 instead of 2.0.

⚡ Quick Revision: Condensation Polymerization

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Monomer Functionality

Monomers must contain at least two reactive functional groups to permit linear or network chain growth via step-wise condensation reactions.

Carothers Equation

Relates degree of polymerization (Xn) to fractional conversion (p) via Xn = 1 / (1 - p).

Common Condensation Polymers

Includes Nylons (polyamides), Dacron (polyesters), Bakelite (phenol-formaldehyde), and Melamine resins.

Kinetics and PDI

Monomers react quickly to form oligomers, and high molecular weight requires high conversion with a theoretical PDI of 2.0.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Degree of Polymerization (Xn)
Average number of repeating units per polymer molecule.
Fractional Conversion (p)
Fraction of functional groups that have reacted.
Polydispersity Index (PDI = Mw / Mn)
Measure of the molecular weight distribution breadth.
Step-Growth (Condensation)
Polymerization proceeding step-by-step through functional group pairing.
Thermosetting (Cross-linked polymer)
Polymer that becomes permanently hard upon heating due to cross-linking.
Transesterification (Ester exchange)
Reaction between an ester and an alcohol used in polyester synthesis.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carothers equation: Xn = 1 / (1 - p)
  • Theoretical PDI for condensation polymers = 2.0
  • Nylon-6,6 contains 6 carbons in diamine and 6 in diacid
  • Dacron is formed from ethylene glycol and dimethyl terephthalate
  • Bakelite is a phenol-formaldehyde polymer

📌 Quick Recall

  • Condensation polymers require bifunctional monomers and eliminate small molecules.
  • High conversion is mandatory to achieve useful high molecular weights.
  • Polyamides and polyesters represent the major classes of linear condensation polymers.
  • Cross-linked networks form when polyfunctional monomers are present.

⚖️ Key Differences

Nylon-6 VS Nylon-6,6

Nylon-6 is formed by ring-opening polymerization of caprolactam (6 carbons), whereas Nylon-6,6 is a condensation polymer of hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid (6+6 carbons).
