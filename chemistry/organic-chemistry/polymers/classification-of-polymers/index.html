Classification of Polymers

Polymers are high molecular mass macromolecular structures formed by joining repeating structural units derived from monomers via covalent bonds. They are systematically classified based on their source, structure, mode of synthesis, molecular forces, and thermal response.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Classification Based on Source and Structure

  • Natural polymers originate from plants and animals, including proteins, cellulose, starch, and natural rubber.
  • Synthetic polymers are man-made via laboratory or industrial synthesis, such as nylon-6,6, polytetrafluoroethylene (Teflon), and Buna-S.
  • Linear polymers consist of long, straight chains packed closely, yielding high tensile strength and high density (e.g., high-density polyethylene, HDPE).
  • Branched-chain polymers contain linear chains with side branches, resulting in irregular packing and lower density (e.g., low-density polyethylene, LDPE).
  • Cross-linked or network polymers possess covalent bonds linking adjacent linear chains, forming rigid, infusible three-dimensional matrices (e.g., bakelite, melamine).

Classification Based on Synthesis Mechanism and Forces

  • Addition polymers (chain-growth) form through self-addition of unsaturated monomers without loss of small molecules (e.g., polyethene, PVC).
  • Condensation polymers (step-growth) form via intermolecular condensation reactions accompanied by the elimination of small molecules like H2O, HCl, or ROH (e.g., dacron, nylon-6,6).
  • Elastomers are rubber-like solids displaying weak intermolecular forces that allow high elastic stretch (e.g., vulcanized rubber, neoprene).
  • Fibers possess strong intermolecular forces such as hydrogen bonding or dipole-dipole interactions, imparting high tensile strength and crystalline nature (e.g., nylon-6,6, terylene).
  • Thermoplastics soften upon heating and harden upon cooling reversibly, exhibiting linear or branched topologies (e.g., polythene, polystyrene).

Thermosetting Polymers and Copolymerization

  • Thermosetting polymers undergo extensive cross-linking during heating to form a permanent, insoluble, and infusible network that degrades rather than melts (e.g., bakelite, urea-formaldehyde).
  • Homopolymers are derived from the polymerization of a single monomeric species (e.g., polyethene).
  • Copolymers are synthesized from two or more different monomer species, categorized as statistical, block, graft, or alternating copolymers.
  • Biodegradable polymers contain functional groups susceptible to enzymatic or hydrolytic cleavage, enabling environmental degradation (e.g., polyhydroxybutyrate-co-β-hydroxyvalerate, PHBV).

Number-Average Molecular Mass
M_n = ∑ N_i M_i∑ N_i
Conditions: N_i = Number of polymer molecules of mass M_i
Units: g mol-1
Weight-Average Molecular Mass
M_w = ∑ N_i M_i^2∑ N_i M_i
Conditions: Calculated from light scattering and ultracentrifugation data
Units: g mol-1
Polydispersity Index (PDI)
PDI = M_wM_n
Conditions: PDI = 1 for monodisperse polymers; PDI > 1 for synthetic heterodisperse polymers
Units: Dimensionless
Degree of Polymerization (DP)
DP = M_nM_0
Conditions: M_n = Number average molecular mass, M_0 = Molar mass of the repeating monomer unit
Units: Dimensionless integer
⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Most addition polymers are formed from monomers containing carbon-carbon double bonds.

Observation: Cyclic ethers (like ethylene oxide) and cyclic amides (like caprolactam) undergo ring-opening polymerization which proceeds by a chain-growth mechanism without the elimination of small molecules. — Ring strain release provides the thermodynamic driving force for chain propagation without requiring condensation by-products.

Exception Polymers generally exhibit a sharp melting point like low-molecular-weight crystalline solids.

Observation: Synthetic polymers melt over a broad temperature range and often exhibit a distinct glass transition temperature (T_g) before melting. — Polymers are polydisperse mixtures of varying chain lengths and degrees of crystallinity rather than uniform chemical entities.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
PDI Monodispersity Rule
PDI = 1 \Rightarrow M_w = M_n

Application: Natural polymers like proteins are often monodisperse (PDI = 1), whereas synthetic polymers always have PDI > 1.

Condensation By-Product Identification
Diacid + Diamine → Amide + H2O

Application: Whenever you see ester or amide linkages in a polymer backbone, identify it immediately as a condensation or step-growth polymer.

Intermolecular Force Hierarchy for Fibers vs Elastomers
Elastomers (Weak van der Waals) < Plastics < Fibers (Strong H-bonding)

Application: Use tensile strength and elasticity to instantly categorize polymeric solids in matching questions.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Natural rubber is a cis-1,4-polyisoprene elastomer capable of undergoing vulcanization with sulfur.
  • Buna-S is a copolymer of 1,3-butadiene and styrene in a 3:1 ratio.
  • Nylon-6,6 is synthesized by the condensation polymerization of hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid.
  • Bakelite is a thermosetting polymer formed by the condensation of phenol and formaldehyde in the presence of an acid or base catalyst.
  • Low-density polyethylene (LDPE) is produced by free-radical polymerization of ethene at high pressure (1000-2000 atm) and high temperature.
  • Polydispersity index (PDI) is always greater than or equal to 1 for synthetic polymers.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing addition polymers with condensation polymers by assuming all polymers containing heteroatoms like O or N are condensation polymers (e.g., polyurethanes can be formed by addition reactions).
  • Assuming natural rubber has a trans configuration (gutta-percha is trans-1,4-polyisoprene, whereas natural rubber is cis-1,4-polyisoprene).
  • Treating PDI as a number less than 1; PDI is mathematically ratio M_w/M_n, which is always ≥ 1.
  • Misclassifying vulcanized rubber as a thermoplastic instead of a cross-linked thermosetting-like elastomer.

⚡ Quick Revision: Classification of Polymers

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Classification by Origin

Polymers are categorized into natural, semi-synthetic (e.g., cellulose acetate, rayon), and synthetic categories based on their natural occurrence or industrial derivation.

Classification by Structure

Structural differentiation separates linear, branched, and cross-linked network polymers, which directly dictate density, melting points, and mechanical resilience.

Classification by Polymerization Mechanism

Chain-growth (addition) and step-growth (condensation) mechanisms govern how monomers bond and whether small molecule by-products are eliminated.

Molecular Mass and PDI

Polymers are characterized by number-average (M_n) and weight-average (M_w) molecular masses, with their ratio defining the polydispersity index (PDI).

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Monofunctional Monomer (Monomer with 1 reactive site)
Stops chain growth; cannot form high polymers.
Copolymer (Mixed monomer polymer)
Polymer formed from two or more different monomer types.
Elastomer (Rubber-like solid)
Polymer with weak intermolecular forces exhibiting high elastic elongation.
Thermoplastic (Heat-softening polymer)
Linear polymer that softens on heating without chemical change.
Thermosetting (Heat-cured polymer)
Cross-linked polymer that hardens permanently upon heating.
Tacticity (Spatial arrangement)
Stereochemical arrangement of pendant groups along the polymer chain backbone.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • PDI = M_w / M_n
  • Natural rubber: cis-1,4-polyisoprene
  • Gutta-percha: trans-1,4-polyisoprene
  • Buna-S: 1,3-Butadiene + Styrene
  • Nylon-6,6: Hexamethylenediamine + Adipic acid

📌 Quick Recall

  • Polymers are macromolecules built from repeating monomer units.
  • Classification depends on source, structure, polymerization type, and intermolecular forces.
  • Addition polymers involve chain-growth without by-products; condensation polymers involve step-growth with small molecules eliminated.
  • Thermosetting polymers possess permanent cross-links, unlike thermoplastics.

⚖️ Key Differences

Addition Polymerization VS Condensation Polymerization

Addition involves no loss of small molecules (chain-growth), whereas condensation involves elimination of water, HCl, etc. (step-growth).

Which of the following is an example of a natural condensation polymer?

Explanation: Proteins are natural condensation polymers formed by amino acids joined via peptide bonds with the elimination of water molecules.

Question 2 of 18

What is the polydispersity index (PDI) for a monodisperse polymer?

Explanation: For a monodisperse polymer where all molecules have identical molecular mass, M_w = M_n, resulting in a PDI of exactly 1.

Which polymer is a copolymer?

Explanation: Buna-S is a copolymer synthesized from 1,3-butadiene and styrene.

Bakelite is classified as which type of polymer?

Explanation: Bakelite is a heavily cross-linked phenol-formaldehyde resin that forms a permanent 3D network, making it a thermosetting polymer.

Which of the following contains amide linkages?

Explanation: Nylon-6,6 is a polyamide containing repeating amide (-CONH-) linkages in its polymer backbone.

Question 6 of 18

Natural rubber is a polymer of:

Question 7 of 18

Which intermolecular forces are predominant in fibers such as nylon?

Explanation: Fibers like nylons exhibit high tensile strength due to strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding between polymer chains.

Question 8 of 18

Low-density polyethylene (LDPE) is obtained by:

Explanation: LDPE is produced via free-radical polymerization of ethene under high pressure and temperature conditions.

Question 9 of 18

Which of the following is a biodegradable polymer?

Question 10 of 18

Which average molecular mass is determined by light scattering measurements?

Explanation: Weight-average molecular mass (M_w) is sensitive to larger molecules and is experimentally determined via light scattering and ultracentrifugation techniques.

Question 11 of 18

Explanation: Terylene is a polyester formed by the condensation reaction between ethylene glycol and terephthalic acid.

Question 12 of 18

Explanation: Neoprene (polychloroprene) is a synthetic elastomer possessing rubber-like elasticity.

Question 13 of 18

What structural feature is characteristic of thermosetting polymers?

Explanation: Thermosetting polymers contain extensive covalent cross-links between polymer chains, forming a rigid 3D network.

Question 14 of 18

Which catalyst is used for the preparation of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) at low pressure?

Explanation: Ziegler-Natta catalysts (e.g., TiCl_4 + Al(C_2H_5)_3) enable coordination polymerization of ethene to form HDPE at low pressures.

Question 15 of 18

Neoprene is the polymer of which monomer?

Explanation: Neoprene is polymerized from chloroprene (2-chlorobut-1,3-diene).

Question 16 of 18

Which of the following polymers is thermoplastic?

Explanation: PVC is a linear or slightly branched thermoplastic polymer that softens on heating.

Question 17 of 18

The monomer units of Nylon-6 are obtained from:

Explanation: Nylon-6 is manufactured by the ring-opening polymerization of caprolactam.

Question 18 of 18

Which parameter always holds true for synthetic heterodisperse polymers?

Explanation: For all synthetic polymers, molecules vary in chain length, making the weight-average molecular mass always greater than the number-average molecular mass (M_w > M_n, PDI > 1).
