Nomenclature of Organic Compounds

The IUPAC nomenclature system provides a standardized method for generating systematic names for organic chemical substances based on structural components. It employs a structured sequence of root words, prefixes, and suffixes to uniquely identify molecular architecture.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Principles of Principal Functional Group Selection

  • The principal functional group determines the primary suffix of the IUPAC name and must be included in the longest continuous carbon chain.
  • Priority order for functional groups: Carboxylic acids > Esters > Amides > Nitriles > Aldehydes > Ketones > Alcohols > Amines > Alkenes > Alkynes.
  • Subordinate functional groups are cited as prefixes (e.g., hydroxy for -OH, oxo for =O).

Chain Selection and Numbering Rules

  • The principal carbon chain is chosen based on maximum length, maximum unsaturation, maximum principal functional groups, and maximum substituents.
  • Numbering of the principal chain begins from the end that gives the lowest locant to the principal functional group.
  • If no functional group is present, numbering follows the lowest locant set for substituents, evaluated point of difference by point of difference.

Stereochemical and Prefixes Ordering

  • Substituents are arranged in alphabetical order, ignoring multiplicative prefixes like di-, tri-, and tetra-.
  • Prefixes are alphabetized with respect to their full names, except when comparing hyphenated prefixes like iso- and neo- which are included, while sec- and tert- are ignored.
  • Stereochemical descriptors such as (R)/(S) and (E)/(Z) are placed in parentheses at the very beginning of the systematic name.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkenes and alkynes take lowest possible locant numbers

Observation: If both double and triple bonds are present at equivalent positions, the double bond gets the lower locant. — Historical IUPAC convention prioritizes alkene unsaturation over alkyne unsaturation when numbering ties occur.

Exception Alkyl substituents are written in alphabetical order

Observation: Prefixes like iso- and neo- are considered part of the base alkyl group name for alphabetization. — Iso and neo form an integral part of the trivial stem name retained in IUPAC guidelines, unlike sec- and tert-.

Exception Principal functional group must be part of the principal chain

Observation: Carboxylic acid derivatives like nitriles or esters sometimes have their functional carbon excluded from the main chain in specific ring systems. — When a principal functional group is directly attached to a ring, special suffixes like -carbaldehyde or -carboxylic acid are employed.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The root word in IUPAC nomenclature indicates the number of carbon atoms in the principal carbon chain.
  • The suffix '-ane', '-ene', and '-yne' denote the presence of single, double, and triple carbon-carbon bonds respectively.
  • When multiple identical substituents are present, multiplying prefixes di-, tri-, tetra- are used.
  • E/Z descriptors are used for geometric isomerism across double bonds based on Cahn-Ingold-Prelog priority rules.
  • R/S stereochemical descriptors indicate absolute configuration at chiral stereocenters.
  • Bridged bicyclic systems are named using the prefix 'bicyclo[x.y.z]' indicating bridgehead lengths in descending order.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Ignoring alphabetical order rules by including multiplicative prefixes like 'di-' or 'tri-' in the alphabetization sequence.
  • Failing to include the principal functional group carbon in the longest carbon chain for nitriles and carboxylic acids.
  • Numbering a chain from the wrong end when substituents appear symmetric but differ at a subsequent position.
  • Using 'sec-' or 'tert-' prefixes for alphabetization instead of relying on the core alkyl name.

⚡ Quick Revision: Nomenclature of Organic Compounds

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Systematic Framework

IUPAC nomenclature relies on three structural units: root word, primary/secondary suffixes, and prefixes.

Functional Group Hierarchy

The principal functional group dictates the suffix and determines the direction of numbering for the principal carbon chain.

Substituent Ordering

Substituents and side-chains are prefixed alphabetically, maintaining strict numerical locant designations.

Stereochemical Notation

Absolute configurations (R/S) and geometric configurations (E/Z) provide complete spatial disambiguation of molecular structures.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Locant (Numerical Locator)
A number indicating the position of a substituent or functional group.
Principal Chain (Parent Hydrocarbon)
The longest continuous chain of carbon atoms satisfying specific criteria.
Functional Group (Characteristic Moiety)
An atom or group of atoms within molecules that has similar chemical properties.
Homologous Series (CnH2n+2 series)
A series of organic compounds with the same functional group and similar chemical properties.
Chiral Center (Asymmetric Carbon)
A carbon atom bonded to four different groups.
Degree of Unsaturation (DBE)
The total number of rings and pi bonds in a molecule.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carboxylic acid has highest functional group priority.
  • Alphabetical ordering ignores di, tri, tetra, but includes iso and neo.
  • Alkene takes precedence over alkyne when locants are identical.
  • Principal functional group must get the lowest possible locant.
  • E/Z nomenclature is based on CIP priority rules.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Nomenclature standardizes chemical communication globally.
  • Proper chain selection is the foundation of IUPAC naming.
  • Strict priority rules resolve multi-functional compound ambiguity.
  • Stereodescriptors complete three-dimensional structural identification.

⚖️ Key Differences

iso- prefix VS sec- prefix

iso- is included in alphabetization, whereas sec- is ignored.
