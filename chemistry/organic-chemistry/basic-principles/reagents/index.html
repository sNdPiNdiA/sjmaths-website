Organic Chemistry

Organic Chemical Reagents

Chemical reagents are substances or mixtures added to a system to cause a chemical reaction or test for one. They are classified fundamentally into electrophiles, nucleophiles, oxidizing agents, and reducing agents.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electrophiles and Nucleophiles

  • Electrophiles are electron-deficient species acting as Lewis acids, including cations like NO2+ and neutral species like AlCl3.
  • Nucleophiles are electron-rich species acting as Lewis bases, possessing lone pairs or pi-bonds, such as OH-, NH3, and CN-.
  • Ambident nucleophiles possess two reactive nucleophilic sites, for example, the cyanide ion (C≡N-) attacking through either carbon or nitrogen.

Oxidizing and Reducing Reagents

  • Strong oxidizing agents like Jones reagent (CrO3/H2SO4) convert primary alcohols directly to carboxylic acids.
  • Mild oxidizing agents like PCC (Pyridinium chlorochromate) stop the oxidation of primary alcohols at the aldehyde stage.
  • Complex metal hydrides such as LiAlH4 reduce polar multiple bonds (carbonyls, esters, nitriles), while NaBH4 selectively reduces aldehydes and ketones.

Name Reactions Reagents

  • Friedel-Crafts alkylation uses alkyl halides with anhydrous Lewis acid catalysts like AlCl3 to introduce alkyl groups onto aromatic rings.
  • Baeyer-Villiger oxidation converts ketones to esters using peroxy acids like m-CPBA.
  • Diazotization uses NaNO2 and HCl at 0-5°C to convert primary aromatic amines into diazonium salts.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception NaBH4 reduces carbonyl compounds efficiently but does not reduce isolated alkenes or alkynes.

Observation: NaBH4 reduces conjugated double bonds in α,β-unsaturated carbonyl compounds under specific conditions. — Delocalization of electrons through the conjugated system lowers the LUMO energy, facilitating hydride attack at the β-carbon.

Exception Grignard reagents react with acidic hydrogen to form alkanes and do not tolerate moisture.

Observation: Grignard reagents can be formed from substrates containing certain protected functional groups if strictly anhydrous conditions are maintained. — Water acts as a protic source that immediately protonates the carbanion character of the Grignard reagent, destroying it.

Exception LiAlH4 reduces all carboxylic acid derivatives to alcohols.

Observation: DIBAL-H at -78°C reduces esters and nitriles selectively to aldehydes instead of primary alcohols. — Low temperatures and controlled stoichiometry stabilize the tetrahedral intermediate, preventing over-reduction.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • PCC is a complex of pyridine, chromium trioxide, and HCl dissolved in dichloromethane.
  • Ozonolysis followed by oxidative workup with H2O2 converts alkenes into carboxylic acids or ketones.
  • Clemmensen reduction uses acidic media (Zn-Hg/HCl), whereas Wolff-Kishner uses strongly basic media (NH2NH2/KOH).
  • Rosenmund reduction uses poisoned palladium catalyst (Pd/BaSO4) to convert acid chlorides selectively into aldehydes.
  • Lucas reagent consists of anhydrous ZnCl2 in concentrated HCl and differentiates between 1°, 2°, and 3° alcohols.
  • Bromination of alkenes using Br2 in CCl4 proceeds via a cyclic bromonium ion intermediate, yielding anti-addition products.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing the role of anhydrous AlCl3 in Friedel-Crafts reactions with a solvent instead of a catalyst.
  • Assuming NaBH4 can reduce carboxylic acids or esters; it lacks sufficient hydridic character.
  • Forgetting that Grignard reagents cannot be prepared in the presence of acidic protons (-OH, -NH, -COOH) within the same molecule.
  • Confusing reductive ozonolysis (yielding aldehydes/ketones) with oxidative ozonolysis (yielding acids/ketones).

⚡ Quick Revision: Organic Chemical Reagents

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Nucleophilic Addition and Substitution Reagents

Nucleophiles attack electron-deficient centers such as carbonyl carbons or saturated carbons bearing leaving groups. The choice of nucleophile dictates whether substitution or addition predominates.

Oxidation State Transformations

Oxidizing agents increase carbon-oxygen or carbon-heteroatom bonds, while reducing agents decrease them. Reagent selection controls the exact oxidation state reached.

Organometallic Reagents

Organometallic reagents like Grignard reagents (RMgX) and organolithium compounds (RLi) invert carbon polarity from electrophilic to nucleophilic, enabling carbon-carbon bond formation.

Elimination Reagents

Strong, sterically hindered bases promote E2 elimination to yield Hofmann alkenes, whereas small, strong bases yield Zaitsev products.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Electrophile (E+)
Electron-deficient chemical species seeking electron density.
Nucleophile (Nu-)
Electron-rich chemical species donating an electron pair.
Grignard Reagent (R-Mg-X)
Organomagnesium halide used for carbon-carbon bond formation.
Oxidizing Agent ([O])
Reagent that accepts electrons or adds oxygen/removes hydrogen.
Reducing Agent ([H])
Reagent that donates electrons or adds hydrogen/removes oxygen.
Ambident Reagent (Dual-site reagent)
Reagent possessing two reactive nucleophilic or electrophilic sites.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Jones Reagent: CrO3 / aq. H2SO4 (1° alcohol → Acid)
  • PCC: Pyridinium chlorochromate (1° alcohol → Aldehyde)
  • Ozonolysis: O3 followed by Zn/H2O
  • Clemmensen Reduction: Zn-Hg / HCl
  • Wolff-Kishner Reduction: NH2NH2 / KOH, ethylene glycol

📌 Quick Recall

  • Reagents dictate reaction pathways, selectivity, and stereochemistry.
  • Hydride reducing agents vary widely in their functional group tolerance.
  • Organometallic reagents require strictly anhydrous conditions to prevent side reactions.
  • Oxidation state changes depend directly on stoichiometric ratios and reagent strength.

⚖️ Key Differences

PCC VS Jones Reagent

PCC stops at the aldehyde stage for primary alcohols, whereas Jones reagent oxidizes them fully to carboxylic acids.

Question 1 of 18

Which reagent is best suited for converting a primary alcohol to an aldehyde?

Explanation: PCC is a mild, anhydrous oxidizing agent that oxidizes primary alcohols specifically to aldehydes without proceeding to carboxylic acids.

Question 2 of 18

What is the primary product formed when benzaldehyde reacts with Tollens' reagent?

Explanation: Tollens' reagent ([Ag(NH3)2]+) oxidizes aldehydes to their corresponding carboxylate ions, yielding benzoic acid upon acidification.

Question 3 of 18

Which of the following reagents effects the Clemmensen reduction?

Explanation: The Clemmensen reduction utilizes zinc-mercury amalgam in concentrated hydrochloric acid to reduce aldehydes and ketones to alkanes.

Question 4 of 18

Grignard reagents are prepared by reacting alkyl halides with magnesium in the presence of:

Explanation: Dry ether stabilizes the organomagnesium halide through coordination of lone pairs, and absence of water prevents premature protonation.

Question 5 of 18

Which reagent converts an alkyne specifically into a trans-alkene?

Explanation: Dissolving metal reduction using sodium in liquid ammonia reduces alkynes stereospecifically to trans-alkenes via a radical anion intermediate.

Question 6 of 18

Which compound is an example of an electrophile?

Explanation: AlCl3 has an incomplete octet on the aluminum atom, making it a Lewis acid and a strong electrophile.

Question 7 of 18

What is the product when propene is treated with B2H6 followed by alkaline H2O2?

Explanation: Hydroboration-oxidation adds water across the double bond in an anti-Markovnikov fashion, yielding propan-1-ol.

Question 8 of 18

Which reagent is used for the conversion of an acid chloride to an aldehyde?

Explanation: The Rosenmund reduction uses H2 over Pd/BaSO4 poisoned with quinoline to reduce acid chlorides to aldehydes.

Question 9 of 18

Lucas reagent is a mixture of:

Explanation: Lucas reagent consists of anhydrous zinc chloride dissolved in concentrated hydrochloric acid, used to classify alcohols.

Question 10 of 18

Which of the following reagents is a strong nucleophile?

Explanation: The cyanide ion (CN-) carries a negative charge and an unshared electron pair, making it an excellent nucleophile.

Question 11 of 18

Ozonolysis of 2-butene followed by Zn/H2O workup gives:

Explanation: Cleavage of the carbon-carbon double bond in 2-butene yields two molecules of acetaldehyde.

Question 12 of 18

Which reducing agent selectively reduces carbonyl groups of aldehydes and ketones without affecting ester groups?

Explanation: NaBH4 is a milder reducing agent that reduces aldehydes and ketones rapidly but does not reduce esters under normal conditions.

Question 13 of 18

What role does pyridine play in acyl chloride reactions with alcohols?

Explanation: Pyridine acts as a weak base that scavenges the HCl byproduct, driving the esterification reaction forward.

Question 14 of 18

Baeyer's reagent is:

Explanation: Cold alkaline potassium permanganate (Baeyer's reagent) hydroxylates alkenes to form vicinal diols, discharging its purple color.

Question 15 of 18

Which reagent is used in the Etard reaction to convert toluene to benzaldehyde?

Explanation: Chromyl chloride selectively oxidizes the methyl group of toluene to a chromium complex, which hydrolyzes to benzaldehyde.

Question 16 of 18

What is the active electrophilic species in aromatic nitration using HNO3 and H2SO4?

Explanation: Protonation and dehydration of nitric acid by sulfuric acid generates the nitronium ion (NO2+).

Question 17 of 18

Which reagent is employed in the HVZ (Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky) reaction?

Explanation: The HVZ reaction uses bromine in the presence of red phosphorus to alpha-brominate carboxylic acids.

Question 18 of 18

Which reagent converts primary amines into isocyanides upon heating with chloroform and alcoholic KOH?

Explanation: The carbylamine reaction uses CHCl3 and alcoholic KOH to test for primary amines, producing foul-smelling isocyanides.
