Organic Chemistry

Basic Principles of Organic Chemistry

Organic chemistry focuses on the study of carbon compounds, structural isomerism, and electronic displacement effects within covalent bonds. Reaction mechanisms depend on bond cleavage pathways, intermediates stability, and reagent characteristics.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electronic Displacement Effects

  • The Inductive Effect (-I / +I) operates permanently through sigma bonds and diminishes rapidly beyond three carbon atoms.
  • The Resonance Effect (+R / -R) operates through delocalized pi-electrons and involves the overlap of p-orbitals.
  • The Electromeric Effect (E) is a temporary polarization of a multiple bond brought about by the demand of an attacking reagent.
  • Hyperconjugation involves the delocalized overlap of sigma C-H electrons with an adjacent empty or partially filled p-orbital.

Fission of Covalent Bonds and Intermediates

  • Homolytic fission yields neutral free radicals possessing an odd electron, promoted by UV light, heat, or peroxides.
  • Heterolytic fission yields carbocations and carbanions, favored by polar solvents and heteronuclear bond polarities.
  • Carbocations are sp2 hybridized with a vacant planar p-orbital, whereas carbanions are sp3 hybridized with pyramidal geometry.
  • Free radical stability follows the order: 3o > 2o > 1o > methyl, identical to carbocation stability trends.

Reagents and Organic Reactions

  • Electrophiles are electron-deficient species (Lewis acids) that attack regions of high electron density.
  • Nucleophiles are electron-rich species (Lewis bases) possessing lone pairs or negative charges that attack electron-deficient centers.
  • Substitution, addition, elimination, and rearrangement represent the four primary classes of organic reactions.
  • Aromatic electrophilic substitution proceeds via the formation of a non-aromatic sigma complex (Wheland intermediate).

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl groups always exert electron-releasing (+I) inductive effects.

Observation: An alkyl group attached to an unsaturated system can show hyperconjugative electron withdrawal or release depending on orientation. — Overlap between C-H sigma orbitals and adjacent pi systems alters simple polarization rules.

Exception Tertiary carbocations are always more stable than secondary carbocations.

Observation: Bridgehead tertiary carbocations (e.g., 1-admantyl cation) resist formation due to Bredt's rule constraints. — Inability of bridgehead carbon atoms to attain planar sp2 geometry without severe angle strain.

Exception Acidity increases with electronegativity of the attached atom.

Observation: HF is a weak acid in water compared to HCl, HBr, and HI. — Extremely high H-F bond dissociation enthalpy and strong hydration energy of fluoride ions outweigh electronegativity trends.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • The order of stability of free radicals and carbocations is 3o > 2o > 1o > methyl.
  • Hyperconjugation is also known as no-bond resonance or Baker-Nathan effect.
  • The dipole moment of trans-1,2-dichloroethene is zero due to vector cancellation of bond dipoles.
  • Electromeric effect is a temporary effect that operates only in the presence of an attacking reagent.
  • Carbanions are sp3 hybridized and possess pyramidal geometry with a lone pair.
  • Phenol is more acidic than ethanol due to resonance stabilization of the phenoxide ion.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing inductive effect with resonance effect; inductive effect operates through sigma bonds and drops off rapidly with distance.
  • Assuming carbanions are planar like carbocations; carbanions are pyramidal with sp3 hybridization.
  • Forgetting that hyperconjugation requires at least one alpha-hydrogen on an sp3 carbon attached to an unsaturated system.
  • Treating the electromeric effect as a permanent molecular property instead of a temporary polarization.

⚡ Quick Revision: Basic Principles of Organic Chemistry

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structural Representation

Organic molecules are depicted via Lewis structures, condensed formulas, and bond-line formulas where carbon and hydrogen atoms bonded to carbon are implied.

Electronic Effects

Permanent effects like inductive and resonance dictate ground-state electron distribution, whereas temporary effects like electromeric govern reaction initiation.

Reaction Intermediates

Carbocations, carbanions, free radicals, and carbenes act as short-lived reactive intermediates determining the stereochemical and regiochemical outcome of reactions.

Acids and Bases

Organic acidity and basicity are rationalized by examining the stability of conjugate bases and conjugate acids using electronic effects.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Inductive Effect (-I / +I)
Permanent displacement of sigma electrons along a carbon chain due to electronegativity differences.
Resonance Effect (-R / +R)
Delocalization of pi electrons or lone pairs across conjugated systems.
Hyperconjugation (C-H σ→p overlap)
Stabilization interaction between sigma C-H bonds and adjacent unsaturated p-orbitals.
Electrophile (E+)
Electron-deficient species seeking electron-rich centers.
Nucleophile (Nu-)
Electron-rich species donating an electron pair to form a covalent bond.
Carbocation (R3C+)
Carbon species bearing a positive charge and six valence electrons.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Carbocation stability: 3o > 2o > 1o
  • Carbanion stability: 1o > 2o > 3o
  • Free radical stability: 3o > 2o > 1o
  • Inductive effect distance decay after 3rd carbon
  • Phenol acidity > Aliphatic alcohol acidity

📌 Quick Recall

  • Bond fission can be homolytic (radicals) or heterolytic (ions).
  • Electronic effects (Inductive, Resonance, Hyperconjugation) dictate molecular stability and reactivity.
  • Electrophiles and nucleophiles drive polar organic reaction mechanisms.
  • Conjugate acid-base stability is the core principle for evaluating organic acidity.

⚖️ Key Differences

Inductive Effect VS Resonance Effect

Inductive effect operates through sigma bonds and drops off with distance; resonance operates through pi bond conjugation and does not decay with distance.

Which group exerts a -I effect?

Explanation: The nitro group (-NO2) is strongly electronegative and withdraws electron density, exhibiting a -I effect.

Question 4 of 20

Homolytic bond fission of a hydrocarbon yields:

Explanation: Homolytic fission involves symmetrical splitting of a bond where each bonded atom retains one electron, producing free radicals.

Question 5 of 20

Which carbocation is most stable?

Explanation: Tertiary carbocations are stabilized by maximum hyperconjugative structures and +I inductive effects from three methyl groups.

Question 6 of 20

Which species acts as an electrophile?

Explanation: AlCl3 is an electron-deficient Lewis acid with an incomplete octet, functioning as an electrophile.

Question 7 of 20

The correct order of stability of free radicals is:

Explanation: Free radicals follow the same stability order as carbocations due to hyperconjugation and alkyl group electron release: 3o > 2o > 1o.

Question 8 of 20

Which of the following shows the +R effect?

Explanation: The hydroxyl group (-OH) donates electrons through resonance via its lone pairs, exhibiting a +R effect.

Question 9 of 20

What is the geometry of a carbanion?

Explanation: Carbanions possess three bond pairs and one lone pair on the carbon atom, giving them sp3 hybridization and pyramidal geometry.

Question 10 of 20

Which effect is temporary?

Explanation: The electromeric effect is a temporary polarization that operates only in the presence of an attacking reagent.

Question 11 of 20

Which of the following compounds has a zero dipole moment?

Explanation: In trans-1,2-dichloroethene, identical polar bonds point in opposite directions, canceling each other out to give a zero dipole moment.

Question 12 of 20

Which molecule is the strongest acid among the following?

Explanation: Acetic acid produces a resonance-stabilized carboxylate ion where the negative charge is delocalized over two electronegative oxygen atoms.

Question 13 of 20

The degree of unsaturation for C6H6 is:

Explanation: Using DBE = C + 1 - (H/2) = 6 + 1 - (6/2) = 7 - 3 = 4.

Question 14 of 20

Which reagent is a strong nucleophile?

Explanation: Ethoxide ion (C2H5O-) has a negative charge and lone pairs, making it a strong nucleophile and Lewis base.

Question 15 of 20

Which effect operates through space rather than through bonds?

Explanation: The field effect operates through space and solvent molecules, whereas the inductive effect operates strictly through sigma bonds.

Question 16 of 20

Which of the following carbocations is least stable?

Explanation: Methyl carbocation lacks any alkyl groups to provide hyperconjugation or +I stabilizing effects, making it the least stable.

Question 17 of 20

What type of cleavage results in the formation of ions?

Explanation: Heterolytic cleavage divides bonding electrons unequally between two atoms, forming a cation and an anion.

Question 18 of 20

Which functional group exerts a -R effect?

Explanation: The aldehyde group (-CHO) withdraws pi electrons from the conjugated system via resonance, exhibiting a -R effect.

Question 19 of 20

In nitrobenzene, the nitro group directs incoming electrophiles to which position?

Explanation: The nitro group is a strong -I and -R group that deactivates the ring and directs electrophilic substitution to the meta position.

Question 20 of 20

Which of the following species is ambident nucleophile?

Explanation: Cyanide ion (CN-) can attack via carbon or nitrogen, making it an ambident nucleophile.

What is the primary factor responsible for the stability of tertiary butyl carbocation?

Which of the following functional groups exhibits a +R effect?

Solution: The amino group (-NH2) possesses a lone pair on nitrogen that can be donated into a conjugated system, showing +R effect.

Question 3 of 10
Homolytic fission of carbon-chlorine bond in chloromethane requires:

Solution: Homolytic cleavage requires high energy input such as UV light or thermal conditions to split covalent bonds symmetrically.

Question 4 of 10
Which intermediate has an incomplete octet of electrons on carbon?

Solution: Carbocations possess 6 valence electrons and free radicals possess 7 valence electrons, both having incomplete octets.

Question 5 of 10
The correct order of acidity for aliphatic alcohols is:

Solution: Alkyl groups exert +I effect which intensifies negative charge on oxygen in alkoxide ions, decreasing acidity; thus 1o > 2o > 3o.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following is not an electrophile?

Solution: Ammonia (NH3) has a lone pair of electrons and acts as a nucleophile or Lewis base, not an electrophile.

Question 7 of 10
Which molecule contains acidic hydrogen atoms attached to sp-hybridized carbon?

Solution: Ethyne (acetylene) has sp-hybridized carbons with 50% s-character, making its hydrogen atoms acidic and easily removed by strong bases.

Question 8 of 10
Which species acts as both a nucleophile and an electrophile?

Solution: Aldehydes possess a carbonyl carbon that is electrophilic and a carbonyl oxygen / pi system that can participate in nucleophilic or donor interactions.

Question 9 of 10
Calculate the degree of unsaturation for a compound with molecular formula C4H6O.

Solution: DBE = C + 1 - (H/2) = 4 + 1 - (6/2) = 5 - 3 = 2.

Question 10 of 10
Which electronic effect is distance-dependent and weakens rapidly across sigma bonds?

Solution: The inductive effect drops off sharply and becomes negligible after three carbon atoms along a sigma chain.
