Organic Chemistry

Free Radicals

Free radicals are reactive chemical species possessing an odd number of electrons, typically containing an unpaired electron in an unhybridized orbital. They are generated via homolytic bond cleavage under thermal, photochemical, or chemical induction.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Generation and Structure of Free Radicals

  • Free radicals are formed through homolytic cleavage where shared electron pairs are equally distributed between fragment atoms.
  • The central carbon atom of an alkyl free radical exhibits sp2 hybridization with a planar or shallow pyramidal geometry.
  • The unpaired electron resides in an unhybridized pure p-orbital oriented perpendicular to the nodal plane of the σ-bonds.
  • Initiators such as benzoyl peroxide (BPO) and azobisisobutyronitrile (AIBN) undergo homolysis upon heating to yield initiating radicals.

Stability Orders and Electronic Factors

  • The relative stability order of carbon free radicals follows: 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl.
  • Stability is heavily governed by hyperconjugation, where adjacent C-H σ-bonds overlap with the half-filled p-orbital.
  • Allylic and benzylic radicals derive exceptional stability from resonance delocalization of the unpaired electron over neighboring unsaturated systems.
  • Bridgehead free radicals are exceptionally unstable due to the geometrical constraint preventing planarization to sp2 hybridization.

Reactions and Chain Mechanisms

  • Radical chain reactions proceed via three distinct steps: initiation, propagation, and termination.
  • Halogenation of alkanes proceeds with varying selectivity; bromination is highly selective for tertiary sites, whereas chlorination is largely non-selective.
  • Free radical addition of HBr to alkenes in the presence of peroxides follows the Kharasch effect (anti-Markownikoff addition) via bromine radical attack.
  • Termination steps involve the combination or disproportionation of two radical species to form stable, non-radical products.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl free radical stability strictly follows 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl based on hyperconjugation.

Observation: Bridgehead radicals (e.g., 1-adamthyl radical) resist formation entirely despite being tertiary. — Inability of the rigid cage framework to attain planar sp2 geometry at the radical center.

Exception Addition of HBr to unsymmetrical alkenes follows Markownikoff's rule.

Observation: In the presence of peroxides, HBr undergoes anti-Markownikoff addition. — Peroxides generate bromine radicals that attack the alkene first to form the more stable carbon radical intermediate.

Exception Free radical halogenation yields substitution products exclusively.

Observation: Allylic and benzylic halogenation using NBS (N-bromosuccinimide) occurs with low concentration of Br2. — NBS maintains a constant, low-level concentration of molecular bromine via radical chain pathways, preventing ionic alkene addition.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Hyperconjugation Count for Stability
Stability ∝ Total number of α-hydrogens

Application: Count the total number of C-H bonds directly attached to carbons adjacent to the radical center. More α-hydrogens mean more canonical forms and higher stability.

Selectivity Rule in Halogenation
Cl2 = Fast & Non-selective; Br2 = Slow & Highly Selective

Application: Use Hammond's postulate: exothermic chlorination has an early transition state (resembles starting material), while endothermic bromination has a late transition state (resembles radical product).

Peroxide Effect Scope
HCl and HI do NOT show peroxide effect

Application: HCl addition has an endothermic propagation step for chlorine abstraction; HI addition has an endothermic step for iodine radical addition to alkene.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Free radicals are neutral, paramagnetic species containing an odd electron.
  • The geometry of simple alkyl radicals is planar or shallow pyramidal with sp2 hybridization.
  • Allylic and benzylic radicals are stabilized by resonance delocalization of the unpaired electron.
  • Bromination of alkanes is endothermic and highly regioselective for tertiary C-H bonds.
  • Chlorination of alkanes is exothermic and shows very low regioselectivity.
  • Anti-Markownikoff addition of HBr (Kharasch effect) proceeds via a free radical chain mechanism in the presence of peroxides.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Assuming free radicals have a complete octet of electrons; they possess only 7 valence electrons and are electron-deficient.
  • Confusing carbocation stability with carbanion stability; carbanions follow the exact reverse stability order (1° > 2° > 3°).
  • Applying the peroxide effect (anti-Markownikoff addition) to HCl or HI, which fail due to thermodynamic constraints in propagation steps.
  • Treating bridgehead carbon atoms as capable of forming stable free radicals.

⚡ Quick Revision: Free Radicals

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Generation Methods

Free radicals are generated via homolytic cleavage of covalent bonds using thermal energy, ultraviolet radiation, or chemical radical initiators like peroxides and azo compounds.

Stability Determinants

Radical stability increases with alkyl substitution due to hyperconjugation and increases dramatically with adjacent pi-systems due to resonance delocalization.

Propagation and Selectivity

Halogenation propagation steps dictate product distributions where thermodynamics (Hammond's postulate) accounts for bromine's high selectivity over chlorine.

Chain Termination

Radical chains are terminated by combination (coupling two radicals) or disproportionation (hydrogen transfer yielding an alkane and an alkene).

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Homolytic Cleavage (A-B → A• + B•)
Symmetric bond breaking where each atom retains one electron.
Hyperconjugation (σ-p conjugation)
Delocalization of electrons from a σ-bond into an adjacent empty or half-filled p-orbital.
Kharasch Effect (Peroxide Effect)
Anti-Markownikoff addition of HBr to alkenes via free radical mechanism.
Initiator (R-O-O-R / AIBN)
Weakly bonded molecule that readily undergoes homolysis to start radical chains.
Disproportionation (Radical Termination)
Redox reaction between two radicals yielding one saturated and one unsaturated molecule.
Hammond Postulate (Transition State Theory)
Transition state resembles the species (reactant or product) nearest to it in energy.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Stability order: Allylic/Benzylic > 3° > 2° > 1° > Methyl > Vinyl/Phenyl
  • Hybridization of alkyl radical center: sp2
  • Peroxide effect works ONLY for HBr among hydrogen halides
  • NBS selectively brominates allylic and benzylic positions
  • Free radicals are electron-deficient, neutral, paramagnetic species with 7 valence electrons

📌 Quick Recall

  • Free radicals form via homolysis and possess an odd number of electrons.
  • Stability is governed by hyperconjugation and resonance.
  • Reactions proceed through initiation, propagation, and termination steps.
  • Halogenation selectivity depends on the endothermicity or exothermicity of the propagation steps.

⚖️ Key Differences

Carbocation Stability VS Free Radical Stability

Both follow 3° > 2° > 1°, but carbocations have a vacant p-orbital (6 electrons, Lewis acid) while radicals have a half-filled p-orbital (7 electrons, neutral).

Which reagent is used for allylic bromination?

Question 4 of 19

The peroxide effect is observed in the addition of which hydrogen halide to alkenes?

Explanation: Only HBr exhibits the peroxide effect (anti-Markownikoff addition) because both propagation steps are energetically favorable.

Question 5 of 19

How many valence electrons are associated with the carbon atom of a free radical?

Explanation: A carbon free radical possesses 7 valence electrons (three paired bonds and one unpaired electron).

Question 6 of 19

Which step in a free radical chain reaction consumes a radical and produces a radical?

Explanation: In the propagation step, a radical reacts with a stable molecule to generate a new radical and a new molecule.

Question 7 of 19

Which of the following properties is characteristic of free radicals?

Explanation: Because free radicals contain an unpaired electron, they are paramagnetic.

Question 8 of 19

Which alkyl radical is the least stable?

Explanation: The methyl radical lacks alpha-hydrogens for hyperconjugation, making it the least stable among simple alkyl radicals.

Question 9 of 19

What type of bond cleavage generates free radicals?

Explanation: Homolytic cleavage involves symmetrical splitting of a covalent bond where each bonded atom takes one electron.

Question 10 of 19

In the chlorination of methane, which step has the highest activation energy?

Explanation: The abstraction of hydrogen by a chlorine radical to form HCl and a methyl radical has a significant activation barrier compared to the subsequent exothermic chlorine atom transfer.

Question 11 of 19

Which factor primarily accounts for the high stability of benzyl and allyl radicals?

Explanation: Allylic and benzylic radicals are exceptionally stable due to resonance delocalization of the unpaired electron over neighboring pi systems.

Question 12 of 19

What is the role of AIBN in free radical polymerization?

Explanation: AIBN (azobisisobutyronitrile) thermally decomposes to release nitrogen gas and form carbon radicals, acting as a radical initiator.

Question 13 of 19

Which compound is most resistant to free radical formation at the bridgehead position?

Explanation: Adamantane contains rigid bridgehead carbons that cannot flatten into sp2 hybridization, making bridgehead radical formation extremely unfavorable.

Question 14 of 19

Termination of two identical alkyl radicals by combination yields:

Explanation: Radical combination joins two odd electrons to form a covalent sigma bond, producing a symmetrical higher alkane.

Question 15 of 19

Disproportionation of two ethyl radicals yields:

Explanation: Disproportionation involves hydrogen transfer from one radical to another, producing one saturated molecule (ethane) and one unsaturated molecule (ethene).

Question 16 of 19

According to Hammond's postulate, the transition state of an endothermic step resembles:

Explanation: Endothermic reaction steps have transition states that lie closer in energy to the products and thus structurally resemble the products.

Question 17 of 19

Which halogenation of alkanes is exothermic and nearly thermoneutral for chlorine?

Explanation: Chlorination of alkanes is exothermic, whereas fluorination is violently exothermic and iodination is endothermic.

Question 18 of 19

What is the main reason why iodine does not react with alkanes under normal radical conditions?

Explanation: The abstraction of hydrogen by an iodine radical to form HI is endothermic and thermodynamically unfavorable.

Question 19 of 19

Which radical species is planar or nearly planar?

Explanation: Simple carbon free radicals like the methyl radical adopt a planar or very shallow pyramidal geometry (sp2).

Question 1 of 10
Which of the following radical intermediates is stabilized by both resonance and hyperconjugation?

Solution: The benzyl radical benefits from resonance with the benzene ring as well as hyperconjugation from the methylene hydrogen atoms.

Question 2 of 10
In the free radical chlorination of propane, how many monochlorinated isomers are formed?

Solution: Chlorination of propane yields 1-chloropropane and 2-chloropropane (two constitutional isomers).

Question 3 of 10
Which reaction does NOT involve a free radical mechanism?

Solution: Acid-catalyzed hydration of propene proceeds via an ionic mechanism involving a carbocation intermediate.

Question 4 of 10
What is the function of hydroquinone when added to commercial samples of ethers or chloroform?

Solution: Hydroquinone acts as a radical scavenger or inhibitor, reacting with trace radicals to prevent dangerous autoxidation chain reactions.

Question 5 of 10
Which of the following represents a chain termination step in radical chlorination?

Solution: Termination steps consume radicals without producing new ones. Combining a methyl radical and a chlorine radical forms methyl chloride.

Question 6 of 10
Why is iodination of alkanes reversible?

Solution: The HI produced in iodination is a strong reducing agent that reduces the alkyl iodide back to the alkane, making the overall reaction reversible.

Question 7 of 10
Which radical possesses the highest s-character in its orbital containing the unpaired electron?

Solution: The vinyl radical carbon is sp-hybridized (in idealized limits, though bent), imparting higher s-character compared to sp2 radicals.

Question 8 of 10
Which statement regarding disproportionation of radicals is correct?

Solution: Disproportionation involves hydrogen atom transfer from one radical to another, yielding one saturated and one unsaturated molecule.

Question 9 of 10
Which compound generates free radicals upon gentle heating without external irradiation?

Solution: Benzoyl peroxide possesses a weak weak oxygen-oxygen single bond (O-O) that undergoes homolytic cleavage at moderate temperatures.

Question 10 of 10
The selectivity of bromination over chlorination of alkanes is explained by:

Solution: Hammond's postulate explains that endothermic bromination has a transition state closely resembling the radical product, making energy differences crucial for selectivity.
