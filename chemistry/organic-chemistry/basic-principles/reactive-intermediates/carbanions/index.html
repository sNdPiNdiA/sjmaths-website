Organic Chemistry

Carbanions

Carbanions are reactive intermediates possessing a trivalent carbon atom bearing a negative charge and an unshared pair of electrons. They act as carbon nucleophiles and typically adopt a pyramidal geometry with sp3 hybridization.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Electronic Structure and Hybridization

  • Carbanion central carbon is sp3 hybridized with three sigma bonds and one lone pair residing in an sp3 orbital.
  • The ground state geometry is trigonal pyramidal, resembling amines with rapid umbrella inversion.
  • Carbanions act as strong nucleophiles and strong bases due to the presence of the non-bonding electron pair.

Factors Affecting Carbanion Stability

  • Inductive Effect (-I): Electron-withdrawing groups stabilize carbanions by dispersing the negative charge through sigma bonds.
  • Resonance Effect: Presence of conjugated π-systems or aromatic rings significantly enhances stability via charge delocalization.
  • S-Character: Stability increases with increasing s-character of the carbanionic carbon (sp > sp2 > sp3).

Stereochemistry and Reactions

  • Simple chiral carbanions undergo rapid inversion leading to racemic mixtures, unlike chiral carbon radicals or carbocations.
  • Carbanions participate prominently in name reactions such as the Aldol condensation, Claisen condensation, and Perkin reaction.
  • Protonation of carbanions by weak acids yields the corresponding saturated hydrocarbons.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl groups stabilize carbocations via hyperconjugation and +I effect.

Observation: Alkyl groups destabilize carbanions. — Electron-releasing +I effect intensifies the negative charge on the carbanionic center.

Exception Bridgehead carbocations are extremely unstable due to planarity constraints.

Observation: Bridgehead carbanions can exist if stabilized by strong electron-withdrawing substituents. — The pyramidal geometry at the bridgehead atom can accommodate the sp3 lone pair without enforcing strict planarity.

Exception Cyclopentadienyl anion is exceptionally stable for a hydrocarbon carbanion.

Observation: Cycloheptatrienide anion is anti-aromatic and exceptionally unstable. — Cyclopentadienyl anion possesses 6 π-electrons obeying Hückel's rule (aromatic), whereas cycloheptatrienide possesses 8 π-electrons (anti-aromatic).

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Carbanions have a complete octet with 8 electrons in the valence shell of the central carbon.
  • The stability order of simple carbanions is methyl > primary > secondary > tertiary.
  • Acetylide anion (HC≡C-) is more stable than vinyl and alkyl carbanions due to 50% s-character.
  • Triphenylmethyl carbanion is exceptionally stable due to extensive resonance delocalization over three phenyl rings.
  • Grignard reagents (RMgX) and organolithium compounds (RLi) react primarily as sources of carbanions.
  • Carbanions act as nucleophiles in aldol, Claisen, and Michael addition reactions.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing carbanion stability order with carbocation stability order; remember they are exact opposites.
  • Assuming carbanions are strictly planar; they possess a pyramidal geometry with a lone pair.
  • Forgetting that electron-withdrawing groups stabilize carbanions while electron-donating groups destabilize them.
  • Neglecting the impact of ring strain and aromaticity (e.g., cyclopentadienyl anion stability).

⚡ Quick Revision: Carbanions

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Structure and Bonding

Carbanions are trivalent carbon species carrying a negative charge and a lone pair, adopting a pyramidal geometry with sp3 hybridization.

Stability Determinants

Stability is enhanced by electronegative atoms, electron-withdrawing inductive groups, resonance delocalization, and higher s-character.

Generation Methods

Formed via abstraction of an acidic proton by strong bases, decarboxylation, or reaction of alkyl halides with active metals.

Synthetic Utility

Serve as key nucleophiles in carbon-carbon bond forming reactions such as aldol condensations and organometallic additions.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Carbanion (R3C-)
A carbon species with a negative charge and an unshared electron pair.
Conjugate Base (R-)
Species formed by the removal of a proton from an acid.
Inductive Effect (-I / +I)
Permanent polarization of a σ-bond due to electronegativity differences.
S-Character (% s)
Fraction of s-orbital contribution in a hybridized orbital.
Organometallic (R-M)
Compound containing a carbon-metal covalent bond acting as a carbanion source.
Inversion (Umbrella effect)
Rapid interconversion of pyramidal enantiomeric forms.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Stability order: Methyl > 1° > 2° > 3°
  • Hybridization of carbanionic carbon: sp3
  • Geometry: Trigonal pyramidal
  • S-character rule: sp (50%) > sp2 (33.3%) > sp3 (25%)
  • Role in organic synthesis: Carbon nucleophiles

📌 Quick Recall

  • Carbanions are negatively charged carbon intermediates possessing an octet of electrons.
  • Electron-withdrawing groups and high s-character stabilize the negative charge.
  • Alkyl substitution decreases carbanion stability via the +I effect.
  • They play central roles in nucleophilic additions and condensations.

⚖️ Key Differences

Carbocation Stability VS Carbanion Stability

Carbocations are stabilized by +I and hyperconjugation (3° > 2° > 1°), whereas carbanions are stabilized by -I and electron withdrawal (Methyl > 1° > 2° > 3°).

Explanation: Chiral carbanions undergo rapid pyramidal inversion, destroying optical activity and resulting in racemization.

Explanation: The negative charge is delocalized over the aromatic rings of all three phenyl groups through resonance.

Question 14 of 19

What role does sodium ethoxide play in the Claisen condensation?

Explanation: Sodium ethoxide abstracts an alpha-proton to generate an enolate/carbanion species.

Question 15 of 19

Which of the following functional groups destabilizes an adjacent carbanion?

Explanation: The methyl group (-CH3) is electron-donating (+I effect), which intensifies negative charge and destabilizes the carbanion.

Question 16 of 19

Question 17 of 19

Explanation: Aldol condensation proceeds via enolate ions, which are resonance-stabilized carbanions.

Question 18 of 19

What is the effect of increasing alkyl substitution on the stability of carbanions?

Explanation: More alkyl groups increase electron density via the inductive effect, destabilizing the negative charge.

Question 19 of 19

Which of the following species is isoelectronic with a carbanion of the type R3C-?

Explanation: Trialkylamines have 3 alkyl groups and 1 lone pair on nitrogen, making them isoelectronic and structurally analogous to carbanions.
