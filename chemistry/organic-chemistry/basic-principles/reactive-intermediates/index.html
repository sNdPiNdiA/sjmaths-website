Reactive Intermediates

Reactive intermediates are short-lived, high-energy chemical species that form during multi-step organic reactions and rapidly convert into more stable products. They possess incomplete octets or unpaired electrons, dictating the regiochemical and stereochemical pathways of organic transformations.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Structure and Stability of Carbocations and Carbanions

  • A carbocation features a positively charged trivalent carbon with an sp2 hybridization and an empty unhybridized p-orbital perpendicular to the molecular plane.
  • Carbocation stability is enhanced by hyperconjugation and +I electron-donating alkyl substituents, establishing the order 3° > 2° > 1°.
  • A carbanion possesses a negatively charged trivalent carbon with an sp3 hybridization and a lone pair occupying the fourth orbital, exhibiting a pyramidal geometry.
  • Carbanion stability increases with electron-withdrawing groups that disperse negative charge via inductive (-I) and resonance (-M) effects.

Carbon Free Radicals and Carbenes

  • A carbon free radical contains an odd electron in an unhybridized p-orbital, adopting a nearly planar or rapidly inverting shallow pyramidal geometry.
  • Free radical stabilization follows the same sequence as carbocations (3° > 2° > 1°) due to hyperconjugation and radical delocalization in allylic or benzylic systems.
  • Carbenes are neutral divalent carbon intermediates with six valence electrons, existing as either singlet carbenes (paired spins, bent sp2) or triplet carbenes (parallel spins, linear or bent sp).
  • Singlet carbenes exhibit stereospecific addition across alkenes, whereas triplet carbenes behave like diradicals yielding non-stereospecific mixtures.

Nitrenes and Arynes

  • Nitrenes are nitrogen analogs of carbenes containing a monovalent or divalent neutral nitrogen atom with six non-bonding electrons.
  • Nitrenes exist in singlet and triplet ground states, readily undergoing insertion into C-H bonds and rearrangement reactions such as the Curtius and Lossen rearrangements.
  • Arynes (such as benzyne, C6H4) are highly reactive intermediates containing a strained triple bond in a six-membered aromatic ring.
  • Aryne generation proceeds via elimination of ortho-halobenzenes using strong bases, followed by rapid nucleophilic addition yielding cine-substitution products.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Alkyl carbocations follow 3° > 2° > 1° stability order

Observation: Cyclopropylcarbinyl cation shows exceptionally high stability — Bisected conformation allows optimal orbital overlap between the bent bonds of the cyclopropyl ring and the empty p-orbital of the cationic center.

Exception Bridgehead carbocations are extremely unstable due to Bredt's rule

Observation: Small bridgehead systems like 1-adamantyl cation are stable and easily formed — The rigid cage prevents planarity at the bridgehead, but tertiary adamantyl cations can delocalize charge symmetrically through multiple C-C sigma bonds.

Exception Free radicals follow 3° > 2° > 1° stability trend

Observation: Triphenylmethyl radical is exceptionally stable despite steric hindrance — Extensive resonance delocalization of the unpaired electron over three aromatic rings compensates for steric crowding.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
Hyperconjugation Count Mnemonic
Number of Hyperconjugative Structures = Total α-hydrogens

Application: Count the number of C-H bonds directly attached to the alpha-carbon next to the radical or carbocation center to quickly determine relative stability.

Carbene Spin State Rule
Singlet = Paired (UP/DOWN), Triplet = Parallel (UP/UP)

Application: Remember that singlet carbenes have paired electrons in an sp2 orbital (spin = 0), while triplet carbenes have parallel electrons in separate orbitals (spin = 1).

Electron Demand Identification
Carbocation = Electrophilic, Carbanion = Nucleophilic

Application: Match the intermediate to its electronic requirement: electron-deficient species seek electron density, while electron-rich species donate electrons.

🎯 Key Exam Points
  • Carbocations possess an empty unhybridized p-orbital and a trigonal planar geometry with an ideal bond angle of 120°.
  • Carbanions act as strong carbon nucleophiles and conjugate bases of hydrocarbons.
  • Free radicals are generated via homolytic cleavage of covalent bonds using heat, UV light, or peroxides.
  • Singlet methylene (:CH2) adds stereospecifically to cis-2-butene to yield cis-1,2-dimethylcyclopropane.
  • Benzyne is a transient intermediate trapped by dienes via Diels-Alder cycloaddition reactions.
  • Rearrangement reactions such as pinacol-pinacolone and Wagner-Meerwein proceed through carbocation intermediates.
🚫 Common Errors & Confusions
  • Confusing carbocation geometry (trigonal planar, sp2) with carbanion geometry (pyramidal, sp3).
  • Assuming free radicals have an octet of electrons; they possess an odd number (seven valence electrons).
  • Forgetting that carbanions can undergo racemization at the chiral center due to rapid umbrella inversion.
  • Misidentifying singlet and triplet carbene reactivity patterns in stereospecific additions.

⚡ Quick Revision: Reactive Intermediates

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Carbocation Generation and Rearrangement

Carbocations are generated via heterolytic bond cleavage and undergo 1,2-hydride or 1,2-alkyl shifts to form more stable isomeric carbocations.

Carbanion Stabilization Factors

Carbanions are stabilized by -I and -M substituents, increased s-character of the carbon orbital, and polarizable heteroatoms.

Free Radical Halogenation Propagation

Free radical chain reactions proceed via initiation, propagation (involving halogen abstraction and radical combination), and termination steps.

Carbene Insertion and Addition

Carbenes insert into single σ-bonds and add across carbon-carbon double bonds to form substituted cyclopropanes.

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Carbocation (R3C+)
A positively charged carbon species with six valence electrons.
Carbanion (R3C-)
A negatively charged carbon species with eight valence electrons.
Free Radical (R3C•)
A neutral species containing an unpaired electron.
Carbene (R2C:)
A divalent neutral carbon intermediate with six valence electrons.
Nitrene (RN:)
A nitrogen analog of a carbene possessing six valence electrons.
Aryne (C6H4)
An aromatic intermediate featuring an aryne strained triple bond.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Stability order: 3° > 2° > 1° for carbocations and free radicals.
  • Stability order: 1° > 2° > 3° for carbanions.
  • Singlet carbenes react stereospecifically; triplet carbenes react non-stereospecifically.
  • Benzyne is generated from halobenzenes using strong bases like NaNH2 in liquid NH3.
  • Hyperconjugation stabilizes carbocations and free radicals through σ-p orbital overlap.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Reactive intermediates are transient, high-energy species dictating organic reaction pathways.
  • Electronic effects (inductive, resonance, hyperconjugation) govern their relative stabilities.
  • Geometrical constraints determine whether intermediates like bridgehead carbocations can form.
  • Trapping and stereochemical analysis provide experimental evidence for their existence.

⚖️ Key Differences

Singlet Carbene VS Triplet Carbene

Singlet has paired spins (spin = 0) and bent sp2 geometry; triplet has parallel spins (spin = 1) and radical-like behavior.

Question 1 of 18

What is the hybridization of the positively charged carbon in a simple alkyl carbocation?

Explanation: The positively charged carbon in a carbocation forms three sigma bonds and has an empty p-orbital, requiring sp2 hybridization.

Question 2 of 18

Which of the following carbocations is the most stable?

Explanation: Tertiary butyl cation ((CH3)3C+) is the most stable due to maximum hyperconjugation and +I effect from three methyl groups.

Question 3 of 18

What is the geometry of a methyl carbanion (CH3-)?

Explanation: The central carbon in a carbanion is sp3 hybridized with one lone pair, resulting in a pyramidal geometry.

Question 4 of 18

Which intermediate is formed during the reaction of chloroform with base to form dichlorocarbene?

Explanation: Base-catalyzed alpha-elimination of HCl from chloroform yields dichlorocarbene (:CCl2).

Question 5 of 18

Which free radical is stabilized by resonance?

Explanation: The allyl radical (CH2=CH-CH2•) exhibits resonance delocalization of the unpaired electron over two terminal carbons.

Question 6 of 18

What type of addition is observed when singlet methylene reacts with cis-2-butene?

Explanation: Singlet carbenes add to alkenes in a concerted, stereospecific syn-addition manner preserving alkene geometry.

Question 7 of 18

Which factor primarily accounts for the stability of tertiary free radicals?

Explanation: Hyperconjugation involving adjacent C-H sigma bonds stabilizes free radicals, supplemented by inductive electron donation.

Question 8 of 18

In the generation of benzyne from chlorobenzene using sodium amide, what is the initial step?

Explanation: The strong amide base abstracts an ortho-proton from chlorobenzene, initiating elimination to form benzyne.

Question 9 of 18

How many valence electrons are associated with the carbon atom in a carbocation?

Explanation: A carbocation possesses three bonds (six bonding electrons) and lacks a complete octet.

Question 10 of 18

Which species acts as an electrophile among the common reactive intermediates?

Explanation: Carbocations are electron-deficient (sextet of electrons) and function as strong electrophiles.

Question 11 of 18

What is the expected order of stability for carbanions?

Explanation: Alkyl groups are electron-donating (+I), which destabilizes the negative charge; hence methyl carbanion is most stable and tertiary is least stable.

Question 12 of 18

Which intermediate is involved in the Hofmann rearrangement of amides?

Explanation: The Hofmann rearrangement proceeds through an acyl nitrene intermediate formed via hypohalite elimination.

Question 13 of 18

What is the spin multiplicity of a triplet carbene?

Explanation: Triplet carbenes have two unpaired electrons with parallel spins, giving a spin multiplicity (2S + 1) of 3.

Question 14 of 18

Which of the following is a neutral reactive intermediate?

Explanation: Free radicals, carbenes, and nitrenes are neutral reactive intermediates possessing unpaired or non-bonding electrons.

Question 15 of 18

During pinacol rearrangement, the migrating group shifts to which intermediate center?

Explanation: Protonation of a diol and loss of water yields a carbocation to which an adjacent alkyl or aryl group migrates.

Question 16 of 18

Which carbonium ion intermediate is stabilized by aromaticity?

Explanation: The tropylium cation (C7H7+) is a planar, cyclic, conjugated species obeying Hückel's rule (4n+2 = 6 π electrons).

Question 17 of 18

What is the primary role of UV light in halogenation of alkanes via free radicals?

Explanation: UV light supplies the energy required to homolytically cleave halogen molecules into reactive free radicals.

Question 18 of 18

Which reagent is typically used to trap aryne intermediates via [4+2] cycloaddition?

Explanation: Furan acts as a diene in Diels-Alder reactions, trapping aryne intermediates to form stable Diels-Alder adducts.

Question 2 of 10
Which species is formed during the Reimer-Tiemann reaction of phenol with chloroform and sodium hydroxide?

Solution: The Reimer-Tiemann reaction proceeds via dichlorocarbene as the active electrophilic intermediate.

Question 3 of 10
Which of the following exhibits the highest stability in the gas phase?

Solution: Tertiary carbocations are the most stable due to hyperconjugative stabilization by nine alpha-hydrogens.

Question 4 of 10
In free radical chlorination of methane, which propagation step is endothermic?

Solution: Hydrogen abstraction from methane by a chlorine radical is slightly endothermic and determines the rate and selectivity.

Question 5 of 10
What is the primary intermediate in the Curtius rearrangement of acyl azides?

Solution: Thermolysis of acyl azides results in loss of N2 to form an acyl nitrene intermediate.

Question 6 of 10
Which intermediate possesses an octet of electrons around the central carbon atom?

Solution: A carbanion has three covalent bonds and a lone pair, giving it a complete octet (eight valence electrons).

Question 7 of 10
Why are bridgehead carbocations generally difficult to form in small bicyclic systems?

Solution: Bredt's rule dictates that bridgehead carbons cannot easily attain the planar sp2 geometry required for carbocations in small bicyclic rings.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following reactions proceeds via a free radical mechanism?

Solution: Anti-Markovnikov addition of HBr in the presence of peroxides proceeds via a free radical chain mechanism (peroxide effect).

Question 9 of 10
What is the reactive intermediate in the Kolbe electrolysis of sodium carboxylates?

Solution: Kolbe electrolysis involves anodic oxidation of carboxylate anions to form acyloxyl radicals, which decarboxylate into alkyl radicals.

Question 10 of 10
Which of the following compounds reacts fastest with aqueous AgNO3 to precipitate AgCl via a carbocation intermediate?

Solution: 2-Chloro-2-methylpropane (tert-butyl chloride) forms a stable tertiary carbocation readily via an SN1 mechanism.
