Effects of Hydrogen Bonding

Hydrogen bonding profoundly alters the physical and chemical properties of organic and inorganic molecules through electrostatic dipole interactions between hydrogen and highly electronegative atoms. These secondary forces dictate boiling points, solubility, conformational stability, and acidity.

UP PGT Chemistry Subject Code 02 B.Sc. / M.Sc. Level

Effects on Physical Properties

  • Intermolecular hydrogen bonding causes extensive association of molecules, leading to abnormally high boiling points and melting points in compounds like H2O, HF, and NH3 compared to their heavier congeners.
  • Viscosity and surface tension of liquids such as glycerol and water are significantly enhanced due to continuous three-dimensional intermolecular hydrogen-bonded networks.
  • Solubility in polar solvents like water is facilitated when solute molecules form hydrogen bonds with solvent molecules, explaining why low molecular weight alcohols and amines are miscible in water.
  • The open cage-like structure of ice caused by directional hydrogen bonding results in a lower density than liquid water, allowing ice to float.

Intramolecular vs Intermolecular Effects

  • Intramolecular hydrogen bonding occurs within the same molecule (chelation), typically forming 5- or 6-membered stable rings as seen in ortho-nitrophenol and salicylic acid.
  • Intramolecular hydrogen bonding decreases boiling point and water solubility because it restricts intermolecular association and shields polar functional groups from the solvent.
  • Ortho-nitrophenol exhibits lower boiling point and steam volatility due to chelation, whereas para-nitrophenol associates intermolecularly and remains non-volatile with a higher boiling point.
  • Acid strength is markedly influenced; intramolecular hydrogen bonding in ortho-hydroxybenzoic acid (salicylic acid) stabilizes the conjugate base or polarizes the O-H bond, altering pKa values relative to benzoic acid.

Structural and Spectroscopic Consequences

  • Infrared (IR) spectroscopy reveals intermolecular hydrogen bonding through a broad, shifted O-H or N-H stretching frequency band at lower wavenumbers (e.g., 3200-3400 cm-1).
  • Intramolecular hydrogen bonding yields a sharp, well-defined IR band that remains invariant upon concentration dilution, unlike intermolecular bonds which vary with concentration.
  • Protein secondary structure ($alpha$-helix and $eta$-pleated sheets) and the double-helical structure of DNA are stabilized by specific backbone and base-pairing hydrogen bonds.
  • Proton NMR spectra display downfield shifts (δ values) for protons involved in hydrogen bonding due to deshielding caused by electron density withdrawal toward the electronegative donor atom.

⚠️ Important Exceptions & Anomalies
Exception Boiling points increase regularly down a periodic group due to rising molecular mass and van der Waals forces.

Observation: H2O, HF, and NH3 have anomalously high boiling points compared to H2S, HCl, and PH3. — Presence of strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding in the first-row hydrides overcomes the trend of increasing London dispersion forces.

Exception Ortho-isomers generally possess higher boiling points and greater water solubility than para-isomers due to compact geometry.

Observation: Ortho-nitrophenol has a lower boiling point and is steam-volatile, whereas para-nitrophenol has a higher boiling point and is non-volatile. — Ortho-nitrophenol forms intramolecular hydrogen bonding, preventing intermolecular association, while para-nitrophenol forms intermolecular chains.

Exception Liquid density is greater than solid density for almost all substances.

Observation: Ice has a lower density (0.917 g cm-3) than liquid water (1.00 g cm-3) at 0 °C. — Directional hydrogen bonding forces water molecules into an open, tetrahedral crystalline lattice with larger intermolecular voids in ice.

💡 Shortcuts & Memory Aids
FON Rule for H-Bonding Capacity
H must be bonded to F, O, or N

Application: Quickly identify whether a molecule can form hydrogen bonds by checking if hydrogen is covalently linked to Fluorine, Oxygen, or Nitrogen.

Ortho vs Para Boiling Point Rule
Intra = Low BP, Inter = High BP

Application: For substituted phenols/nitrobenzoic acids, ortho-isomers with intramolecular H-bonding have lower boiling points and higher volatility than intermolecularly bonded para-isomers.

IR Spectra Dilution Test
Broad peak shifts on dilution = Inter; Sharp peak fixed on dilution = Intra

Application: Differentiate intra- and intermolecular hydrogen bonding in structural elucidation problems by observing IR peak behavior upon solvent dilution.

⚡ Quick Revision: Effects of Hydrogen Bonding

Core formulas, rapid recall points, and distinguishing criteria at a glance.

Origin and Nature of Hydrogen Bonding

Hydrogen bonding is a specialized electrostatic dipole-dipole attraction occurring when hydrogen is bound to high electronegativity atoms (F, O, N), creating a highly electron-deficient proton.

Macroscopic Physical Manifestations

Intermolecular hydrogen bonding elevates boiling points, melting points, viscosity, and enthalpy of vaporization across hydrides, alcohols, carboxylic acids, and amines.

Chelation and Intramolecular Effects

Intramolecular hydrogen bonding forms stable pseudo-aromatic rings (chelation), reducing boiling points, increasing steam volatility, and suppressing polar interactions with solvents.

Biological and Macromolecular Significance

Directional hydrogen bonding dictates the structural integrity of nucleic acids (DNA base pairing) and proteins ($alpha$-helices and $eta$-sheets).

📚 Terms & Units Glossary

Hydrogen Bond (X-H ••• Y)
Electrostatic attractive interaction between a hydrogen atom bound to an electronegative atom X and an electronegative atom Y.
Chelation (Intramolecular Ring)
Formation of a ring structure via intramolecular hydrogen bonding within a single molecule.
Association ((MX)n)
Aggregation of multiple molecules into larger complexes via intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
Steam Volatility (Vaporization with Steam)
Property of a substance to distill readily with water vapor, characteristic of molecules with intramolecular hydrogen bonding.
Van't Hoff Factor (i)
Ratio of actual concentration of particles to calculated concentration, where i < 1 for associating species.
Deshielding (δ shift)
Reduction of electron density around a proton involved in hydrogen bonding, shifting its NMR signal downfield.

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • H must be bonded to F, O, or N for classical hydrogen bonding.
  • Intermolecular H-bonding increases boiling point; Intramolecular H-bonding decreases boiling point.
  • Ice has an open tetrahedral cage structure making it less dense than liquid water.
  • Acetic acid forms a stable cyclic dimer in non-polar solvents.
  • Ortho-nitrophenol is steam volatile due to intramolecular H-bonding.

📌 Quick Recall

  • Hydrogen bonds are stronger than van der Waals forces but weaker than covalent or ionic bonds.
  • Physical properties like boiling point, melting point, and viscosity are directly amplified by intermolecular association.
  • Spectroscopic techniques (IR and NMR) provide definitive identification of intra- vs intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
  • Biological polymers rely entirely on cooperative hydrogen bonding for tertiary and quaternary folding.

⚖️ Key Differences

Intermolecular H-Bond VS Intramolecular H-Bond

Intermolecular occurs between distinct molecules increasing boiling point; intramolecular occurs within one molecule forming a chelate ring and decreasing boiling point.

The abnormally high boiling point of water is primarily due to:

Explanation: Extensive 3D intermolecular hydrogen bonding network in water requires high thermal energy to break during vaporization.

Why is ice less dense than liquid water?

Explanation: Directional hydrogen bonds force water molecules into an open, spacious tetrahedral lattice in the solid state, lowering its density.

Question 4 of 19

Which molecule exists as a dimer in benzene solution?

Explanation: Carboxylic acids like acetic acid form stable cyclic dimers linked by two intermolecular hydrogen bonds in non-polar solvents.

Question 5 of 19

Between ortho-nitrophenol and para-nitrophenol, which is more steam volatile?

Explanation: ortho-Nitrophenol possesses intramolecular hydrogen bonding which reduces intermolecular association, making it steam volatile.

Question 6 of 19

Hydrogen bonding is strongest when the electronegativity of the donor and acceptor atoms is:

Explanation: High electronegativity (such as F, O, N) creates high partial charges and strong electrostatic attraction.

Question 7 of 19

Which of the following hydrides has the lowest boiling point?

Explanation: PH3 does not undergo hydrogen bonding and has lower molecular weight and weaker dispersion forces than heavier congeners, but compared to NH3, PH3 lacks H-bonding and boils much lower.

Question 8 of 19

In infrared spectroscopy, intermolecular hydrogen bonding is characterized by:

Explanation: Intermolecular hydrogen bonds form varied polymeric aggregates, producing a broad IR band that changes with concentration.

Question 9 of 19

Which atom does NOT form effective hydrogen bonds?

Explanation: Chlorine has a larger atomic radius and lower charge density, making its hydrogen bonding capability negligible under normal conditions.

Question 10 of 19

The secondary structure of proteins such as alpha-helix is stabilized by:

Explanation: Alpha-helices and beta-sheets are stabilized by regular repeating hydrogen bonds between backbone carbonyl oxygens and amide hydrogens.

Question 11 of 19

Salicylic acid (ortho-hydroxybenzoic acid) has a lower pKa than benzoic acid primarily due to:

Explanation: Intramolecular hydrogen bonding in the conjugate base (salicylate anion) stabilizes the negative charge, enhancing acidity.

Question 12 of 19

What is the approximate bond energy range of a standard hydrogen bond?

Explanation: Hydrogen bonds are relatively weak secondary interactions, typically ranging between 10 to 40 kJ/mol.

Question 13 of 19

Which liquid exhibits the highest viscosity due to extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding?

Explanation: Glycerol contains three hydroxyl groups per molecule, allowing a dense 3D hydrogen-bonded network that imparts high viscosity.

Question 14 of 19

What happens to the NMR chemical shift (δ) of an O-H proton when hydrogen bonding becomes stronger?

Explanation: Stronger hydrogen bonding causes greater deshielding of the proton, moving its NMR signal downfield to higher δ values.

Question 15 of 19

KHF2 exists because of:

Explanation: Potassium hydrogen difluoride contains the symmetrical [F-H-F]- ion, held together by an extremely strong hydrogen bond.

Question 16 of 19

Which property is decreased by intramolecular hydrogen bonding?

Explanation: Intramolecular H-bonding prevents intermolecular association, resulting in a lower boiling point compared to the intermolecularly bonded para isomer.

Question 17 of 19

Ethanol is completely miscible in water primarily because:

Explanation: The hydroxyl group of ethanol forms hydrogen bonds with water molecules, ensuring complete aqueous miscibility.

Question 18 of 19

Which of the following does NOT possess hydrogen bonding?

Explanation: Methane (CH4) contains non-polar C-H bonds and lacks any electronegative donor atom (F, O, N) required for hydrogen bonding.

Question 19 of 19

The directional nature of hydrogen bonds is attributed to:

Explanation: Hydrogen bonds have partial covalent character and require specific directional alignment of lone pairs on the acceptor atom with the X-H bond axis.

Question 1 of 10
Which isomer of dihydroxybenzene is expected to have the highest intramolecular hydrogen bonding?

Solution: Catechol (1,2-dihydroxybenzene) places the two hydroxyl groups adjacent to each other (ortho), facilitating stable intramolecular hydrogen bonding.

Question 2 of 10
What is the primary reason for the ice structure to expand upon freezing?

Solution: Hydrogen bonding forces water molecules into an open, spacious crystal lattice in ice, increasing volume and decreasing density.

Question 3 of 10
In protein structures, α-helices are stabilized by hydrogen bonds parallel to:

Solution: The hydrogen bonds in an alpha-helix run nearly parallel to the axis of the helix, connecting C=O of residue i to N-H of residue i+4.

Question 4 of 10
Which statement regarding intramolecular hydrogen bonding is correct?

Solution: Intramolecular H-bonding causes chelation within the molecule, reducing intermolecular interaction and lowering boiling point and solubility.

Question 5 of 10
The boiling point of ethanol (CH3CH2OH) is much higher than that of dimethyl ether (CH3OCH3) because:

Solution: Ethanol possesses an O-H group capable of intermolecular hydrogen bonding, whereas dimethyl ether (an ether) cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds.

Question 6 of 10
Which of the following compounds does NOT form hydrogen bonds with water?

Solution: Methane lacks polar bonds and heteroatoms with lone pairs, preventing it from forming hydrogen bonds with water.

Question 7 of 10
What is the effect of intermolecular hydrogen bonding on the enthalpy of vaporization (ΔH_vap)?

Solution: Additional thermal energy is required to break intermolecular hydrogen bonds during vaporization, increasing the enthalpy of vaporization.

Question 8 of 10
Which of the following has the strongest hydrogen bonding?

Solution: Fluorine has the highest electronegativity, resulting in the strongest electrostatic dipole and hydrogen bond strength in F-H ••• F systems.

Question 9 of 10
Why does primary amine have a higher boiling point than tertiary amine of comparable molecular mass?

Solution: Primary amines (RNH2) have two hydrogen atoms bonded to nitrogen, enabling an extensive intermolecular hydrogen-bonded network compared to tertiary amines (R3N) which lack N-H bonds.

Question 10 of 10
In an infrared spectrum, intramolecular hydrogen bonding can be distinguished from intermolecular hydrogen bonding because intramolecular bands:

Solution: Intramolecular hydrogen bonding occurs within the same molecule, so its IR absorption band position and sharpness do not change when the solution is diluted.
