Gupta Dynasty

Ajanta Caves

Appears in: UP PGT History Tracker →

Topic overview

  • Ajanta Caves are a series of 29 (or 30) rock-cut Buddhist cave monuments located in Aurangabad district, Maharashtra.
  • The caves are carved into a sheer vertical cliff of a horseshoe-shaped meander of the Waghora River.
  • The site represents the golden age of Indian classical art and architecture, spanning primarily the Satavahana and Vakataka-Gupta periods.
  • Recognized globally, Ajanta was designated as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983.
Concept 1

Architectural Evolution and Chronological Phases

Ajanta development occurred in two distinct historical phases separated by centuries.

Details

  • Phase I is associated with the Satavahana dynasty dating back to the 2nd century BCE to 1st century BCE.
  • Phase II is linked to the Vakataka dynasty and their imperial Gupta contemporaries during the 5th to 6th century CE.
  • Early Hinayana phase caves were devoid of Buddha images, focusing on aniconic worship of stupas.
  • Later Mahayana phase introduced elaborate anthropomorphic Buddha icons and bodhisattva sculptures.
  • Total excavation count stands officially at 29 caves, though Cave 15A was discovered later making it 30.

Remember

  • Phase I caves include Caves 9, 10, 12, 13, and 15A.
  • Phase II caves comprise the vast majority including Caves 1, 2, 4, 16, 17, 19, 24, and 26.
  • Satavahana patronage is marked by simple chaityagrihas and viharas.
  • Vakataka patronage under Harishena saw the peak of architectural embellishment.
Examples & sources
  • Cave 10 represents the early Hinayana chaitya hall with horseshoe arched windows.
  • Cave 19 represents the fully developed Mahayana chaitya hall with a monolithic carved Buddha facade.
Compare
  • Phase I caves feature plain walls, rock-cut wooden beams, and aniconic stupa worship.
  • Phase II caves feature painted walls, elaborate pillar brackets, and monumental Buddha images.
Common confusions
  • All Ajanta caves belong to the Gupta period.
  • Ajanta caves were excavated in a continuous single timeline.
Exam points
  • Identify Phase I vs Phase II cave numbers.
  • Know the ruling dynasties associated with each phase.
  • Understand the transition from Hinayana to Mahayana architecture.
Concept 2

Vakataka Patronage and Emperor Harishena

The second and most magnificent phase of Ajanta was driven by Vakataka rulers, notably Harishena.

Details

  • Emperor Harishena of the Vakataka dynasty ruled the Deccan during the late 5th century CE.
  • His prime minister Varahadeva sponsored the excavation of Cave 16.
  • Feudatories and high officials of Harishena sponsored numerous other caves like Caves 17, 19, and 20.
  • Inscriptions in Cave 17 provide a detailed genealogy of the Vakataka kings and their feudatories.
  • The sudden termination of work in several caves indicates the abrupt collapse or political instability following Harishena's death.

Remember

  • Harishena ruled from c. 475 to 500 CE.
  • Cave 16 inscription explicitly mentions minister Varahadeva as the patron.
  • Upendragupta, a feudatory of Harishena, patronized Caves 17, 18, 19, and 20.
  • The political umbrella of the Vakatakas enabled peace and artistic flourishing in central India.
Examples & sources
  • Cave 17 contains the famous Yasodharman or Upendragupta inscription detailing Vakataka lineage.
  • Cave 26 was excavated largely through the patronage of a Buddhist monk named Buddhabhadra.
Compare
  • Satavahana patrons focused on austere, communal monastic spaces.
  • Vakataka-Gupta patrons focused on individual, richly decorated shrines and pillared halls.
Common confusions
  • Ajanta was directly commissioned by the Gupta emperors of Pataliputra.
  • Vakatakas were mere puppets of the Guptas with no independent cultural output.
Exam points
  • Remember Emperor Harishena as the master patron of Phase II.
  • Identify Varahadeva and Upendragupta as key epigraphical figures.
  • Link the end of Ajanta excavations to the decline of the Vakataka dynasty.
Concept 3

Chaityagrihas and Vihara Typology

Ajanta architecture is classified strictly into devotional prayer halls and residential monastic units.

Details

  • Chaityagrihas are apsidal halls with a central nave, side aisles, and a stupa at the apse.
  • Viharas are rectangular monastic residential halls surrounded by small monk cells along the walls.
  • Early chaityagrihas feature ribbed ceilings mimicking wooden construction despite being solid rock.
  • Later viharas evolved from simple single-storey square halls to multi-pillared halls with inner sanctums.
  • Porches, pillared verandahs, and elaborate facades became standard features in the Vakataka phase.

Remember

  • Chaitya caves at Ajanta include Caves 9, 10, 19, 26, and 29.
  • Vihara caves comprise the rest, serving as living quarters, study halls, and shrines.
  • Cave 19 and Cave 26 are ornate Mahayana chaitya halls with carved pillars and Buddha reliefs.
  • Cave 1 and Cave 4 represent massive, highly sophisticated Vihara plans.
Examples & sources
  • Cave 10 is the oldest chaitya hall with an extensive timber-emulating arched ceiling.
  • Cave 1 is a grand Vihara featuring elaborate verandah pillars and a sanctum housing the seated Buddha.
Compare
  • Chaityagrihas served a congregational worship and circumambulation function around a central stupa.
  • Viharas served primarily as residential dormitories with an attached sanctum in later phases.
Common confusions
  • All caves at Ajanta contain stupas.
  • Viharas were purely secular administrative buildings.
Exam points
  • Distinguish between Chaitya (prayer hall) and Vihara (monastery).
  • List the specific Chaitya caves at Ajanta.
  • Recognize structural shifts in Vihara design during Phase II.
Concept 4

Painting Techniques and Masterpiece Murals

Ajanta houses the finest surviving specimens of ancient Indian mural painting executed in tempera style.

Details

  • The technique involved applying a rough clay, cow dung, and rice husk ground directly onto the porous basalt rock.
  • A fine coat of lime plaster was then applied over the clay layer to serve as the smooth canvas.
  • Artists outlined the figures in bold red or black pigment before filling in natural mineral and vegetable colors.
  • Pigments included lapis lazuli, lampblack, red ochre, yellow ochre, terraverte, and chalk.
  • Unlike true fresco, Ajanta murals were painted on dry plaster, making them tempera works.

Remember

  • The subject matter of paintings revolves heavily around Jataka tales, past lives of Gautama Buddha, and Avadanas.
  • Cave 1 contains the celebrated paintings of Padmapani (Avalokiteshvara) and Vajrapani.
  • Cave 2 features the famous 'Thring' or Flying Apsaras and the 'Mother and Child before Buddha' mural.
  • Cave 16 houses the touching narrative of the 'Dying Princess' (Nanda's wife Sundari).
Examples & sources
  • Padmapani in Cave 1 is an epitome of classical Indian aesthetic grace, compassion, and poise.
  • Simhala Avadana in Cave 17 depicts maritime trade scenes and the story of Prince Vijaya.
Compare
  • True fresco involves painting on wet plaster where pigments chemically bond with lime.
  • Ajanta tempera involves painting on dry plaster bonded with organic animal or plant glue mediums.
Common confusions
  • Ajanta paintings are executed using the Italian true fresco (fresco buono) technique.
  • The paintings are purely decorative court scenes without religious context.
Exam points
  • Identify Padmapani and Vajrapani in Cave 1.
  • Identify the Dying Princess mural in Cave 16.
  • Understand the plaster preparation method and dry tempera technique.
Concept 5

Sculptural Art and Gupta-Vakataka Iconography

Sculptures at Ajanta define the classic Gupta aesthetic canon with balanced proportions and serene expressions.

Details

  • Phase I sculptures were sparse, focusing on aniconic symbols, railing patterns, and dwarf figures (yakshas).
  • Phase II introduced massive monolithic Buddha figures in various mudras (gestures) like dharmachakra and bhumisparsha.
  • Bodhisattvas such as Padmapani and Manjushri flanked the central Buddha shrine doors.
  • Flying figures, gandharvas, nagas, and apsaras adorned door jambs and pillar capitals.
  • Relief carvings demonstrated deep mastery over volume, subtle facial expressions, and graceful body tribhanga postures.

Remember

  • Cave 19 features a prominent standing Buddha carved directly onto the facade stupa.
  • Cave 26 houses the monumental reclining Buddha depicting the Mahaparinirvana scene.
  • Naga kings and their queens are frequently carved as protectors at cave entrances.
  • Sculptures were originally painted with bright pigments, traces of which can still be observed.
Examples & sources
  • The Mahaparinirvana relief in Cave 26 showing mourning disciples and rejoicing celestials.
  • The colossal seated Buddha preaching in the sanctum of Cave 1.
Compare
  • Mathura school sculptures used spotted red sandstone with bold, dynamic linear energy.
  • Ajanta sculptures utilized local basalt rock with soft, deeply meditative, and restrained spiritual grace.
Common confusions
  • Ajanta art is exclusively pictorial and lacks significant rock-cut sculpture.
  • Sculptures at Ajanta were always left unpainted in bare stone.
Exam points
  • Recognize the Mahaparinirvana sculpture location (Cave 26).
  • Understand the integration of sculpture with architectural sanctums.
  • Know the characteristic features of Gupta-Vakataka sculptural canons.
Concept 6

Historical Rediscovery and Conservation History

After centuries of abandonment, Ajanta was accidentally rediscovered by British troops in the 19th century.

Details

  • The caves were rediscovered on April 28, 1819, by British cavalry officer John Smith of the 28th Cavalry.
  • John Smith famously scratched his name and the date into the plaster of Cave 10 while viewing the paintings.
  • Following rediscovery, the site suffered severe damage from treasure hunters, bats, smoke, and crude early cleaning attempts.
  • Major conservation efforts were initiated by the Nizam of Hyderabad state in the early 20th century, employing Italian conservator Lorenzo Cecconi.
  • Epigraphist James Burgess and archaeologist John Marshall contributed immensely to documenting and preserving the site.

Remember

  • Rediscovery year: 1819 by John Smith.
  • Cave 10 is the exact cave where John Smith left his graffiti.
  • The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) currently manages and protects the monument.
  • Chemical conservation treatments were applied in the 20th century to arrest flaking plaster and mold growth.
Examples & sources
  • John Smith's etched signature on the painted pillar of Cave 10 remains visible today as a historical marker.
  • Early 20th-century photographs by Robert Gill documented the paintings before further decay occurred.
Compare
  • Ellora caves remained relatively more visible and continuously known to local populations compared to the jungle-overgrown Ajanta.
  • Ajanta required extensive jungle clearing and bat eradication during British colonial intervention.
Common confusions
  • Ajanta caves were discovered by Alexander Cunningham.
  • The paintings have remained in pristine condition since the 5th century without human intervention.
Exam points
  • Remember John Smith and the year 1819.
  • Know the role of the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) in modern preservation.
  • Identify Cave 10 as the site of John Smith's discovery graffiti.

Quick facts

  • Total Caves: 29 official excavations (30 including 15A).
  • River: Waghora River horseshoe bend.
  • Dynasties: Satavahana (Phase I) and Vakataka (Phase II).
  • Key Ruler: Emperor Harishena of Vakataka dynasty.
  • Rediscovery: John Smith in April 1819 (Cave 10).
  • UNESCO Status: World Heritage Site since 1983.
  • Painting Technique: Dry tempera on clay-lime plaster.
  • Famous Murals: Padmapani, Vajrapani, Dying Princess.

Memory aids

  • Satavahana Simple, Vakataka Vast
  • Harishena Had Huge Vakataka Halls
  • John Smith Saw Cave Ten

  • Memorize key cave numbers associated with specific masterpieces (Cave 1, 10, 16, 17, 19, 26).
  • Keep in mind that Ajanta paintings are tempera, not true fresco.
  • Revise Vakataka feudatories like Upendragupta and minister Varahadeva.

Question cues

  • If the question mentions stupa worship without statues, select Satavahana Phase I.

  • If the question mentions the Dying Princess, select Cave 16.
  • If the question mentions Mahaparinirvana sculpture, select Cave 26.

Compare

Ajanta CavesvsEllora Caves
  • Ajanta is exclusively Buddhist; Ellora represents a synthesis of Buddhist, Hindu, and Jain monuments.
  • Ajanta is carved along a single horseshoe river cliff; Ellora is carved along a multi-kilometre basalt scarp.
  • Ajanta is globally renowned for its mural paintings; Ellora is renowned for massive monolithic rock-cut architecture like the Kailash Temple.

Watch for

  • Assuming all Ajanta caves were built during the Gupta empire.
  • Confusing Ajanta's dry tempera painting method with wet fresco.
  • Believing Ajanta caves contain only paintings and lack monumental rock-cut sculptures.
  • Mistaking Chaitya caves for residential Viharas.
  • Attributing the rediscovery of Ajanta to Alexander Cunningham instead of John Smith.
