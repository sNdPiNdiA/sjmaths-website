Gupta Dynasty

Chandragupta Vikramaditya

Appears in: UP PGT History Tracker → UP TGT Social Science Tracker →

Topic overview

  • Chandragupta II, also known as Vikramaditya, was one of the most powerful and illustrious emperors of the Gupta dynasty, ruling from approximately 380 CE to 415 CE.
  • He succeeded his father, Samudragupta, after a brief succession crisis involving his elder brother Ramagupta, as narrated in Vishakhadatta's play Devichandraguptam.
  • His reign represents the golden age of the Gupta Empire, characterized by supreme political stability, unprecedented military expansion, and remarkable achievements in art, literature, and science.
  • He assumed the imperial titles of Vikramaditya, Sakari (conqueror of the Sakas), and Narendrasimha, symbolizing his military triumphs and sovereign authority.
  • His court at Ujjain was adorned by the legendary Navaratnas (Nine Gems), including the celebrated poet and dramatist Kalidasa.
Concept 1

Accession and Early Reign

The controversial succession of Chandragupta II following Ramagupta.

Details

  • Samudragupta was succeeded by his elder son Ramagupta, whose historical existence is confirmed by literary sources and epigraphic finds.
  • According to the Sanskrit drama Devichandraguptam by Vishakhadatta, Ramagupta suffered a military defeat against a Saka ruler and agreed to surrender his queen Dhruvadevi.
  • Chandragupta disguised himself as queen Dhruvadevi, entered the enemy camp, and assassinated the Saka ruler to salvage the imperial honor.
  • Chandragupta subsequently overthrew his weak brother Ramagupta, married his widow Dhruvadevi, and ascended the imperial throne.
  • This dramatic political maneuver consolidated his position and initiated a period of aggressive foreign policy.

Remember

  • Predecessor: Ramagupta
  • Literary source: Devichandraguptam by Vishakhadatta
  • Key queen: Dhruvadevi
  • Assumed title: Vikramaditya
Examples & sources
  • The play Devichandraguptam detailing the Saka crisis
  • Banabhatta's Harshacharita referencing the episode of the Saka chief's assassination
Compare
  • Ramagupta was weak and cowardly, whereas Chandragupta II was aggressive and decisive.
  • Ramagupta's reign left numismatic traces in central India, while Chandragupta's coinage spanned a vast empire.
Common confusions
  • Students often confuse Chandragupta II's accession with Chandragupta Maurya's overthrow of the Nanda dynasty.
  • Many assume Dhruvadevi was Chandragupta's first wife without considering the Devichandraguptam narrative.
Exam points
  • Identify the author of Devichandraguptam.
  • Name the weak predecessor ousted by Chandragupta II.
  • Recognize the historical significance of the Saka episode.
Concept 2

Military Conquests and Saka War

The decisive campaigns against the Western Satraps and northern tribes.

Details

  • Chandragupta II's greatest military achievement was the total annihilation of the Saka Kshatrapas (Western Satraps) of Gujarat and Malwa.
  • The war against the Sakas lasted from approximately 388 CE to 409 CE, ending with the defeat of the last Saka ruler, Rudrasimha III.
  • This conquest brought the rich western ports of Gujarat and Kathiawar under Gupta control, opening direct trade links with the Roman Empire through the Arabian Sea.
  • Following this victory, he earned the title of Sakari (destroyer of the Sakas) and introduced silver coinage modeled on the Saka currency.
  • He also conducted military campaigns against the Vahlikas (Bactrians) across the Oxus river and subdued the eastern and western border tribes.

Remember

  • Last Saka ruler defeated: Rudrasimha III
  • Chronology of Saka war: c. 388 CE - 409 CE
  • Major economic gain: Control of western ports like Bharuch and Sopara
  • New currency introduced: Silver coins replacing Saka prototypes
Examples & sources
  • Mehrauli Iron Pillar Inscription recording victories in Vanga and Vahlika countries
  • Silver coinage issues carrying the Garuda motif and Saka weight standard
Compare
  • Samudragupta focused primarily on the Deccan and southern campaigns, whereas Chandragupta II focused on western and northwestern expansions.
  • Chandragupta II permanently annexed western India, unlike Samudragupta who practiced Dhana-da-grahana (tribute extraction) in the south.
Common confusions
  • Some texts misattribute the end of the Saka power in western India to Samudragupta.
  • Students often think the Mehrauli inscription belongs to Samudragupta instead of Chandragupta.
Exam points
  • Identify the title 'Sakari' with Chandragupta II.
  • Name the last Western Satrap ruler defeated by the Guptas.
  • Analyze the economic impact of the Gujarat and Kathiawar annexation.
Concept 3

Matrimonial Alliances and Diplomacy

Strategic marital ties that secured the Gupta northern and southern flanks.

Details

  • Chandragupta II utilized matrimonial alliances as a key instrument of statecraft to neutralize potential enemies without waging war.
  • He married Kuberanaga, a princess of the Naga dynasty, which secured the crucial central Indian region and aided his expansion against the Sakas.
  • He married his daughter, Prabhavatigupta, to Rudrasena II of the powerful Vakataka dynasty of the Deccan.
  • Following the premature death of Rudrasena II, Prabhavatigupta ruled as regent for her minor sons, effectively bringing the Vakataka kingdom into the Gupta sphere of influence.
  • This Vakataka alliance provided a secure southern flank during his protracted war against the Western Satraps.

Remember

  • Naga queen: Kuberanaga
  • Vakataka alliance: Daughter Prabhavatigupta married to Rudrasena II
  • Geographical benefit: Secured central and southern approaches
  • Political outcome: De facto Gupta control over the Deccan plateau during the regency
Examples & sources
  • Pune copper plate inscription issued by Queen Prabhavatigupta
  • Riddhapur copper plate inscription detailing Vakataka-Gupta administrative collaboration
Compare
  • Chandragupta I used the Lichchhavi alliance for legitimacy, while Chandragupta II used Naga and Vakataka alliances for tactical military security.
  • Bindusara and Ashoka used diplomatic missions, whereas Chandragupta II used strategic dynastic marriages.
Common confusions
  • Students frequently confuse Prabhavatigupta's marriage with Samudragupta's alliances.
  • Many assume the Vakatakas were conquered by force rather than integrated through diplomacy.
Exam points
  • Name the Vakataka king who married Chandragupta II's daughter.
  • Explain the strategic importance of the Vakataka alliance in the Saka campaign.
  • Identify Prabhavatigupta's role as queen regent.
Concept 4

Administration and Foreign Accounts

Administrative efficiency and Fa-hien's observations of Gupta society.

Details

  • The Gupta administrative machinery under Chandragupta II was decentralized yet highly efficient, featuring provincial divisions (Bhuktis) and districts (Vishayas).
  • Local governance involved municipal boards (Adhisthana) and corporate bodies of merchants and artisans (Shrenis).
  • The Chinese Buddhist pilgrim Fa-hien (Faxian) visited India between 399 CE and 414 CE during his reign.
  • Fa-hien noted the peace and prosperity of the empire, light taxation, absence of capital punishment, and the freedom of movement for citizens.
  • He observed that the majority of the population did not consume meat, onions, or garlic, and that Chandalas lived outside city limits and notified their approach by striking pieces of wood.

Remember

  • Chinese pilgrim: Fa-hien (Faxian)
  • Chronology of visit: 399 CE to 414 CE
  • Key observation: Low crime rate, no registration required for travelers
  • Social note: Practice of untouchability noted regarding Chandalas
Examples & sources
  • Fa-hien's travelogue Fo-ko-ki (Record of Buddhist Kingdoms)
  • Udayagiri cave inscriptions documenting local administrative officials like Virasena
Compare
  • Megasthenes reported a severe Mauryan penal system, whereas Fa-hien reported a lenient Gupta penal system with fines rather than corporal punishment.
  • Fa-hien focused primarily on Buddhist monasteries and moral order, while Hiuen Tsang later focused on philosophy and royal patronage.
Common confusions
  • Students often confuse Fa-hien with Hiuen Tsang, placing the former in Harsha's reign.
  • Many believe Fa-hien met Chandragupta II personally, though his travelogue does not mention the king by name.
Exam points
  • Identify Fa-hien's travel dates and Chinese title of his book.
  • Recall Fa-hien's observations on the socio-economic condition of the Gupta empire.
  • Name the administrative officers mentioned in Udayagiri inscriptions.
Concept 5

Art, Literature, and Cultural Milieu

The cultural zenith of ancient India under the Navaratnas.

Details

  • Chandragupta II's court was renowned for the Navaratnas (Nine Gems) of Sanskrit literature and science.
  • Kalidasa, the greatest classical Sanskrit poet and dramatist, wrote masterpieces like Abhijnanashakuntalam, Meghaduta, and Raghuvamsha during this era.
  • Other luminaries included Amarasimha (lexicographer of Amarakosha), Varahamihira (astronomer), Dhanvantari (physician), and Kshapanaka.
  • Architecture transitioned from rock-cut caves to freestanding stone temples with the emergence of the Nagara style.
  • The famous iron pillar at Mehrauli, Delhi, stands as a metallurgical marvel of this era, resisting rust for over sixteen centuries.

Remember

  • Court circle: Navaratnas (Nine Gems)
  • Prominent literary figure: Kalidasa
  • Lexicon: Amarakosha by Amarasimha
  • Architectural milestone: Early stone temples like Dashavatara at Deogarh and temples at Udayagiri and Bhitargaon
Examples & sources
  • Mehrauli Iron Pillar inscription mentioning King Chandra
  • Udayagiri rock-cut cave sanctuaries featuring the Varaha incarnation sculpture
Compare
  • Maurya art favored monolithic pillars and stupas with polished sandstone, whereas Gupta art featured refined temple architecture and classical sculpture.
  • Kushan art showed heavy Hellenistic and Gandhara influence, while Gupta art displayed classical Indian purity and idealized forms.
Common confusions
  • Many students attribute the Mehrauli iron pillar to Chandragupta Maurya.
  • Some believe all nine Navaratnas lived simultaneously at court, whereas some were legendary additions.
Exam points
  • List the primary works of Kalidasa.
  • Identify the author of Amarakosha.
  • Analyze the metallurgical significance of the Mehrauli Iron Pillar.
Concept 6

Coinage and Epigraphic Sources

Numismatic evolution and inscriptions documenting Chandragupta II's reign.

Details

  • Chandragupta II issued a wide variety of gold coins (Dinaras) depicting different motifs and personal achievements.
  • Major coin types include the Archer type, Couch type, Lion-tamer type, Horseman type, and Chattra (Umbrella) type.
  • His silver coins were introduced specifically for western territories after defeating the Sakas, utilizing the local silver weight standard.
  • The Mehrauli Iron Pillar inscription in Delhi is a principal epigraphic source, attributing conquests in the Vanga and Vahlika lands to a king named Chandra.
  • Udayagiri cave inscriptions provide concrete evidence of his military ministers, religious tolerance, and patronage of Vaishnavism and Shaivism.

Remember

  • Gold coin standard: Dinara
  • Unique coin types: Archer, Lion-tamer, Chattra, Horseman
  • Key epigraphs: Mehrauli Iron Pillar, Udayagiri Cave Inscriptions, Mathura stone inscription
  • Religious identity: Paramabhagavata (devotee of Vishnu)
Examples & sources
  • Chattra type gold coins showing an attendant holding an umbrella over the king
  • Udayagiri cave inscription of Sanakanika chief belonging to the Gupta year 82 (401 CE)
Compare
  • Samudragupta's coins emphasized martial vigor like the Battle-axe type, while Chandragupta II's coins highlighted cultural pursuits alongside martial types like the Archer and Lion-tamer.
  • Gupta gold coins were initially pure but saw a gradual decline in purity by the late Gupta period, though Chandragupta II's coins remained of high standard.
Common confusions
  • Students sometimes confuse Gupta silver coins with Kushan copper issues.
  • Many assume all 'Chandra' inscriptions refer to Chandragupta Maurya without checking epigraphic contexts.
Exam points
  • Identify the specific coin types unique to Chandragupta II.
  • Explain the historical context behind Gupta silver coinage.
  • Evaluate the evidence provided by the Mehrauli Iron Pillar.
