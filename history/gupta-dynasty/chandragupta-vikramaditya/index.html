Gupta Dynasty

Chandragupta Vikramaditya

Core study module, theoretical overview, and exam revision checklist for Chandragupta Vikramaditya under the History curriculum.

Appears in: UP PGT History Tracker → UP TGT Social Science Tracker →

1. Syllabus Overview & Exam Focus

In competitive examinations (UP TGT Social Science / UP PGT History / UGC NET), Chandragupta Vikramaditya is a key tested topic. Focus your preparation on the following high-priority points:

  • Political expansion, military campaigns, and western conquest of the Shakas by Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya).
  • Literary developments, the legendary Navaratnas (Nine Gems), and accounts of the Chinese pilgrim Faxian (Fa-Hien).
  • Numismatic evidence, gold coinage, and socioeconomic prosperity during the Gupta Golden Age.
  • Matrimonial alliances and diplomatic strategies employed to consolidate the Gupta Empire.

2. Historical Background, Succession & Sources

  • • Accession to the Throne: Ruled from approximately 380 CE to 415 CE, succeeding his father Samudragupta after a brief interlude involving his brother Ramagupta.
  • • Literary Sources: Visakhadatta’s Sanskrit drama Devichandraguptam details the overthrow of Ramagupta and Chandragupta's marriage to Dhruvadevi.
  • • Mehrauli Iron Pillar Inscription: Located in Delhi, it records the exploits of a ruler named 'Chandra' who defeated the Vahlikas across the seven mouths of the Indus and established supreme sovereignty.
  • • Udayagiri Cave Inscriptions: Epigraphs near Vidisha, Madhya Pradesh, provide direct evidence of his military campaigns, territorial expansion, and patron-minister alliances (such as Sanakanika feudatory and minister Virasena Saba).

3. Military Conquests, Expansion & Foreign Policy

  • • Destruction of Western Satraps: Overthrew the Shaka-Kshatrapas of Western India (Gujarat and Malwa) between 388 CE and 409 CE, ending more than three centuries of foreign rule in western India.
  • • Adoption of Title 'Sakari': Assumed the victorious title 'Sakari' (Destroyer of the Shakas) and 'Vikramaditya' (Sun of Valour) after defeating Rudrasimha III.
  • • Matrimonial Alliances: Strengthened his southern flank by marrying his daughter Prabhatagupta to Rudrasena II of the Vakataka dynasty, effectively bringing the Vakatakas into a sphere of Gupta paramountcy.
  • • NW Frontier Campaigns: Extended imperial borders toward the northwestern frontiers by subduing the Vahlikas (Bactrians) as corroborated by the Mehrauli Iron Pillar.

4. Administration, Society & Faxian's Account

  • • Faxian’s Visit: The Chinese Buddhist pilgrim Faxian (Fa-Hien) visited India between 399 CE and 414 CE during his reign, noting the peaceful administration, mild penal code, and absence of capital punishment.
  • • Provincial Administration: The empire was divided into provinces (Bhuktis), headed by Uparikas, which were further subdivided into districts (Vishayas) administered by Vishayapatis.
  • • Religious Tolerance: Although a devout Vaishnavite (taking titles like Paramabhagavata), his court accommodated diverse religious beliefs, including Buddhism and Jainism.
  • • Capital City: Pataliputra remained the magnificent imperial capital, while Ujjain served as a prominent secondary capital and center of trade and culture.

5. Art, Culture, Numismatics & The Navaratnas

  • • The Navaratnas (Nine Gems): His court was legendary for hosting nine luminaries, most notably the poet and dramatist Kalidasa, physician Dhanvantari, lexicographer Amarasimha, astronomer Varahamihira, and mathematician/astronomer Aryabhata.
  • • Gold Coinage (Dinaras): Issued diverse and artistically superior gold coins including the Archer type, Couch type, Lion-tamer type, and Chhatra (umbrella) type.
  • • Silver Currency: First Gupta ruler to issue silver coins, specifically minted to commemorate his decisive victory over the Western Satraps.
  • • Temple Architecture: Marked the transition from rock-cut shrines to structural stone temples, exemplified by the Dashavatara Temple at Deogarh and the early brick/stone temples at Bhitargaon and Udayagiri.

6. High-Yield Key Facts & Exam Memory Points

  • Key Fact: Chandragupta II assumed the title 'Vikramaditya' after defeating the Shakas of Western India.
  • Key Fact: Chinese pilgrim Faxian visited India during the reign of Chandragupta II.
  • Key Fact: The Mehrauli Iron Pillar inscription in Delhi is attributed to king 'Chandra' (Chandragupta II).
  • Key Fact: His daughter, Prabhatagupta, married the Vakataka king Rudrasena II.
  • Key Fact: Kalidasa, the immortal Sanskrit poet and playwright, adorned the court of Chandragupta Vikramaditya.
  • Key Fact: Chandragupta II was the first Gupta monarch to issue silver coins in imitation of the defeated Western Satraps.

7. Common Exam Pitfalls & Confusions to Avoid

  • Do not confuse Chandragupta I (who started the Gupta era and married Kumaradevi) with his grandson Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya) who conquered western India.
  • Remember that Faxian (Fa-Hien) came during Chandragupta II's reign, whereas Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang) came much later during Harshavardhana's reign.
  • The title 'Vikramaditya' was adopted by several Indian kings historically (such as Hemu and Vikramaditya of Ujjain folklore); ensure you correctly link it to Chandragupta II in Gupta history MCQs.
