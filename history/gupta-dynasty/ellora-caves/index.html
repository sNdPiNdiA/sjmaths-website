Ellora Caves

Core study module, theoretical overview, and exam revision checklist for Ellora Caves under the History curriculum.

Appears in: UP PGT History Tracker →

1. Syllabus Overview & Exam Focus

In competitive examinations (UP TGT Social Science / UP PGT History / UGC NET), Ellora Caves is a key tested topic. Focus your preparation on the following high-priority points:

  • Comprehensive study of Ellora Caves architecture, rock-cut techniques, and sculptural chronology for UP PGT History and UP TGT Social Science.
  • Dynastic patronage analysis focusing on the Rashtrakutas, Kalachuris, and Yadavas of Devagiri.
  • Religious synthesis and syncretism reflected in the Buddhist, Hindu, and Jain monuments (Caves 1 to 34).
  • Detailed examination of Cave 16 (Kailasa Temple) as a pinnacle of monolithic rock architecture.

2. Historical Background & Geographical Context

  • • Geographical Location: Situated in the Aurangabad (officially Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar) district of Maharashtra, carved into the Charanandri hills of the Sahyadri mountain range.
  • • Ancient Nomenclature: Known historically in ancient inscriptions as Elapura or Verul, serving as a major commercial and religious hub on ancient trade routes.
  • • Chronological Span: Construction and rock-cut excavation spanned from the 6th century CE to the 10th century CE, reflecting continuous religious and political patronage.
  • • UNESCO Recognition: Designated as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983 for its peerless artistic achievement and architectural brilliance in rock-cut tradition.

3. Architectural Typology & Religious Distribution

  • • Total Cave Count: Comprises 34 principal excavated caves open to the public, numbered sequentially from South to North based on religious affiliation.
  • • Buddhist Group (Caves 1–12): Earliest phase of excavation (6th–8th centuries CE), featuring massive monasteries (Viharas) and prayer halls (Chaityas) like Cave 10 (Vishwakarma Cave).
  • • Hindu Group (Caves 13–29): Patronized largely during the Kalachuri and Rashtrakuta periods, featuring elaborate temples dedicated to Lord Shiva, Vishnu, and various manifestations of Shakti.
  • • Jain Group (Caves 30–34): Later phase of excavation (9th–10th centuries CE) belonging to the Digambara sect, featuring refined detailing, structural symmetry, and icons of Tirthankaras like Mahavira and Parshvanatha.

4. Key Monuments & Royal Patronage

  • • Rashtrakuta Patronage: The peak of Ellora's artistic output occurred under the Rashtrakuta dynasty, particularly during the reigns of Krishna I, Dhruva Dharavarsha, and Amoghavarsha I.
  • • Kailasa Temple (Cave 16): Commissioned by Rashtrakuta King Krishna I (8th century CE), it is the world's largest monolithic rock excavation, carved top-down from a single basalt cliff.
  • • Ravan ki Phadi (Cave 14) & Dasavatara (Cave 15): Early Hindu excavations containing dramatic narrative reliefs depicting mythological episodes such as Hiranyakashipu's slaying and Ravana shaking Mount Kailasa.
  • • Indra Sabha (Cave 32): The finest Jain cave at Ellora, featuring a monolithic shrine in the courtyard, intricate ceiling carvings, and seated images of Ambika and various Tirthankaras.

5. Sculptural Style, Technique & Historiography

  • • Top-Down Excavation Technique: Master craftsmen utilized the monolithic subtractive method, beginning at the summit of the rock cliff and carving downward to prevent structural failure.
  • • Deccan Art Style: Reflects a transition from post-Gupta classicism to a robust, dynamic regional Deccan style characterized by deep undercutting and emotional intensity.
  • • Religious Harmony: The juxtaposition of Buddhist, Hindu, and Jain caves within close proximity illustrates the era's profound religious tolerance, theological debate, and cultural synthesis.
  • • Epigraphical Evidence: The Baroda copper plate and the famous copper plate inscription of Dantidurga provide vital historical chronological anchors for dating the Ellora excavations.

6. High-Yield Key Facts & Exam Memory Points

  • Key Fact: Ellora Caves consist of 34 major caves divided into Buddhist (1-12), Hindu (13-29), and Jain (30-34) groups.
  • Key Fact: Cave 10, known as the 'Carpenter's Cave' or Vishwakarma Cave, is the only Buddhist Chaitya hall at Ellora.
  • Key Fact: The magnificent Kailasa Temple (Cave 16) was built under the patronage of the Rashtrakuta ruler Krishna I.
  • Key Fact: The Kailasa Temple was carved using a top-down vertical excavation method, removing an estimated 400,000 tons of rock.
  • Key Fact: Ellora is locally known as 'Verul' and was historically referred to as 'Elapura' in Rashtrakuta inscriptions.
  • Key Fact: The site was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in the year 1983, alongside Agra Fort, Ajanta Caves, and Taj Mahal.

7. Common Exam Pitfalls & Confusions to Avoid

  • Exam Trap: Do not confuse the patron of Ellora's Kailasa Temple (Krishna I of the Rashtrakutas) with Krishna II or builders of structural temples like the Brihadisvara.
  • Distinction: Unlike Ajanta, which is exclusively Buddhist and predominantly utilizes mural paintings, Ellora is multi-religious (Buddhist, Hindu, Jain) and primarily famous for rock-cut architecture and stone sculptures.
  • Chronological Trap: Ensure you do not attribute all caves to the Gupta period; while Gupta artistic influence is strong, the caves span from the 6th to the 10th century CE under the Kalachuris, Rashtrakutas, and Yadavas.
