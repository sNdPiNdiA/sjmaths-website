Arab Invasions on India

Core study module, theoretical overview, and exam revision checklist for Arab Invasions on India under the History curriculum.

Appears in: UP PGT History Tracker →

1. Syllabus Overview & Exam Focus

In competitive examinations (UP TGT Social Science / UP PGT History / UGC NET), Arab Invasions on India is a key tested topic. Focus your preparation on the following high-priority points:

  • Nature and political condition of Sindh on the eve of the Arab invasion under King Dahir.
  • Early unsuccessful maritime and land expeditions led by Ubaidullah and Budail against Sindh.
  • Successful expedition of Muhammad bin Qasim in 712 CE, including the battles of Debal, Nerun, Sehwan, and Raor.
  • Socio-economic, administrative, and cultural impact of the Arab conquest of Sindh, including the imposition of Jizya and translation of Indian texts.

2. Historical Background & Primary Sources

  • • Chachnama: The primary and most authentic Persian source of the Arab invasion, translated from an earlier Arabic manuscript by Ali bin Hamid Kufi during the reign of Nasiruddin Qubacha.
  • • Kitab Futuh al-Buldan: Authored by the 9th-century Arab historian Al-Baladhuri, providing critical contemporary accounts of early Islamic conquests including Sindh.
  • • Tuhfat-ul-Kiram: A later historical chronicle providing supplementary details regarding the administrative divisions and geographical layout of medieval Sindh.
  • • Geopolitical Condition: Sindh was ruled by the Brahmin dynasty founded by Chach (father of King Dahir), characterized by a feudal socio-political structure and alienation of the Buddhist population.

3. Chronology & Military Campaigns

  • • Early Expeditions (711 CE): Initial naval and land raids directed by Caliph Al-Walid through Ubaidullah and Budail met with failure, resulting in the death of the commanders.
  • • Muhammad bin Qasim's Command: Appointed by Al-Hajjaj bin Yusuf (Governor of Iraq), Qasim advanced with a well-equipped army of 12,000 cavalry and siege engines (Manjaniks) in 712 CE.
  • • Fall of Debal (712 CE): Qasim captured the port city of Debal, where the giant temple flag was brought down and the first instance of Jizya in India was imposed on non-Muslim subjects.
  • • Battle of Raor (712 CE): Decisive engagement fought on the banks of the Indus where King Dahir was slain, followed by the capture of Brahmanabad and the capital Al-Madinah (Alor).

4. Administration, Policy & Religious Impact

  • • Brahmanabad Settlement: Muhammad bin Qasim issued the historic 'Brahmanabad Settlement' granting freedom of worship, protection of temples, and administrative retention of local Brahmins (Ranas and Thakurs).
  • • Imposition of Jizya: Non-Muslims (Zimmis) were categorized into three slabs based on income and were required to pay Jizya in exchange for military exemption and protection of life and property.
  • • Ahl-al-Kitab Status: Hindus and Buddhists were granted the status of 'People of the Book' (Zimmis) by Caliph Al-Hajjaj, extending customary Islamic tolerance to Indian polytheists.
  • • Administrative Division: Sindh was divided into various Iqtas or districts placed under Arab military commanders (Amirs), while revenue collection was left largely in the hands of native officials.

5. Cultural Exchange & Historiographical Significance

  • • Transmission of Science & Mathematics: Arab scholars translated foundational Indian treatises on astronomy (Brahma-sphuta-siddhanta) and medicine (Charaka and Sushruta Samhitas) into Arabic.
  • • Introduction of Numerals: The Arab world learned the decimal system and zero from India, subsequently transmitting it to Europe as 'Arabic numerals' (referred to by Arabs as Hindasa).
  • • Limited Territorial Expansion: The Arab invasion remained confined largely to Sindh and Southern Multan, failing to penetrate deeper into the Indo-Gangetic plains due to the strong resistance by Gurjara-Pratiharas.
  • • Stanley Lane-Poole's Verdict: Famously characterized the Arab conquest of Sindh as 'an episode in the history of India' that left little permanent political footprint on the Indian mainland.

6. High-Yield Key Facts & Exam Memory Points

  • Key Fact: Year of Muhammad bin Qasim's invasion of Sindh: 712 CE.
  • Key Fact: Ruling dynasty of Sindh at the time of invasion: Brahmin Dynasty (King Dahir).
  • Key Fact: Primary contemporary Persian source for the invasion: Chachnama.
  • Key Fact: Caliph during the successful invasion: Al-Walid I, and the Governor of Iraq who orchestrated it: Al-Hajjaj bin Yusuf.
  • Key Fact: First place in India where Jizya was levied: Debal.
  • Key Fact: Indian astronomical text translated into Arabic during this era: Brahma-sphuta-siddhanta (as Zij al-Sindhind).

7. Common Exam Pitfalls & Confusions to Avoid

  • Trap: Assuming Muhammad bin Qasim established permanent Muslim rule across Northern India. Fact: His conquests were strictly restricted to Sindh and parts of Multan; the greater consolidation of Islamic rule occurred centuries later under Mahmud of Ghazni and Muhammad Ghori.
  • Trap: Believing Hindus were forced to convert en masse. Fact: Qasim recognized Hindus and Buddhists as Zimmis under the Brahmanabad Settlement, allowing freedom of worship in lieu of Jizya.
  • Trap: Confusing Chachnama author. Fact: The original work was in Arabic by an unknown author, but the surviving Persian version was translated by Ali bin Hamid Kufi.
