Political and Religious Landscape of the Sixth Century BCE

Jain Religion

Appears in: UP PGT History Tracker → UP TGT Social Science Tracker →

Topic overview

  • Jainism is one of the ancient Śramaṇa traditions that emerged in the Gangetic plains during the socio-religious turmoil of the sixth century BCE.
  • The religion emphasizes extreme asceticism, non-violence (Ahiṃsā), and self-purification as the primary means to attain liberation from the cycle of rebirth.
  • Jain tradition traces its lineage through a succession of twenty-four Tīrthaṅkaras, with Rishabhanatha as the first and Mahavira as the twenty-fourth.
  • Epigraphical and archaeological evidence, such as the Hathigumpha inscription of Kharavela and early Mathura sculptures, corroborate the antiquity and spread of the Jaina faith.
  • The movement gained substantial patronage from merchant communities (Vaṇiks) and monarchical states like Magadha, Avanti, and Videha.
Concept 1

Origins and Evolution of Tīrthaṅkaras

Jainism traces its doctrinal roots through twenty-four liberated teachers known as Tīrthaṅkaras.

Details

  • The term Tīrthaṅkara signifies a maker of a ford or crossing across the stream of endless births and deaths (Saṃsāra).
  • Rigvedic references mention Ṛṣabha and Arishtanemi, indicating the hoary antiquity of early Jaina thought.
  • Pārśvanātha, the twenty-third Tīrthaṅkara, historically preceded Mahavira and laid down the fourfold restraint (Cuyāma).
  • Vardhamāna Mahavira, the twenty-fourth Tīrthaṅkara, reorganized the ascetic order and added absolute celibacy (Brahmacarya) to the earlier four vows.
  • Jaina historiography categorizes Tīrthaṅkaras into distinct mythical and historical eras, cementing their cosmological framework.

Remember

  • First Tīrthaṅkara: Ṛṣabhanātha or Ādinātha.
  • Twenty-third Tīrthaṅkara: Pārśvanātha, associated with Varanasi and the snake-hood iconography.
  • Twenty-fourth Tīrthaṅkara: Vardhamāna Mahavira, associated with Kundagrama and Pavapuri.
  • The four vows of Parsva: Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, and Aparigraha.
Examples & sources
  • The association of Ṛṣabhanātha with Mount Ashtapada or Kailasa in Jaina mythology.
  • The historical historicity of Pārśvanātha supported by canonical accounts of his followers called Nirgranthas.
Compare
  • Unlike Buddhism which rejects eternal souls and focuses on the Middle Path, Jainism posits living souls (Jīva) and advocates rigorous asceticism.
  • Unlike the Vedic tradition that validated ritual sacrifice, Jaina Tīrthaṅkaras categorically denounced animal slaughter in yajñas.
Common confusions
  • Mahavira is often erroneously believed to be the founder of Jainism rather than its last reformer.
  • Jainism is frequently misconstrued as a breakaway sect of Buddhism due to their contemporary emergence.
Exam points
  • Match the Tīrthaṅkaras with their respective cognizances or symbols as frequently asked in UGC NET.
  • Distinguish between the four vows of Pārśvanātha and the five vows of Mahavira.
  • Identify the historical dates and locations associated with Mahavira's birth and Nirvana.
Concept 2

Life and Teachings of Vardhamāna Mahavira

Vardhamāna Mahavira systematized Jaina philosophy and expanded its monastic order across northern India.

Details

  • Mahavira was born at Kundagrama near Vaishali to King Siddhartha of the Jnatrika clan and Queen Trishala.
  • At the age of thirty, after the death of his parents, he renounced worldly life and undertook rigorous asceticism for twelve and a half years.
  • He attained Kevala Jñāna (supreme omniscience) on the banks of the Rujupalika river near the village of Jrimbhikagrama under a Sal tree.
  • He spent the rest of his life preaching his doctrines in various Mahajanapadas, organizing followers into monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen.
  • He attained Mahaparinirvana or Moksha at Pavapuri at the age of seventy-two around 468 BCE, according to traditional chronology.

Remember

  • Cln: Jnatrika Kshatriya clan.
  • Primary title: Mahavira (Great Hero), Jina (Conqueror), and Nigantha Nataputta in Buddhist texts.
  • First disciple: Indrabhuti Gautama.
  • Core preaching medium: Ardhamagadhi Prakrit dialect to reach common masses.
Examples & sources
  • Mahavira's severe penances recorded in the Acaraṅga Sūtra.
  • His prolonged spiritual journey alongside Gosala Mankhaliputta before their doctrinal split.
Compare
  • Mahavira preached without utilizing Sanskrit, whereas orthodox Vedic teachers strictly guarded Sanskrit liturgical texts.
  • Unlike the Buddha who avoided metaphysical extremes through the Middle Path, Mahavira embraced rigorous physical mortification.
Common confusions
  • Students often confuse Mahavira's place of death with that of Gautama Buddha (Kushinagar).
  • It is a misconception that Mahavira prohibited all forms of labor; householders were permitted non-agricultural livelihoods that minimized harm.
Exam points
  • Identify the parental lineage and clan of Vardhamāna Mahavira for UP PCS and UP PGT exams.
  • Remember the names of key ganadharas (chief disciples) like Indrabhuti Gautama.
  • Note the river and tree associated with his attainment of Kevala Jñāna.
Concept 3

Core Doctrines and Epistemology of Jainism

Jain philosophy rests upon a dualistic metaphysics, relativistic epistemology, and stringent ethical codes.

Details

  • The universe is uncreated, eternal, and governed by universal laws, divided fundamentally into Jīva (animate conscious soul) and Ajīva (inanimate matter).
  • Karma is conceived as microscopic physical particles that infiltrate and bind the soul to the material world based on moral actions.
  • Samyak Darshana (right faith), Samyak Jnana (right knowledge), and Samyak Charitra (right conduct) constitute the Triratna (three jewels) for liberation.
  • Syadvada (doctrine of conditional assertion) and Anekantavada (doctrine of manifold aspects) form the bedrock of Jaina epistemology.
  • Ahimsa (non-injury) is absolute, extending not only to humans and animals but also to microscopic organisms in earth, water, fire, and air.

Remember

  • Ajiva categories: Pudgala (matter), Dharma (medium of motion), Adharma (medium of rest), Akasha (space), and Kala (time).
  • Asrava: Influx of karmic particles into the soul.
  • Bandha: Bondage of the soul caused by karma.
  • Nirjara: Shedding of accumulated karma through penance.
  • Moksha/Mukti: Complete liberation of the soul residing at the apex of the universe (Siddhiloka).
Examples & sources
  • The parable of the blind men and the elephant used to explain Anekantavada.
  • The strict filtering of drinking water and wearing of cloth masks by ascetics to practice Mahavrata (great vows).
Compare
  • Unlike Advaita Vedanta which posits a singular universal consciousness (Brahman), Jainism asserts an infinite multiplicity of individual souls.
  • Unlike Charvaka materialism that rejects karma and soul, Jainism quantifies karma as material particles adhering to the soul.
Common confusions
  • Anekantavada is often misconstrued as philosophical skepticism or relativism without objective truth.
  • Jainism is sometimes mistakenly viewed as passive fatalism, whereas it emphasizes intense personal free will and moral effort.
Exam points
  • Memorize the precise definitions of Triratna, Anekantavada, and Syadvada for UGC NET matching questions.
  • Understand the distinction between Asrava, Bandha, Samvara, and Nirjara in karmic mechanics.
  • Identify the five major vows (Mahavratas) for ascetics and minor vows (Anuvratas) for laypersons.
Concept 4

Jaina Councils, Schisms, and Canonical Literature

Historical schisms and council codifications shaped the transmission of Jaina scriptures and monastic traditions.

Details

  • A severe twelve-year famine in Magadha during the fourth century BCE led ascetic leader Bhadrabahu to migrate south to Karnataka with his followers.
  • Upon their return, doctrinal differences emerged between the southern group and those who stayed behind under Sthulabhadra.
  • This led to the permanent division of Jainism into two primary sects: Digambara (sky-clad) and Svetambara (white-robed).
  • The First Jaina Council was convened at Pataliputra under the chairmanship of Sthulabhadra to compile the early Angas.
  • The Second Jaina Council was held at Vallabhi in Gujarat under Devardhi Kshamasramana to systematically record the canon in writing.

Remember

  • Digambara tenets: Monks renounce all clothing, women cannot attain direct liberation, and Mahavira remained unmarried.
  • Svetambara tenets: Monks wear white garments, women can attain liberation, and Mallinath is considered female by them.
  • Canonical texts: 12 Angas, 12 Upangas, 10 Painnas, 6 Chedasutras, 4 Mulasutras.
  • Language of texts: Originally compiled in Purvas (lost) and later preserved in Ardhamagadhi and Sauraseni Prakrit.
Examples & sources
  • The compilation of the Siddhanta or Agama literature at the Vallabhi Council in 512 CE.
  • Hemachandra's influential Sanskrit and Prakrit literary contributions in the Solanki royal court of Gujarat.
Compare
  • Unlike Buddhism which held four major councils recognized across traditions, Jainism officially convened two principal councils.
  • Unlike Buddhist canons preserved in Pali, early Jaina scriptures were recorded in Prakrit variants like Ardhamagadhi.
Common confusions
  • It is incorrect to assume that Svetambaras and Digambaras disagreed on the core philosophical teachings of Mahavira; their disputes primarily concern monastic discipline and ascetic practices.
  • The Digambara sect does not recognize the canonical Angas compiled by the Svetambaras as authentic ancient scriptures.
Exam points
  • Memorize the details of the Pataliputra and Vallabhi Councils (chairpersons, locations, outcomes).
  • Distinguish clearly between the doctrinal differences of Svetambara and Digambara sects for UPSC and State PCS exams.
  • Identify major canonical texts like the Acaraṅga Sūtra and Kalpasutra by Bhadrabahu.
Concept 5

Royal Patronage and Epigraphical Evidence

Jainism spread rapidly across ancient India due to active support from monarchs, merchants, and regional rulers.

Details

  • King Bimbisara and Ajatashatru of Magadha maintained cordial diplomatic and personal relations with contemporary Śramaṇa leaders including Mahavira.
  • King Udayin of the Haryanka dynasty shifted his capital to Pataliputra, which subsequently became a major stronghold of Jaina culture.
  • Emperor Chandragupta Maurya reportedly abdicated his throne, embraced Jainism under Bhadrabahu, and spent his final days in fasting at Shravanabelagola.
  • King Kharavela of Kalinga patronized Jainism extensively, as recorded in his famous Hathigumpha inscription.
  • The Rashtrakutas, Gangas, and Chalukyas of Karnataka patronized Digambara Jainism, resulting in monumental temple architecture.

Remember

  • Key epigraph: Hathigumpha Inscription of Kharavela detailing repairs to Jaina shrines and honoring ascetic monks.
  • Key monument: Gommateshwara (Bahubali) monolithic statue at Shravanabelagola, erected by Chavundaraya.
  • Geographical spread: Mathura, Udayagiri-Khandagiri caves in Odisha, and Ellora caves in Maharashtra.
  • Merchant support: Wealthy mercantile guilds (Śreṇis) financed monastic cave dwellings and manuscript copying.
Examples & sources
  • The rock-cut Jaina caves at Udayagiri and Khandagiri commissioned by Kharavela.
  • The Dilwara Temples at Mount Abu built by ministers Vastupala and Tejapala during the Solanki rule.
Compare
  • Unlike Buddhism which received monumental imperial patronage under Ashoka and Kanishka across international trade routes, Jainism relied more heavily on indigenous merchant guilds and regional dynasties.
  • Jaina architectural sites frequently feature colossal freestanding statues of Tīrthaṅkaras, unlike early Buddhist stupa architecture.
Common confusions
  • Chandragupta Maurya's conversion to Jainism is often overlooked by students who exclusively associate Mauryan patronage with Buddhist expansion under Ashoka.
  • Jainism is incorrectly viewed as a religion restricted to Gujarat today; it held dominant political and cultural sway in South India for centuries.
Exam points
  • Memorize the contents and historical significance of the Hathigumpha inscription for UP PGT History.
  • Link southern dynasties (Gangas, Rashtrakutas) with major Jaina architectural sites like Shravanabelagola.
  • Note the role of merchant communities in the survival of Jainism through periods of political adversity.
Concept 6

Socio-Economic Impact and Historiographical Significance

Jainism profoundly influenced Indian social structures, commercial ethics, and literary development.

Details

  • By condemning animal sacrifice in Vedic rituals, Jainism indirectly protected cattle wealth, facilitating the agrarian expansion of the Iron Age.
  • The rigorous code of non-violence discouraged involvement in agriculture (due to accidental harm to soil organisms) and warfare, pushing followers toward trade and commerce.
  • Jain merchants established vast commercial networks across the subcontinent, utilizing strict ethical vows to build high mutual trust in business.
  • Jaina scholars made pioneering contributions to grammar, lexicography, and mathematics, preserving indigenous knowledge systems.
  • Modern historiography highlights how Jainism provided a counter-cultural voice against Brahmanical caste rigidities while maintaining conservative social hierarchies within its lay community.

Remember

  • Socio-economic outcome: Dominance of Jaina communities in banking, trade, and urban commerce.
  • Literary contributions: Development of Apabhramsha and regional languages through storytelling forms like Charitrakavyas.
  • Ethical impact: Institutionalization of vegetarianism across wide sections of Indian society.
  • Caste stance: While rejecting birth-based supremacy in spiritual liberation, Jaina texts maintained social stratification for laity.
Examples & sources
  • The compilation of mathematical treatises like Mahaviracharya's Ganitasarasangraha.
  • The widespread adoption of strict dietary vegetarianism among Gujarati and Marwari merchant classes.
Compare
  • Unlike Buddhism which eventually faded from mainland India due to assimilation and foreign invasions, Jainism survived continuously on Indian soil through strict adherence by its lay community.
  • Unlike the Buddhist monastic order that depended entirely on royal and public alms, Jaina lay patrons established enduring institutional endowments.
Common confusions
  • It is a myth that Jainism promoted economic stagnation; rather, its emphasis on honesty and network trust fueled dynamic merchant capitalism.
  • Jainism is often mistakenly thought to have abolished the caste system entirely; internal caste distinctions persisted among lay followers.
Exam points
  • Analyze the connection between the Jaina vow of Ahimsa and the historical rise of urban trade guilds for UPSC mains and UGC NET.
  • Evaluate Jaina contributions to regional languages and mathematics.
  • Understand the historiographical debate surrounding the survival mechanism of Jainism in medieval India.

Concept 1

Origins and Evolution of Tīrthaṅkaras

Quick recall

  • Jainism traces its doctrinal lineage through twenty-four liberated spiritual teachers known as Tīrthaṅkaras.
  • The first Tīrthaṅkara was Ṛṣabhanātha (or Ādinātha), whose historicity is embedded in ancient Vedic and puranic traditions.
  • Pārśvanātha was the twenty-third Tīrthaṅkara, historically recognized and associated with the fourfold restraint (chāuyāma-dharma).
  • Vardhamāna Mahavira was the twenty-fourth and final Tīrthaṅkara of the current cosmic age (avasarpiṇī).

Memory aid

  • RAV: Rishabha (1st), Arishtanemi (22nd), Parshva (23rd), Vardhamana (24th).

Review tips

  • Remember that Tīrthaṅkaras are ford-builders who help souls cross the ocean of samsara.

Quick checks

  • Questions often confuse Pārśvanātha's four vows with Mahavira's five vows (Mahavira added brahmacharya).

Watch for

  • Assuming Mahavira founded Jainism rather than systematizing and reforming an existing tradition.
Concept 2

Life and Teachings of Vardhamāna Mahavira

Quick recall

  • Vardhamāna Mahavira was born at Kundagrama near Vaishali to parents Siddhartha (Ikshvaku clan) and Trishala.
  • He attained Kevala Jnana (supreme omniscience) under a sal tree on the banks of the Rujupalika river after 12 years of severe asceticism.
  • He added celibacy (brahmacharya) to the earlier four vows of Pārśvanātha to form the five great vows (Mahavratas).
  • He expanded the monastic order (sangha) comprising monks, nuns, śrāvakas (laymen), and śrāvikās (laywomen).

Memory aid

  • KSKT: Kundagrama (birth), Siddhartha (father), Kundalapura (alternative), Trishala (mother).

Review tips

  • Note the river Rujupalika and tree type (Sal) for Mahavira, contrast with Buddha's Niranjana river and Peepal tree.

Quick checks

  • When examining chronological details, place Mahavira as an older contemporary or slightly later than Gautama Buddha.

Watch for

  • Confusing the birth place of Mahavira (Kundagrama) with his death place (Pava).
Concept 3

Core Doctrines and Epistemology of Jainism

Quick recall

  • Jainism rests upon a dualistic metaphysics dividing existence into Jiva (living soul) and Ajiva (non-living matter).
  • Epistemology features Syadvada (theory of conditioned predication) and Anekantavada (doctrine of manifold aspects).
  • Karma is conceptualized as microscopic physical particles that bind to the soul, necessitating rigorous ascetic purification.
  • Liberation (Moksha) is achieved via Triratna (Three Jewels): Right Faith (Samyak Darshana), Right Knowledge (Samyak Jnana), and Right Conduct (Samyak Charitra).

Memory aid

  • DKT: Darshana, Jnana, Charitra make up the Triratna.

Review tips

  • Anekantavada is the ontological reality; Syadvada is its linguistic expression.

Quick checks

  • Match Anekantavada with pluralism and Syadvada with relativity of truth in matching questions.

Watch for

  • Mistaking Jain karma as an abstract divine law instead of physical substance particles.
Concept 4

Jaina Councils, Schisms, and Canonical Literature

Quick recall

  • The First Jaina Council was held at Pataliputra under Sthulabahu, compiling the 12 Angas, though the 12th (Drrishtivada) was lost.
  • The Second Jaina Council was held at Vallabhi under Devardhi Kshamasramana to write down the canon in Ardhamagadhi Prakrit.
  • The great schism resulted in two major sects: Shwetambara (white-clad, led by Sthulabahu) and Digambara (sky-clad, led by Bhadrabahu).
  • Jaina literature includes Angas, Upangas, Purvas, and commentary texts written in Prakrit, Apabhramsha, and later Sanskrit.

Memory aid

  • PA-VA: Pataliputra (1st Council), Vallabhi (2nd Council).

Review tips

  • Remember that Digambaras hold that absolute nudity is required for liberation and women cannot attain direct liberation.

Quick checks

  • Sthulabahu = Shwetambara (both start with St/Sh), Bhadrabahu = Digambara.

Watch for

  • Assuming both sects agreed on the completeness of the ancient canon; Digambaras reject the extant Shwetambara Agamas.
Concept 5

Royal Patronage and Epigraphical Evidence

Quick recall

  • Jainism spread across western and southern India through active patronage from monarchs, merchants, and regional rulers.
  • Maurya Emperor Chandragupta Maurya abdicated his throne, became a Jain monk under Bhadrabahu, and migrated to Shravanabelagola.
  • Kharavela of the Chedi dynasty (Hathigumpha inscription) was a devout patron of Jain ascetics.
  • Epigraphical records like the Hathigumpha and Mathura inscriptions provide crucial archaeological proof of ancient Jaina prevalence.

Memory aid

  • CM-HK: Chandragupta Maurya, Kharavela.

Review tips

  • Link Shravanabelagola directly with Chandragupta Maurya and the Digambara migration south.

Quick checks

  • Questions love testing the location of Hathigumpha inscription (Udayagiri-Khandagiri hills, Odisha).

Watch for

  • Confusing Ashoka's patronage (predominantly Buddhist) with his grandfather Chandragupta's Jain leanings.
Concept 6

Socio-Economic Impact and Historiographical Significance

Quick recall

  • Jainism rejected the caste system based on birth, attracting lower varnas and mercantile communities seeking social mobility.
  • Strict adherence to ahimsa (non-injury) restricted Jains from agriculture, steering them toward trade, commerce, and banking.
  • Jain monks significantly enriched regional languages like Sauraseni, Ardhamagadhi, Kannada, and Apabhramsha through extensive literary output.
  • Historiographically, Jain texts provide alternate chronologies and crucial data on ancient Indian trade routes and political histories.

Memory aid

  • TAM: Trade, Ahimsa, Mercantile networks.

Review tips

  • Connect strict non-violence in Jainism directly to the historical dominance of Jains in banking and commerce.

Quick checks

  • Exams often ask why Jains dominated trade: answer is absolute adherence to ahimsa ruling out tilling the soil.

Watch for

  • Believing Jainism entirely eradicated the varna system in society; it only criticized caste discrimination theoretically.

Quick facts

  • There are 24 Tīrthaṅkaras in Jainism, beginning with Rishabhanatha and ending with Mahavira.
  • Mahavira attained Kevala Jnana on the banks of the Rujupalika river.
  • The four vows of Parshvanatha were augmented by Mahavira with Brahmacharya.
  • The First Jaina Council was convened at Pataliputra under the leadership of Sthulabahu.
  • The Second Jaina Council at Vallabhi codified the canonical texts into Ardhamagadhi Prakrit.
  • Chandragupta Maurya spent his final days practicing Jain asceticism at Shravanabelagola.
  • The Hathigumpha inscription of King Kharavela is a primary epigraphical source for Jain history.
  • Jain metaphysics divides the universe fundamentally into Jiva and Ajiva.

Memory aids

  • TRIRATNA: Right Faith, Right Knowledge, Right Conduct form the core path to liberation.
  • SECTS: Sthulabahu = Shwetambara, Bhadrabahu = Digambara.
  • VOWS: Non-violence, Truth, Non-stealing, Non-possession, Celibacy.

Review tips

  • Always differentiate between Buddhist Middle Path and Jain extreme asceticism.
  • Remember that Jainism believes even inanimate objects possess minute souls (Jiva/Hylozoism).
  • Pay close attention to council chairpersons and locations during revision.
  • Keep track of epigraphical evidence like Hathigumpha for matching-type questions.

Question cues

  • Eliminate options that claim Jainism rejected the existence of God entirely; Jainism denies a creator God, but acknowledges liberated souls.
  • Check whether a question refers to the 23rd Tirthankara (Parshva) or 24th (Mahavira) before answering.
  • Remember that Prakrit was used for early Jaina canonical literature, while Sanskrit came later.
  • Look for commercial and mercantile keywords when evaluating Jainism's socio-economic footprint.

  • Monks wear white garments versus monks practice absolute nudity.
  • Led traditionally by Sthulabahu versus led by Bhadrabahu.
  • Accepts the possibility of women attaining liberation versus denies women can attain direct liberation without rebirth as a male.

Watch for

  • Confusing the 12th Anga (Drrishtivada) being lost at the Pataliputra council with the total loss of all Jaina literature.
  • Assuming Anekantavada and Syadvada are completely separate philosophical systems rather than complementary aspects.
  • Attributing the introduction of all five vows to Parshvanatha instead of recognizing Mahavira added the fifth vow.
  • Failing to recognize that Jainism did not completely discard the caste system for lay followers, despite opposing birth-based hierarchy.
  • Confusing the locations of the two Jaina councils (Pataliputra and Vallabhi) with Buddhist councils.
