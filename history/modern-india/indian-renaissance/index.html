Modern India (1858 to 1958 AD.)

Indian Renaissance

Appears in: UP TGT Social Science Tracker →

Topic overview

  • The Indian Renaissance refers to the socio-religious and cultural awakening in 19th-century India.
  • It aimed to reform traditional Indian society by eradicating superstitious beliefs, rigid caste hierarchies, and regressive social customs.
  • The movement drew inspiration from rationalism, humanism, modern Western scientific thought, and rediscovery of ancient Indian heritage.
  • Key epicentres of this movement were Bengal, Maharashtra, and Madras, led by visionary intellectuals and social reformers.
Concept 1

Brahmo Samaj and Raja Ram Mohan Roy

Raja Ram Mohan Roy founded the Brahmo Samaj in 1828 as a monotheistic and reformist movement against idol worship and ritualism.

Details

  • Raja Ram Mohan Roy is widely hailed as the Father of Modern India and the pioneer of the Indian Renaissance.
  • He established the Atmiya Sabha in 1815, which later evolved into the Brahmo Sabha in 1828 and subsequently the Brahmo Samaj.
  • The movement vehemently opposed idolatry, polytheism, ritualistic sacrifices, and the hereditary caste system.
  • Roy launched an aggressive campaign against Sati, leading to its formal abolition by Lord William Bentinck through Regulation XVII in 1829.
  • He advocated strongly for women's rights, including female education, widow remarriage, and property rights for women.

Remember

  • Founded: August 20, 1828 in Calcutta.
  • Key Personality: Raja Ram Mohan Roy.
  • Anti-Sati Law: Regulation XVII of 1829.
  • Publications: Sambad Kaumudi (Bengali) and Mirat-ul-Akbar (Persian).
Examples & sources
  • Establishment of the Hindu College in Calcutta in 1817 with Roy's active support.
  • Publication of Tuhfat-ul-Muwahhidin (A Gift to Monotheists) in 1803.
Compare
  • Brahmo Samaj relied heavily on Western rationalism and Upanishadic monotheism compared to orthodox revivalist movements.
  • Unlike the later Arya Samaj, Brahmo Samaj was more eclectic and open to assimilation of Christian ethical principles.
Common confusions
  • Misconception: Brahmo Samaj was a political party fighting British rule directly. Fact: It was strictly a socio-religious reform movement.
  • Misconception: Raja Ram Mohan Roy supported traditional Hindu customs entirely. Fact: He selectively critiqued Puranic Hinduism using scriptural authority.
Exam points
  • Exact year of Brahmo Samaj foundation and Regulation XVII of 1829.
  • Names of journals edited by Raja Ram Mohan Roy.
  • Role of Debendranath Tagore and Keshab Chandra Sen in later schisms.
Concept 2

Young Bengal Movement and Henry Louis Vivian Derozio

Henry Louis Vivian Derozio inspired radical free-thinking and intellectual rebellion among Hindu College students in Calcutta.

Details

  • Henry Louis Vivian Derozio taught at the Hindu College, Calcutta, from 1826 to 1831 and galvanized radical youth.
  • The Derozians, known as the Young Bengal group, advocated for radical intellectual freedom, liberty, and women's rights.
  • They questioned all traditional authorities, religious dogma, and social taboos openly and aggressively.
  • The movement lacked deep roots among the rural masses and remained largely confined to urban upper-caste Bengali intellectuals.
  • Derozio was forced to resign from Hindu College due to orthodox Hindu backlash against his unorthodox teachings.

Remember

  • Key Leader: Henry Louis Vivian Derozio (1809–1831).
  • Core Institution: Hindu College, Calcutta.
  • Main Ideology: Radical rationalism, French Revolution ideals, skepticism.
  • Associated Organizations: Academic Association, Society for the Acquisition of General Knowledge.
Examples & sources
  • Debates held at the Academic Association questioning the existence of God and traditional social norms.
  • Publication of journals like Parthenon and East Indian by Derozio's students.
Compare
  • Unlike the moderate reformism of Brahmo Samaj, Young Bengal advocated uncompromising and radical rejection of all indigenous traditions.
  • Young Bengal failed to build a mass base unlike the Prarthana Samaj or Arya Samaj later.
Common confusions
  • Misconception: Young Bengal successfully transformed rural Bengal society. Fact: Their impact was limited to urban Calcutta intelligentsia.
  • Misconception: Derozio was an Indian national. Fact: He was of Anglo-Indian descent.
Exam points
  • Tenure of Derozio at Hindu College.
  • Names of student societies founded by Derozians.
  • Reasons for the decline and isolation of the Young Bengal movement.
Concept 3

Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar and Social Reform

Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar championed female education and successfully lobbied for the legalisation of widow remarriage in India.

Details

  • Vidyasagar served as the Principal of Sanskrit College, Calcutta, and modernised its curriculum by introducing Western thought.
  • He proved through ancient shastras that Hindu scriptures did not forbid widow remarriage.
  • His relentless petitioning resulted in Lord Dalhousie passing the Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act of 1856 (Act XV).
  • He played a monumental role in promoting female education by setting up numerous girls' schools in Bengal.
  • He also introduced institutional reforms to break Brahminical monopolies on Sanskrit education by opening it to lower castes.

Remember

  • Key Legislation: Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856 (Act XV).
  • Title: Vidyasagar (Ocean of Knowledge) conferred by Sanskrit College.
  • Key Contribution: Establishment of Metropolitan Institution (now Vidyasagar College).
  • Literary Work: Barnaparichay (Bengali primer).
Examples & sources
  • Personally sponsoring and facilitating the first legal Hindu widow marriage in 1856.
  • Establishing and managing nearly 35 girls' schools across Bengal during his tenure as Inspector of Schools.
Compare
  • Unlike Raja Ram Mohan Roy who relied on Brahmo monotheism, Vidyasagar used classical Sanskrit authority to justify social reform.
  • Vidyasagar focused intensely on practical institution-building and legislation rather than philosophical debates.
Common confusions
  • Misconception: Vidyasagar was a political nationalist leader. Fact: He was primarily an educationalist and social reformer.
  • Misconception: The 1856 Act was universally welcomed by orthodox Hindus. Fact: It faced severe societal resistance and death threats.
Exam points
  • Act number and year for Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act.
  • Vidyasagar's role as Inspector of Schools.
  • Books authored by Vidyasagar for primary education.
Concept 4

Prarthana Samaj and Western India Reform

Founded in Bombay in 1867, the Prarthana Samaj focused on religious and social reform without breaking away from Hindu society.

Details

  • The Prarthana Samaj was established in Bombay in 1867 with the guidance of Keshab Chandra Sen.
  • Key leaders included Mahadev Govind Ranade, R.G. Bhandarkar, and N.G. Chandavarkar.
  • The Samaj focused on monotheistic worship, social upliftment, inter-caste dining, and removal of caste barriers.
  • It established institutions like the Widow Marriage Association and the Deccan Education Society.
  • Unlike Bengal reformers, Maharashtra reformers worked cautiously within the fold of existing traditions.

Remember

  • Founded: 1867 in Bombay.
  • Key Founders: Atmaram Pandurang, M.G. Ranade, R.G. Bhandarkar.
  • Core Organ: Subodha Patrika (journal).
  • Associated Institution: Widow Marriage Association (1861).
Examples & sources
  • Establishment of the Poona Sarvajanik Sabha in 1870 by M.G. Ranade.
  • Founding of Fergusson College in Pune under Deccan Education Society.
Compare
  • Prarthana Samaj in Bombay was more moderate and less iconoclastic than the Brahmo Samaj in Bengal.
  • Maharashtra reform relied on the Bhakti tradition saints like Tukaram and Dnyaneshwar rather than direct scriptural confrontation.
Common confusions
  • Misconception: Prarthana Samaj was a breakaway sect from Hinduism. Fact: It functioned as a reformist prayer society within Hindu society.
  • Misconception: M.G. Ranade founded the Samaj alone. Fact: Dr. Atmaram Pandurang was its primary founder.
Exam points
  • Founding year and key personalities of Prarthana Samaj.
  • Role of M.G. Ranade in social reform institutions.
  • Difference in approach between Brahmo Samaj and Prarthana Samaj.
Concept 5

Arya Samaj and Swami Dayananda Saraswati

Swami Dayananda Saraswati founded the Arya Samaj in 1875, advocating 'Back to the Vedas' and aggressive socio-religious purification.

Details

  • Swami Dayananda Saraswati founded the Arya Samaj in Bombay in 1875, later shifting its headquarters to Lahore.
  • His slogan 'Back to the Vedas' asserted the infallibility of Vedic literature and rejected Puranic Hinduism, idol worship, and caste by birth.
  • The Samaj initiated the Shuddhi movement to reconvert individuals who had converted to Islam or Christianity.
  • It made immense contributions to education through the establishment of Dayananda Anglo-Vedic (DAV) schools and colleges.
  • A later schism led to the formation of Gurukul Kangri, emphasizing traditional Vedic education alongside modern sciences.

Remember

  • Founded: 1875 in Bombay, headquarters moved to Lahore in 1877.
  • Key Personality: Swami Dayananda Saraswati (original name: Mul Shankar).
  • Major Text: Satyarth Prakash (The True Exposition).
  • Core Tenet: Vedas contain all knowledge and are infallible.
Examples & sources
  • Establishment of the first DAV College in Lahore in 1886.
  • Organization of Shuddhi campaigns in Punjab and Western United Provinces.
Compare
  • Arya Samaj was explicitly revivalist and militant in its defense of Hinduism compared to the universalist syncretism of Brahmo Samaj.
  • Unlike Brahmo Samaj which welcomed Western ideas, Arya Samaj claimed ancient Indian science already encompassed modern knowledge.
Common confusions
  • Misconception: Arya Samaj accepted the hierarchical caste system based on birth. Fact: Dayananda strongly advocated caturvarnya based on merit and occupation.
  • Misconception: Arya Samaj was initially founded in Delhi. Fact: It was founded in Bombay.
Exam points
  • Authorship and significance of Satyarth Prakash.
  • Objective and methodology of the Shuddhi movement.
  • Split between DAV college faction and Gurukul faction.
Concept 6

Ramakrishna Mission and Swami Vivekananda

Swami Vivekananda founded the Ramakrishna Mission in 1897 to propagate practical Vedanta and social service inspired by Ramakrishna Paramahamsa.

Details

  • Gadadhar Chattopadhyay, later known as Ramakrishna Paramahamsa, emphasized the essential unity of all religions through direct mystical experience.
  • His chief disciple, Narendranath Datta (Swami Vivekananda), popularized his teachings globally after the 1893 Chicago Parliament of Religions.
  • Swami Vivekananda founded the Ramakrishna Mission in 1897 to carry out humanitarian relief, educational work, and propagation of Practical Vedanta.
  • The Mission combined spiritual salvation with relentless social service, viewing service to man as service to God (Shiv Jnane Jiwa Seva).
  • Vivekananda critiqued caste tyranny, poverty, and religious dogmatism while instilling national pride and self-confidence among Indians.

Remember

  • Mission Founded: 1897 by Swami Vivekananda.
  • Core Philosophy: Advaita Vedanta and Practical Vedanta.
  • Famous Speech: Parliament of Religions, Chicago, September 11, 1893.
  • Headquarters: Belur Math, Howrah, West Bengal.
Examples & sources
  • Famine relief work and medical camps organized during plague outbreaks in Calcutta by the Mission.
  • Establishment of Advaita Ashrama at Mayawati in the Himalayas.
Compare
  • Unlike Arya Samaj which rejected other religions, Ramakrishna Mission preached the ultimate harmony of all religions.
  • Unlike Western rationalist reformers, the Mission grounded modern social reform deeply in mystical spiritual realization.
Common confusions
  • Misconception: Ramakrishna Mission was a political nationalist organization. Fact: It was strictly a spiritual and philanthropic non-political body.
  • Misconception: Ramakrishna Paramahamsa founded the Mission. Fact: He was the spiritual mentor; the Mission was founded by Vivekananda.
Exam points
  • Year of foundation of Ramakrishna Mission and Belur Math.
  • Significance of Vivekananda's Chicago address.
  • Core concept of Practical Vedanta.

Compare

Brahmo Samaj (1828)vsArya Samaj (1875)
  • Brahmo Samaj was founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy in Calcutta; Arya Samaj by Swami Dayananda Saraswati in 1875.
  • Brahmo Samaj incorporated universal monotheism often drawing from Western and diverse scriptures; Arya Samaj strictly advocated 'Back to Vedic authority'.
  • Arya Samaj engaged in active socio-religious purification and reconversion, whereas Brahmo Samaj focused on rationalist socio-religious reform and monotheistic prayer.
Prarthana Samaj (1867)vsYoung Bengal Movement
  • Prarthana Samaj was founded in Bombay focusing on moderate religious and social reform within Hindu society bounds.
  • Young Bengal was an intense, radical free-thinking student movement led by Derozio in Calcutta.
  • Prarthana Samaj survived as an institutional reform body, while Young Bengal was a short-lived intellectual rebellion.

Watch for

  • Do not confuse the founder of Prarthana Samaj with Raja Ram Mohan Roy.
  • Remember that Swami Vivekananda founded the Ramakrishna Mission in 1897, long after Ramakrishna Paramahamsa passed away.
  • Be careful not to attribute the 'Back to the Vedas' slogan to Brahmo Samaj or Ramakrishna Mission.
  • Verify whether a question asks for Sati abolition (Roy) or Widow Remarriage Act (Vidyasagar).
  • Do not assume all nineteenth-century reformers rejected Hinduism entirely; organizations like Prarthana Samaj worked within Hindu society.
