Mughal Dynasty and Contemporary Rulers

Life and Achievement of Humayun

Appears in: UP TGT Social Science Tracker →

Topic overview

  • Nasir-ud-Din Muhammad Humayun ruled the Mughal Empire from 1530 to 1540 and again from 1555 to 1556.
  • He was the eldest son and successor of Babur, inheriting an unstable and consolidated empire beset by internal dissent and formidable Afghan rivals.
  • His reign is uniquely characterized by the loss of the nascent Mughal Empire to Sher Shah Suri and its subsequent dramatic restoration.
  • Humayun's administrative, cultural, and architectural initiatives laid vital foundations for the mature Mughal state later consolidated by Akbar.
Concept 1

Accession and Division of the Empire

Humayun's succession in 1530 and the fatal execution of Babur's advice regarding territorial partition among his brothers.

Details

  • Babur died on December 26, 1530, at Agra, leaving behind an unsolidified empire bound together largely by his personal military charisma.
  • Humayun ascended the throne at the age of 23 on December 30, 1530, facing immediate opposition from nobles and rival claimants.
  • Complying with Babur's dying wishes, Humayun partitioned the empire among his brothers to prevent fratricidal conflict.
  • Kamran was given Kabul and Qandahar, Askari received Sambhal, and Hindal was assigned Alwar.
  • This territorial division fatally weakened Humayun by depriving him of the northern recruiting grounds and financial resources of Afghanistan.
  • His brothers, particularly Kamran, frequently pursued independent policies and withheld military assistance during critical campaigns against Sher Shah.

Remember

  • Babur died on December 26, 1530.
  • Humayun ascended the throne on December 30, 1530.
  • Kamran received Kabul and Qandahar.
  • Askari received Sambhal and Hindal received Alwar.
Examples & sources
  • Kamran's seizure of Lahore in 1536 effectively cut off Humayun's direct communications with his ancestral base in Central Asia.
  • Askari's lukewarm support during the Gujarat campaign hampered consolidation of western territories.
Compare
  • Unlike the centralized succession practices established by Akbar later, Humayun's early reign was hampered by Timurid traditions of joint sovereignty.
  • Babur's unified command structure was replaced by fractured administrative zones controlled by divergent royal princes.
Common confusions
  • It is a misconception that Humayun seized power through usurpation; he followed his father's explicit instructions on territorial division.
  • Many assume Kamran remained a loyal subordinate throughout the reign, whereas he frequently acted as an independent rival.
Exam points
  • Exact dates of Babur's death and Humayun's accession.
  • Specific territorial distribution among Humayun's three brothers.
  • Strategic consequences of dividing Kabul and Qandahar from Delhi-Agra.
Concept 2

Early Military Campaigns and Administrative Missteps

Humayun's initial military engagements against the Afghans of Bihar and Bahadur Shah of Gujarat.

Details

  • In 1531, Humayun launched his first major campaign against Mahmud Lodi and captured Kalinjar in Bundelkhand.
  • He besieged the fortress of Chunar held by Sher Khan Sur in 1532, but lifted the siege prematurely upon accepting nominal submission.
  • This premature settlement allowed Sher Khan crucial time to consolidate his military and economic resources in Bihar and Bengal.
  • Humayun then turned his attention to Gujarat, where Bahadur Shah had expanded his territory and offered shelter to Mughal political refugees.
  • Humayun successfully defeated Bahadur Shah in 1535, overrunning Malwa and Gujarat rapidly.
  • Due to poor administrative planning and lack of local support, the newly conquered western provinces were lost almost as quickly as they were won.

Remember

  • Battle of Kalinjar took place in 1531.
  • First Siege of Chunar occurred in 1532.
  • Campaign against Bahadur Shah of Gujarat lasted from 1534 to 1535.
  • Malwa and Gujarat were temporarily annexed but lost due to poor administrative follow-up.
Examples & sources
  • The lightning capture of Mandu and Champaner during the Gujarat campaign demonstrated Humayun's tactical speed.
  • The hasty abandonment of Gujarat to rush back to Agra upon hearing of Afghan resurgence in the east.
Compare
  • Humayun's campaign strategy relied on rapid cavalry strikes, contrasting with Sher Shah's systematic administrative consolidation.
  • Unlike Sher Shah who built permanent administrative infrastructure, Humayun treated conquests as temporary military occupations.
Common confusions
  • It is incorrect to think Humayun completely ignored the Afghan threat in the east; he engaged them multiple times but lacked long-term strategic follow-through.
  • Bahadur Shah of Gujarat was not a direct Mughal noble but an independent Sultan harboring anti-Mughal elements.
Exam points
  • Chronology of the Gujarat and Malwa campaigns (1534-1535).
  • Strategic error of lifting the first siege of Chunar in 1532.
  • Ruler of Gujarat during Humayun's western expedition.
Concept 3

Conflict with Sher Shah Suri and Loss of Empire

The decisive military confrontations between Humayun and Sher Shah Suri leading to Mughal expulsion.

Details

  • Sher Khan capitalized on Humayun's preoccupation in Gujarat to expand his control over Bengal and the strategic fort of Rohtas.
  • Humayun marched east in 1537 and successfully laid siege to the fortress of Chunar for six months, capturing it with heavy losses.
  • Sher Shah used this prolonged siege to safely evacuate his accumulated wealth from Gaur to Rohtas.
  • Humayun advanced into Bengal, occupying Gaur, but his army was soon immobilized by the monsoon and rampant disease.
  • Sher Shah cut off Humayun's lines of communication back to Agra, trapping the Mughal forces in Bengal.
  • In the Battle of Chausa fought on June 26, 1539, Sher Shah decisively defeated Humayun, who narrowly escaped by crossing the Ganges with the help of a water-carrier (Nizam).

Remember

  • Second Siege of Chunar occurred in 1537.
  • Battle of Chausa was fought on June 26, 1539.
  • Battle of Bilgram (Kannauj) was fought on May 17, 1540.
  • Nizam the water-carrier was granted the privilege of ruling for half a day for saving Humayun's life at Chausa.
Examples & sources
  • Humayun's escape across the Ganges at Chausa using a tattered skin float provided by a local water-carrier.
  • The total rout of the Mughal army at Kannauj in 1540 where thousands drowned in the Ganges without putting up a structured defense.
Compare
  • Sher Shah's superior tactical discipline and intelligence network contrasted sharply with Humayun's fragmented command structure.
  • The Battle of Chausa highlighted the vulnerability of Mughal heavy cavalry during the monsoon season against agile Afghan archers.
Common confusions
  • Humayun did not lose the empire in a single battle; it was a progressive military collapse spanning Chausa and Kannauj.
  • Sher Shah was not a minor chieftain at this point but an experienced military commander with a disciplined professional army.
Exam points
  • Exact dates of the Battle of Chausa (1539) and Battle of Kannauj (1540).
  • The role of Nizam the water-carrier.
  • Climatic and logistical causes of the Mughal defeat in Bengal.
Concept 4

Years in Exile and Journey to Persia

Humayun's fifteen years of wandering across Sindh, Rajasthan, and his refuge in the Safavid court of Persia.

Details

  • Following the defeat at Kannauj in 1540, Humayun became a royal refugee, wandering through Sindh and Marwar for nearly five years.
  • He sought shelter with local Hindu rulers in Rajasthan, including Rana Prasad of Amarkot, facing severe hardships.
  • Akbar was born on October 15, 1542, at Amarkot during this arduous desert journey.
  • Humayun eventually proceeded to Persia, entering the territories of Shah Tahmasp of the Safavid Dynasty.
  • Shah Tahmasp granted asylum on condition that Humayun convert to Shia Islam and cede the strategic fortress of Qandahar to Persia.
  • With Safavid military assistance, Humayun captured Qandahar and Kabul from his rebellious brother Kamran in 1545.

Remember

  • Birth of Akbar took place on October 15, 1542, at Amarkot.
  • Safavid ruler who hosted Humayun was Shah Tahmasp I.
  • Key condition of Safavid aid was conversion to Shia Islam and surrender of Qandahar.
  • Recovery of Kabul and Qandahar from Kamran occurred in 1545.
Examples & sources
  • Humayun's stay at the court of Shah Tahmasp where Persian artists were patronized, laying the seed for future Indo-Persian artistic synthesis.
  • The grueling trek across the Thar Desert where the royal entourage ran out of water and horses died of thirst.
Compare
  • Humayun's dependence on foreign Shia assistance contrasted with Babur's reliance on Central Asian Sunni Turco-Mongol allies.
  • The hardship of exile transformed Humayun from a casual conqueror into a hardened, patient political strategist.
Common confusions
  • Humayun's conversion to Shia Islam was a political concession to secure Persian arms, not a permanent personal theological shift.
  • Akbar was not born in a palace, but in a modest fortress tent in Amarkot while his parents were fleeing.
Exam points
  • Birth date and location of Akbar (October 15, 1542, Amarkot).
  • Name of the Safavid monarch (Shah Tahmasp).
  • Geographical route of Humayun's exile through Sindh and Baluchistan.
Concept 5

Restoration of the Mughal Empire and Accidental Death

The recovery of Delhi in 1555 and Humayun's untimely death in 1556.

Details

  • The Sur Empire fractured rapidly after the death of Sher Shah Sur in 1545 and Islam Shah Sur in 1554.
  • Seizing this opportunity, Humayun advanced from Kabul into Punjab with a well-trained army led by Bairam Khan.
  • In the Battle of Machhiwara (1555), Mughal forces defeated the Afghan army under Nasib Khan and Tatar Khan.
  • In the decisive Battle of Sirhind on July 22, 1555, Humayun and Bairam Khan defeated Sikandar Shah Sur, clearing the road to Delhi.
  • Humayun reoccupied Delhi in July 1555, successfully restoring the Mughal dynasty after a 15-year interregnum.
  • On January 24, 1556, Humayun died from injuries sustained after falling down the stone stairs of his library (Sher Mandal) in Delhi.

Remember

  • Battle of Machhiwara took place in 1555.
  • Battle of Sirhind was fought on July 22, 1555.
  • Commander instrumental in the restoration was Bairam Khan.
  • Death occurred on January 24, 1556, due to a fall from the Sher Mandal library.
Examples & sources
  • Sikandar Shah Sur fleeing to the Siwalik hills following the catastrophic defeat at Sirhind.
  • Lane-Poole's famous contemporary epigram stating that Humayun tumbled through life and tumbled out of it.
Compare
  • The rapid collapse of the Sur dynasty after Sher Shah's death contrasted with the enduring stability established by the restored Mughals.
  • Humayun's second reign lasted less than a year, serving primarily as a bridge between the founding era and the Akbar golden age.
Common confusions
  • It is a popular myth that Humayun was drunk when he fell; contemporary chroniclers note he was rushing to catch the evening call to prayer (Azan) while his robe caught on his feet.
  • The Sur empire was not destroyed solely by Humayun, but largely by severe internal succession civil wars among Afghan princes.
Exam points
  • Details of the Battle of Sirhind (1555) and Bairam Khan's role.
  • Exact date and cause of Humayun's death (January 24, 1556).
  • Name of the library building (Sher Mandal in Dinpanah, Delhi).
Concept 6

Cultural Patronage, Administration, and Historiography

Humayun's contributions to painting, architecture, literature, and astrological obsessions.

Details

  • Humayun was a deeply cultured monarch with a profound interest in astronomy, geography, mathematics, and mysticism.
  • During his exile in Persia, he invited master painters Mir Sayyid Ali and Khwaja Abdus Samad to his court, laying the foundation for Mughal miniature painting.
  • He founded the city of Dinpanah ('Refuge of the Faithful') in Delhi as his imperial capital.
  • His sister, Gulbadan Begum, wrote the invaluable historical source text 'Humayun-nama' at the behest of Emperor Akbar.
  • Humayun organized his court according to astrological principles, dividing his state officials into four departments corresponding to the four elements: Fire, Air, Water, and Earth.
  • He possessed a massive travelling library and lost his life while rushing down from its shelves to offer prayers.

Remember

  • Author of Humayun-nama was Gulbadan Begum.
  • Persian master painters brought to India were Mir Sayyid Ali and Khwaja Abdus Samad.
  • Capital city founded by Humayun was Dinpanah in Delhi.
  • Court administration was organized based on the four traditional elements.
Examples & sources
  • The commissioning of illustrated Persian manuscripts like the Dastan-i-Amir Hamza initiated under his early cultural patronage.
  • Gulbadan Begum's detailed eyewitness descriptions of court intrigues and royal domestic life in Humayun-nama.
Compare
  • Unlike Babur's brisk military memoirs (Tuzuk-i-Baburi), Humayun's reign is documented primarily through secondary chronicles like Khwandamir's Qanun-i-Humayuni and Gulbadan's Humayun-nama.
  • Humayun's eclectic interest in astrology and mysticism contrasted with the pragmatic secularism later developed by Akbar.
Common confusions
  • Humayun-nama was not written during Humayun's reign, but commissioned by Akbar decades later around 1589.
  • Humayun's architectural legacy was not limited to tombs; his foundation of Dinpanah was a major urban undertaking later absorbed into Sher Shah's Purana Qila.
Exam points
  • Authorship and significance of Humayun-nama.
  • Role of Mir Sayyid Ali and Abdus Samad in Mughal painting origins.
  • Founding of Dinpanah and administrative division by the four elements.

Rapid revision

Concept 1

Accession and Division of the Empire

Quick recall

  • Succeeded Babur to the Mughal throne in December 1530 AD at age 22.
  • Inherited an unstable, consolidated empire without a stable administrative apparatus.
  • Divided the empire among his three brothers (Kamran, Askari, and Hindal) as per Babur's deathbed advice.
  • Kamran received Kabul and Kandahar, creating a hostile power base that choked Humayun's rear.

Memory aid

  • K-A-H-H: Kings Always Have Headaches (Kamran, Askari, Hindal, Humayun).

Review tips

  • Focus on why the division of the empire was a strategic blunder, weakening centralized defense.

Quick checks

  • Remember that division of territories among brothers directly violated the policy of centralized indivisible sovereignty adopted later by Akbar.

Watch for

  • Thinking Babur forced Humayun to divide the empire permanently; it was friendly advice that Humayun executed politically unwisely.
Concept 2

Early Military Campaigns and Administrative Missteps

Quick recall

  • Fought early campaigns against Mahmud Lodi and the Afghans of Bihar (Battle of Douhar, 1532).
  • Besieged Chunar Fort under Sher Khan (Sher Shah Suri) but compromised prematurely.
  • Fought Bahadur Shah of Gujarat (1535-1536), capturing Gujarat and Malwa swiftly.
  • Abandoned Gujarat due to poor administrative control and lack of consolidation, letting Bahadur Shah retake it.

Memory aid

  • D-C-G: Don't Compromise Gujarat.

Review tips

  • Trace how Humayun's hasty retreats and leniency ruined his military gains.

Quick checks

  • Associate Gujarat campaign with the loss of momentum because Humayun engaged in prolonged celebrations instead of settling administration.

Watch for

  • Confusing the siege of Chunar (1532) with the major battles against Sher Shah Suri later at Chausa and Kanauj.
Concept 3

Conflict with Sher Shah Suri and Loss of Empire

Quick recall

  • Suffered a catastrophic defeat at the Battle of Chausa in 1539 against Sher Khan, barely escaping with his life.
  • Decisively defeated at the Battle of Kanauj (Bilhaur) in 1540, forcing him to flee India entirely.
  • Sher Shah Suri established the Sur Empire, cutting short Mughal rule for 15 years.
  • Humayun's lack of tactical foresight and slow communication caused these military debacles.

Memory aid

  • C-K: Chausa (1539) then Kanauj (1540) = Crown Lost.

Review tips

  • Memorize the exact chronology: Chausa (1539) precedes Kanauj (1540).

Quick checks

  • Use the mnemonic CK = Lost Crown to remember Humayun lost his throne at Chausa and Kanauj.

Watch for

  • Assuming Humayun lost the empire in a single battle; it was a two-step fall at Chausa and Kanauj.
Concept 4

Years in Exile and Journey to Persia

Quick recall

  • Spent 15 years (1540–1555) wandering through Sindh, Marwar, and Rajputana.
  • Married Hamida Banu Begam during exile; Akbar was born at Amarkot in 1542 under Raja Prasad's shelter.
  • Sought refuge in the Safavid court of Shah Tahmasp of Persia.
  • Agreed to convert to Shia Islam and cede Kandahar as conditions for Persian military assistance.

Memory aid

  • P-S-A: Persia Supports Akbar's ancestor.

Review tips

  • Remember the geopolitical price Humayun paid to the Safavids for their military backup.

Quick checks

  • Connect the birth of Akbar at Amarkot (1542) directly with the lowest point of Humayun's exile.

Watch for

  • Believing the Persian monarch helped Humayun out of pure altruism; it was purely strategic and sectarian alignment.
Concept 5

Restoration of the Mughal Empire and Accidental Death

Quick recall

  • Captured Kandahar and Kabul from his brother Kamran with Persian help.
  • Defeated the Suri remnants at the Battle of Macchiwara (1555) and Battle of Sirhind (1555).
  • Restored the Mughal throne in Delhi in July 1555 under Bairam Khan's regency.
  • Died in January 1556 after slipping from the stairs of his library (Sher Mandal) in Delhi.

Memory aid

  • M-S-D: Macchiwara, Sirhind, Delhi restored.

Review tips

  • Note Bairam Khan's crucial role as the military mastermind behind the 1555 restoration.

Quick checks

  • Remember Lane-Poole's famous quote: 'Humayun tumbled through life and tumbled out of it.'

Watch for

  • Confusing the date of accession (1530), restoration (1555), and death (1556).
Concept 6

Cultural Patronage, Administration, and Historiography

Quick recall

  • Laid the foundation of Mughal miniature painting by bringing Persian artists Mir Sayyid Ali and Abdus Samad.
  • Founded the Dinpanah city in Delhi as his imperial capital.
  • Deeply interested in astronomy, astrology, and designed his court dress according to the movements of celestial bodies.
  • Sister Gulbadan Begam authored the famous historical account 'Humayun-nama'.

Memory aid

  • P-A-D-H: Painting, Astrology, Dinpanah, Humayun-nama.

Review tips

  • Attribute the start of Persian master-painters in India to Humayun, not Akbar.

Quick checks

  • Remember that Humayun's library stairs led to his fatal fall, linking his intellectual pursuits to his demise.

Watch for

  • Assuming Akbar wrote or commissioned Humayun-nama; it was commissioned by Akbar but written by his aunt Gulbadan Begam.

Quick facts

  • Humayun means 'Fortunate', though his reign proved otherwise.
  • He ascended the throne in December 1530 at Agra.
  • The Battle of Chausa was fought in 1539 against Sher Shah Suri.
  • The Battle of Kanauj took place in 1540, ending early Mughal rule.
  • Akbar was born in Amarkot in 1542.
  • Humayun married Hamida Banu Begam in 1541.
  • He recaptured Delhi in July 1555 after winning the Battle of Sirhind.
  • He died in January 1556 due to a fall from his library steps.

Memory aids

  • C-K-S: Chausa (1539), Kanauj (1540), Sirhind (1555).
  • K-A-H-H: Kamran, Askari, Hindal, Humayun (Brothers).
  • P-A-D: Persia, Amarkot, Delhi (Exile trajectory).

Review tips

  • Always connect Humayun's military setbacks to his strategic delays and brotherly leniency.
  • Keep track of the 15-year interregnum period (1540-1555) of Suri rule.
  • Differentiate between the Persian alliance terms and local Rajput alliances.
  • Memorize Lane-Poole's critical quote regarding his life character.

Question cues

  • Use chronological anchoring: 1530 (Accession) -> 1539/1540 (Loss) -> 1555 (Restoration) -> 1556 (Death).
  • Associate Safavid refuge with Shia conversion requirement.
  • Link Sher Mandal library stairs to his unusual death via astrology obsession.
  • Remember Bairam Khan as the real muscle behind the 1555 comeback.

Compare

Battle of Chausa (1539)vsBattle of Kanauj (1540)
  • Chausa was a sudden surprise attack near the Ganges where Humayun narrowly escaped drowning.
  • Kanauj was a full-scale formal pitched battle that decisively destroyed Mughal field strength and drove Humayun out of India.
  • Chausa marked the beginning of Sher Khan's royal title assertion, while Kanauj finalized the establishment of the Sur Empire.

Watch for

  • Mistaking the Battle of Panipat for Humayun's restoration instead of Sirhind (1555).
  • Believing Humayun ruled continuously from 1530 to 1556 without interruption.
  • Attributing the architectural marvel Humayun's Tomb to Humayun himself (it was built by his widow Hamida Banu Begam).
  • Confusing the painter duo Mir Sayyid Ali and Abdus Samad's patron with Akbar instead of Humayun.
  • Assuming Kamran assisted Humayun loyally throughout his exile instead of obstructing him.
