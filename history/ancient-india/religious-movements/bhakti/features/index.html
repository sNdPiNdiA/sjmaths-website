Features of Bhakti

Core study module, theoretical overview, and exam revision checklist for Features of Bhakti under the History curriculum.

Appears in: UP TGT Social Science Tracker →

1. Syllabus Overview & Exam Focus

In competitive examinations (UP TGT Social Science / UP PGT History / UGC NET), Features of Bhakti is a key tested topic. Focus your preparation on the following high-priority points:

  • Evolution of Bhakti movement from South Indian Alvars and Nayanars to pan-Indian medieval socio-religious phenomenon
  • Core theological and philosophical tenets including Nirguna vs. Saguna traditions, anti-caste stance, and vernacular democratization
  • Key texts, saint-poets, and regional schools (Virashaivas, Warkaris, Sants) with emphasis on socio-economic contexts
  • Historiographical debates regarding the origins of Bhakti (indigenous development vs. Islamic impact)

2. Historical Background & Literary Sources

  • • Early Epigraphical Evidence: The Besnagar Inscription (c. 113 BCE) of Heliodorus refers to himself as a Bhagavata, proving early devotion to Vasudeva.
  • • Shvetashvatara Upanishad: Contains the earliest explicit use of the term 'Bhakti', emphasizing devotion to the Supreme God (Rudra-Shiva).
  • • Bhagavad Gita: Serves as the foundational scripture of the Bhakti marga, synthesizing karma, jnana, and bhakti yoga.
  • • South Indian Corpus: The Nalayira Divya Prabandham (compilation of Alvar hymns by Nathamuni) and the Tevaram (compilation of Nayanar hymns by Nambi Andar Nambi) are primary literary pillars.

3. Core Principles, Beliefs & Socio-Religious Features

  • • Personalized Monotheism: Focuses on an intimate, emotional, and personal bond between the devotee (bhakta) and God, bypassing elaborate ritualism.
  • • Rejection of Caste & Ritual Hierarchies: Most Bhakti saints rejected birth-based caste privileges, ritual purity, and Sanskrit hegemony, opening spiritual paths to Shudras and women.
  • • Vernacular Medium: Use of local languages (Tamil, Kannada, Marathi, Hindi, Bengali, Awadhi, Brajbhasha) instead of Sanskrit to make religious discourse accessible to the masses.
  • • Saguna vs. Nirguna Streams: Saguna saints (e.g., Tulsidas, Surdas, Chaitanya) worshipped God with attributes (incarnations/avatars), while Nirguna saints (e.g., Kabir, Nanak, Dadu) worshipped a formless, abstract, and transcendent reality.

4. Significant Figures, Regional Movements & Chronology

  • • Alvars and Nayanars (6th to 9th Century CE): 12 Vaishnava Alvars (including Andal, the female mystic) and 63 Shaiva Nayanars (including Appar, Sundarar, Sambandar) spearheaded the movement in Tamil Nadu.
  • • Virashaiva / Lingayat Movement (12th Century CE): Founded by Basaveshwara in Karnataka; rejected Vedic authority, child marriage, and cremation; promoted post-puberty marriage and the wearing of the Ishtalinga.
  • • Warkari Panth (13th-17th Century CE): Maharashtra-based movement centered around Vithoba (Pandharpur); key figures include Jnanesvar (author of Jnanesvari), Namdev, Eknath, and Tukaram.
  • • Northern Sant Tradition (15th-16th Century CE): Kabir, Guru Nanak, and Ravidas emphasized internal devotion, castigating both Brahmanical orthodoxy and Islamic ritualism.

5. Impact, Legacy & Historiography

  • • Socio-Cultural Synthesis: Fostered syncretic cultural traditions, influencing music (bhajans, kirtans, Sufi qawwali), dance, and art across medieval India.
  • • Empowerment of Marginalized Groups: Provided platforms for women (Mirabai, Andal, Akka Mahadevi) and lower castes to achieve spiritual and social authority through poetry.
  • • Tara Chand & Yusuf Husain Thesis: Early nationalist historians argued that the Bhakti movement was a direct reaction or synthesis prompted by Islamic arrival and Sufi mysticism.
  • • Modern Revisionist Historiography: Scholars like K.A. Nilakanta Sastri and R. Champakalakshmi trace deep indigenous roots to South India predating large-scale Islamic settlement in the North.

6. High-Yield Key Facts & Exam Memory Points

  • Key Fact: The 12 Alvar saints were Vaishnava devotees, and the 63 Nayanar saints were Shaiva devotees.
  • Key Fact: Basaveshwara established the Anubhava Mantapa (Hall of Experience) in Kalyana for socio-religious debate across castes.
  • Key Fact: Jnanesvar wrote the Jnanesvari ( Bhavartha Dipika), a Marathi commentary on the Bhagavad Gita, at age 15.
  • Key Fact: Kabir belonged to the Nirguna tradition and was a weaver (julaha) by profession.
  • Key Fact: Mirabai famously defied Rajput patriarchal norms to worship Lord Krishna as her ultimate husband.
  • Key Fact: Chaitanya Mahaprabhu popularized Gaudiya Vaishnavism and Sankirtan (public devotional chanting) in Bengal and Odisha.

7. Common Exam Pitfalls & Confusions to Avoid

  • Do not confuse Alvars (Vaishnava) with Nayanars (Shaiva); remember 'A' for Alvars/Avatara/Vishnu and 'N' for Nayanar/Nataraja/Shiva.
  • Nirguna Bhakti does not utilize idol worship or temple rituals, whereas Saguna Bhakti heavily relies on murti puja and avatars.
  • The Bhakti movement did not completely eradicate the caste system; rather, it provided spiritual equality while social inequalities largely persisted in everyday life.
