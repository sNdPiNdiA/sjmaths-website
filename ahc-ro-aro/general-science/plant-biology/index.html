Plant Biology Core Study Notes

Plant Biology holds significant weightage in the General Science section of the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations. Questions frequently target plant tissues, photosynthesis, plant hormones (phytohormones), vascular systems, and economic botany.

Plant Tissues and Anatomy

Understanding the structural organization of plants through meristematic and permanent tissues.

  • Meristematic Tissues: Actively dividing cells responsible for plant growth. Classified into apical (length), lateral (girth/secondary growth), and intercalary (internodal elongation).
  • Simple Permanent Tissues: Comprise Parenchyma (living, storage, photosynthesis), Collenchyma (living, flexible mechanical support), and Sclerenchyma (dead, rigid support containing lignin).
  • Complex Permanent Tissues: Xylem transports water and minerals (composed of tracheids, vessels, xylem parenchyma, and xylem fibers). Phloem transports organic food (composed of sieve tubes, companion cells, phloem parenchyma, and phloem fibers).
  • Stomata and Transpiration: Microscopic pores on leaves regulated by guard cells that control gaseous exchange and transpiration. Potassium ions ($K^+$) play a key role in the opening and closing mechanism.

Plant Physiology & Photosynthesis

Core biochemical processes enabling plant survival, energy conversion, and nutrient uptake.

  • Photosynthesis Equation: $6CO_2 + 6H_2O \xrightarrow{Light/Chlorophyll} C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2$. Oxygen released comes from water photolysis, not carbon dioxide.
  • Photosynthetic Pigments: Chlorophyll 'a' is the primary photosynthetic pigment. Chlorophyll 'b', carotenoids, and xanthophylls act as accessory pigments absorbing different light spectra.
  • C3 and C4 Pathways: C3 plants (wheat, rice) fix $CO_2$ directly via RuBisCO. C4 plants (maize, sugarcane) have Kranz anatomy and use PEP carboxylase for efficient carbon fixation in high temperatures.
  • Respiration in Plants: Occurs via Glycolysis (in cytoplasm) and Krebs cycle / Electron Transport System (in mitochondria), releasing energy as ATP.

Plant Hormones (Phytohormones)

Chemical messengers regulating growth, development, flowering, and stress responses in plants.

  • Auxins: Discovered by F.W. Went. Promotes cell elongation, apical dominance, and root initiation. Example: Indole-3-acetic acid (IAA).
  • Gibberellins (GA): Promotes stem elongation, seed germination, and bolting in rosette plants. Breaks seed dormancy.
  • Cytokinins: Promotes cell division (cytokinesis), delays leaf senescence (aging), and overcomes apical dominance.
  • Ethylene and Abscisic Acid (ABA): Ethylene is a gaseous hormone promoting fruit ripening. Abscisic Acid (ABA) is a stress hormone inducing stomatal closure and seed dormancy.

Plant Taxonomy & Reproduction

Classification of plant kingdom and reproductive mechanisms in flowering and non-flowering plants.

  • Plant Kingdom Classification: Divided into Cryptogams (non-seed bearing: Thallophyta, Bryophyta, Pteridophyta) and Phanerogams (seed bearing: Gymnosperms, Angiosperms).
  • Bryophytes and Pteridophytes: Bryophytes ('Amphibians of plant kingdom') lack true vascular tissue. Pteridophytes are the first vascular cryptogams possessing xylem and phloem.
  • Gymnosperms vs Angiosperms: Gymnosperms bear naked seeds without fruits (e.g., Pinus, Cycas). Angiosperms bear seeds enclosed within fruits, further divided into Monocots and Dicots.
  • Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants: Stamen is the male reproductive organ (produces pollen); Pistil/Carpel is the female reproductive organ (ovary, style, stigma). Double fertilization is unique to angiosperms.
