Genetics Core Study Notes

Genetics is a fundamental pillar of General Science for the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations. Questions frequently test laws of inheritance, DNA structure, genetic disorders, and recent biotechnological applications, requiring precise knowledge of scientific terminology and historical milestones.

Mendelian Genetics and Laws of Inheritance

Core principles governing how traits are transmitted from parents to offspring, formulated using Pisum sativum.

  • Law of Segregation: Also known as Mendel's First Law; states that allele pairs separate during gamete formation and randomly unite at fertilization.
  • Law of Independent Assortment: Mendel's Second Law; states that allele pairs separate independently during the formation of gametes, applying primarily to genes on different chromosomes.
  • Monohybrid and Dihybrid Crosses: Monohybrid cross yields a phenotypic ratio of 3:1 and genotypic ratio of 1:2:1 in F2 generation; Dihybrid cross yields a phenotypic ratio of 9:3:3:1.
  • Test Cross: A cross between an organism with an unknown genotype and a homozygous recessive parent to determine if the organism is homozygous or heterozygous.

Molecular Basis of Inheritance (DNA and RNA)

Detailed study of nucleic acids, genetic code, transcription, translation, and replication mechanisms.

  • DNA Structure: Deoxyribonucleic acid is a double-stranded helix where purines (Adenine, Guanine) pair with pyrimidines (Thymine, Cytosine) via hydrogen bonds (A=T, G≡C).
  • Central Dogma of Molecular Biology: Proposed by Francis Crick, it describes the directional flow of genetic information: DNA -> mRNA -> Protein (with reverse transcription as an exception in retroviruses).
  • Genetic Code Characteristics: The code is triplet, universal, degenerate (multiple codons for one amino acid), non-overlapping, and comma-less. AUG acts as the start codon (Methionine).
  • Types of RNA: mRNA carries genetic message from DNA; tRNA brings specific amino acids during translation; rRNA forms the structural core of ribosomes.

Genetic Disorders and Chromosomal Abnormalities

Pathological conditions arising from mutations in single genes or numerical/structural changes in chromosomes.

  • Down Syndrome (Trisomy 21): Caused by an extra copy of chromosome 21; characterized by intellectual disability, epicanthic folds, and a flattened facial profile.
  • Turner Syndrome (45, X0): A monosomy condition in females where one X chromosome is missing, leading to short stature, webbed neck, and sterility.
  • Klinefelter Syndrome (47, XXY): Affects males with an extra X chromosome; characterized by gynecomastia, small testes, and reduced fertility.
  • Hemophilia and Color Blindness: X-linked recessive disorders where Hemophilia impairs blood clotting and color blindness affects red-green color differentiation.

Modern Biotechnology and Genetic Engineering

Advanced techniques involving recombinant DNA technology, gene cloning, PCR, and transgenic organisms.

  • Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR): Invented by Kary Mullis; used to amplify a single or few copies of a piece of DNA across several orders of magnitude, generating millions of copies.
  • Restriction Endonucleases: Known as 'molecular scissors', these enzymes cut DNA molecules at specific palindromic nucleotide sequences.
  • Recombinant DNA Technology: Involves joining together DNA strands ('molecular cloning') to create useful biological products, pioneered by Boyer and Cohen in 1973.
  • CRISPR-Cas9: A revolutionary gene-editing tool derived from bacterial defense systems, allowing precise addition, removal, or alteration of genetic material.
