Human Body Systems Core Study Notes

Human Body Systems form a cornerstone of General Science in the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations. Questions frequently target organ functions, deficiency diseases, hormonal regulation, and biochemical pathways, demanding high factual accuracy.

Digestive and Respiratory Systems

Comprehensive breakdown of nutrient assimilation and gaseous exchange in humans.

  • Salivary Amylase (Ptyalin): Secreted by salivary glands; initiates the digestion of carbohydrates (starch) in the oral cavity at an optimum pH of ~6.8.
  • Gastric Juice & HCl: Secreted by parietal cells of the stomach; provides acidic medium (pH 1.5-2.0), activates pepsinogen into pepsin, and kills ingested bacteria.
  • Bile Juice Production: Produced by the liver and stored in the gall bladder; contains no enzymes but performs emulsification of fats.
  • Alveolar Gaseous Exchange: Occurs via simple diffusion across the respiratory membrane between alveoli and pulmonary capillaries driven by partial pressure gradients.
  • Oxygen Transport: Around 97% of O2 is transported by binding reversibly with hemoglobin in erythrocytes as oxyhemoglobin; 3% is dissolved in plasma.

Circulatory and Excretory Systems

Analysis of the cardiovascular network and renal filtration mechanisms.

  • Cardiac Cycle & Pacemaker: Sinoatrial node (SAN) acts as the natural pacemaker generating the cardiac impulse; normal human heart rate averages 72 beats per minute.
  • Blood Pressure Parameters: Normal resting blood pressure is 120/80 mmHg, where 120 represents systolic pressure and 80 represents diastolic pressure, measured via sphygmomanometer.
  • Nephron Functional Unit: The structural and functional unit of the kidney; comprises Bowman's capsule, glomerulus, proximal convoluted tubule, Loop of Henle, and distal tubule.
  • Countercurrent Mechanism: Operates in the Loop of Henle and vasa recta to concentrate urine, conserving water in the human body under dehydrated states.
  • Composition of Urine: Normal human urine consists of 95% water, 2% urea, along with uric acid, creatinine, and various ions; normal daily excretion is about 1.5 litres.

Nervous and Endocrine Systems

Coordination, integration, and chemical messaging in human physiology.

  • Central Nervous System (CNS): Comprises the brain and spinal cord; processes sensory information and coordinates motor responses.
  • Synaptic Transmission: Impulses across neurons are transmitted via chemical neurotransmitters such as acetylcholine and noradrenaline across synaptic clefts.
  • Master Gland (Pituitary): Located in the sella turcica of the sphenoid bone; secretes tropic hormones that regulate multiple secondary endocrine glands.
  • Thyroid and Iodine Regulation: Secretes thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3); regulates basal metabolic rate (BMR); iodine deficiency leads to simple goitre.
  • Adrenal Glands (Emergency Response): Adrenal medulla secretes adrenaline ('fight or flight' hormone); cortex secretes cortisol and aldosterone regulating electrolytes and stress.
