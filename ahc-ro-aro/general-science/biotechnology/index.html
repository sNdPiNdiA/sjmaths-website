Biotechnology Core Study Notes

Biotechnology is a high-yield, core segment of the General Science curriculum for the AHC RO/ARO and UPPSC exams. It covers the application of biological systems and organisms to develop products, focusing heavily on recombinant DNA technology, transgenic crops, medical advancements, and key regulatory bodies in India.

Principles of Genetic Engineering

Genetic engineering involves manipulating an organism's genome using biotechnology. Understanding its core tools is essential for exam success.

  • Restriction Endonucleases (Molecular Scissors): Enzymes that cut DNA at specific nucleotide sequences known as palindromic sequences. Discovered by Arber, Smith, and Nathans (Nobel Prize 1978). EcoRI is a classic example.
  • DNA Ligase (Molecular Glue): An enzyme that facilitates the joining of DNA strands together by catalyzing the formation of a phosphodiester bond, crucial for sealing recombinant DNA molecules.
  • Plasmids and Vectors: Plasmids are extra-chromosomal, self-replicating circular DNA molecules found in bacteria. They act as vectors to transfer foreign genetic material into host cells (e.g., pBR322, Ti Plasmid of Agrobacterium tumefaciens).
  • Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR): Developed by Kary Mullis in 1983, PCR is used to amplify a single or a few copies of a piece of DNA across several orders of magnitude. It utilizes Taq Polymerase, a thermostable enzyme isolated from the bacterium Thermus aquaticus.

Agricultural Biotechnology & GM Crops

Genetically Modified (GM) crops are plants whose DNA has been modified using genetic engineering methods to introduce traits like pest resistance, herbicide tolerance, or enhanced nutrition.

  • Bt Cotton: The only commercially cultivated GM crop in India. It incorporates genes (Cry1Ac and Cry2Ab) from the soil bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis to produce insecticidal proteins toxic to bollworms.
  • Dhara Mustard Hybrid-11 (DMH-11): An indigenous GM mustard hybrid developed by Delhi University's Centre for Genetic Manipulation of Crop Plants (CGMCP) led by Deepak Pental. It uses the Barnase-Barstar gene system for hybridization.
  • Golden Rice: A variety of Oryza sativa produced through genetic engineering to biosynthesize beta-carotene, a precursor of Vitamin A. It aims to combat Vitamin A deficiency (VAD) in developing nations.
  • Flavr Savr Tomato: The first genetically engineered food to be granted license for human consumption (1994). It was modified to have a longer shelf-life by inhibiting the enzyme polygalacturonase using antisense RNA technology.

Branches and Applications of Biotechnology

Biotechnology is classified into color-coded branches based on its area of application, ranging from medicine to marine science.

  • Red Biotechnology (Medical): Deals with medical processes, including the production of vaccines, antibiotics, regenerative therapies, and gene therapy (e.g., treatment of ADA deficiency).
  • White Biotechnology (Industrial): Applied to industrial processes, such as using enzymes as industrial catalysts to produce chemicals, bio-plastics, and biofuels (e.g., ethanol production).
  • Green Biotechnology (Agricultural): Focuses on agricultural applications, including micropropagation (tissue culture), developing transgenic crops, and biofertilizers.

Regulatory Framework & Institutes in India

India has established a robust regulatory mechanism and premier research institutes to govern and promote biotechnology, which are highly relevant for UPPSC and AHC exams.

  • Department of Biotechnology (DBT): Established in 1986 under the Ministry of Science and Technology, DBT is the nodal agency for promoting biotechnology research, development, and commercialization in India.
  • Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee (GEAC): A statutory body operating under the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) under the Environment Protection Act, 1986. It is the apex body that approves the commercial release of GM crops in India.
  • Key Biotech Institutes in Uttar Pradesh: UP hosts several premier institutes: Central Drug Research Institute (CDRI, Lucknow), National Botanical Research Institute (NBRI, Lucknow), and the Biotech Park in Lucknow (jointly set up by DBT and UP Govt).
  • Bioremediation Techniques: Use of microorganisms to clean up contaminated environments. Key methods include 'Biostimulation' (adding nutrients to stimulate existing bacteria) and 'Bioaugmentation' (adding external specialized microbes, like Pseudomonas putida for oil spills).
