Space Technology Core Study Notes

Space Technology is a high-yielding core area in General Science and Technology for Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations. Questions frequently target ISRO's major launch vehicles, historical milestones, recent deep-space missions, and fundamental orbital mechanics.

Launch Vehicles and Propulsion Systems

Understanding ISRO's evolution from experimental sounding rockets to heavy-lift launch vehicles and cryogenic engines.

  • PSLV (Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle): Known as the 'Workhorse of ISRO', it is a 4-stage launch vehicle alternating solid and liquid fuel stages (Solid-Liquid-Solid-Liquid). It is primarily used for Sun-Synchronous Polar Orbits (SSPO) and remote sensing satellites.
  • GSLV (Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle): A 3-stage heavy-lift vehicle featuring a solid first stage, liquid second stage, and an indigenous cryogenic upper stage (CE-7.5 or CE-20). Designed for placing communication satellites into Geostationary Transfer Orbit (GTO).
  • LVM3 (Launch Vehicle Mark 3 / GSLV Mk III): ISRO's heaviest operational rocket featuring two solid strap-on boosters (S200), a core liquid stage (L110), and a high-thrust cryogenic upper stage (C25). Successfully deployed Chandrayaan-2, Chandrayaan-3, and OneWeb commercial payloads.
  • Cryogenic Technology: Utilizes liquid hydrogen (LH2 as fuel at -253°C) and liquid oxygen (LOX as oxidizer at -183°C). Provides significantly higher specific impulse compared to solid and earth-storable liquid propellants.
  • SSLV (Small Satellite Launch Vehicle): Designed to cater to the booming commercial nano and microsatellite launch market on an 'on-demand' basis. Features three solid propulsion stages and a terminal velocity correction module (TVCM).

Orbits and Orbital Mechanics

Fundamental classifications of Earth orbits based on altitude, inclination, and operational utility.

  • LEO (Low Earth Orbit): Altitude ranges from 160 km to 2,000 km above Earth's surface. Used for Earth observation, reconnaissance, meteorological monitoring, and the International Space Station (ISS).
  • SSPO (Sun-Synchronous Polar Orbit): A retrograde near-polar orbit at an altitude of 600-800 km where the satellite passes over any given point of Earth's surface at the exact same local solar time, ensuring uniform illumination.
  • MEO (Medium Earth Orbit): Located between 2,000 km and 35,786 km. Primarily utilized for global navigation satellite systems (GNSS) like GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, and India's NAVIC.
  • GEO / GSO (Geostationary / Geosynchronous Orbit): Altitude of exactly 35,786 km. In GEO, the satellite matches Earth's rotation and appears stationary over a fixed point on the equator (zero inclination). GSO may have an orbital inclination other than zero.
  • Lagrange Points (L1 to L5): Positions in space where the gravitational pull of two large masses precisely equals the centripetal force required for a small object to move with them. L1 is used for solar observation (Aditya-L1).

Major Indian Space Missions

Highlighting landmark scientific and exploratory missions executed by ISRO.

  • Chandrayaan-3 (2023): Successfully launched on July 14, 2023, via LVM3-M4. Achieved historic soft landing near the lunar South Pole on August 23, 2023, making India the fourth country to land on the Moon and the first near the polar region.
  • Aditya-L1 (2023): India's first dedicated solar mission launched on September 2, 2023, by PSLV-C57. Inserted into a halo orbit around the Lagrange point 1 (L1), approximately 1.5 million km from Earth, to study the solar corona.
  • Gaganyaan Programme: India's maiden human spaceflight mission aiming to send a crew of Indian astronauts into LEO (400 km orbit) for a 3-day mission and safely return them to Earth, utilizing the LVM3 launch vehicle.
  • Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan / MOM): Launched on November 5, 2013, via PSLV-C25. It made ISRO the first space agency in the world to successfully insert a spacecraft into Martian orbit on its very first attempt, and the first Asian nation to do so.
  • ASTROSAT: India's first dedicated multi-wavelength space observatory launched on September 28, 2015, by PSLV-C30. Capable of simultaneous multi-band observations of cosmic sources across ultraviolet, optical, and X-ray spectral bands.

Satellite Applications and Navigation

Comprehensive categorization of satellites based on utility including communication, earth observation, and navigation.

  • INSAT / GSAT Series: Geostationary communication satellites providing services for telecommunications, television broadcasting, meteorological forecasting, disaster warning, and satellite-based search and rescue.
  • IRS Series (Remote Sensing): Placed in sun-synchronous polar orbits to acquire high-resolution imagery for agriculture, forestry, water resource management, mineral prospecting, and cartography.
  • NavIC (Navigation with Indian Constellation): Formerly IRNSS, it is an independent regional navigation satellite system developed by India. It consists of 7 operational satellites (3 in GEO, 4 in GSO) providing positional accuracy better than 20 meters over India and a 1,500 km primary service area.
  • RISAT (Radar Imaging Satellite): Equipped with Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR), enabling all-weather, day-and-night imaging capabilities for surveillance, border security, disaster management, and flood mapping.
