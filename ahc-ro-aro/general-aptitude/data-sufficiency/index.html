Data Sufficiency Core Study Notes

Data Sufficiency tests a candidate's ability to determine whether the information provided in given statements is sufficient to solve a specific problem, without requiring the actual numerical or logical calculation to be completed. It holds high weightage in Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations as it evaluates analytical rigour and time-management under pressure.

Core Concepts and Standard Answer Options

Understanding the fundamental rules of evaluation and standard choices provided in standard state-level administrative exams.

  • Definition of Data Sufficiency: A question is followed by two or more statements. You must determine if the data given in the statements is sufficient to answer the question, without actually solving the problem.
  • Statement I Alone: If the data in Statement I alone is sufficient to answer the question, while the data in Statement II alone is not sufficient.
  • Statement II Alone: If the data in Statement II alone is sufficient to answer the question, while the data in Statement I alone is not sufficient.
  • Either I or II: If either Statement I alone or Statement II alone is independently sufficient to answer the question.
  • Both Statements Required: If the data given in both Statements I and II together are necessary to answer the question, but neither statement alone is sufficient.
  • Neither Statement Sufficient: If the data in both Statements I and II together are not sufficient to answer the question, and further data is required.
Option Code

Quantitative Data Sufficiency Techniques

Applying sufficiency checks to arithmetic, algebraic, and number-system problems frequently tested in AHC RO/ARO.

  • Percentage & Profit-Loss Sufficiency: To find selling price, knowing either cost price and profit percentage, or cost price and marked price with discount is sufficient. Absolute values are often unnecessary if ratios are provided.
  • Time, Speed & Distance: Using the relation $D = S \times T$, knowing any two variables or equivalent proportional ratios is sufficient to determine the third. Individual speeds of two bodies require relative speed parameters.
  • Age and Partnership Problems: A single linear equation with two variables cannot be solved unless a second independent relation or ratio is provided in the second statement.
  • Number System Properties: Statements specifying whether a number is prime, composite, even, odd, or divisible by specific integers can uniquely determine outcomes without calculating exact values.

Logical & Reasoning Data Sufficiency

Evaluating sufficiency in blood relations, directions, ordering, and coded information.

  • Blood Relations Sufficiency: Gender of individuals is paramount. If a statement mentions 'A is the parent of B' without specifying A's gender (father or mother), the statement alone is insufficient to determine paternal/maternal relations.
  • Direction Sense Sufficiency: Distance and direction require vector components. Knowing only the distance walked without turn angles or cardinal directions is insufficient to find the shortest distance from the starting point.
  • Seating Arrangement Sufficiency: Circular or linear arrangements require relative positions. Statements providing end positions or immediate neighbors for all individuals are required to fix absolute seats.
  • Ordering and Ranking: To find the total number of persons in a row, knowing an individual's rank from the left and from the right is sufficient. If only one rank is known, the statement is insufficient.

Advanced Strategies & Elimination Tactics

Time-saving shortcuts to solve Data Sufficiency questions accurately in competitive examinations.

  • Independent Testing First: Always test Statement I independently. Assume Statement II does not exist. Then test Statement II independently. Combine them only if both fail individually.
  • Beware of Hidden Assumptions: Do not assume standard real-world values unless stated. For instance, do not assume a person's working hours are 8 hours daily unless explicitly mentioned in the statement.
  • Consistency Check: When combining statements, ensure they do not contradict each other. If Statement I says X > 5 and Statement II says X < 3, the data is inconsistent and contradictory.
