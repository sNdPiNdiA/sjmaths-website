Time and Work Core Study Notes

Time and Work is a critical quantitative aptitude topic frequently tested in the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations. It evaluates a candidate's ability to logically analyze efficiency, rates of working, and fractional contributions of labor over specific durations.

Fundamental Concepts of Time and Work

Understanding the inverse relationship between time taken and efficiency is vital for solving basic and advanced problems.

  • Work-Time Inverse Proportion: If a person completes a piece of work in 'n' days, the work done by that person in 1 day is 1/n part of the total work.
  • Efficiency and Time Relation: Efficiency is inversely proportional to the time taken. If Person A is twice as efficient as Person B, A takes half the time B takes to complete the same work.
  • Total Work Equation: Total Work = Efficiency (Rate) × Time. In unit terms, it is measured in Man-Days or total units of work derived from LCM.
  • Combined Work Rate: If A can do a work in 'x' days and B in 'y' days, together they can finish it in (x × y) / (x + y) days.

Advanced Methods: LCM and Pipe-Cistern

Applying the LCM method eliminates fractions, reducing calculation errors significantly in competitive examinations.

  • LCM Method Principle: Assume total work to be the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of the given individual days, and calculate per-day unit efficiency for each entity.
  • Alternate Day Working: When workers take turns alternatively, calculate the cumulative work done in a complete cycle of turns before determining the remaining units.
  • Pipes and Cisterns Correlation: Inlets are treated as positive work rates (efficiency), while outlets or leaks are treated as negative work rates.
  • Negative Work / Leakage Formula: If inlet fills in 'x' hours and outlet empties in 'y' hours (y > x), net time to fill is (x × y) / (y - x) hours.

Chain Rule and M-D-H Concepts

The Chain Rule extends time-work principles to handle variables like number of workers, daily working hours, and fraction of work completed.

  • General Formula (M-D-H): (M₁ × D₁ × H₁ × E₁) / W₁ = (M₂ × D₂ × H₂ × E₂) / W₂, where M=Men, D=Days, H=Hours, E=Efficiency, and W=Work.
  • Constant Parameters: If hours or efficiency are not mentioned, they are treated as equal and omitted from both sides of the equation.
  • Work Placement in Formula: Always place the actual work done (or units produced, trenches dug) in the denominator (W₁ and W₂).
  • Proportionate Wages: Wages are always distributed among workers in the ratio of their individual efficiency or daily work done, not time taken.
