Coding Decoding Core Study Notes

Coding-Decoding is a foundational component of the General Aptitude & Reasoning section in the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations. It tests an applicant's analytical capability, speed, and accuracy by evaluating how efficiently they can decipher hidden patterns in letters, numbers, and symbols.

Alphabet Coding & Positional Values

Mastering forward and backward alphabetical positions is the primary prerequisite for cracking letter-based coding questions in AHC RO/ARO exams.

  • Forward Letter Positions (A-Z): Letters are numbered sequentially from A=1 to Z=26. Memorizing these values instantly saves critical time during the preliminary examination.
  • Backward Letter Positions (Z-A): Z=1, Y=2, ... A=26. Alternatively calculated using the formula: (27 - Forward Position).
  • Opposite Letter Pairs: Pairs whose positional sum equals 27 (e.g., A-Z, B-Y, C-X, D-W, E-V, F-U, G-T, H-S, I-R, J-Q, K-P, L-O, M-N). Frequently tested in direct opposite coding.
  • Forward Shifting Patterns: Letters are shifted forward by a constant or variable numerical value (+1, +2, +3 or +1, +2, +1, +2).
  • Backward Shifting Patterns: Letters are shifted backward by a fixed decrement or progressive subtraction sequence.

Number & Substitution Coding

Number coding involves converting words into numbers or vice-versa through arithmetic operations on positional values.

  • Sum of Positional Values: The coded value is the exact sum of the forward or backward alphabetical positions of the letters in the given word.
  • Product / Average Operations: Sometimes the sum is multiplied by the total number of letters or divided to find the average value.
  • Direct Letter-to-Number Substitution: Specific digits are assigned arbitrarily to specific letters without following standard alphabetical positions.
  • Vowel vs Consonant Rules: Different arithmetic treatments are applied depending on whether the letter is a vowel (A, E, I, O, U) or a consonant.
  • Digit Summation: If a position is two-digit (e.g., K=11), sometimes the individual digits are added (1+1=2) to arrive at the final code.

Mixed & Message Coding (Substitution)

Commonly featured in UPPSC and High Court exams where complete sentences or phrases are translated into coded messages.

  • Common Word Identification: Compare two or more sentences to find common words and match them with their corresponding common coded words.
  • Elimination Technique: Systematically eliminate words that appear across multiple statements to isolate the code for a specific target word.
  • Direct Substitution Code: Statements like 'sky is blue' coded as 'pit na sa' require direct position matching rather than alphabetical logic.
  • Conditional Message Decoding: Advanced questions where specific conditions apply if a word starts with a vowel or ends with a consonant.

Matrix & Conditional Coding

Matrix coding uses grid systems where letters are represented by their row and column intersection coordinates.

  • Row-Column Rule (RO-CO): Standard convention dictates that the row number is always stated first, followed by the column number (e.g., [Row, Column]).
  • Dual Matrix System: Letters are distributed across two separate matrices (Matrix I: 0-4, Matrix II: 5-9) to increase complexity.
  • Conditional Coding Rules: Specific riders given at the beginning of the question alter standard codes when certain letter combinations occur.
  • Option Elimination Method: Always decode from the right end (last letter) of the given word to rapidly eliminate incorrect options in matrix questions.
