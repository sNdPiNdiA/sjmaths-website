Profit Loss Core Study Notes

Profit and Loss is a high-yielding arithmetic topic frequently tested in the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations. Mastery of cost price, selling price, marked price, successive discounts, and dishonest dealer concepts is essential for scoring high in the General Aptitude & Reasoning paper.

Core Terminology & Basic Formulas

Understanding foundational terms such as Cost Price (CP), Selling Price (SP), Marked Price (MP), Profit, and Loss is mandatory for solving basic arithmetic problems.

  • Cost Price (CP): The price at which an article is purchased. Total CP includes additional overhead expenses like transportation, repair, and taxes.
  • Selling Price (SP): The price at which an article is sold to the consumer or end-user.
  • Profit and Loss Calculations: Profit = SP - CP (when SP > CP). Loss = CP - SP (when CP > SP). Both profit percentage and loss percentage are always calculated on the Cost Price unless specified otherwise.
  • Marked Price & Discount: Marked Price (MP) is the labeled price. Discount is the reduction offered on MP. Discount = MP - SP, and Discount % = (Discount / MP) * 100.
Condition Formula / Equation Percentage Equivalent
Profit Percentage [(SP - CP) / CP] * 100 Profit% = (Profit / CP) * 100
Loss Percentage [(CP - SP) / CP] * 100 Loss% = (Loss / CP) * 100
Selling Price (given Profit %) SP = CP * [(100 + Profit%) / 100] CP = SP * [100 / (100 + Profit%)]
Selling Price (given Discount %) SP = MP * [(100 - Discount%) / 100] MP = SP * [100 / (100 - Discount%)]

Advanced Concepts: Dishonest Dealers & False Weights

Examiners frequently test concepts involving fraudulent practices such as using faulty weights or claiming to sell goods at cost price while generating hidden profits.

  • False Weight Formula: When a dealer sells goods at cost price using a false weight, True Profit% = [(True Weight - False Weight) / False Weight] * 100.
  • Combined Profit and False Weight: If a dealer promises a profit of x% on CP and also uses a false weight of y grams instead of 1000 grams, Overall Gain% = [{(100 + x) * (1000 / y)} - 100]%.
  • Successive Discount Equivalents: Two successive discounts of a% and b% are equivalent to a single net discount of [a + b - (ab / 100)]%.
  • Equal Percentage Profit and Loss on SP: If two articles are sold at the same selling price, one at a profit of x% and another at a loss of x%, there is always a net loss given by Loss% = (x / 10)^2.

Ratio & Proportion Methods in Profit & Loss

Time-saving short tricks using fraction equivalents of percentages are vital for clearing the AHC RO/ARO preliminary and mains examinations within the stipulated time.

  • Fractional Equivalents: Memorizing standard fractions like 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/6 = 16.67%, and 1/7 = 14.28% accelerates calculation speed for complex profit percentage problems.
  • CP to SP Ratio Method: If profit is 16.67% (1/6), then CP:SP = 6:7. If loss is 12.5% (1/8), then CP:SP = 8:7. This enables direct proportional scaling.
  • Marked Price to Cost Price Ratio: Direct relation between MP and CP given Discount% (D) and Profit% (P): MP / CP = (100 + P) / (100 - D).

Previous Years' Exam Trends & Question Types

Analysis of past Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC exams reveals specific recurring patterns in numerical aptitude questions.

  • Based on Quantity of Articles: If the Cost Price of 'x' articles equals the Selling Price of 'y' articles, Profit/Loss % = [(x - y) / y] * 100.
  • Two Successive Sales (Chain Transactions): When A sells an article to B at a profit of x%, and B sells to C at a profit of y%, final effective price for C = CP_A * [(100+x)/100] * [(100+y)/100].
  • Fluctuation in SP and CP: Problems where both CP and SP are changed by fixed amounts or percentages require equating initial and final profit equations to solve for original CP.
