Delhi Sultanate Core Study Notes

The Delhi Sultanate covers the period from 1206 CE to 1526 CE, during which five successive dynasties ruled over large parts of Northern India. For AHC RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations, questions frequently focus on administrative terminologies, agrarian reforms, architectural marvels, and chronologies of rulers.

Administrative Structure & Terminology

Detailed breakdown of central and provincial administrative offices, vital for AHC RO/ARO matching questions.

  • Diwan-i-Wizarat: The department of finance headed by the Wazir, responsible for revenue collection, state expenditure, and financial management.
  • Diwan-i-Arz: The military department established by Balban, headed by the Ariz-i-Mumalik, responsible for maintaining troop rolls and inspections.
  • Diwan-i-Riyasat: Market control department instituted by Ala-ud-din Khilji to regulate prices of essential commodities and oversee merchants.
  • Iqta System: A revenue-assignment system where territorial units (Iqtas) were granted to nobles (Iqtadars or Muqtis) in lieu of cash salaries.
  • Diwan-i-Kohi: Department of agriculture created by Muhammad bin Tughlaq to bring wasteland under cultivation through state-sponsored loans (Taqavi).

Economy, Land Revenue & Agrarian Measures

Tax structures, monetary reforms, and agrarian policies enforced by various Sultans.

  • Kharaj: Land tax levied on non-Muslim peasants, usually fixed at one-third to one-half of the produce.
  • Jizya: Religious tax levied on non-Muslim subjects (Zimmis) in exchange for protection and freedom of worship; Firoz Shah Tughlaq levied it on Brahmins too.
  • Tanka and Jital: Silver Tanka and copper Jital coins introduced by Iltutmish, establishing a standard currency system for the Delhi Sultanate.
  • Measurement of Land: Ala-ud-din Khilji was the first Sultan to measure cultivated land (Biswa as standard unit) and demand revenue directly from cultivators (Kharis).

Architecture & Cultural Developments

Indo-Islamic architecture evolution, literary milestones, and patronage of learning.

  • Qutb Minar: Started by Qutb-ud-din Aibak in honor of Sufi saint Qutb-ud-din Bakhtiyar Kaki; completed by Iltutmish; top storeys repaired by Firoz Shah Tughlaq.
  • Alai Darwaza: Built by Ala-ud-din Khilji at the Qutb complex; considered a jewel of Islamic architecture in India, featuring true arches and red sandstone.
  • Tughlaq Architecture Style: Characterized by sloping walls (batter), plain grey stone, and absence of decorative excess, reflecting austerity (e.g., Tughlaqabad Fort).
  • Amir Khusrau: Known as 'Parrot of India' (Tuti-i-Hind); adorned the courts of seven Sultans; pioneer of Khayal style and creator of Urdu/Hindi/Persian mixed verse.
