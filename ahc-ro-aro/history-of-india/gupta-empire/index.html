Gupta Empire Core Study Notes

The Gupta Empire represents the 'Golden Age of India' in arts, science, literature, and trade, making it a high-yielding topic for Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations. Questions frequently target administrative terminologies, architectural milestones, Sanskrit literary figures, and coin types from this era.

Political History & Rulers

Detailed chronology and achievements of the prominent Gupta emperors.

  • Sri Gupta & Ghatotkacha: Founders of the dynasty holding the title 'Maharaja'. Mentioned in the Poona copper plate of Prabhavatigupta as Adhiraja.
  • Chandragupta I (319–335 CE): First independent ruler to assume 'Maharajadhiraja'. Strengthened status by marrying Lichchhavi princess Kumaradevi.
  • Samudragupta (335–380 CE): Aerosmith of India (Vincent Smith). His military campaigns are detailed in the Allahabad Pillar Inscription (Prayag Prashasti) composed by Harishena.
  • Chandragupta II / Vikramaditya (380–415 CE): Extended empire via matrimonial alliance (dhvaja marriage with Nagas) and conquest of Western Kshatrapas, issuing silver coins.
  • Kumaragupta I & Skandagupta: Kumaragupta I (Shakraditya) founded Nalanda Mahavihara. Skandagupta successfully repelled the first Huna invasion, as recorded in the Bhitari Pillar Inscription.

Administration and Economy

Decentralized polity, taxation structures, and commercial systems of the Gupta state.

  • Administrative Hierarchy: Empire (Bhukti) managed by Uparika, divided into districts (Vishaya) under Vishayapati, further subdivided into Villages (Gramas) led by Gramika.
  • Land Classifications: Kshetra (cultivable), Khila (wastable), Aprahata (forest/jungle), Vasti (habitable), and Gapata (pasture lands).
  • Economic Currency: Dinaras: Issued gold coins known as 'Dinaras'. Although initially high in purity, later Gupta coins showed a decline in gold content, indicating economic distress.
  • Agrarian Economy & Feudalism: Increase in land grants (Agrahara to Brahmins) gave rise to intermediaries, laying early foundations of Indian feudalism (Samanta system).
  • Trade and Guilds (Shrenis): Guilds maintained autonomous judicial and executive powers. Ports like Tamralipti in the east and Bharuch in the west facilitated flourishing maritime trade.

Society, Religion, and Culture

Social stratification, resurgence of Brahmanical Hinduism, and temple architecture.

  • Resurgence of Bhagavatism: Vaishnavism became the state religion; Garuda was adopted as the imperial insignia. Idol worship and Puranic Hinduism became widespread.
  • Position of Women: Women lost rights to property inheritance (except Stridhana). The earliest epigraphical evidence of Sati is found at Eran Inscription (510 CE) of Bhanugupta.
  • Nagara Style Temple Architecture: Emergence of stone temples; Dashavatara Temple at Deogarh (Jhansi, UP) and Bhitargaon Brick Temple (Kanpur, UP) are prime examples.
  • Buddhist and Jain Art: Ajanta Caves (Maharashtra) Phase 19, 16, and 17 paintings and Sarnath Buddha statues reflect unsurpassed mastery of Gupta plastic art.

Science, Technology, and Literature

Classical Sanskrit literary explosion and breakthroughs in mathematics and astronomy.

  • Aryabhata: Authored 'Aryabhatiya' and 'Surya Siddhanta'. Proved that the Earth rotates on its axis, calculated pi value (3.1416), and propounded the concept of zero.
  • Varahamihira: Polymath who wrote 'Brihat Samhita', 'Panchasiddhantika', and 'Brihat Jataka', contributing immensely to astronomy, astrology, and botany.
  • Kalidasa: Jewel of Chandragupta II's court. Masterpieces include 'Abhijñānaśākuntalam', 'Meghaduta', 'Malavikagnimitram', 'Raghuvamsha', and 'Kumarasambhava'.
  • Medicine: Sushruta & Vagbhata: Advancements in Ayurveda and surgical instruments. Vagbhata compiled 'Ashtanga Hridaya', an essential medical text.
  • Amarakosha & Panchatantra: Amarasimha composed 'Amarakosha' (Sanskrit lexicon/thesaurus). Vishnu Sharma authored the fables of 'Panchatantra'.
