Early Medieval India Core Study Notes

The Early Medieval India period (approx. 750–1200 CE) marks the transition from the classical age to the Sultanate era, characterized by regional kingdoms, feudalism, and vibrant temple architecture. This topic is vital for AHC RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations as questions frequently target dynastic origins, literary figures, architectural styles, and socio-economic shifts.

Northern and Eastern Kingdoms (Palas and Pratiharas)

The Palas dominated eastern India while the Gurjara-Pratiharas held sway over western India and parts of the upper Gangetic plains.

  • Founder of Pala Dynasty: Gopala founded the Pala dynasty around 750 CE, chosen by the prominent men of Bengal to end the anarchy (Matsyanyaya).
  • Patronage of Buddhism by Dharmapala: Dharmapala revived Buddhism and founded the famous Vikramshila University and Odantapuri University.
  • Gurjara-Pratihara Power: Nagabhatta I was the real founder of the Pratihara dynasty, known for successfully resisting Arab invasions from Sind.
  • Mihira Bhoja: The greatest ruler of the Pratihara dynasty, who adopted the title 'Adivaraha' and patronized the great poet Rajashekhara.

The Deccan and Southern Powers (Rashtrakutas)

The Rashtrakutas played a pivotal role in peninsular politics and architectural marvels, bridging North and South India.

  • Dantidurga and Establishment: Dantidurga overthrew the Chalukyas of Badami in 753 CE and performed the Hiranyagarbha sacrifice at Ujjain.
  • Amoghavarsha I: One of the greatest Rashtrakuta kings, who ruled for over 64 years, wrote 'Kavirajamarga' (Kannada), and shifted capital to Manyakheta.
  • Ellora Kailash Temple: Built during the reign of Krishna I, this monolithic rock-cut wonder is a masterpiece of Indian rock-cut architecture.
  • Arab Accounts: Arab traveler Al-Masudi visited the Rashtrakuta empire and praised their monarchs as among the greatest kings of the world (Balhara).

The Rajput States and Central India

A cluster of fierce warrior clans rose to prominence in northern and western India, known for their chivalry, clan loyalty, and magnificent temple building.

  • Chandelas of Jejakabhukti: Famous for constructing the world-renowned Nagara-style temples of Khajuraho (e.g., Kandariya Mahadeva Temple built by Dhanga).
  • Chauhans of Ajmer / Sakambari: Vigraharaja IV built the Saraswati Mandir (later converted to Adhai Din Ka Jhonpra). Prithviraj III (Prithviraj Chauhan) was the prominent hero of Tarain battles.
  • Paramaras of Malwa: Bhoja Paramara was a polymath ruler, warrior, and patron of arts, credited with writing works on grammar, astronomy, architecture, and medicine.
  • Solankis / Chalukyas of Gujarat: Constructed the famous Sun Temple at Modhera and the Dilwara Jain Temples at Mount Abu during the 11th-12th centuries.

Society, Economy, and Feudalism (Samantavada)

Early medieval society experienced profound decentralization of power, rise of regional languages, and the consolidation of the Jati system.

  • Rise of Feudalism (Samantas): Land grants (Agraharas and secular land grants) to officials and Brahmins created a class of intermediaries with judicial and military powers.
  • Decline of Trade and Urbanization: Decline in long-distance maritime trade and Roman coinage led to a localized agrarian economy, though guild activities revived somewhat by the 11th century.
  • Proliferation of Castes (Jatis): Assimilation of various tribal groups, foreign immigrants, and occupational guilds into the Hindu varna framework as sub-castes (jatis).
  • Literary and Cultural Growth: Regional languages like Apabhramsha, early Bengali, Gujarati, and Marathi began to develop alongside classical Sanskrit works like Kalhana's Rajatarangini.
