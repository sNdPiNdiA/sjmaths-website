Mughal Empire Core Study Notes

The Mughal Empire represents a defining epoch in medieval Indian history, characterized by centralized administration, architectural brilliance, and sweeping economic reforms. For Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations, questions frequently focus on administrative terminologies, revenue systems (like Zabti and Dahsala), religious policies, and architectural chronology in Uttar Pradesh.

Political History and Military Campaigns

The military strategies and political alliances that shaped the boundaries and stability of the Mughal Empire.

  • First Battle of Panipat (1526): Fought on April 21, 1526, between Babur and Ibrahim Lodi. Babur used the Tulgama warfare tactic and heavy artillery.
  • Battle of Khanwa (1527): Fought between Babur and Rana Sanga of Mewar. Babur declared it a 'Jihad' and assumed the title of 'Ghazi' after victory.
  • Second Battle of Panipat (1556): Fought between Bairam Khan (representing young Akbar) and Hemu (Hem Chandra Vikramaditya), securing Mughal victory over Delhi.
  • Battle of Haldighati (1576): Fought between Mughal forces led by Raja Man Singh of Amber and Maharana Pratap of Mewar. Mewar forces faced a severe tactical defeat.
  • Deccan Campaigns of Aurangzeb: Aurangzeb personally moved to the Deccan in 1681, annexing Bijapur (1686) and Golconda (1687), which overextended imperial resources.

Administration, Revenue, and Mansabdari System

The intricate administrative framework, military-civil hierarchy, and land revenue mechanisms established primarily under Akbar.

  • Mansabdari System: Introduced by Akbar in 1595, it graded officials based on Zat (personal rank/salary) and Sawar (number of horsemen to maintain).
  • Dahsala System / Zabti System: Formulated by Raja Todar Mal in 1580, it calculated average crop yields and prices over the preceding 10 years for tax assessment.
  • Provincial Administration (Subas): Empire was divided into Subas (provinces, numbering 15 under Akbar, rising to 21 under Aurangzeb), headed by a Subedar (Governor) and Diwan (Revenue head).
  • Local Administration Units: Subas were subdivided into Sarkars (districts, headed by Faujdar and Amalguzar), which were further divided into Parganas/Mahals and villages (Gram).
  • Land Classifications: Land was categorized as Polaj (constantly cultivated), Parauti (left fallow temporarily), Chachar (fallow for 3-4 years), and Banjar (uncultivated for 5+ years).

Religious Policies and Social Reforms

The evolution of religious tolerance, philosophical debates, and social legislations from Akbar to Aurangzeb.

  • Abolition of Jizya and Pilgrimage Tax: Akbar abolished the pilgrimage tax in 1563 and Jizya (tax on non-Muslims) in 1564, fostering a pluralistic society (Sulh-i-Kul).
  • Ibadat Khana and Din-i Ilahi: Akbar built Ibadat Khana at Fatehpur Sikri in 1575 for inter-faith discussions; in 1582, he propounded Din-i Ilahi (Tawhid-i-Ilahi), an elite spiritual order.
  • Social Reforms by Akbar: Akbar discouraged child marriage, legalized widow remarriage, prohibited sati, and raised the age of marriage for boys (16) and girls (14).
  • Religious Reversal under Aurangzeb: Aurangzeb reimposed Jizya in 1679, banned music at court, discontinued Jharokha Darshan and weighing of emperors (Tuladan), and executed Guru Tegh Bahadur in 1675.

Art, Architecture, and Literature

The synthesis of Persian, Central Asian, and Indian styles that culminated in unmatched architectural and literary heritage.

  • Architectural Marvels of Akbar: Agra Fort, Fatehpur Sikri (Buland Darwaza, Salim Chishti's Tomb, Panch Mahal), and Humayun's Tomb in Delhi (first garden-tomb with double dome).
  • Shah Jahan: The Golden Age of Architecture: Extensive use of white marble and Pietra Dura inlay work seen in the Taj Mahal, Red Fort (Delhi), Jama Masjid, and Lahore Fort structures.
  • Akbar's Translation Bureau (Maktab Khana): Sanskrit texts like Mahabharata and Ramayana were translated into Persian as 'Razmnama' and 'Ramayana', spearheaded by scholars like Badauni and Abul Fazl.
  • Navaratnas (Nine Gems) of Akbar: Included Birbal (wit), Abul Fazl (historian), Faizi (poet), Tansen (musician), Raja Todar Mal (finance), Raja Man Singh, Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khana, Fakir Aziao-din, and Mullah Do-Piyaza.
