Mindmap: Pre-Historic India

1. Introduction to Pre-History

The Pre-historic period refers to the time before the invention of writing systems. Since there are no written records, our entire understanding of this era relies heavily on archaeological excavations. The pre-history of India is generally divided into the Stone Age (Paleolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic) and the Metal Ages (Chalcolithic, Iron Age).

  • Robert Bruce Foote is considered the Father of Indian Prehistory. In 1863, he discovered the first Paleolithic tool in India (a handaxe) at Pallavaram near Chennai.
  • Sir Mortimer Wheeler and H.D. Sankalia made immense contributions to mapping the prehistoric cultures of India.

2. Sources of Information for Pre-History

Since literary sources do not exist for the prehistoric period, historians rely on material remains. These sources can be broadly classified into:

A. Archaeological Sources (Lithics & Tools)

Acheulean Handaxe (Lower Paleolithic)

Stone tools are the most abundant and crucial sources. By analyzing the shape, size, and technique of stone tools, archaeologists track the cognitive and technological evolution of early humans.

  • Core Tools & Flake Tools: Used in Lower and Middle Paleolithic eras (e.g., handaxes, cleavers, choppers).
  • Microliths: Tiny, sharp stone tools characterizing the Mesolithic period, indicating a shift towards hunting smaller, faster prey.
  • Polished Stone Tools: Indicate the Neolithic era, reflecting settled agricultural life and woodworking.

B. Fossils and Biological Remains

Fossils of humans, flora, and fauna help reconstruct the environment, diet, and evolutionary stage of humans.

  • The most famous discovery is the Narmada Human (a skull cap of Homo erectus) found at Hathnora (Madhya Pradesh) by Arun Sonakia in 1982. This is the earliest hominid fossil found in the Indian subcontinent.

C. Rock Art & Cave Paintings

Bhimbetka Cave Paintings (Madhya Pradesh)

Prehistoric art serves as a window into the socio-cultural, spiritual, and daily lives of early humans. It provides details on hunting techniques, animal species, gender roles, and community life.

  • Bhimbetka Caves (Madhya Pradesh): Discovered by V.S. Wakankar in 1957. It features paintings spanning from the Upper Paleolithic to the historical period. Most paintings belong to the Mesolithic age, depicting hunting scenes, dancing, and animal figures.
Trick to Remember Bhimbetka Discoverer:
Wakankar sounds like "Walk & Car". He walked and drove his car to discover the Bhimbetka caves in 1957.

D. Burials and Megaliths

Megalithic Burial Structure

Burial practices reflect spiritual beliefs and the concept of life after death.

  • Burzahom (Kashmir): Evidence of dogs buried along with their masters in pit dwellings.
  • Megaliths: Large stone structures marking burial sites, primarily associated with the Iron Age in South India, but originating earlier in some regions.
AHC RO/ARO Focus: Questions frequently target specific discoveries. Remember Pallavaram (first handaxe), Hathnora (first human fossil), and Bhimbetka (cave paintings).

3. Quick Comparison: Stone Age Phases

Period Key Tools Lifestyle & Economy
Paleolithic Handaxes, Cleavers, Choppers (Quartzite stone) Nomadic Hunters & Food Gatherers. Use of Fire discovered (late phase).
Mesolithic Microliths (tiny stone tools < 5cm) Hunting smaller prey. Earliest Domestication of Animals (Dogs, Sheep).
Neolithic Polished Stone Tools, Bone Tools Agriculture, Settled Life, Pottery invention, Wheel.

4. Timeline of Pre-Historic Phases

A. The Paleolithic Age (Old Stone Age)
2 Million BC – 10,000 BC

Developed entirely during the Pleistocene (Ice Age). People were nomadic hunter-gatherers. They used unpolished, undressed rough stones (mainly quartzite, hence called 'Quartzite Men').

Sub-Classifications:
  • Lower Paleolithic: Handaxes, Cleavers. Sites: Soan Valley, Belan Valley (UP), Bori (Maharashtra - earliest evidence of human presence).
  • Middle Paleolithic: Flake tools (scrapers, borers). Sites: Nevasa (MH), Didwana (RJ).
  • Upper Paleolithic: Blades and Bone tools. Emergence of Homo sapiens. Sites: Kurnool Caves (AP - traces of ash/fire found).
B. The Mesolithic Age (Middle Stone Age)
10,000 BC – 6,000 BC

A transitional phase coinciding with the onset of the Holocene era (warmer climate). Humans shifted from large game hunting to hunting smaller animals, fishing, and fowling.

  • Key Feature: Use of Microliths (tiny stone tools like points, crescents, triangles).
  • Domestication of Animals: Earliest evidence from Bagor (Rajasthan) and Adamgarh (Madhya Pradesh).
C. The Neolithic Age (New Stone Age)
6,000 BC – 1,000 BC

Often termed the "Neolithic Revolution" by V. Gordon Childe due to massive socio-economic shifts like advent of agriculture and permanent settlements.

  • Mehrgarh (Balochistan): The earliest known agricultural settlement (wheat, barley, cotton) dating back to ~7000 BC.
  • Chirand (Bihar): Massive collection of bone tools made entirely of deer antlers.
  • Piklihal & Utnur (South India): Known for "Ash Mounds" indicating large-scale cattle penning.
D. The Chalcolithic Age (Copper-Stone Age)
3,000 BC – 500 BC

The first phase where metal (Copper) was used alongside stone tools. They were primarily rural farming communities.

  • Key Features: Black and Red Ware (BRW) pottery. Did not know the use of burnt bricks. High infant mortality evident from urn burials.
  • Ahar Culture (Rajasthan): Also known as Tambavati (place of copper).
  • Jorwe Culture (Maharashtra): Daimabad is the largest Jorwe site, famous for bronze goods (rhino, elephant, chariot).
