Historical Monuments and Tourism in Uttar Pradesh Core Study Notes

Uttar Pradesh holds immense significance for the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations due to its rich repository of ancient, medieval, and modern architectural heritage. Questions frequently test specific UNESCO World Heritage sites, architectural styles, district locations, and state tourism policies.

UNESCO World Heritage Sites in Uttar Pradesh

Uttar Pradesh hosts globally acclaimed heritage monuments inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list, acting as anchors for international tourism.

  • Taj Mahal (Agra): Built by Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan in memory of Mumtaz Mahal; inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983. Masterpiece of white marble pietra dura architecture designed by Ustad Ahmad Lahori.
  • Agra Fort (Agra): Constructed primarily by Emperor Akbar in red sandstone starting 1565; inscribed in 1983. Houses notable structures like Jahangiri Mahal, Khas Mahal, and Sheesh Mahal.
  • Fatehpur Sikri (Agra): Found as the capital of the Mughal Empire by Akbar in 1571; inscribed in 1986. Key attractions include Buland Darwaza, Salim Chishti's Tomb, Diwan-i-Aam, and Panch Mahal.

Buddhist and Spiritual Tourism Circuits

UP contains the core sites associated with Gautama Buddha's life, forming the international Buddhist Circuit.

  • Sarnath (Varanasi): The site where Buddha delivered his first sermon (Dharmachakra Pravartana) to his five disciples. Houses the Dhamek Stupa and the national emblem Ashoka Pillar capital.
  • Kushinagar: The place of Mahaparinirvana (death) of Gautama Buddha. Features the Mahaparinirvana Temple with a reclining Buddha statue and Rambhar Stupa.
  • Kapilvastu & Piprahwa (Siddharthnagar): Associated with the childhood and early life of Prince Siddhartha before renouncing worldly life.
  • Shravasti: Where Buddha spent maximum rainy seasons (Vasavasa) and performed miracles. Contains Jetavana monastery and Angulimala Stupa.

Awadh Architecture and Nawabi Monuments of Lucknow

Lucknow showcases the unique syncretic Indo-Persian Awadh architectural style marked by grand arches, asymmetrical motifs, and subterranean structures.

  • Bara Imambara (Lucknow): Built by Nawab Asaf-ud-Daula in 1784. Famous for its magnificent central hall unsupported by any beams and the enigmatic labyrinth known as Bhulbhulaiya.
  • Rumi Darwaza: A 60-foot tall imposing gateway built by Nawab Asaf-ud-Daula in 1784, inspired by the Sublime Porte of Istanbul. Serves as the iconic symbol of Lucknow.
  • Chota Imambara (Hussainabad Imambara): Built by Nawab Muhammad Ali Shah in 1838. Known as the 'Palace of Lights' due to elaborate chandeliers used during Muharram; houses the tombs of the Nawab and his mother.
  • The Residency (Lucknow): A group of buildings that served as the residence of the British Resident General during the 1857 Revolt, bearing witness to the historic Siege of Lucknow.

Medieval Islamic and Sharqi Monuments

Cities like Jaunpur and Agra boast exceptional Sultanate and Mughal period mosques, forts, and mausoleums.

  • Atala Masjid (Jaunpur): Commissioned by Ibrahim Shah Sharqi in 1408. Exemplifies the distinct Sharqi architectural style with towering pylons masking the dome.
  • जामा मस्जिद और शाही पुल (जौनपुर): Built during the reign of Hussain Shah Sharqi around 1478. The Shahi Bridge over the Gomti River was constructed during Mughal rule under Munim Khan.
  • Akbar's Tomb (Sikandra, Agra): Designed by Akbar himself and completed by his son Jahangir in 1613. Combines Hindu and Islamic architectural motifs without a traditional dome.
  • Itimad-ud-Daulah's Tomb (Agra): Commissioned by Nur Jahan for her father Mirza Ghiyas Beg; built between 1622 and 1628. Widely regarded as the first structure in India made entirely of white marble and using extensive pietra dura.
