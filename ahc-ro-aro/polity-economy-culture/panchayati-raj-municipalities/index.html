Panchayati Raj & Municipalities Core Study Notes

Panchayati Raj and Municipalities form the bedrock of grassroots democracy in India, established constitutionally via the 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts of 1992. For Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations, questions frequently test Articles (243 to 243-ZG), schedules (11th and 12th), financial devolution, Uttar Pradesh-specific amendments, and reservation provisions.

73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 (Panchayats)

This landmark act institutionalized rural local bodies, adding Part IX (Articles 243 to 243-O) and the 11th Schedule.

  • Article 243B - Three-Tier System: Mandates a three-tier system of Panchayats at the village, intermediate, and district levels for all states having a population exceeding 20 lakhs.
  • Article 243C - Composition: All seats in a Panchayat shall be filled by persons chosen by direct election from territorial constituencies in the Panchayat area.
  • Article 243D - Reservation of Seats: Reservation of seats for SCs and STs in proportion to their population. Not less than one-third of total seats reserved for women, applicable to offices of chairpersons as well.
  • Article 243E - Duration of Panchayats: Every Panchayat continues for 5 years from the date appointed for its first meeting. Elections must be held before the expiry of the 5-year term or within 6 months of dissolution.
  • Eleventh Schedule Subjects: Contains 21 functional items placed within the purview of Panchayats, ranging from agriculture and land improvement to drinking water and rural electrification.

74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 (Municipalities)

Established urban local governance by adding Part IX-A and the Twelfth Schedule to the Indian Constitution.

  • Article 243Q - Types of Municipalities: Provides for three types of municipalities: Nagar Panchayat (transition area), Municipal Council (small urban area), and Municipal Corporation (large urban area).
  • Article 243R - Composition: All seats in a municipality shall be filled by persons chosen by direct election from territorial constituencies (wards). State legislatures may provide representation to MPs, MLAs, and experts without voting rights.
  • Article 243S - Wards Committees: Constitution of Wards Committees consisting of one or more wards, within the territorial area of a municipality having a population of 3 lakhs or more.
  • Article 243ZE - Committee for District Planning: Every state shall constitute at district level a District Planning Committee (DPC) to consolidate plans prepared by panchayats and municipalities.
  • Twelfth Schedule Subjects: Contains 18 functional items including urban planning, regulation of land-use, fire services, public health, and economic/social development.

Financial Administration & State Election Commission

Robust financial autonomy and independent election machinery are foundational to the successful functioning of local bodies.

  • Article 243I - State Finance Commission: Constituted by the Governor every 5 years to review the financial position of panchayats and recommend principles for tax distribution and grants-in-aid.
  • Article 243Y - Finance Commission for Municipalities: The Finance Commission constituted under Article 243I also reviews the financial position of municipalities and makes recommendations to the Governor.
  • Article 243K - State Election Commission: Superintendence, direction, and control of the preparation of electoral rolls and the conduct of all elections to the Panchayats vest in a State Election Commissioner appointed by the Governor.
  • Article 243ZA - Elections to Municipalities: The superintendence, direction, and control of the preparation of electoral rolls and conduct of all municipal elections are vested in the State Election Commission.

Important Committees on Panchayati Raj

Various committees were appointed post-independence to strengthen local self-government structures.

  • Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957): Recommended democratic decentralization resulting in the 3-tier Panchayati Raj system. Zilla Parishad should be the advisory body, Panchayat Samiti the executive body.
  • Ashok Mehta Committee (1977-78): Recommended 2-tier system (Zilla Parishad at district level, Mandal Panchayat comprising a group of villages). Suggested constitutional recognition and open political party participation.
  • G.V.K. Rao Committee (1985): Appointed by Planning Commission; concluded that 'developmenter bureaucracy' had 'grassrooted' local bodies, termed them as 'grass without roots'. Recommended regular elections.
  • L.M. Singhvi Committee (1986): Recommended constitutional status for Panchayati Raj institutions and creation of Nyaya Panchayats for clusters of villages.
