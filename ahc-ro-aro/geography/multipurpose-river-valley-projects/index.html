Multipurpose River Valley Projects Core Study Notes

Multipurpose River Valley Projects are termed 'Temples of Modern India' by Pt. Jawaharlal Nehru, integrating flood control, irrigation, hydro-power generation, and navigation. For the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations, questions frequently focus on river basins, dam locations, state jurisdictions, reservoir names, and interstate water disputes.

Major River Valley Projects of Northern & Eastern India

Himalayan and eastern river systems possess immense hydro-electric and irrigation potential, powering the agricultural heartland of the Indo-Gangetic plain.

  • Bhakra Nangal Project: Built across the Sutlej River, it is India's highest concrete gravity dam (226m). Its reservoir is named Govind Sagar Lake, located in Himachal Pradesh and Punjab.
  • Damodar Valley Corporation (DVC): India's first multipurpose river valley project established on July 7, 1948. Comprises dams at Tilaiya, Konar, Maithon, and Panchet Hill across the Damodar river basin.
  • Tehri Dam Project: Constructed on the Bhagirathi River in Uttarakhand, it is India's highest earth-fill and rock-fill dam (260.5m). It is executed in collaboration with Russia.
  • Kosi Project: An international treaty project between India and Nepal executed on the Kosi River (Sorrow of Bihar), featuring the Hanumannagar Barrage for flood control and irrigation.
  • Rihand Project (UP Special): Located in Sonbhadra district, Uttar Pradesh on the Rihand River (tributary of Son). Its reservoir is Govind Ballabh Pant Sagar, India's largest artificial lake.

Major River Valley Projects of Peninsular & Western India

Peninsular rivers rely heavily on seasonal monsoonal flows, making massive storage reservoirs vital for drought mitigation in Maharashtra, Gujarat, Karnataka, and Andhra Pradesh.

  • Narmada Valley Project (Sardar Sarovar): Built on the Narmada River in Gujarat, benefiting Gujarat, MP, Maharashtra, and Rajasthan. Associated with the Statue of Unity and Narmada Bachao Andolan.
  • Nagarjuna Sagar Project: Constructed across the Krishna River on the border of Telangana and Andhra Pradesh. It is world's largest masonry dam with an extensive canal network.
  • Hirakud Dam Project: Built across the Mahanadi River in Odisha, it is one of the world's longest mainstream dams with a total length of 25.8 km including dykes.
  • Tungabhadra Project: A joint venture between Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh on the Tungabhadra River, a major tributary of the Krishna River, providing robust irrigation.
  • Koyna Hydroelectric Project: Located in Maharashtra on the Koyna River (tributary of Krishna), it is the largest completed hydroelectric power plant in India, known as the 'Lifeline of Maharashtra'.

Interlinking of Rivers (ILR) & National Water Grid

The Interlinking of Rivers project aims to transfer surplus water from flood-prone river basins to water-deficit and drought-prone regions through canal networks and reservoirs.

  • Ken-Betwa Link Project (KBLP): India's first interlinking project under the National Perspective Plan, transferring water from the Ken River to the Betwa River to irrigate the drought-prone Bundelkhand region (UP & MP).
  • National Water Development Agency (NWDA): Established in 1982 under the Ministry of Jal Shakti to carry out studies and feasibility reports for the interlinking of Himalayan and Peninsular rivers.
  • Himalayan Component: Envisages construction of storage reservoirs on the Kosi, Gandak, Ganga, and Brahmaputra rivers to transfer surplus flows to western UP, Haryana, and Rajasthan.
  • Peninsular Component: Connects Mahanadi and Godavari to Krishna, Pennar, and Cauveri rivers, featuring major links like Godavari-Krishna and Cauveri-Vellar-Vaigai.

Interstate Water Disputes & Institutional Framework

Constitutional provisions and tribunals regulate the equitable distribution and dispute resolution of interstate river waters in federal India.

  • Article 262 of the Constitution: Empowers Parliament to enact laws providing for the adjudication of any dispute or complaint with respect to the use, distribution, or control of interstate river waters.
  • Inter-State Water Disputes Act, 1956: Enacted by Parliament to establish ad-hoc tribunals for adjudicating water disputes when states fail to reach a negotiated settlement.
  • Key River Water Tribunals: Major tribunals include Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal, Krishna Water Disputes Tribunal, and Mahanadi Water Disputes Tribunal.
  • Inter-State Council (Article 263): Presidential body established to investigate and discuss subjects of common interest between the Union and States, including water resource policies.
