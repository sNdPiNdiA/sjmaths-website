Fossil Fuels (Coal, Petroleum, and Natural Gas)

Fossil fuels remain the backbone of India's baseload energy supply, heavily concentrated in the Gondwana and Tertiary rock systems.

  • Gondwana Coal Reserves: Accounts for 98% of India's total coal reserves and 99% of total coal production. Major river valleys include Damodar, Mahanadi, Son, and Godavari.
  • Tertiary Coal Deposits: Found in the northeastern states (Assam, Meghalaya, Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland) and Jammu & Kashmir; characterized by high sulfur content and low ash.
  • Petroleum & Crude Oil Basins: India has 26 sedimentary basins covering 3.14 million sq km. Major producing regions include Mumbai High, Cambay Basin (Gujarat), and Upper Assam Basin.
  • Natural Gas & GAIL: Major offshore reserves found in Krishna-Godavari (KG) Basin (Reliance D6 fields) and Mumbai Offshore. GAIL (India) Limited manages the HVJ (Hazira-Vijaipur-Jagdishpur) pipeline.
  • UP Energy Profile (Coal & Thermal): Uttar Pradesh has major NTPC thermal power plants located at Singrauli (Sonbhadra), Rihand, Dadri, and Unchahar, relying on coal transported from central/eastern coalfields.

Solar Energy Resources

Situated in the tropical and subtropical belt, India receives abundant solar radiation averaging 4-7 kWh/sq.m per day.

  • National Solar Mission Targets: Target scaled up to 280 GW of solar power by 2030 as part of India's commitment to 500 GW non-fossil fuel electricity capacity.
  • Bhadla Solar Park (Rajasthan): Located in Jodhpur district, Rajasthan, Bhadla is the world's largest ultra-mega solar park spread over 14,000 acres with a capacity of over 2,245 MW.
  • UP Solar Energy Policy: Uttar Pradesh New and Renewable Energy Development Agency (UPNEDA) spearheads the state's target to achieve 22,000 MW of solar power through solar parks and rooftop installations.
  • PM-KUSUM Scheme: Pradhan Mantri Kisan Urja Suraksha evam Utthaan Mahabhiyan aims to add solar capacity of 30,800 MW by installing standalone solar pumps and grid-connected solar power plants.
  • International Solar Alliance (ISA): Jointly launched by India and France at COP21 in Paris (2015). Headquartered at National Institute of Solar Energy (NISE) Gwalpahari, Gurugram.

Wind Energy Resources

India possesses a vast coastline and plateau margins providing high wind power potential, ranking 4th globally in wind power capacity.

  • Total Wind Potential at 150m: National Institute of Wind Energy (NIWE) estimates India's gross wind power potential at 695 GW at 150 meters above ground level.
  • Leading States in Wind Generation: Gujarat and Tamil Nadu lead in installed wind capacity, followed by Karnataka, Rajasthan, and Andhra Pradesh due to favorable coastal and wind-gap topography.
  • Offshore Wind Energy Policy: Notified in 2015 to harness wind energy potential in the territorial waters of India, particularly along the Gujarat and Tamil Nadu coasts.
  • National Institute of Wind Energy: NIWE is an autonomous research institution under the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) located in Chennai, Tamil Nadu.

Atomic and Hydro Energy

Nuclear and hydroelectric power complement the baseload grid requirements while reducing greenhouse gas emissions.

  • Nuclear Power Plants & Uranium: Key operational plants include Kudankulam (TN), Tarapur (Maharashtra), Kakrapar (Gujarat), and Narora (Bulandshahr, UP). Uranium mines located in Jaduguda (Jharkhand).
  • Three-Stage Nuclear Power Programme: Conceived by Homi Bhabha: Stage I (PHWR using Natural Uranium), Stage II (FBR using Plutonium-239), Stage III (Thorium-Uranium-233 cycle).
  • Hydroelectric Potential: Classified as renewable up to 25 MW scale. Major hydropower projects located in Himalayan river basins (Tehri Dam on Bhagirathi, Bhakra Nangal on Sutlej).
