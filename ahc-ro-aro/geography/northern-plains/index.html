Northern Plains of India Core Study Notes

The Northern Plains of India represent one of the world's most extensive continuous alluvial tracts, formed by the interplay of three major river systems: the Indus, the Ganga, and the Brahmaputra. Spanning across northern and eastern India, this geomorphic unit is vital for Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations due to its high agricultural productivity, dense population, and distinct physiographic subdivisions (Bhabhar, Terai, Bhangar, and Khadar).

Geological Formation and Extent

Understanding the boundaries, dimensions, and underlying basement rock structure of the Indo-Gangetic trough.

  • Geosynclinal Depression: The Northern Plain is a structural depression formed as a fore-runner of the Himalayan mountain-building process, later filled by alluvium.
  • Geographical Dimensions: The plains stretch approximately 3,200 km from the Indus delta in the west to the Brahmaputra valley in the east, with a width varying between 150 to 300 km.
  • Alluvial Depth: Borehole data reveals that the depth of alluvium varies from 1,000 to 2,,000 meters, being deepest near the northern boundary close to the Siwaliks.
  • Delhi Ridge: The Delhi Ridge acts as a water divide separating the Indus river system basin from the Ganga river system basin within the plains.

Regional Divisions of the Northern Plains

Classification of the plains from west to east based on drainage basins and regional topography.

  • Punjab-Haryana Plains: Formed by the Indus and its tributaries (Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej); characterized by 'Doabs' (inter-river zones) like Bist Doab and Bari Doab.
  • Rajasthan Plains: Occupied by the Thar Desert; represents an aggraded plain formed by the recession of the sea in Mesozoic era; drained by the ephemeral Luni river.
  • Ganga Plains: Extends from the Yamuna river in the west to the Bangladesh border in the east; subdivided into Upper, Middle, and Lower Ganga plains across UP, Bihar, and West Bengal.
  • Brahmaputra Plains: Known as the Assam Valley; forms an aggradational plain oriented east-west, characterized by river islands like Majuli and braided river channels.

Agricultural, Economic, and Social Significance

The pivotal role played by the Northern Plains in India's food security, demographics, and economic development.

  • Granary of India: Rich alluvial soil, abundant groundwater, and extensive canal networks make it the highest producer of wheat, rice, sugarcane, and pulses.
  • Demographic Density: Flat topography, fertile land, and perennial water supply support nearly 40% of India's population, with states like UP and Bihar having very high population densities.
  • Inland Waterways and Transport: Gentle gradients facilitate dense road and rail networks, and National Waterway 1 (Allahabad to Haldia on Ganga) utilizes its river systems.
  • Cultural Cradle: The plains have been the cradle of major Indian civilizations, Vedic culture, and historical empires, centered around sacred rivers like Ganga and Yamuna.
