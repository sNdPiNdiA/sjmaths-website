Socio Economic Issues Poverty Unemployment Core Study Notes

Poverty and unemployment represent core structural impediments to India's inclusive growth. For Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC aspirants, mastering this topic requires an integration of constitutional mandates, multidimensional indices, welfare schemes, and Uttar Pradesh-specific demographic challenges.

Constitutional Framework and Judicial Mandates

The Indian Constitution implicitly and explicitly mandates the eradication of poverty and the provisioning of right to work through directive principles and fundamental rights.

  • Article 21 (Right to Life): The Supreme Court in landmark rulings (e.g., Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation) held that 'Right to Life' includes the right to livelihood, making extreme poverty a direct violation of constitutional guarantees.
  • Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP): Article 39(a) and 39(b) direct the State to secure adequate means of livelihood for all citizens and ensure distribution of material resources to subserve the common good.
  • Article 41 (Right to Work): Directs the State, within the limits of its economic capacity and development, to make effective provision for securing the right to work, to education, and to public assistance in cases of unemployment and unmerited want.
  • Local Governance and Poverty Alleviation: Eleventh Schedule (Article 243G) empowers Panchayati Raj Institutions with 29 functional items, prominently including poverty alleviation programmes and rural housing.

Dimensions of Unemployment and Labor Market Dynamics

Unemployment in developing economies is structural and cyclical. Understanding standard labor market terminologies is crucial for objective descriptive answers.

  • Structural Unemployment: Arises from a mismatch between the skills possessed by the workforce and the skills demanded by employers, exacerbated by rapid automation and artificial intelligence integration.
  • Disguised Unemployment: A phenomenon wherein more people are employed in an activity (particularly agriculture) than technically necessary; the marginal productivity of labor is zero or negative.
  • Educated Unemployment: A critical socio-economic crisis in states like Uttar Pradesh where graduates and post-graduates fail to find suitable employment matching their qualifications, leading to demographic frustration.
  • Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS): Conducted by the National Statistical Office (NSO) to provide estimates of key employment and unemployment indicators such as Worker Population Ratio (WPR), Labor Force Participation Rate (LFPR), and Unemployment Rate (UR).

Multidimensional Poverty and Uttar Pradesh Specific Realities

Poverty is no longer viewed purely through income deprivation. The Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) measures deprivations across health, education, and standard of living.

  • National MPI Framework: Published by NITI Aayog based on global Alkire-Foster methodology, utilizing 12 sustainable development goal-aligned indicators spanning nutrition, schooling, sanitation, cooking fuel, and electricity.
  • UP Demographic Challenge: Despite significant poverty reduction in recent years, Uttar Pradesh accounts for a large absolute number of multidimensionally poor individuals, driven by inter-district disparities (e.g., western UP vs. eastern/Bundelkhand regions).
  • Role of Rural Development Schemes: Flagship interventions like Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana (PMAY), Jal Jeevan Mission, and Swachh Bharat Mission directly target deprivations in living standards, accelerating exit from multidimensional poverty.

Policy Interventions and Social Protection Mechanisms

India maintains a vast network of social safety nets aimed at direct income supplementation, subsidized food security, and livelihood enhancement.

  • National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013: Legally entitles up to 75% of the rural population and 50% of the urban population to receive subsidized food grains under the Targeted Public Distribution System (TPDS).
  • Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana (DAY-NRLM): A flagship poverty alleviation program designed to mobilize rural poor households into Self-Help Groups (SHGs), providing bank linkages and capacity building.
  • Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana (PMMY): Extends collateral-free institutional loans up to Rs. 10 Lakh under Shishu, Kishore, and Tarun categories to micro-entrepreneurs, stimulating self-employment.
  • Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT): Leverages the JAM trinity (Jan Dhan, Aadhaar, Mobile) to plug leakages, eliminate intermediaries, and ensure transparent transfer of welfare funds directly to beneficiaries.
