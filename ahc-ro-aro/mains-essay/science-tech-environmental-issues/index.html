Science, Technology & Environmental Issues in Mains Essay Writing Core Study Notes

This topic is vital for the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC Mains examinations, focusing on the intersection of technological advancement, ecological balance, and constitutional mandates. Aspirants must articulate balanced essay frameworks integrating Article 21, sustainable development goals, and Uttar Pradesh-specific ecological challenges.

Constitutional & Legal Architecture of Environmental Protection

The Indian Constitution explicitly and implicitly integrates ecological protection into its fundamental fabric, bolstered by robust statutory enactments and judicial pronouncements.

  • Article 21 & Right to Clean Environment: Through judicial interpretation in cases like Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar, the Supreme Court held that the Right to Life under Article 21 includes the right to pollution-free water and air.
  • Directive Principles & Fundamental Duties: Article 48A directs the State to protect and improve the environment and safeguard forests and wildlife, while Article 51A(g) places a duty on citizens to protect natural ecosystems.
  • The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974: The pioneering legislation establishing Central and State Pollution Control Boards (CPCB/SPCBs) to regulate discharge of effluents into water bodies.
  • The Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981: Empowers SPCBs to declare air pollution control areas and set emission standards for industrial units operating within state boundaries.
  • National Green Tribunal (NGT) Act, 2010: Created a specialized judicial body bypassing civil courts to handle civil cases relating to environmental laws, applying principles of sustainable development and strict liability.

Science & Technology Interventions in Environmental Mitigation

Modern scientific innovations, ranging from artificial intelligence to green hydrogen, serve as critical levers for curbing pollution and achieving climate resilience.

  • Green Hydrogen Mission: National Green Hydrogen Mission aims to produce 5 million metric tonnes of green hydrogen annually by 2030, drastically reducing industrial carbon footprints in sectors like steel and refineries.
  • Carbon Capture, Utilization, and Storage (CCUS): Technological frameworks designed to capture CO2 emissions from power generation and industrial sources, either utilizing them in commercial products or permanently storing them deep underground.
  • IoT & AI in Environmental Monitoring: Deployment of Internet of Things (IoT) sensors and Artificial Intelligence models for real-time Air Quality Index (AQI) tracking, effluent discharge detection in rivers like Ganga and Yamuna.
  • Biotech Solutions: Bio-remediation & Enzyme Engineering: Utilization of genetically engineered microbes and bacteria strains to clean up oil spills, degrade plastic polymers, and treat heavy metal toxic sludge in industrial effluents.
  • Electric Mobility & Battery Recycling Tech: FAME-II and PM E-Bus Sewa schemes accelerating EV adoption, supported by advanced hydrometallurgical recycling techniques to recover lithium, cobalt, and nickel from spent batteries.

Socio-Economic Dimensions & UP-Specific Environmental Challenges

Environmental degradation disproportionately impacts vulnerable socio-economic strata, with Uttar Pradesh facing unique geographical, agricultural, and industrial ecological burdens.

  • Stubble Burning & Indo-Gangetic Plain Air Quality: Post-monsoon crop residue burning in northwestern states combines with local stubble and industrial emissions, causing severe winter smog episodes across western and central UP districts.
  • Ganga Basin Pollution & Namami Gange Impact: High urban sewage load from cities like Kanpur, Prayagraj, and Varanasi entering the Ganga, addressed through the Namami Gange Integrated Conservation Mission via Sewerage Infrastructure projects.
  • Groundwater Depletion in Western UP: Intensive paddy and sugarcane cultivation reliant on tube-wells has led to critical groundwater table depletion in districts like Meerut, Muzaffarnagar, and Bulandshahr.
  • Climate Vulnerability: Floods and Droughts in Eastern UP: Eastern UP districts (Gorakhpur, Ballia, Deoria) suffer recurrent monsoon flooding from tributaries of the Ghaghra and Rapti, alternating with agricultural drought spells.
  • Poverty-Environment Nexus: Rural poverty compels over-reliance on biomass fuels and minor forest produce, leading to indoor air pollution and deforestation, creating a vicious cycle of ecological and human deprivation.

Global Conventions, International Frameworks & Way Forward

Global environmental governance relies on multilateral treaties, climate finance commitments, and inclusive policy frameworks to guide sustainable nation-building.

  • UNFCCC & Conference of Parties (COP): The apex global treaty framework governing climate change mitigation, featuring landmark accords like the Kyoto Protocol (1997) and Paris Agreement (2015) limiting warming well below 2°C.
  • UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs): Particularly SDG 13 (Climate Action), SDG 15 (Life on Land), SDG 6 (Clean Water and Sanitation), and SDG 7 (Affordable and Clean Energy) acting as benchmarks for state policy evaluation.
  • International Solar Alliance (ISA): Headquartered in Gurugram, India, ISA promotes solar energy deployment across tropical sunshine-rich countries to reduce global carbon dependency.
  • LiFE Movement (Lifestyle for Environment): India-led global initiative advocating mindful and deliberate utilization of resources instead of mindless consumption, transforming individual actions into a mass environmental movement.
  • Circular Economy Roadmap: Transitioning from the linear 'Take-Make-Dispose' model to a circular model focusing on recycling, re-manufacturing, and resource efficiency across industrial sectors.
