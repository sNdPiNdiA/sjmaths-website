Constitutional Accuracy Checks Core Study Notes

Constitutional Accuracy Checks form the cornerstone of judicial review, legislative validation, and administrative accountability in India. For Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC aspirants, mastering this topic is vital for drafting rigorous essays on rule of law, judicial activism, and separation of powers.

Constitutional Framework & Fundamental Doctrines

The Indian Constitution incorporates explicit and implicit checks and balances to prevent authoritarianism and maintain federal equilibrium.

  • Article 13 and Judicial Review: Declares that all laws inconsistent with or in derogation of Fundamental Rights shall be void, empowering courts to perform constitutional accuracy checks.
  • Basic Structure Doctrine: An unwritten yardstick derived from Kesavananda Bharati (1973) comprising secularism, federalism, judicial review, and parliamentary democracy.
  • Separation of Powers (Article 50): Directs the State to take steps to separate the judiciary from the executive in the public services of the State.
  • Doctrine of Pith and Substance: Used by courts to examine the true nature and character of legislation to determine if it falls within the legislative competence of the respective List (VII Schedule).

Legislative Oversight & Executive Accountability

Parliamentary control mechanisms ensure that executive actions remain within constitutional limits and fiscal propriety is maintained.

  • Parliamentary Committees: Public Accounts Committee (PAC), Estimates Committee, and Committee on Subordinate Legislation scrutinize delegated legislation and government expenditure.
  • Question Hour and Zero Hour: Vital parliamentary instruments allowing MPs to extract information from ministers and keep administrative machinery on check.
  • Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG - Art 148): Acts as the guardian of the public purse, conducting constitutional accuracy checks on financial transactions of Union and State governments.

Judicial Reforms and Contemporary Challenges

Ensuring constitutional accuracy requires addressing structural delays, judicial backlog, and maintaining institutional credibility.

  • Pendency and Case Backlog: Over 5 crore cases pending across Indian courts undermine the fundamental right to speedy trial under Article 21.
  • Subordinate Judiciary Strengthening: National Mission for Justice Delivery and Legal Reforms focuses on infrastructure upgrades and digital courts (e-Courts project phase III).
  • Judicial Overreach Debates: Balancing judicial activism with judicial restraint to prevent encroachment into executive and legislative domains.

Socio-Economic & Federal Governance Checks

Constitutional accuracy also extends to distributive justice, federal cooperation, and localized governance integrity under Panchayats and Municipalities.

  • Directive Principles (Part IV): Serve as fundamental benchmarks for socio-economic justice, tested by courts for welfare policy alignment (e.g., Article 39(b) and (c)).
  • Cooperative Federalism (Art 246): Union and State lists division audited through judicial interpretation to prevent encroachment on regional legislative sovereignty.
  • Local Self-Governance (73rd & 74th Amendments): State Finance Commissions and District Planning Committees ensure decentralization accuracy and resource devolution.
