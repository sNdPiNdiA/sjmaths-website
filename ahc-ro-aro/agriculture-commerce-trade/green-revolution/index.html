Green Revolution Core Study Notes

The Green Revolution marks a fundamental transformation in Indian agriculture during the mid-1960s, turning food-deficit India into a self-sufficient nation. For Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations, questions heavily target pioneering scientists, high-yielding variety (HYV) seeds, geographical spread, and associated ecological and socio-economic impacts.

Foundational Concepts & Pioneers

Understanding the global genesis, domestic leadership, and key inputs that drove the Green Revolution.

  • Global Father of Green Revolution: Dr. Norman Borlaug, an American agronomist based at CIMMYT in Mexico, developed semi-dwarf, disease-resistant, high-yielding wheat varieties and won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1970.
  • Architect of India's Green Revolution: Dr. M.S. Swaminathan spearheaded the scientific and administrative rollout in India, introducing Mexican wheat varieties and collaborating with Indian agricultural institutes.
  • Etymology & Coining: The term 'Green Revolution' was first coined by William S. Gaud, Administrator of USAID, in March 1968 to describe the spectacular rise in food grain production.
  • Core Components of the Package: The strategy relied on four pillars: HYV seeds, chemical fertilizers (NPK), controlled irrigation (tube-wells/canals), and intensive plant protection measures (pesticides/insecticides).

Crop Varieties and Technological Inputs

Examining specific high-yielding wheat and rice strains introduced during the agricultural shift.

  • Wheat Varieties: High-yielding dwarf wheat varieties imported and adapted included Lerma Rojo 64-A, Sonora 64, Kalyan Sona, and Sharbati Sonora.
  • Rice Varieties: IR-8 (dubbed 'Miracle Rice') developed at IRRI, Philippines, along with native crosses like Padma, Jaya, and Ratna, drove the paddy revolution.
  • Fertilizer Responsiveness: Traditional tall crops lodged (fell flat) under heavy nitrogen application. Dwarf HYV varieties absorbed high nitrogen doses without lodging, multiplying yields.
  • Role of Irrigation Infrastructure: Rapid expansion of minor irrigation works, electric and diesel pump-sets, and command area development enabled precise water management required by HYV crops.

Geographical Distribution & Impact on Uttar Pradesh

Analyzing regional disparities and specific impacts on Uttar Pradesh's agrarian economy.

  • Initial Regional Concentration: The Green Revolution was initially restricted to northwestern states—Punjab, Haryana, and Western Uttar Pradesh—due to better irrigation and progressive farming communities.
  • Impact on Western UP: Districts like Meerut, Muzaffarnagar, Bulandshahr, and Saharanpur witnessed rapid mechanization, high wheat-paddy cropping intensity, and commercialization of agriculture.
  • Eastern UP Lag: Eastern UP experienced delayed benefits due to small fragmented landholdings, lower groundwater development, and socio-economic constraints, prompting targeted government interventions.
  • Institutional Support: Establishment of Agricultural Universities (e.g., GBPUAT Pantnagar, CSAUAT Kanpur) provided regional research support and trained extension workers.

Socio-Economic, Ecological & Second Generation Impacts

Evaluating the positive economic outcomes alongside ecological challenges and critiques of the revolution.

  • Food Security Achieved: India transformed from a 'ship-to-mouth' existence dependent on PL-480 US wheat imports to a food-surplus nation, boosting buffer stocks managed by FCI.
  • Ecological Degradation: Excessive chemical fertilizer and pesticide use led to soil degradation, loss of micro-flora, groundwater depletion, and nitrate contamination in water bodies.
  • Inter-Personal Disparities: Capital-intensive farming favored large landowners who could afford tractors, tube-wells, and HYV inputs, widening the wealth gap between rich and marginal farmers.
  • Second Generation Problems: Coined by agricultural scientists to describe plateauing yields, pest resistance, secondary nutrient deficiencies (Zinc/Sulphur), and salinity in intensively farmed soils.
