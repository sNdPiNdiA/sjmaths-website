Green Revolution

Comprehensive, high-yield study notes, mnemonics, tables, and concept breakdown for Allahabad High Court RO/ARO preparation.

Core Concepts & Definitions

  • The **Green Revolution** refers to a period of rapid agricultural growth and increased food production, primarily in developing countries, starting in the mid-20th century.
  • It involved the adoption of new technologies and practices to significantly boost crop yields.
  • **Key Elements:**
    • **High-Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds** (especially wheat and rice).
    • Increased use of **chemical fertilizers** and **pesticides**.
    • Expansion of **irrigation facilities**.
    • Modern **farm machinery** (tractors, harvesters).
  • **Objective:** To achieve food self-sufficiency and combat hunger.
**Exam Tip:** The Green Revolution was a technology-driven agricultural transformation, not just a policy change.

Key Figures, Crops & Regions (India & World)

  • **Father of Green Revolution (World):** **Dr. Norman Borlaug** (Nobel Peace Prize laureate).
  • **Father of Green Revolution (India):** **Dr. M.S. Swaminathan**.
  • **Primary Crops Impacted:**
    • Initially and most significantly: **Wheat**.
    • Later, also **Rice**.
    • Other crops like maize, jowar, bajra saw some impact but less pronounced.
  • **Regions in India (First Phase):**
    • **Punjab**
    • **Haryana**
    • **Western Uttar Pradesh**
    • These regions had better irrigation infrastructure and progressive farmers.
**Memory Aid:** Think "SWAMI BORLAUG WHEAT RICE PUNJAB" to remember the key people, crops, and initial region.

Impacts: Pros & Cons (Comparison Table)

Positive Impacts (Pros) Negative Impacts (Cons)
Achieved **food self-sufficiency**; reduced imports. Increased **regional disparities** (favored irrigated areas).
Increased **farmer income** in prosperous regions. Increased **inter-personal disparities** (rich farmers benefited more).
Boosted **rural employment** (initially). **Environmental degradation** (soil salinity, water depletion, pesticide residue).
Stimulated **industrial growth** (fertilizers, machinery). Increased **input costs** for farmers (seeds, fertilizers, water).
Reduced **famine risk** and improved food security. Loss of **traditional crop varieties** (biodiversity).
**High-Yield Fact:** While increasing food production was a major success, the Green Revolution also led to significant socio-economic and environmental challenges.

Chronology & Phases in India

  • **Mid-1960s:** Introduction of HYV seeds in India, particularly wheat varieties from Mexico.
  • **1965-66:** Launch of the **Intensive Agricultural District Programme (IADP)** and **Intensive Agricultural Area Programme (IAAP)**, laying groundwork.
  • **1966-67:** Often considered the official start of the **First Green Revolution** in India.
  • **1968:** The term "Green Revolution" was coined by **William S. Gaud**.
  • **First Green Revolution (Mid-1960s to Mid-1970s):** Focused on wheat and rice in well-irrigated areas.
  • **Second Green Revolution (Mid-1980s onwards):** Aims for sustainable growth, diversification, and reaching rain-fed areas, often incorporating biotechnology.
**Important Landmark Fact:** India's food grain production nearly doubled between 1960-61 and 1980-81, largely due to the Green Revolution.

Related Concepts & Future Outlook

  • **Evergreen Revolution:** A term coined by M.S. Swaminathan, advocating for sustainable productivity enhancement without ecological harm, ensuring food security for all time.
  • **Rainbow Revolution:** An umbrella term encompassing various agricultural revolutions in India (e.g., Green for food grains, White for milk, Blue for fish, Yellow for oilseeds, Golden for horticulture, Grey for fertilizers, Black for petroleum, Red for meat/tomatoes, Round for potatoes, Silver for eggs, Pink for prawns/pharmaceuticals).
  • **Sustainable Agriculture:** The current focus, aiming to integrate environmental health, economic profitability, and social and economic equity.
  • **Precision Farming:** Using technology (GPS, sensors) to manage farm inputs more efficiently.
**Trick:** Remember "Evergreen" implies sustainability, going beyond just "Green" (initial productivity boost). "Rainbow" signifies the spectrum of agricultural sectors.
