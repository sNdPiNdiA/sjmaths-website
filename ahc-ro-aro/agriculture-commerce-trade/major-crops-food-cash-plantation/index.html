Major Crops Food Cash Plantation

Comprehensive, high-yield study notes, mnemonics, tables, and concept breakdown for Allahabad High Court RO/ARO preparation.

1. Crop Classification & Cropping Seasons

  • Core Concepts & Definitions:
    • Food Crops: Grown for human consumption (e.g., Rice, Wheat, Pulses).
    • Cash Crops: Grown for sale in the market, not primarily for self-consumption (e.g., Sugarcane, Cotton, Tobacco).
    • Plantation Crops: Cultivated on large estates, usually for export or industrial processing (e.g., Tea, Coffee, Rubber).
  • Cropping Seasons in India:
    • Kharif (Monsoon Crops): Sown with onset of monsoon (June-July), harvested Sept-Oct.
    • Rabi (Winter Crops): Sown Oct-Dec, harvested April-June.
    • Zaid (Summer Crops): Short season between Kharif & Rabi, March-June.

  • Memory Aid: Cropping Seasons

    • Kharif: "Kharif Kills Rain" (needs rain, harvested before winter). Examples: Rice, Maize, Jowar, Bajra, Cotton, Jute, Groundnut.
    • Rabi: "Rabi Rises Cold" (sown in cold, harvested before summer). Examples: Wheat, Barley, Gram, Mustard, Peas.
    • Zaid: "Zaid Zaps Thirst" (water-intensive, summer crops). Examples: Watermelon, Muskmelon, Cucumber, Fodder crops.

2. Major Food Crops: Cereals & Pulses

  • Core Concepts:
    • Rice (Paddy): Staple food, tropical/subtropical, high temp (>25°C), high rainfall (>100cm). Leading producers: West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab.
    • Wheat: Second most important cereal, temperate crop, cool growing season, bright sunshine at harvest. Leading producers: Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana.
    • Maize: Used as food & fodder, Kharif crop (can be Rabi in some areas). Requires 21-27°C, old alluvial soil. Leading producers: Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh.
    • Millets (Jowar, Bajra, Ragi): Coarse grains, high nutritional value, rain-fed crops. Jowar (Maharashtra), Bajra (Rajasthan), Ragi (Karnataka).
    • Pulses (Dal): Major source of protein, leguminous crops (fix nitrogen). Tur (Arhar), Urad, Moong, Masur, Peas, Gram. Leading producers: Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Maharashtra.

  • Important Comparison: Rice vs. Wheat

    Feature Rice Wheat
    Climate Tropical/Subtropical Temperate
    Temperature >25°C 10-15°C (sowing), 20-25°C (harvest)
    Rainfall >100 cm 50-75 cm
    Soil Alluvial, Clayey Loamy, Black, Alluvial
    Season Kharif Rabi
    Top Producers (India) West Bengal, UP, Punjab UP, Punjab, Haryana

  • High-Yield Fact: Green Revolution

    Primarily boosted production of Wheat and Rice in the 1960s-70s, especially in Punjab, Haryana, and Western Uttar Pradesh, using HYV seeds, fertilizers, and irrigation.

3. Major Cash Crops

  • Core Concepts:
    • Sugarcane: Tropical & subtropical crop. Hot & humid climate (21-27°C, 75-100cm rainfall). Leading producers: Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka. India is 2nd largest producer globally.
    • Cotton: Kharif crop, requires high temp, light rainfall, 210 frost-free days, black soil. Leading producers: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Telangana. India is 3rd largest producer globally.
    • Jute (Golden Fibre): Kharif crop, high temp, heavy rainfall, well-drained fertile alluvial soil. Leading producers: West Bengal, Bihar, Assam. India is largest producer globally.
    • Oilseeds: Groundnut, Mustard, Coconut, Sesamum, Soybean, Castor seeds, Linseed, Sunflower. Used for cooking oil, industrial purposes. Leading producers: Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat.
    • Tobacco: Requires warm climate, well-drained sandy loam soil. Leading producers: Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh.

  • Exam Tip: India's Global Ranks

    • Sugarcane: 2nd largest producer.
    • Cotton: 3rd largest producer.
    • Jute: Largest producer.
    • Oilseeds: Among top producers (e.g., Groundnut 2nd, Rapeseed 3rd).

  • Memory Aid: Major Oilseeds

    Think "Good Mustard Seeds Can Light Sun" for Groundnut, Mustard, Soybean, Castor, Linseed, Sunflower.

4. Major Plantation Crops & Horticulture

  • Core Concepts:
    • Tea: Tropical & subtropical, deep fertile well-drained loamy soil, warm & moist frost-free climate, frequent showers. Leading producers: Assam, West Bengal (Darjeeling, Dooars), Tamil Nadu, Kerala. India is 2nd largest producer globally.
    • Coffee: Requires warm & wet climate, well-drained loamy soil, hilly slopes. India produces Arabica variety (high quality). Leading producers: Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu. India is 7th largest producer globally.
    • Rubber: Equatorial crop, hot & humid climate (>25°C, >200cm rainfall). Leading producers: Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka.
    • Spices: India is a major producer and exporter of spices (pepper, cardamom, chili, turmeric). Kerala is known as the "Spice Garden of India."
    • Horticulture (Fruits & Vegetables): India is the 2nd largest producer of fruits and vegetables globally.
      • Fruits: Mangoes (UP, Maharashtra), Oranges (Maharashtra, MP), Bananas (Maharashtra, TN), Grapes (Maharashtra).
      • Vegetables: Potato, Onion, Tomato, Cabbage.

  • Important Landmark Facts / Rules:

    • Spice Board of India: Established in 1987 under the Ministry of Commerce & Industry, responsible for the development and worldwide promotion of Indian spices.
    • National Horticulture Mission (NHM): Launched in 2005-06, a centrally sponsored scheme for the holistic development of the horticulture sector in India.
    • "Golden Revolution": Refers to the period of increased productivity in horticulture, especially fruits and honey, from 1991 to 2003.

  • Exam Tip: Geographical Indications (GI Tags)

    Many crops have GI tags, e.g., Darjeeling Tea, Coorg Arabica Coffee, Alphonso Mango. Be aware of prominent ones.
