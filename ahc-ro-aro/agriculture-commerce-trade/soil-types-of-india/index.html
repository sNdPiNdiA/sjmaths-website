Soil Types Of India

Comprehensive, high-yield study notes, mnemonics, tables, and concept breakdown for Allahabad High Court RO/ARO preparation.

1. Alluvial Soil: The Most Fertile & Widespread

  • Core Concept: Formed by the deposition of sediments brought by rivers. Covers the largest area in India (approx. 40%).
  • Definition: Alluvial Soil is a fine-grained fertile soil deposited by water flowing over flood plains or in river beds.
  • Characteristics:
    • Rich in potash, phosphoric acid, and lime.
    • Deficient in nitrogen and humus.
    • Highly fertile and suitable for intensive cultivation.
    • Ranges from sandy loam to clay.
  • Regions: Northern Plains (Ganga, Brahmaputra, Indus basins), coastal plains, deltas.
  • Crops: Wheat, rice, sugarcane, jute, pulses, oilseeds.
High-Yield Fact: Alluvial soil is divided into Khadar (new alluvium, more fertile) and Bhangar (old alluvium, less fertile, kankars present).

2. Black Soil (Regur Soil): Ideal for Cotton

  • Core Concept: Formed from weathered basaltic rocks (volcanic origin). Known for its high moisture retention.
  • Definition: Black Soil (also called Regur Soil) is a dark, clayey soil rich in minerals, formed from volcanic lava, primarily found in the Deccan Trap region.
  • Characteristics:
    • Rich in lime, iron, magnesia, alumina, and potash.
    • Deficient in phosphoric content, nitrogen, and organic matter.
    • Clayey texture, highly retentive of moisture.
    • Develops deep cracks during dry periods (self-ploughing).
  • Regions: Deccan Trap (Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, parts of Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka).
  • Crops: Cotton (most important), sugarcane, jowar, wheat, tobacco, oilseeds.
Exam Tip: The black color is due to the presence of titaniferous magnetite compounds.

3. Red & Yellow Soil: Crystalline Rock Origin

  • Core Concept: Formed from the weathering of ancient crystalline and metamorphic rocks. Red color due to iron content.
  • Definition: Red Soil is a soil type developed on crystalline igneous rocks in areas of low rainfall, characterized by a reddish color due to iron oxides.
  • Characteristics:
    • Rich in iron oxides.
    • Deficient in nitrogen, phosphorus, and humus.
    • Generally sandy to clayey loam, porous, and friable.
    • Appears yellow when hydrated.
  • Regions: Eastern and Southern parts of the Deccan Plateau (Odisha, Chhattisgarh, parts of Ganga plain, Western Ghats).
  • Crops: Groundnuts, potatoes, rice, ragi, tobacco, pulses.
Memory Aid: "Red Iron Man" - Red Soil gets its color from Iron oxides.

4. Laterite Soil & Arid/Desert Soil

  • Laterite Soil:
    • Core Concept: Formed under conditions of high temperature and heavy rainfall, leading to intense leaching.
    • Characteristics: Rich in iron and aluminum, but deficient in silica, lime, potash, and nitrogen. Coarse texture.
    • Regions: Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats, North-Eastern regions, parts of Odisha.
    • Crops: Tea, coffee, cashew nuts, rubber. (Suitable for plantation crops).
    • Use: Building material (bricks) due to hardening upon exposure to air.
  • Arid/Desert Soil:
    • Core Concept: Found in dry regions with high temperatures and low rainfall.
    • Characteristics: Sandy texture, saline, low humus and moisture content. High salt content, sometimes with kankar layers.
    • Regions: Western Rajasthan, parts of Gujarat and Haryana.
    • Crops: Bajra, jowar, pulses (with irrigation).
High-Yield Fact: Laterite soil is poor in fertility due to intense leaching, but can be made productive with fertilizers.

5. Other Soil Types & Key Comparisons

  • Mountain/Forest Soil:
    • Characteristics: Loamy and silty in valley sides, coarse-grained in upper slopes. Rich in humus but deficient in potash, phosphorus, and lime.
    • Regions: Himalayan region, Western and Eastern Ghats.
    • Crops: Tea, coffee, spices, temperate fruits.
  • Saline and Alkaline Soil (Usar/Reh):
    • Characteristics: High proportion of sodium, potassium, and magnesium salts. Infertile.
    • Regions: Arid/semi-arid regions, areas with poor drainage (e.g., parts of UP, Haryana, Punjab).
