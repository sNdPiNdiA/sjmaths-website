Irrigation Systems in Agriculture Core Study Notes

Irrigation systems form the backbone of Indian and Uttar Pradesh agriculture, compensating for erratic monsoon patterns. For the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations, questions frequently target sources of irrigation (tube wells, canals), irrigation potential, efficiency of modern methods (drip/sprinkler), and UP-specific canal networks like the Upper Ganga Canal and Sharda Canal.

Major Sources of Irrigation in Uttar Pradesh

Uttar Pradesh relies heavily on groundwater and canal networks due to its vast fertile plains and perennial rivers.

  • Tube Wells and Groundwater (नलकूप और भूजल): Tube wells constitute the largest source of irrigation in Uttar Pradesh, accounting for nearly 74-75% of the total net irrigated area. Western UP districts have the highest concentration of private tube wells.
  • Canal Irrigation (नहरी सिंचाई): Canals are the second largest source of irrigation in UP, covering around 18-20% of the net irrigated area. UP possesses one of the largest canal networks in India, maintained by the state irrigation department.
  • Wells and Ponds (कुएं और तालाब): Traditional masonry wells and tanks/ponds account for a minor share (around 3-5%) of irrigation, predominantly prevalent in the rocky terrain of southern Bundelkhand region where tube well construction is difficult.
  • Other Sources (अन्य स्रोत): Lift irrigation, check dams, and pumping sets installed on rivers/streams contribute to marginal irrigation in plateau and undulating regions.

Prominent Canal Systems of Uttar Pradesh

The Gangetic river system feeds multiple monumental canals that irrigate vast agricultural tracts across UP.

  • Upper Ganga Canal (ऊपरी गंगा नहर): Take-off point is Bhimgoda Barrage near Haridwar (Uttarakhand). Opened in 1854, it irrigates Saharanpur, Muzaffarnagar, Meerut, Bulandshahr, Aligarh, and Mathura.
  • Lower Ganga Canal (निचली गंगा नहर): Originates from Narora (Bulandshahr) on the Ganga River, commissioned in 1878, serving districts like Aligarh, Etah, Farrukhabad, Kanpur, and Fatehpur.
  • Sharda Canal System (शारदा नहर प्रणाली): The largest canal system in Uttar Pradesh, originating from Banbassa on the Sarda River (near Indo-Nepal border). It has an extensive network serving Rohilkhand and central UP districts.
  • Eastern Yamuna Canal (पूर्वी यमुना नहर): One of the oldest canals, originating from the Yamuna River at Faizlabad (Tajewala headworks), irrigating Saharanpur, Muzaffarnagar, and Meerut districts.
  • Ken Canal System in Bundelkhand (केन नहर प्रणाली): Originates from the Ken River at Panna (Madhya Pradesh) and extends into Banda district of UP, acting as a lifeline for drought-prone Bundelkhand.

Modern Irrigation Techniques & Efficiency

Shifting from flood irrigation to micro-irrigation is vital for sustainable groundwater management in water-stressed zones.

  • Drip Irrigation (ड्रिप सिंचाई): Delivers water drop-by-drop directly to the plant roots via a network of valves, pipes, and emitters. It achieves water use efficiency of up to 90% and reduces weed growth.
  • Sprinkler Irrigation (स्प्रिंकलर सिंचाई): Simulates natural rainfall by spraying water under pressure through overhead nozzles. Highly effective for undulating terrains and sandy soils where flood irrigation causes soil erosion.
  • Micro Irrigation Fund (MIF): Established with NABARD under PMKSY with an initial corpus of ₹5,000 crore to facilitate states in mobilizing resources for expanding micro-irrigation coverage.
  • Per Drop More Crop Component: A key pillar of PMKSY focusing on micro-irrigation adoption (drip/sprinkler), water-efficient agronomic practices, and capacity building of farmers.

Water Conservation & Institutional Framework

Governance, water policy, and legal frameworks governing surface and groundwater use in Uttar Pradesh.

  • Groundwater Extraction Regulation: The UP Ground Water Management and Regulation Act provides norms for sinking new tube wells, mandating registration in over-exploited and critical blocks to prevent aquifer depletion.
  • Amrit Sarovar Mission: A nationwide campaign aiming to develop/rejuvenate 75 water bodies in each district of India to enhance rural water security and groundwater recharge.
  • Khet Talab Yojana (Farm Pond Scheme): A UP government scheme providing financial subsidies to farmers in Bundelkhand and Vindhyan regions for constructing farm ponds to harvest rainwater for protective irrigation.
