Heavy Industries

Comprehensive, high-yield study notes, mnemonics, tables, and concept breakdown for Allahabad High Court RO/ARO preparation.

Core Concepts: Heavy Industries

  • Definition: Industries characterized by large capital investment, heavy raw materials, large-scale production, and significant energy consumption. They produce basic goods for other industries.
  • Characteristics:
    • Capital Intensive: Requires substantial investment in machinery and infrastructure.
    • Raw Material Dependent: Often located near sources of bulky, weight-losing raw materials.
    • Large Scale: High production volumes, often employing many workers.
    • Infrastructure Dependent: Requires robust transport, power, and communication networks.
  • Examples in India: Iron & Steel, Cement, Heavy Engineering, Shipbuilding, Automobiles, Petrochemicals.
Exam Tip: Heavy industries are considered the "backbone" of industrial development as they provide inputs for a wide range of other industries.

Key Heavy Industries in India: Iron & Steel

  • Significance: A basic or backbone industry; its output is used as raw material for almost all other industries.
  • Raw Materials: Iron ore, coking coal, limestone (in a ratio of approximately 4:2:1), manganese, silica.
  • Major Integrated Steel Plants (Public Sector - SAIL):
    • Bhilai Steel Plant (Chhattisgarh): With Russian collaboration.
    • Durgapur Steel Plant (West Bengal): With British collaboration.
    • Rourkela Steel Plant (Odisha): With German collaboration.
    • Bokaro Steel Plant (Jharkhand): With Russian collaboration.
  • Private Sector:
    • Tata Iron and Steel Company (TISCO), Jamshedpur (Jharkhand): India's oldest and largest private sector plant, established in 1907.
Mnemonic for SAIL Plants & Collaborators:

Bhilai Russia, Durgapur Britain, Rourkela Germany, Bokaro Russia.

Think: "BRitish DRagon RGrows BRight." (Bhilai-Russia, Durgapur-Britain, Rourkela-Germany, Bokaro-Russia)

Other Major Heavy Industries & Facts

Industry Key Raw Materials Major Hubs/Facts
Cement Industry Limestone, Silica, Alumina, Gypsum India is the 2nd largest producer globally. First plant in Chennai (1904).
Automobile Industry Iron & Steel, Rubber, Glass, Plastics "Sunrise Industry." Major hubs: Gurugram, Chennai ("Detroit of Asia"), Pune, Jamshedpur.
Shipbuilding Industry Steel plates, Machinery Major Public Sector Yards: Hindustan Shipyard Ltd (Visakhapatnam), Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders Ltd (Mumbai), Garden Reach Shipbuilders & Engineers (Kolkata), Cochin Shipyard Ltd (Kochi).
Heavy Electricals Steel, Copper, Aluminium Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited (BHEL) - a Maharatna PSU, manufactures power generation equipment.
High-Yield Fact: The first modern cement plant was set up in Chennai in 1904, but it failed. The first successful plant was established in Porbandar, Gujarat, in 1914.

Industrial Policies & Economic Impact

  • Industrial Policy Resolution, 1956: Emphasized the role of the public sector in heavy industries for rapid industrialization and self-reliance. Classified industries into three schedules.
  • Industrial Policy, 1991 (LPG Reforms): Liberalization, Privatization, Globalization. Reduced the role of the public sector, opened up most industries to private and foreign investment, including heavy industries.
  • Make in India Initiative (2014): Aims to boost manufacturing, including heavy industries, by encouraging domestic and foreign investment, improving ease of doing business, and promoting skill development.
  • Impact on Economy:
    • Employment Generation: Direct and indirect jobs.
    • Infrastructure Development: Drives demand for transport, power, and logistics.
    • Export Potential: Contributes to foreign exchange earnings.
    • Technological Advancement: Fosters innovation and R&D.
Exam Tip: Understand the shift from a state-led heavy industrial model (1956 policy) to a more market-oriented approach (1991 policy) and its implications for India's economic growth.
