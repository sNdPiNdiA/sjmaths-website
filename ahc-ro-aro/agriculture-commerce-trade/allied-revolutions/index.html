Syllabus Core Study Notes (Deep-Dive)

Explore the various allied revolutions in Agriculture, Commerce and Trade that transformed India's economy.

1. Green Revolution

The Green Revolution refers to a set of research and technology transfer initiatives that significantly increased agricultural production in India, particularly in the 1960s-70s.

Key Features
  • Introduction of High-Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds
  • Increased use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides
  • Expansion of irrigation facilities
  • Mechanization of agriculture (tractors, harvesters)
  • Focus on wheat and rice production
Achievements
  • India became self-sufficient in foodgrain production
  • Wheat production increased from 11 million tonnes (1960s) to 70+ million tonnes
  • Prevented famines and reduced food imports
  • Strengthened food security
Limitations & Criticisms
  • Regional disparities (Punjab-Haryana benefited most)
  • Increased groundwater depletion and soil degradation
  • Marginal farmers unable to afford inputs
  • Increase in inter-crop inequalities
  • Neglect of millets, pulses, and oilseeds

2. White Revolution (Operation Flood)

The White Revolution, also known as Operation Flood, was the world's largest dairy development program launched in 1970 by the National Dairy Development Board (NDDB).

Key Features
  • Launched by Dr. Verghese Kurien under NDDB
  • Based on cooperative model (Anand Pattern)
  • Organized milk producers into village-level cooperatives
  • Eliminated middlemen and ensured fair prices to farmers
  • Implemented in three phases (1970-1996)
Achievements
  • India became world's largest milk producer
  • Milk production increased from 20 million tonnes (1970) to 180+ million tonnes
  • Improved rural incomes and women empowerment
  • Created employment for millions of rural households
  • Replicated across 70,000+ villages
Cooperative Model

The Anand Pattern (named after Anand, Gujarat) involved three-tier structure: Village Dairy Cooperative Societies (Village level) → District Milk Unions (District level) → State Dairy Federations (State level). Profits were reinvested in rural development and farmer welfare.

3. Other Allied Revolutions

Besides Green and White Revolutions, several other color revolutions transformed different sectors of Indian agriculture.

Blue Revolution (Fisheries)

Focused on increasing fish production through aquaculture, modernization of fisheries, and improved hatchery techniques. India is now the second-largest fish producer in the world.

Yellow Revolution (Edible Oils)

Aimed at achieving self-sufficiency in edible oils through increased production of mustard, sunflower, soybean, and groundnut. Reduced India's dependence on edible oil imports.

Golden Revolution (Horticulture)

Focused on increasing production of fruits, vegetables, flowers, and honey. Led to diversification of agriculture and increased farmer incomes through high-value crops.

4. Agricultural Reforms & WTO Impact

Economic reforms of 1991 and India's entry into WTO brought significant changes to the agricultural sector.

Economic Reforms (1991)
  • Reduction in subsidies on fertilizers
  • Promotion of contract farming
  • Encouragement of private investment in agriculture
  • Deregulation of sugar industry
WTO Impact
  • Agreement on Agriculture (AoA) led to reduction in subsidies
  • Intellectual Property Rights (Trips) affected seeds
  • Increased competition from imports
  • Push for export-oriented agriculture
Recent Initiatives
  • e-NAM: Online National Agriculture Market
  • PM-KISAN: Direct income support to farmers
  • Soil Health Card: Soil testing and nutrient management
  • Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana: Organic farming promotion
