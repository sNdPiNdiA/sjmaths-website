Fundamental Concepts of Ecosystems

Understanding structure, functional components, and thermodynamic laws governing ecosystems.

  • Biotic Components: Includes producers (autotrophs like green plants), consumers (heterotrophs like herbivores and carnivores), and decomposers (saprotrophs like fungi and bacteria).
  • Abiotic Components: Comprises climatic factors (sunlight, temperature, precipitation) and topographic/edaphic factors (soil nutrients, pH, topography).
  • Energy Flow (Lindeman's 10% Law): Proposed by Raymond Lindeman in 1942; only about 10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next, limiting food chain lengths to 4-5 steps.
  • Ecological Pyramids: Graphical representations of trophic levels showing numbers, biomass, or energy. Pyramids of energy are always upright, whereas biomass pyramids in aquatic ecosystems can be inverted.

Terrestrial and Aquatic Biomes

Major global biomes categorized by dominant vegetation and climatic conditions.

  • Tropical Rainforest Biome: Found near the equator (Amazon, Congo Basin, Western Ghats in India); highest biodiversity with multi-layered canopy structures.
  • Savanna (Tropical Grassland): Characterized by continuous grass cover with scattered trees; found in East Africa, parts of Central India (scrub forests), and Northern Australia.
  • Taiga / Boreal Forest: Largest terrestrial biome; dominated by cold-tolerant evergreen conifers (pines, firs, spruces) spanning across Siberia, Canada, and Scandinavia.
  • Marine Pelagic and Benthic Biomes: Largest aquatic biome covering 71% of Earth; divided into photic (sunlit) and aphotic (dark) zones, with coral reefs serving as marine hotspots.

Biodiversity and Ecological Succession

Exploration of species diversity, ecological niches, and the step-by-step evolution of communities.

  • Levels of Biodiversity: Classified into Genetic diversity (variation within species), Species diversity (richness across species in a region), and Ecosystem diversity (variety of ecological niches).
  • Ecological Succession Phases: The directional change in vegetation over time: Nudation (bare area) -> Invasion -> Competition & Co-action -> Reaction -> Climax community.
  • Ecological Niche: Coined by J. Grinnell; describes the functional role and position of a species in its ecosystem, including its resource use and interaction with other organisms.
  • Ecotone and Edge Effect: An ecotone is a transition zone between two diverse ecosystems (e.g., mangroves between terrestrial and marine biomes). The 'edge effect' refers to higher species density in ecotones.

Biodiversity Hotspots in India and Conservation

Identification of critical conservation zones, endemism, and protected area frameworks in India and UP.

  • Criteria for Biodiversity Hotspot: Must contain at least 1,500 species of vascular plants (> 0.5% of the world's total) as endemics, and have lost at least 70% of its original primary habitat.
  • India's Four Hotspots: India hosts four recognized hotspots: The Himalayas, Indo-Burma, The Western Ghats (and Sri Lanka), and Sundaland (including Andaman and Nicobar Islands).
  • Dudhwa National Park (UP): Located in Lakhimpur Kheri district, UP; established as a national park in 1977, famous for Swamp Deer (Barasingha) and Tigers.
  • Protected Areas Category under Wildlife Act: Includes National Parks (strictest protection, no human activity), Wildlife Sanctuaries (limited human activities allowed), Conservation Reserves, and Community Reserves.
