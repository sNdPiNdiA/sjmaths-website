Pollution Control Core Study Notes

Pollution Control is a high-yielding topic in the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations, covering critical environmental legislation, statutory bodies, ambient air quality standards, and urban waste management frameworks. Aspirants must master statutory acts, landmark Supreme Court rulings, international protocols, and Uttar Pradesh-specific pollution mitigation initiatives.

Statutory Framework & Pollution Control Acts

Detailed analysis of core Indian legislations enacted to curb water, air, and environmental pollution.

  • Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974: Aims to maintain and restore the wholesomeness of water bodies. Empowered the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs) to set effluent standards.
  • Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981: Provided for the prevention, control, and abatement of air pollution. Included noise pollution within the ambit of air pollutants in 1987 amendment.
  • Environment (Protection) Act, 1986: An umbrella legislation enacted under Article 252 of the Constitution pursuant to the Stockholm Conference (1972). Grants sweeping powers to the Central Government to set emission and discharge standards.
  • National Green Tribunal (NGT) Act, 2010: Established a specialized tribunal for effective and expeditious disposal of cases relating to environmental protection and conservation of forests. Mandates civil cases to be disposed of within 6 months.

Air Pollution, NAAQS & Smog Parameters

Understanding particulate matter, criteria pollutants, and national ambient air quality standards critical for UPPSC and RO/ARO questions.

  • National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS): Formulated by CPCB under the Air Act, 1981, covering 12 pollutants including PM10, PM2.5, SO2, NO2, CO, Ozone, Lead, Ammonia, Benzene, Benzo(a)pyrene, Arsenic, and Nickel.
  • Particulate Matter (PM2.5 & PM10): PM2.5 refers to fine particles with diameter less than 2.5 micrometers that can penetrate deep into pulmonary alveoli; PM10 refers to inhalable particles less than 10 micrometers.
  • Photochemical Smog (Los Angeles Smog): Formed by the interaction of solar UV radiation with nitrogen oxides (NOx) and volatile organic compounds (VOCs). It is oxidizing in nature and produces secondary pollutants like Ozone and PAN.
  • London Smog (Classical Smog): Characterized by high concentration of sulfur dioxide (SO2) and particulate matter from coal combustion combined with cold, humid atmospheric conditions. It is reducing in nature.

Water Pollution, Eutrophication & Biochemical Oxygen Demand

Examination-focused concepts regarding water quality indicators, heavy metal toxicity, and aquatic ecosystem degradation.

  • Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD): The amount of dissolved oxygen consumed by aerobic microorganisms to biologically oxidize organic matter in water at 20°C over 5 days. Higher BOD indicates lower dissolved oxygen and higher organic pollution.
  • Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD): Measures the total quantity of oxygen required to oxidize both organic and inorganic chemical compounds in water using a strong chemical oxidant like potassium dichromate.
  • Eutrophication Process: Nutrient enrichment (Nitrates and Phosphates) in water bodies leading to algal blooms, depletion of dissolved oxygen, light cutoff, and ultimate aquatic ecosystem death.
  • Minamata and Itai-Itai Diseases: Minamata is caused by biological magnification of organic Mercury (methylmercury) in fish; Itai-Itai ('It hurts me, hurts me') is caused by chronic Cadmium poisoning affecting bones and joints.

Solid Waste Management & Hazardous Rules

Key provisions of the Solid Waste Management Rules, plastic waste bans, and e-waste handling frameworks in India and UP.

  • Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016: Replaced the 2000 Municipal Solid Wastes Rules. Mandated source segregation into Biodegradable, Non-biodegradable, and Domestic Hazardous wastes, and introduced Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR).
  • Plastic Waste Management (Amendment) Rules, 2021: Banned identified Single-Use Plastic (SUP) items with low utility and high littering potential starting July 1, 2022. Prohibited manufacture, import, stocking, distribution, and sale of items like earbuds, balloon sticks, and plastic flags.
  • E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022: Enforced strict recycling targets for manufacturers of electrical and electronic equipment. Introduced digital recycling certificates and centralized portal registration via CPCB.
  • Biomedical Waste Management Rules, 2016: Expanded coverage to veterinary hospitals and vaccination camps. Categorized waste into 4 distinct color-coded streams (Yellow, Red, White, Blue) for segregation and treatment.
