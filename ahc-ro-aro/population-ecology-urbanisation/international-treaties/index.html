International Treaties in Ecology and Environment Core Study Notes

International environmental treaties form the foundational bedrock for global conservation, climate mitigation, and sustainable development. For Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations, questions frequently target key conventions, implementation years, secretariat headquarters, and specific protocols ratified by India.

Key Global Biodiversity & Conservation Treaties

Conventions focused on protecting flora, fauna, wetlands, and migratory species across borders.

  • Ramsar Convention (1971): Intergovernmental treaty for the conservation and wise use of wetlands. Signed in Ramsar, Iran; came into force in 1975. India is a contracting party since 1982. Uttar Pradesh hosts multiple Ramsar sites including Upper Ganga River.
  • CITES (1973): Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora. It ensures that international trade in specimens of wild animals and plants does not threaten their survival.
  • Bonn Convention (CMS - 1979): Convention on the Conservation of Migratory Species of Wild Animals. It aims to conserve terrestrial, aquatic and avian migratory species throughout their range.
  • Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD - 1992): Adopted at the Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro, CBD has three main objectives: conservation of biological diversity, sustainable use of its components, and fair sharing of benefits arising from genetic resources.

Climate Change & Hazardous Substances Treaties

Global agreements aimed at tackling global warming, hazardous waste management, and chemical pollutants.

  • Basel Convention (1989): Controls the transboundary movements of hazardous wastes and their disposal. It aims to protect human health and the environment against the adverse effects of hazardous wastes.
  • Rotterdam Convention (1998): Promotes shared responsibilities in relation to importation of hazardous chemicals. Implements the Prior Informed Consent (PIC) procedure for certain hazardous chemicals and pesticides.
  • Stockholm Convention on POPs (2001): Global treaty to protect human health and the environment from Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs)—chemical substances that persist in the environment, bioaccumulate, and pose a risk of causing adverse effects.
  • Kigali Amendment (2016): An amendment to the Montreal Protocol to phase down Hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs)—potent greenhouse gases—by more than 80 over the next 30 years.
