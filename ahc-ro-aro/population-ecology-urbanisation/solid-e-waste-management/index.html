Solid and E-Waste Management Core Study Notes

Solid and Electronic Waste Management is an increasingly high-yield topic for the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations. Aspirants must master statutory definitions, rules notified by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC), Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) norms, and specific state-level data pertaining to Uttar Pradesh.

Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 - Key Provisions

Detailed breakdown of the statutory framework governing municipal solid waste across urban and rural local bodies in India.

  • Applicability: Applies beyond municipal areas to outgrowths, industrial townships, railway stations, pilgrim places, and special economic zones under state jurisdiction.
  • Mandatory Source Segregation: Households and commercial establishments must segregate waste into three streams: Biodegradable (Wet), Dry (Recyclable/Non-recyclable), and Domestic Hazardous Waste (diapers, expired medicines, batteries).
  • Responsibility of Bulk Waste Generators (BWGs): Establishments generating >100 kg/day waste, or gated communities with >50 flats, must treat biodegradable waste in-house via composting or biomethanation.
  • User Fee and Spot Fines: Local authorities are empowered to prescribe user fees for waste collection from households and levy spot fines for littering and non-segregation.
Waste Category Examples Proper Disposal Method
Wet Waste (Biodegradable) Kitchen waste, food remains, garden leaves, fruit peels Composting, vermicomposting, biomethanation
Dry Waste (Non-Biodegradable) Paper, plastic, glass, metal, packaging materials Recycling units, material recovery facilities (MRFs)

E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022

Comprehensive analysis of the updated legal framework governing electrical and electronic equipment (EEE) and Extended Producer Responsibility.

  • Scope of Coverage: Covers 106 items under expanded categories, including IT and telecommunication equipment, consumer electricals, large and small electrical appliances, and solar photo-voltaic panels/modules.
  • Centralized Portal Registration: All manufacturers, producers, refurbishers, and recyclers must compulsorily register on the centralized portal developed by the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB).
  • Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) Certificates: Producers fulfill obligations by purchasing EPR certificates from registered recyclers, thereby creating a market-based mechanism for e-waste recycling compliance.
  • Environmental Compensation: Strict financial penalties are imposed on entities failing to meet collection targets, adhering to the 'Polluter Pays' principle enshrined in environmental jurisprudence.
Stakeholder Primary Obligation Regulatory Authority
Producers (Manufacturers/Importers) Fulfill EPR targets, finance collection and recycling channels CPCB / State Pollution Control Boards
Recyclers Ensure environmentally sound dismantling and recycling without hazardous emissions CPCB & SPCB Joint Inspection
Refurbishers Collect old electronic items, extend life cycle, and channel residue to recyclers State Pollution Control Board (SPCB)

Hazardous Wastes and Bio-Medical Waste Rules

Overview of regulatory controls governing hazardous industrial chemicals, heavy metals, and clinical waste generation in India.

  • Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2016: Prohibits import of hazardous waste for disposal; regulates import for recycling, reuse, and recovery. Imposes liability on occupier for damages caused to environment.
  • Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016: Categorizes clinical waste into four color-coded streams (Yellow, Red, White, Blue) to ensure segregated incineration, autoclaving, and chemical disinfection.
  • Phase-out of Chlorinated Plastics: Bio-medical waste rules mandate phasing out chlorinated plastic bags, blood bags, and gloves to prevent toxic dioxin and furan emissions during incineration.
  • Role of Treatment, Storage and Disposal Facilities (TSDF): Industrial hazardous waste must be scientifically landfilled or incinerated in authorized TSDFs operated under strict SPCB surveillance.

Plastic Waste Management & UP-Specific Initiatives

Examination of single-use plastic bans, Extended Producer Responsibility for plastic packaging, and Uttar Pradesh's implementation strategies.

  • Single-Use Plastic (SUP) Ban: Nationwide ban enforced from July 1, 2022, on identified SUP items including earbuds with plastic sticks, plastic sticks for balloons, candy/ice-cream sticks, and thermocol decoration items.
  • Plastic Packaging EPR Guidelines: Notification of comprehensive guidelines on EPR for plastic packaging under Plastic Waste Management Amendment Rules, 2022, categorizing plastics into four distinct types.
  • Uttar Pradesh Solid Waste Management Initiatives: Establishment of Material Recovery Facilities (MRFs) across urban local bodies in UP, integrated waste-to-energy plants in major cities like Lucknow and Varanasi, and plastic road construction pilots.
  • Swachh Survekshan & UP Rankings: Cities in Uttar Pradesh like Ghaziabad, Noida, and Lucknow have progressively improved rankings under the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA) Swachh Survekshan framework through door-to-door collection and segregation.
