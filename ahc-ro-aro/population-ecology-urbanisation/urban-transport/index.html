Urban Transport, Ecology, and Environmental Impact Core Study Notes

Urban transport is a crucial component of urbanisation and environmental studies for the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations. It covers sustainable mobility, mass transit systems like metros in Uttar Pradesh, vehicular pollution control laws, and green transport initiatives essential for balancing rapid urbanization with ecological preservation.

Urban Transport Systems & Mass Rapid Transit (MRTS)

Detailed analysis of public transport infrastructure, metro networks in Uttar Pradesh, and transit-oriented development.

  • National Urban Transport Policy (NUTP): Formulated in 2006 by the Ministry of Urban Development, NUTP shifts the focus from 'moving vehicles' to 'moving people' by prioritizing public transport, non-motorized transport, and integrated land-use planning.
  • UP Metro Rail Networks: Uttar Pradesh has operational metro systems in Lucknow, Kanpur, Agra, Ghaziabad, Noida, and Greater Noida, managed by UP Metro Rail Corporation (UPMRC) and DMRC, significantly reducing urban congestion and carbon emissions.
  • Rapid Rail Transit (RRTS): The Delhi-Ghaziabad-Meerut RRTS (Namo Bharat) is India's first semi-high-speed rail corridor designed to connect regional urban nodes and reduce private vehicular commuting.
  • Transit-Oriented Development (TOD): A planning strategy that maximizes residential, business, and leisure space within walking distance of public transport stations, heavily promoted under UP Urban Planning policies.

Vehicular Pollution, Emission Norms & Control Laws

Examination of legislative acts, regulatory frameworks, and fuel standards governing air pollution in urban areas.

  • Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981: Enacted under Article 253 of the Constitution, this central legislation empowers State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs) to set standards for automobile emissions and penalize polluting transport vehicles.
  • Bharat Stage (BS) Emission Standards: Instituted by the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB), BS-VI norms mandate ultra-low sulfur diesel and petrol (max 10 ppm sulfur) and enforce advanced OBD (On-Board Diagnostics) systems, reducing particulate matter (PM) by up to 80%.
  • National Air Quality Index (AQI): Launched in 2014, AQI monitors 8 major pollutants including PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, CO, O3, NH3, and Pb, with urban transport acting as a primary contributor to poor and severe AQI brackets in UP cities like Ghaziabad and Meerut.
  • Electric Vehicles (EV) Policies in UP: The UP Electric Vehicle Manufacturing and Mobility Policy provides heavy subsidies on e-buses, e-autos, and two-wheelers to phase out fossil-fuel public transport and achieve zero-emission urban corridors.
