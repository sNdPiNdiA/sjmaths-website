Symmetry And Conciseness in Précis Writing Core Study Notes

Précis writing is a critical component of the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC Mains examinations. Mastery over symmetry and conciseness ensures that candidates can distill lengthy administrative, judicial, or general texts into one-third of their original length while preserving core arguments and formal tone.

Core Principles of Précis Writing

Fundamental rules governing clarity, brevity, and accurate condensation of source material.

  • The One-Third Rule: A standard précis must strictly be reduced to approximately 33% (one-third) of the word count of the original passage, with a permissible variance of plus or minus 5% unless specified otherwise.
  • Clarity of Central Theme: Skimming the passage enables candidates to identify the nucleus of the argument, ensuring peripheral details, redundant examples, and rhetorical flourishes are discarded.
  • Objective Third-Person Narrative: Précis must be written in indirect speech and third-person perspective ('It is stated', 'The author argues'), strictly avoiding first-person ('I', 'We') and second-person ('You') pronouns.
  • Elimination of Redundancy: Superfluous adjectives, repetitive idioms, figures of speech, and descriptive amplifications should be excised to achieve structural tightness.

Techniques of Symmetry and Logical Flow

Methods to maintain structural balance and smooth transition of thoughts in the condensed text.

  • Paragraph Cohesion: A précis should ideally consist of a single unbroken paragraph that reflects the chronological or logical progression of the original text without abrupt shifts.
  • Proportional Representation: If the source text spends 70% of its content on problem analysis and 30% on solutions, the précis must preserve roughly the same proportionate weight.
  • Syntactic Economy: Convert complex sentences into simple ones and long subordinate clauses into concise phrases or single-word modifiers (e.g., changing 'a man who possesses great courage' to 'a courageous man').
  • Avoiding Personal Commentary: Candidates must never add their own critique, agreement, or disagreement. The summary must reflect solely the author's viewpoint.

Formulation of Suitable Titles

Guidelines for creating concise, apt, and thematic titles that encapsulate the passage's essence.

  • Brevity in Titles: An effective title should be concise, ideally spanning 3 to 6 words, capturing the core theme without being overly vague or excessively detailed.
  • Alignment with Central Idea: The title must act as a signpost for the entire précis. If the passage deals with judicial delays in India, titles like 'Judicial Delays: Causes and Cures' are appropriate.
  • Avoid Interrogative Titles: Titles posed as questions (e.g., 'Is Justice Delayed?') should be avoided in formal administrative précis writing; declarative titles are preferred.

Administrative & Judicial Vocabulary Enhancement

Using precise bureaucratic terminology to replace verbose phrasing in UPPSC and AHC examinations.

  • Substitution of Phrases: Replace 'take into consideration' with 'consider', 'give authorization to' with 'authorize', and 'due to the fact that' with 'because'.
  • Legal and Official Terminology: Incorporate standardized terms like 'Prima facie' (प्रथम दृष्टया), 'Ultra vires' (अधिकार क्षेत्र से बाहर), and 'Suo motu' (स्वतः संज्ञान) where contextually exact.
  • Hindi Equivalents for Administrative Drafts: Use formal Hindi administrative terms such as 'संक्षेपण' (Précis), 'पूर्वाग्रह' (Prejudice), 'कार्यान्वयन' (Implementation), and 'पुनरीक्षण' (Revision) to maintain official tone.
