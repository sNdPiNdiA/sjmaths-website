Grammar Formatting Clarity Checks for Précis Writing Core Study Notes

Mastering grammar, formatting, and clarity checks is crucial for the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC Mains examinations. This topic ensures candidates condense passages accurately to one-third length while maintaining formal administrative tone, logical flow, and absolute syntactic correctness.

Core Techniques of Skimming and Central Theme Identification

The foundation of an effective précis lies in swift reading, recognizing core arguments, and discarding extraneous details without missing legal or administrative nuances.

  • First and Last Sentence Rule: Paragraphs in administrative texts usually introduce the central premise in the opening sentence and summarize or conclude in the final sentence, acting as high-yield zones for theme extraction.
  • Filtering Rhetoric and Examples: Aspirants must eliminate illustrative examples, metaphors, rhetorical flourishes, and detailed statistics, retaining only the overarching principle or statutory rule.
  • Keyword Tracking: Identify recurring legal terms, statutory sections (e.g., IPC, CrPC/BNSS, CPC), and administrative mandates that form the skeleton of the given passage.
  • Drafting a Working Title: The title must be concise (3-6 words), encapsulate the core theme precisely, and avoid being overly broad or cryptic.

Maintaining One-Third Length Proportion and Word Economy

Strict adherence to the one-third proportion is mandatory in Allahabad High Court RO/ARO examinations. Exceeding or falling drastically short of this limit invites negative marking.

  • Calculating Target Word Count: If the original passage contains 300 words, the précis must strictly fall within 95 to 105 words, maintaining the precise 1/3rd ratio.
  • Replacing Phrases with Single Words: Use one-word substitutions for lengthy clauses (e.g., replace 'a period of ten years' with 'a decade' or 'दशक'; 'that which cannot be avoided' with 'inevitable' or 'अपरिहार्य').
  • Eliminating Filler Words: Remove conversational fillers, redundant prepositional phrases, and tautologies like 'true fact', 'future projection', or 'परस्पर सहयोग'.
  • Counting Rule Precision: Hyphenated words generally count as a single word, but prepositions, articles, and conjunctions must be vigilantly accounted for during final tallying.

Grammar, Syntax, and Punctuation Standards in Précis

Grammatical accuracy is non-negotiable. Examiners deduct marks heavily for tense inconsistencies, incorrect agreements, and faulty punctuation in both English and Hindi precis.

  • Tense Consistency: If the original passage is primarily in the past tense (e.g., historical judicial rulings), the précis must maintain past tense unless stating a universal or statutory truth.
  • Subject-Verb Agreement: Ensure singular subjects take singular verbs and collective nouns (like 'bench', 'committee', 'पीठ', 'समिति') follow standard administrative concord rules in English and Hindi.
  • Punctuation Discipline: Avoid excessive comma splices. Use semi-colons and full stops correctly to break complex, rambling sentences into clean, readable administrative units.
  • Active vs. Passive Voice Preference: While both voices are acceptable, passive voice is often preferred in formal legal and administrative drafting when the focus is on the action or law rather than the doer.

Eliminating Personal Opinions and Preserving Logical Flow

A précis is an objective reflection of the source text, not an editorial commentary. Candidates must maintain third-person narration and seamless transitions.

  • Ban on First and Second Person Pronouns: Never use 'I', 'we', 'you', 'my', 'our', or Hindi equivalents like 'मैं', 'हम', 'आप' unless quoting directly from the text.
  • Neutral Reporting Verbs: Use objective reporting terms such as 'the author states', 'the judgment notes', 'लेखक उल्लेख करता है', 'न्यायालय इंगित करता है' instead of emotive verbs.
  • Seamless Logical Transitions: Use appropriate conjunctions and transitional markers (e.g., 'consequently', 'furthermore', 'परिणामस्वरूप', 'इसके अतिरिक्त') to link ideas without abrupt leaps.
  • Omission of External Knowledge: Do not add your own analytical insights, critiques, or contemporary updates to the passage, even if factually correct.
