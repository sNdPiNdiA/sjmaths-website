Constitutional Posts: Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) & Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Core Study Notes

This comprehensive study module covers the constitutional provisions, powers, duties, and recent legislative updates regarding the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) and the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) of India. It is highly curated for UPPSC and Allahabad High Court RO/ARO exams, focusing on Articles 148-151, Article 324, and the landmark CEC Act of 2023.

Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) - Constitutional Framework

The CAG is the guardian of the public purse and heads the Indian Audit and Accounts Department. Articles 148 to 151 of the Constitution govern this office.

  • Article 148: Appointment and Oath: The CAG is appointed by the President of India by warrant under his hand and seal. The CAG takes an oath before the President as per the Third Schedule.
  • Article 149: Duties and Powers: Authorizes Parliament to prescribe the duties and powers of the CAG. The CAG audits accounts related to all expenditure from the Consolidated Fund of India, Consolidated Fund of each State, and Union Territories.
  • Article 150: Form of Accounts: The accounts of the Union and of the States shall be kept in such form as the President may, on the advice of the CAG, prescribe.
  • Article 151: Audit Reports: The CAG submits audit reports of the Union to the President (who places them before Parliament) and of the States to the Governor (who places them before the State Legislature).
  • Tenure and Independence: The CAG holds office for a term of 6 years or up to the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier. The CAG can only be removed in like manner and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court Judge. Salary and administrative expenses are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India (non-votable).

Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and Election Commission

Article 324 of the Constitution vests the superintendence, direction, and control of elections in the Election Commission of India.

  • Article 324: Composition: The Election Commission consists of the Chief Election Commissioner and such number of other Election Commissioners, if any, as the President may from time to time fix.
  • Tenure and Conditions of Service: The CEC and other ECs hold office for a term of 6 years or up to the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier. They enjoy the same status, salary, and perks as a Judge of the Supreme Court of India.
  • Removal Safeguards: The CEC cannot be removed from office except in like manner and on the like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court. However, other Election Commissioners can be removed by the President only on the recommendation of the CEC.
  • The CEC and other ECs Act, 2023: This Act establishes a Selection Committee for appointments: Prime Minister (Chairperson), Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha (Member), and a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the PM (Member). A Search Committee headed by the Cabinet Secretary prepares a panel of 5 names.

Comparative Analysis: CAG vs. CEC

A direct comparison of the constitutional safeguards, post-retirement eligibility, and financial charges of the two critical watchdog offices.

  • Post-Retirement Employment: The CAG is strictly ineligible for further office under the Government of India or any State Government after retirement. The Constitution does not explicitly bar the CEC or ECs from further government appointments.
  • Financial Autonomy: The administrative expenses of the CAG's office are charged upon the Consolidated Fund of India. The expenses of the Election Commission are voted upon by Parliament, though the salaries of the commissioners are equivalent to Supreme Court judges.

Recent Appointments and Current Affairs (2023-2024)

Key updates on current office holders and landmark judicial rulings crucial for RO/ARO aspirants.

  • Current CAG of India: Girish Chandra Murmu is the 14th Comptroller and Auditor General of India. He assumed office on August 8, 2020. He also serves as the Chairman of the UN Panel of External Auditors.
  • Current CEC of India: Rajiv Kumar is the 25th Chief Election Commissioner of India, heading the commission during the 2024 General Elections.
  • Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India (2023): A landmark Supreme Court ruling which directed that the CEC and ECs should be appointed by the President on the advice of a committee comprising the PM, the Leader of Opposition, and the Chief Justice of India (CJI), until Parliament enacts a law. This prompted the 2023 Act.
  • First Appointments under the 2023 Act: In March 2024, Gyanesh Kumar and Sukhbir Singh Sandhu were appointed as Election Commissioners, becoming the first to be selected under the new panel system excluding the CJI.
