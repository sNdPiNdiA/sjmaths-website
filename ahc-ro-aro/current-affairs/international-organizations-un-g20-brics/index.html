International Organizations: UN, G20, and BRICS Core Study Notes

This comprehensive study guide covers the structural frameworks, recent summits, and geopolitical significance of the United Nations (UN), G20, and BRICS. Designed specifically for the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC exams, it highlights India's leadership roles, key administrative appointments, and specific events hosted in Uttar Pradesh.

United Nations (UN): Organs, Key Appointments & India

The United Nations remains the cornerstone of multilateralism. Understanding its principal organs and India's active diplomatic engagements is vital for state civil services.

  • Principal Organs and Headquarters: The UN has six principal organs: General Assembly (UNGA), Security Council (UNSC), Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC), Trusteeship Council, International Court of Justice (ICJ - located in The Hague, Netherlands), and the Secretariat. All except the ICJ are headquartered in New York.
  • UN Security Council (UNSC) Structure: Consists of 15 members: 5 permanent members (P5: US, UK, France, Russia, China) with veto power, and 10 non-permanent members elected for two-year terms by the UNGA. India has served as a non-permanent member eight times, most recently in 2021-2022.
  • India's Permanent Representative to the UN: Senior diplomat Parvathaneni Harish was appointed as India's Permanent Representative to the United Nations in New York in August 2024, succeeding Ruchira Kamboj.
  • UN General Assembly (UNGA) 79th Session: The 79th session of the UNGA opened in September 2024. Philemon Yang of Cameroon was elected as the President of the 79th session. The theme focused on unity in diversity for the advancement of peace, sustainable development, and human dignity.

G20: India's Presidency and Uttar Pradesh Connect

India's G20 Presidency from December 1, 2022, to November 30, 2023, was a landmark event. It showcased India's cultural heritage and leadership in the Global South, with significant events hosted in Uttar Pradesh.

  • Theme and Logo: The theme of India's G20 Presidency was 'Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam' or 'One Earth · One Family · One Future', drawn from the ancient Sanskrit text of the Maha Upanishad. The logo featured a blooming lotus with the Earth.
  • Historic Inclusion of the African Union: Under India's initiative, the African Union (AU) was admitted as a permanent member of the G20 during the New Delhi Summit, making the group more representative of the Global South.
  • G20 Meetings in Uttar Pradesh: Uttar Pradesh hosted 11 G20 meetings across four cities: Varanasi, Lucknow, Agra, and Greater Noida. Key meetings included the G20 Development Ministers' Meeting in Varanasi (chaired by EAM S. Jaishankar) and the Digital Economy Working Group Meeting in Lucknow.
  • G20 Troika: The G20 Troika consists of the previous, current, and incoming presidencies. During India's presidency, the Troika comprised Indonesia, India, and Brazil. Currently (2024), it comprises India, Brazil, and South Africa.

BRICS: Expansion and Geopolitical Realignment

BRICS has evolved from an investment acronym into a powerful geopolitical bloc representing major emerging economies, challenging traditional Western-dominated financial systems.

  • The 2024 Expansion: Effective January 1, 2024, BRICS officially welcomed four new members: Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, and the United Arab Emirates (UAE). Saudi Arabia was invited and participates in activities but has not finalized formal integration. Argentina declined the invitation.
  • 16th BRICS Summit (Kazan, Russia): Held in October 2024 under the theme 'Strengthening Multilateralism for Just Global Development and Security'. It was the first summit attended by the newly expanded membership. The 'Kazan Declaration' was adopted.
  • New Development Bank (NDB): Established during the 6th BRICS Summit in Fortaleza (2014) with an initial authorized capital of $100 billion. Headquartered in Shanghai, China. Dilma Rousseff (former President of Brazil) is the current President of NDB.
  • Contingent Reserve Arrangement (CRA): A framework established by BRICS in 2015 to provide protection against global liquidity pressures. It acts as a financial safety net for member countries during balance of payment crises.
