Role Of Press Literature in Indian National Movement Core Study Notes

The Indian press and literature acted as the primary ideological catalysts and communication networks during the freedom struggle. For Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations, questions frequently focus on founding editors, newspapers, repressive acts, and literary masterpieces that shaped national consciousness.

Pioneering Newspapers and Founders

Core journalistic ventures launched by social reformers and nationalist leaders to awaken political consciousness.

  • Bengal Gazette (1780): India's first newspaper, published by James Augustus Hickey. It was confiscated by the government in 1882 due to its fearless criticism of the East India Company administration.
  • Raja Ram Mohan Roy's Publications: Published Sambad Kaumudi (Bengali weekly, 1821) championing social reforms and Mirat-ul-Akbar (Persian journal, 1822), which he voluntarily ceased publishing to protest the 1823 Press Licensing Regulations.
  • Amrita Bazar Patrika (1868): Founded by Sisir Kumar Ghosh and Motilal Ghosh in Jessore. To evade the Vernacular Press Act of 1878, it converted overnight into an English-language newspaper.
  • Kesari and Mahratta (1881): Launched by Lokmanya Bal Gangadhar Tilak. Kesari was published in Marathi (edited initially by Agarkar and later Tilak) and Mahratta was published in English, serving as the militant voice of nationalism.
  • The Leader and Abhyudaya: Important UP-centric nationalist publications. 'The Leader' was founded in 1909 by Madan Mohan Malaviya in Allahabad to propagate Moderate nationalist views.

Colonial Repressive Press Legislation

A chronological overview of legislative tools utilized by the British administration to muzzle the free press in India.

  • Censorship of Press Act, 1799: Enacted by Lord Wellesley anticipating French invasion fears; mandated pre-censorship on all publications and required printer's/editor's name on materials.
  • Licensing Regulations, 1823: Introduced by Acting Governor-General John Adams; made starting or operating a press without a government license a penal offense, targeting native-owned journals.
  • Vernacular Press Act (VPA), 1878: Nicknamed the 'Gagging Act', it was passed by Lord Lytton to stop seditious writing in Indian language papers. District magistrates were empowered to demand security bonds and confiscate printing equipment without appeal.
  • Repeal of VPA, 1882: Lord Ripon repealed the infamous Vernacular Press Act, restoring freedom to vernacular newspapers and earning immense goodwill from the Indian intelligentsia.
  • Indian Press Act, 1910: Empowered provincial governments to demand heavy security deposits from printing presses and forfeit them for publishing 'objectionable material'. Over 1,000 presses were penalized.

Literary Contributions and Revolutionary Literature

How novels, poems, plays, and pamphlets galvanized the masses and infused patriotic fervor across the nation.

  • Anandamath (1882): Authored by Bankim Chandra Chatterjee; set against the backdrop of the Sannyasi Rebellion. It contains the immortal song 'Vande Mataram', which became the national anthem of the freedom struggle.
  • Bharat Durdasha (1880): Written by Bharatendu Harishchandra, the father of modern Hindi literature. This allegorical play depicted the miserable political and economic plight of India under British rule.
  • Ghadr Journal and Revolutionary Pamphlets: The Ghadr newspaper (1913), founded by Lala Hardayal and Sohan Singh Bhakna, was printed in multiple languages in San Francisco to ignite armed rebellion among overseas Indians.
  • Bhagat Singh's 'Why I Am an Atheist': Written in Lahore Jail in 1930, this seminal essay articulated a rationalist, socialist ideological framework for the Indian revolutionary movement, moving beyond mere terrorism.
  • Rabindranath Tagore's Literary Stand: Composed 'Jana Gana Mana' and numerous patriotic poems and essays; famously renounced his British Knighthood in 1919 in protest against the Jallianwala Bagh massacre.

Role of Press in Uttar Pradesh Freedom Struggle

Key newspapers and literary hubs based in United Provinces (modern UP) that drove the anti-colonial narrative.

  • Pioneer of Allahabad: Started in 1865 by George Allen in Allahabad; primarily served as the mouthpiece of British bureaucratic interests in North India, prompting counter-nationalist papers.
  • Pratap of Kanpur: Founded in 1913 by Ganesh Shankar Vidyarthi. It played an exemplary role in exposing oppression against peasants (Bijolia movement) and supporting revolutionary figures like Chandrashekhar Azad.
  • Hindi Journalism in UP: Journals like 'Saraswati' (edited by Mahavir Prasad Dwivedi from 1903 in Allahabad) standardized modern Hindi prose and infused national pride through literary critiques.
  • Aaj of Varanasi: Launched in 1920 by Shiv Prasad Gupta in Varanasi, 'Aaj' was a prominent nationalist Hindi daily that strongly supported the Non-Cooperation Movement and reported nationalist activities across UP.
