INA Trials and the RIN Mutiny Core Study Notes

The INA Trials and the Royal Indian Navy (RIN) Mutiny of 1945-1946 marked the final revolutionary crescendo of the Indian National Movement. They demonstrated the collapse of British colonial authority over the armed forces, accelerating the decision to transfer power.

Background and Context of INA Trials

Following the defeat of the Axis powers, the British government decided to prosecute captured personnel of Subhash Chandra Bose's Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj) for treason.

  • Red Fort Trials (लाल किला मुकदमे): Public and court-martial proceedings held at the Red Fort in Delhi from November 1945 to May 1946 against officers of the INA.
  • The Accused Trio (अभियुक्त त्रय): The first and most prominent trial grouped three officers representing different communities and arms: G.S. Dhillon (Sikh), Prem Kumar Sahgal (Hindu), and Shah Nawaz Khan (Muslim).
  • Defense Committee (बचाव समिति): The Indian National Congress set up a strong defense team including prominent lawyers like Bhulabhai Desai (lead counsel), Jawaharlal Nehru, Tej Bahadur Sapru, Asaf Ali, and Kailash Nath Katju.
  • Verdict and Commutation (फैसला और सजा कम करना): Although the court found them guilty of treason and sentenced them to transportation for life, massive public pressure forced Commander-in-Chief Claude Auchinleck to remit the sentences.

The Royal Indian Navy (RIN) Mutiny

Starting as a grievance over food and racial abuse, the naval strike transformed into an anti-imperialist political revolt that spread across multiple ports.

  • HMIS Talwar (एच.एम.आई.एस. तलवार): The mutiny began on February 18, 1946, at the Royal Indian Navy signal school (HMIS Talwar) in Bombay, when ratings went on a hunger strike protesting bad food and racist insult by commanding officers.
  • Spread of the Revolt (विद्रोह का विस्तार): The strike spread rapidly to other ships, shore establishments, and naval bases across India, including Karachi, Madras, Vizag, and Calcutta, involving over 20,000 ratings.
  • Naval Central Strike Committee (नौसैनिक केंद्रीय हड़ताल समिति): Led by M.S. Khan, the ratings formed a Central Strike Committee, raising the Congress, Muslim League, and Communist flags unitedly on ship masts.
  • Popular Solidarity (लोकप्रिय एकजुटता): The working class and general public in Bombay expressed massive solidarity by organizing general strikes, bringing food to striking sailors, and erecting barricades against British troops.

Political Response and Surrender

The nationalist leadership found themselves in a delicate dilemma: supporting an armed military revolt could disrupt orderly transfer of power and invite brutal repression.

  • Role of Sardar Patel (सरदार पटेल की भूमिका): Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel played a decisive role in persuading the mutineers to surrender, assuring them that the Congress would prevent victimization by authorities.
  • Surrender Statement (आत्मसमर्पण वक्तव्य): The mutineers surrendered on February 23, 1946, with the historic declaration: 'Our strike has been a historic event in the life of our nation... We surrenders [sic] to our people, not to the British.'
  • Attlee's Announcement (एटली की घोषणा): Deeply shaken by the realization that the loyalty of the armed forces could no longer be taken for granted, British Prime Minister Clement Attlee announced the Cabinet Mission and the definitive deadline for British withdrawal.

Significance and Impact on British Rule

These uprisings shattered the foundational myth that the British Raj rested on the willing obedience of the Indian armed forces.

  • Collapse of Administrative Pillar (प्रशासनिक स्तंभ का पतन): The British realized that maintaining India by force was no longer militarily or economically viable after World War II, as Indian soldiers could no longer be trusted to suppress national movements.
  • Communal Unity in Action (कार्रवाई में सांप्रदायिक एकता): Both the INA trials and the RIN mutiny witnessed remarkable solidarity across Hindu, Muslim, and Sikh lines, temporarily blunting the divisive narrative of the Muslim League.
