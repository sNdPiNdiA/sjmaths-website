Home Rule League Movement Core Study Notes

The Home Rule League movement represented a new phase of the Indian national movement, drawing inspiration from the Irish Home Rule movement. Initiated by Bal Gangadhar Tilak and Annie Besant in 1916, it aimed to achieve self-government (Home Rule) within the British Empire through constitutional means, significantly bridging the gap between moderates and extremists.

Background and Causes of the Movement

The stagnation of the moderate politics, the impact of World War I, and the need for a national mass agitation created a fertile ground for the Home Rule movement.

  • Disillusionment with Moderates: The moderate method of 'prayer and petition' had failed to yield substantial political concessions from the British colonial administration, necessitating aggressive mass action.
  • Impact of World War I: India's forced participation in World War I (1914-1918) caused severe economic hardship, high taxation, and inflation, creating widespread anti-British sentiment among the masses.
  • Readiness of the Masses: The Indian masses were politically awakened through earlier nationalist struggles like the Swadeshi Movement and were ready to participate in a structured national campaign.
  • Role of Annie Besant and Tilak: Both leaders recognized that the Congress party was inactive due to the Surat Split (1907) and required an energetic auxiliary movement to pressurize the British government.

Objectives, Methods, and Program

The Home Rule League utilized innovative organizational methods to popularize the demand for self-government across urban and rural India.

  • Core Objective: To attain self-government (Home Rule) within the British Empire through constitutional, peaceful, and legitimate means.
  • Propaganda and Education: Extensive use of public meetings, lectures, pamphlets, newspapers (Tilak's *Kesari* and *Maratha*; Besant's *New India* and *Commonweal*), and libraries to educate citizens.
  • Organizational Structure: Establishment of active local committees, reading rooms, and youth training sections to maintain round-the-clock political agitation.
  • Social Reform Links: Leagues actively campaigned against untouchability, promoted vernacular education, and advocated for linguistic provinces and social equality.

Significance and Impact of the Movement

The Home Rule movement fundamentally transformed the character of the Indian National Congress and set the stage for the mass Gandhian era.

  • Shift from Elite to Mass Politics: It converted the national movement from a middle-class intellectual discussion into a vibrant mass agitation involving peasants, students, and workers.
  • Lucknow Pact (1916): The pressure exerted by the Home Rule Leagues facilitated the reunion of Moderates and Extremists at the Lucknow Congress session, and also brought the INC and Muslim League closer.
  • Montagu Declaration (1917): Fearing the intensity of the agitation, Edwin Montagu, the Secretary of State, issued the historic August 1917 Declaration promising progressive realization of responsible government in India.
  • Training Ground for Future Leaders: The movement provided a cadre of dedicated leaders (such as Jawaharlal Nehru, B.G. Horniman, and K.M. Munshi) who later became frontline leaders of the national movement under Gandhi.

Decline and Merger into Congress

By 1918-1920, the movement lost its separate organizational identity as political dynamics shifted towards Gandhian methods.

  • Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms Disappointment: The announcement of the Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (Diarchy) in 1918 divided the nationalist ranks on whether to cooperate with the reforms or reject them outright.
  • Tilak's Absence for Libel Case: Tilak went to England in 1918 to pursue a defamation case against Sir Valentine Chirol (author of *Indian Unrest*), leaving the movement temporarily without its principal co-founder.
  • Rise of Mahatma Gandhi: Gandhi's successful local Satyagrahas in Champaran (1917), Kheda (1918), and Ahmedabad (1918) captured the imagination of the masses and redirected national energy.
  • Merger into Swarajya Sabha: In 1920, Mahatma Gandhi was elected President of the All India Home Rule League, and the organization was renamed 'Swarajya Sabha', eventually merging into the Indian National Congress.
