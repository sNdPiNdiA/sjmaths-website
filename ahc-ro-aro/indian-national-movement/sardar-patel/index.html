Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel and National Integration Core Study Notes

Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, revered as the 'Iron Man of India', played a monumental role in the Indian National Movement, the organization of the Congress party, and the Herculean task of integrating over 500 princely states. For Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC aspirants, questions frequently focus on the Bardoli Satyagraha, Karachi Session 1931, and Instrument of Accession.

Early Political Activism & Satyagrahas

Patel's transition from a successful barrister to a mass leader rooted in Gandhian philosophy.

  • Godhra Municipal Board (1917): Patel entered public life by being elected as the sanitation commissioner of Godhra, marking his administrative competence early on.
  • Kheda Satyagraha (1918): Organized peasants of Kheda district to demand revenue remission due to crop failure. Mahatma Gandhi acknowledged Patel's exceptional organizational capabilities during this campaign.
  • Ahmedabad Municipality (1924): Elected as the President of Ahmedabad Municipality, where he worked extensively on improving sanitation, clean water supply, and educational infrastructure.
  • Nagpur Flag Satyagraha (1923): Successfully led the Satyagraha against the British ban on hoisting the national flag in Nagpur, showcasing his firm resolve and tactical brilliance.

Bardoli Satyagraha (1928) & The Title 'Sardar'

The landmark agrarian movement that showcased Patel's unmatched prowess in peasant mobilization.

  • Cause of Agitation: The colonial government arbitrarily hiked land revenue by 22% in the Bardoli taluka of Surat district despite agricultural distress.
  • Role of Women: Women of Bardoli, notably led by activists like Mithubeen Petit, actively participated, and it was during this movement that women gave him the title 'Sardar' (Leader).
  • Maxwell-Broomfield Inquiry: Faced with massive non-violent resistance and social boycott of government officials, the British government appointed the Maxwell-Broomfield Commission, which reduced the hike to 6.03%.
  • Organizational Blueprint: Patel divided Bardoli into camps, established intelligence networks, and ensured non-payment of taxes with strict discipline and unity among Patidars and Kaliparaj communities.

Integration of Princely States & State Reorganization

Patel's crowning achievement in preventing the Balkanization of India through diplomacy, persuasion, and decisive action.

  • States Department (July 1947): Created by the Interim Government; Sardar Patel was placed at its helm with V.P. Menon as the Secretary to handle the accession of 562+ princely states.
  • Instrument of Accession & Standstill Agreement: States were required to accede to the Indian Dominion on three core subjects: Defence, External Affairs, and Communications, while retaining internal autonomy initially.
  • Integration of Junagadh, Hyderabad, and Kashmir: Junagadh acceded via a decisive public plebiscite; Hyderabad was integrated through 'Operation Polo' (Police Action, Sept 1948); Kashmir acceded via Maharaja Hari Singh signing the Instrument of Accession.
  • Iron Will & Pragmatism: Patel used a balanced mix of 'Iron Fist in a Velvet Glove' approach, offering privy purses to princes while making it clear that secession was not an option.

Role in the Constituent Assembly & Administrative Architecture

Patel's foundational contributions to India's constitutional framework and civil services.

  • Chairman of Crucial Committees: He headed key committees of the Constituent Assembly, including the Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities and Tribal and Excluded Areas, and the Provincial Constitution Committee.
  • Architect of All India Services: Patel staunchly defended the continuance of an all-India administrative apparatus, successfully championing the creation of the Indian Administrative Service (IAS) and Indian Police Service (IPS), calling them the 'Steel Frame of India'.
  • Secular and Democratic Vision: Despite allegations of being anti-minority by critics, Patel consistently advocated for equal rights and protection for minorities in independent India, emphasizing that loyalty to the state must be absolute.
