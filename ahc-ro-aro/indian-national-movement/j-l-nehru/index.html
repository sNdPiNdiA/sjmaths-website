Jawaharlal Nehru in the Indian National Movement Core Study Notes

Jawaharlal Nehru was a central figure in Indian politics before and after independence, serving as the first Prime Minister of India. For Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations, his role as a socialist leader, architect of modern Indian foreign policy, author of key historical works, and president of landmark Congress sessions is of high yield.

Early Influences, Non-Cooperation and Trade Unionism

Nehru's transition from a moderate liberal background to a radical nationalist and socialist under Mahatma Gandhi and international Marxist thought.

  • Meeting Mahatma Gandhi (1916): Nehru first met Gandhi at the Lucknow Congress in 1916. Gandhi's methods deeply fascinated Nehru, drawing him directly into mass-based national politics.
  • Oudh Kisan Sabha & Peasant Mobilization (1920): Nehru toured the rural interiors of Awadh in 1920, witnessing acute peasant distress. This led to the formation of the Oudh Kisan Sabha, laying the foundation of his lifelong connect with Indian peasantry.
  • Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22): He actively participated in the Non-Cooperation Movement and was arrested for the first time in December 1921. He steadfastly opposed Gandhi's suspension of the movement after the Chauri Chaura incident.
  • Trade Union Affiliation: Nehru expanded the Congress base into the working class. He served as the President of the All India Trade Union Congress (AITUC) in 1929 at Jharia.

Radicalization, Socialism and the Lahore Congress (1929)

Nehru's tour of Europe and Soviet Russia in 1927 shaped his socialist ideology, culminating in his historic presidency of the 1929 Lahore Congress.

  • Brussels Congress (1927): Nehru attended the Congress of Oppressed Nationalities in Brussels as an official delegate of the INC, where he came into contact with international anti-imperialist leaders and Marxists.
  • Independence for India League (1928): Along with Subhas Chandra Bose, Nehru founded the Independence for India League within the Congress to pressure the leadership into demanding complete independence rather than dominion status.
  • Lahore Congress Presidential Address (1929): Elected Congress President at a young age, Nehru famously declared in his presidential address that 'I am a socialist and a republican,' steering the INC toward complete independence (Purna Swaraj).
  • First Independence Day (Jan 26, 1930): Under Nehru's leadership as Congress President, the tricolor flag was unfurled on the banks of the Ravi River in Lahore on January 26, 1930, celebrating the first Independence Day.

Literary Works, Ideology and Constitutional Role

Nehru's extensive prison writings shaped intellectual discourse in India, while his institutional contributions laid the groundwork for Indian democracy.

  • Glimpses of World History (1934): A collection of 196 letters written from various district and central jails to his daughter Indira Gandhi, detailing global history from an anti-colonial and socialist perspective.
  • An Autobiography (1936): Also known as 'Toward Freedom', this deeply introspective work examines the Indian national movement, his evolution under Gandhi, and his critique of Indian society and British rule.
  • The Discovery of India (1946): Written during his imprisonment in Ahmednagar Fort (1942-45), it presents a panoramic survey of Indian history, culture, and philosophy, emphasizing the composite nature of Indian civilization.
  • The Objectives Resolution (1946): On December 13, 1946, Nehru moved the historic Objectives Resolution in the Constituent Assembly, outlining the foundational principles and philosophy of the future Indian Constitution.
