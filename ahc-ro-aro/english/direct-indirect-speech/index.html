Direct and Indirect Speech for Allahabad High Court RO/ARO Core Study Notes

Direct and Indirect Speech is a high-scoring and critical component of the General English section in the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations. Candidates are tested on their ability to accurately transform statements, interrogatives, imperatives, and exclamatories while adhering strictly to tense shifts, pronoun changes, and adverbial modifications.

Fundamentals of Reporting Verb and Speech

Understanding the structural division of a sentence into Reporting Clause and Reported Clause is the prerequisite for transformation.

  • Reporting Verb Clause: The clause containing the speaker and the reporting verb (e.g., He said to me). It dictates whether the tense of the reported speech will change.
  • Reported Speech Clause: The actual words spoken by the speaker enclosed within quotation marks ("..."). These undergo changes in pronouns, tenses, and time markers.
  • Change of 'Said to': 'Said to' transforms into 'told' for statements, 'asked/inquired' for questions, 'ordered/requested/advised' for commands, and 'exclaimed' for exclamations.
  • Use of Conjunctions: Statements take 'that', interrogatives take 'if/whether' (for yes/no) or retain wh-word, and imperatives take 'to' infinitive.
Sentence Type Reporting Verb Change Conjunction Used
Assertive (Statement) said to -> told that
Interrogative (Question) said to -> asked / inquired if / whether or Wh-word
Imperative (Command/Request) ordered / requested / advised to / not to
Exclamatory (Emotion) exclaimed with joy/sorrow that

Rules for Pronoun and Possessive Shift

Pronouns inside the reported speech must change according to the speaker and listener based on the universal SON formula.

  • First Person Pronouns: Pronouns like I, me, my, we, us, our change according to the Subject of the reporting verb.
  • Second Person Pronouns: Pronouns like you, your, yours change according to the Object of the reporting verb.
  • Third Person Pronouns: Pronouns like he, she, it, they, him, them remain unchanged (No Change).
  • Case Retention: When changing pronouns, ensure the grammatical case (subjective, objective, possessive) matches the target noun/pronoun.

Tense and Modal Auxiliary Transformations

Tense changes occur strictly when the reporting verb is in the Past Tense, unless a universal truth or historical fact is stated.

  • Simple Present to Simple Past: V1/Do/Does changes to V2/Did. Example: 'He writes' becomes 'He wrote'.
  • Present Continuous to Past Continuous: Is/am/are + V1-ing changes to was/were + V1-ing.
  • Present Perfect to Past Perfect: Has/have + V3 changes to had + V3.
  • Modal Verbs Conversion: Shall/Will change to should/would, Can changes to could, May changes to might. Must changes to had to or remains must.
  • Exceptions to Tense Change: If the reported speech expresses a universal truth, habitual action, or historical fact, the tense remains unchanged regardless of the reporting verb.

Time and Place Adverbs Conversion

Words denoting nearness in time or place are changed to words showing distance when converting to indirect speech.

  • Now to Then: 'Now' changes to 'then' in indirect narration.
  • Here to There: 'Here' changes to 'there'.
  • This to That: 'This' changes to 'that', and 'these' changes to 'those'.
  • Tomorrow and Yesterday: 'Tomorrow' changes to 'the next day' or 'the following day'. 'Yesterday' changes to 'the previous day' or 'the day before'.
