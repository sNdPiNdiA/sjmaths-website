Parts Of Speech Core Study Notes

Parts of Speech form the foundational bedrock of English grammar assessed in Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations. Mastery over word classes enables candidates to excel in sentence correction, spot the error, fill-in-the-blanks, and active-passive transformations with pinpoint accuracy.

Nouns and Pronouns

Nouns name entities, while pronouns replace them to avoid repetition. High Court exams frequently test uncountable noun usages, compound nouns, and relative pronoun cases.

  • Proper and Common Nouns: Proper nouns (e.g., Allahabad, Ganga) begin with capital letters and rarely take articles unless used metaphorically. Common nouns represent generic classes.
  • Collective Nouns and Agreement: Collective nouns (jury, committee, flock) take singular verbs when acting as a unit, but plural verbs when members act individually.
  • Uncountable Nouns Trap: Words like scenery, machinery, furniture, information, and luggage are strictly singular, never take '-s' or '-es', and use singular verbs.
  • Case of Pronouns after 'Than'/'As': Pronouns following 'than' or 'as' must be in the subjective case if comparing subjects (e.g., 'He is taller than I [am]').
  • Relative Pronouns Usage: 'Who' is subjective, 'whom' is objective, and 'whose' denotes possession. Use 'that' after superlative degrees, 'all', 'same', and 'only'.

Verbs and Adverbs

Verbs express action, state, or occurrence, while adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. Subject-verb agreement and adverb placement errors are frequent in Allahabad High Court papers.

  • Transitive vs. Intransitive Verbs: Transitive verbs require a direct object to complete meaning (e.g., 'She wrote a letter'), whereas intransitive verbs do not ('The baby sleeps').
  • Subject-Verb Agreement Rule: Two or more singular subjects connected by 'and' usually take a plural verb unless they express a single idea (e.g., 'Bread and butter is my favorite').
  • Inversion of Verbs: Negative adverbs starting a sentence (e.g., 'Seldom', 'Hardly', 'Never') require verb inversion (helping verb precedes the subject).
  • Placement of Adverbs: Adverbs of frequency (always, never, often) are placed before the main verb but after auxiliary verbs (e.g., 'He always arrives late').
  • Confusing Verbs (Lie vs. Lay): 'Lie' (to recline) is intransitive (lie, lay, lain); 'Lay' (to place) is transitive (lay, laid, laid).

Adjectives and Determiners

Adjectives qualify nouns and pronouns. Competitive exams test degree formations, correct order of adjectives, and subtle distinctions between determiners like 'few/little' and 'each/every'.

  • Degrees of Comparison: Positive, comparative, and superlative forms. Adjectives ending in '-ior' (junior, senior, superior) take 'to' instead of 'than'.
  • Order of Adjectives (OSASCOMP): Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Color, Origin, Material, Purpose. (e.g., 'A beautiful large old Indian wooden desk').
  • Few vs. Little Usage: 'Few', 'a few', 'the few' are used with countable plural nouns. 'Little', 'a little', 'the little' are used with uncountable singular nouns.
  • Each vs. Every: 'Each' is used for two or more items considered individually. 'Every' is used for three or more items considered as a group.
  • Double Comparatives Error: Never use double comparatives or superlatives like 'more taller' or 'most best' in formal English.

Prepositions, Conjunctions and Interjections

Prepositions link nouns/pronouns to other words, conjunctions join clauses or sentences, and interjections express sudden emotion. Fixed prepositions carry high weightage in UPPSC/RO examinations.

  • Fixed Prepositions: Specific words demand specific prepositions, e.g., 'differ from', 'abide by', 'accustomed to', 'angry with (person) at (thing)'.
  • Prepositions of Time and Place: 'In' is used for months, centuries, and large cities; 'at' for precise times and small towns; 'on' for days and dates.
  • Correlative Conjunctions: Pairs like 'Either... or', 'Neither... nor', 'Not only... but also' must be placed immediately before the parallel grammatical structures.
  • Conjunction Pairs with 'Hardly/Scarcely' and 'No Sooner': 'Hardly' or 'Scarcely' is followed by 'when' or 'before'; 'No Sooner' is strictly followed by 'than'.
  • Interjections and Punctuation: Interjections (Alas!, Hurrah!, Wow!) express emotional outbursts and are typically followed by an exclamation mark (!) or comma.
