Etymological Roots and Latin/Greek Derivatives

Understanding root words dramatically expands the ability to deduce synonyms and antonyms of unfamiliar words in the exam.

  • Root 'Bene' (Good/Well): Forms words like 'Benevolent' (Synonym: Generous, Altruistic; Antonym: Malevolent, Parsimonious). Vital for AHC RO/ARO lexical questions.
  • Root 'Mal / Male' (Bad/Evil): Encapsulates terms like 'Malediction' (Synonym: Curse, Imprecation; Antonym: Benediction, Blessing). Frequently asked in matching sets.
  • Root 'Cred' (To Believe/Trust): Comprises words such as 'Credulous' (Synonym: Gullible, Naive; Antonym: Skeptical, Discerning).
  • Root 'Bell' (War/Fighting): Yields terms like 'Belligerent' and 'Bellicose' (Synonym: Hostile, Pugnacious; Antonym: Peaceful, Pacifist).

Nuance-Based Synonyms in Judicial Contexts

Certain words appear identical in general translation but carry distinct legal and administrative nuances in High Court examinations.

  • Adjourn vs. Postpone: 'Adjourn' is strictly used for official court sessions or legislative bodies pausing until another time. 'Postpone' is general.
  • Abolish vs. Demolish: 'Abolish' refers to laws, systems, or customs (Synonym: Eradicate). 'Demolish' refers to physical structures (Synonym: Raze, Destroy).
  • Compliment vs. Complement: 'Compliment' means praise or formal regard. 'Complement' means something that completes or enhances something else.
  • Judicial vs. Judicious: 'Judicial' relates to courts of law or administration of justice. 'Judicious' means having, showing, or done with good judgment.

Advanced Antonym Derivation Rules

Mastering prefix and suffix patterns enables rapid identification of direct opposites during high-pressure objective examinations.

  • Prefix 'In-', 'Im-', 'Il-', 'Ir-': Denotes negation. Example: Flawless (Immaculate) -> Flawed; Legal -> Illegal; Rational -> Irrational.
  • Prefix 'Dis-', 'De-', 'Mis-': Indicates reversal or opposition. Example: Agree -> Disagree; Increase -> Decrease; Interpret -> Misinterpret.
  • Suffix Conversion ('-ful' to '-less'): Direct antonym creation by shifting abundance suffixes to deprivation suffixes. Example: Hopeful -> Hopeless; Careful -> Careless.
