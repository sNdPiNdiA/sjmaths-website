Spellings Core Study Notes

Spellings form a crucial component of the General English and Hindi paper in the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO examination. Mastery over frequently misspelled words, phonetic traps, and standard administrative vocabulary ensures high accuracy and prevents negative marking.

Commonly Confused English Spellings

A deep dive into English words that feature silent letters, double consonants, and tricky suffixes frequently tested in AHC exams.

  • Double Consonants in Middle: Words like 'Accommodation' (two c's, two m's) and 'Committee' (two m's, two t's, two e's) are frequent stumbling blocks.
  • IE vs EI Rule (I Before E Except After C): Follow 'ie' normally (Achieve, Believe), but use 'ei' after 'c' (Receive, Deceive) or with long 'a' sound (Neighbour, Weigh).
  • Suffix Additions (-able vs -ible): If the root is a complete word, add '-able' (Read-readable). If the root is not a standalone word, add '-ible' (Aud-audible).
  • Silent Letters Trap: Words with hidden letters such as 'Environment' (n before m), 'Government' (n before m), and 'Dumbbell' (b at the end).
  • Suffixes with 'E' Dropping: Drop the silent 'e' when adding a vowel suffix (Argument from Argue, but retain it in 'Acreage').

Hindi Vartani Shuddhi (वर्तनी शुद्धि)

Essential Hindi spelling rules covering Matra placements, Panchakshar usage, and common Devanagari script pitfalls.

  • Anusvara (ं) vs Panchakshar (ङ, ञ, ण, न, म): Modern Hindi permits Anusvara for nasal sounds before any sparsha consonant, but strict rules apply to compounds (e.g., 'Ganga' or 'Ganga').
  • Use of 'R' (Reph and Padraten): Reph (र्) is placed above the subsequent consonant when half 'r' precedes it (e.g., 'Karm'). Padraten is placed below (e.g., 'Kram').
  • Commonly Misspelled Administrative Words: Words like 'Anudharan', 'Ujjwal' (two half j's), 'Kaviyatri' (not Kavitri), and 'Shrikant' require precise practice.
  • Usage of 'Sh' (श, ष, स): Distinguishing between Talavya Sh (श), Moordhanya Sh (స/ष), and Dantya S (स) is vital for standard UPSC and UPPSC Hindi formats.

Foreign and Loan Words Spelled in Hindi

Handling words borrowed from English, Arabic, and Persian into Hindi orthography with Nukta rules.

  • Nukta Placement Rules: Nukta is used for foreign sounds like qa (क़), khah (ख़), gazal (ग़), zameen (ज़), and firoz (फ़).
  • English Loan Words in Hindi: Words like 'Doctor' use 'ॉ' (Chandrabindu/ अर्धचंद्र), written as 'डॉक्टर', not 'डाक्टर' in strict Hindi standardization.
