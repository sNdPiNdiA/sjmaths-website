Sandhi & Samas Core Study Notes

Sandhi and Samas form the absolute bedrock of the General Hindi paper in the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations. Mastery over phonetic combinations (Sandhi) and word-compounding (Samas) ensures high accuracy in identifying word origins, breaking down complex terms, and scoring maximum negative-marking-proof marks.

Fundamentals of Sandhi (Phonetic Combination)

Sandhi is the fusion of two close sounds or letters. It is broadly categorized into Swar Sandhi, Vyanjan Sandhi, and Visarga Sandhi.

  • Swar Sandhi (Vowel Sandhi): Combination of two vowels resulting in five sub-types: Dirgh ( दीर्घ ), Gun ( गुण ), Vriddhi ( वृद्धि ), Yan ( यण ), and Ayadi ( अयादि ).
  • Vyanjan Sandhi (Consonant Sandhi): Combination of a consonant with a consonant or a vowel, governed by rules of class transposition, nasalization, and 'Ta' ( त ) transformations.
  • Visarga Sandhi: Transformation of Visarga ( ः ) into 'O', 'R', 'S', 'Sh', 'Shh', or deletion based on preceding and succeeding sounds.
  • Dirgh Swar Sandhi Formula: Akkah Savarne Dirghah: When short or long a, i, u, ri are followed by similar short or long vowels, they merge into their respective long counterpart.
  • Yan Swar Sandhi Formula: Eko Yanachy: When i, u, ri, lri are followed by any dissimilar vowel, they convert to y, v, r, l respectively.

Fundamentals of Samas (Word Compounding)

Samas is the process of compacting two or more independent words into a single meaningful compound word (Samasik Pad).

  • Avyayibhava Samas: First term is an indeclinable (Avyay) or a pre-positional prefix (e.g., yatha, prati, aan), and the entire compound acts as an adverb.
  • Tatpurusha Samas: Second term is dominant, and case markers (Karak Vibhakti) between the terms are omitted during compounding (e.g., Karm, Karan, Sampradan).
  • Karmadharye Samas: A special sub-type of Tatpurusha where the relation between terms is based on Special-Specialized (Visheshya-Visheshan) or Comparison (Upamna-Upameya).
  • Dvigu Samas: A sub-type of Tatpurusha where the first term is a numeral adjective indicating a group or collection (Samahar).
  • Dvandva Samas: Both terms are equally dominant. Conjunctions like 'aur' (and), 'ya' (or) are dropped (e.g., Mata-Pita).
  • Bahuvrihi Samas: Neither of the constituent terms is dominant; instead, they point to a third external entity or implied meaning (e.g., Dashanana).

Advanced Sandhi Rules: Vyanjan and Visarga

High Court exams frequently test complex Vyanjan and Visarga exceptions which separate high scorers from average candidates.

  • First to Third Letter Transformation: If a voiceless unvoiced stop (k, ch, t, t, p) is followed by a voiced sound, vowel, or nasal, it converts to the 3rd letter of its class (e.g., Vak + Jaal = Vagjaal).
  • First to Fifth Letter (Nasal) Rule: If a stop letter (k, c, t, t, p) is followed by a nasal letter (m or n), it changes to the corresponding nasal of its own class (e.g., Vak + May = Vangmay).
  • Visarga to 'O' Conversion: If Visarga is preceded by 'A' and followed by a soft voiced consonant (3rd, 4th, 5th of any class or y, r, l, v, h), Visarga changes to 'O' (e.g., Manah + Rath = Manorath).
  • Visarga to 'R' Conversion: If Visarga is preceded by 'I' or 'U' and followed by a voiced sound, Visarga changes to half 'R' (Reph) (e.g., Ni + Aash = Niraash).

Complex Samas Identification & Vigrah Tricky Cases

Discerning between Karmadharye and Bahuvrihi, or differentiating Dvigu from Tatpurusha, requires deep structural analysis.

  • Karmadharye vs. Bahuvrihi Overlap: Neelkanth: If interpreted as 'Blue throat', it is Karmadharye (Visheshya-Visheshan). If interpreted as 'One who has a blue throat (Shiva)', it is Bahuvrihi.
  • Dvigu vs. Bahuvrihi Overlap: Churbhuj: If parsed as 'Collection of four arms', it is Dvigu. If parsed as 'One who has four arms (Vishnu)', it is Bahuvrihi.
  • Aluk Tatpurusha Samas: A rare sub-type of Tatpurusha where the case marker (Vibhakti) of the first term is not dropped during compounding (e.g., Yudhishthir, Vachaspati).
  • Nai Tatpurusha Samas: When the first term indicates negation (non-existence, absence, or opposition), prefixed with 'a' or 'an' before vowels/consonants (e.g., Ashatya, Anadar).
