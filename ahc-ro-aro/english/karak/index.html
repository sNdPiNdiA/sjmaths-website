Karak (Case Markers in Hindi Grammar) Core Study Notes

Karak (Cases) play a fundamental role in Allahabad High Court RO/ARO and UPPSC examinations. Questions frequently test the identification of correct case markers (Vibhakti), differentiation between similar markers like 'Ko' (को) for object and recipient, and contextual usage in complex sentences.

Introduction and Definition of Karak

Understanding the core concept of how nouns and pronouns relate to the verb in a sentence.

  • Definition of Karak: Words (nouns or pronouns) that have a direct relation with the action (verb) in a sentence are called Karak.
  • Vibhakti or Parisarga: The markers or particles (like ne, ko, se) used after nouns/pronouns to indicate their case are called Vibhakti or Parisarga.
  • Total Number of Karakas: In standard Hindi grammar, there are 8 Karakas recognized for competitive examinations.
  • Difference between Sambandh and Sambodhan: Sambandh and Sambodhan do not have a direct connection with the verb, yet they are included in the 8 Hindi Karakas.
Karak Name Vibhakti / Parisarga Core Meaning / Function
Karta (Nominative) Ne (ने) [Sometimes hidden] Doer of the action
Karma (Accusative) Ko (को) [Sometimes hidden] Receiver of the action
Karan (Instrumental) Se, Ke dwara (से, के द्वारा) Medium or instrument of action
Sampradan (Dative) Ko, Ke liye (को, के लिए) Giving or doing something for someone
Apadan (Ablative) Se [Separation] (से [अलग होना]) Separation, fear, comparison

Detailed Analysis of First Four Karakas

Deep dive into Karta, Karma, Karan, and Sampradan with high-yield exam examples.

  • Karta Karak Exceptions: In present and future tense, the 'Ne' marker is generally omitted (e.g., 'Ram padhta hai'). It appears primarily in past perfect/completed tenses.
  • Karma Karak without 'Ko': Inanimate objects often do not take the 'Ko' marker even when they act as the direct object (e.g., 'Ram pustak padhta hai').
  • Karan vs Apadan Distinction: Both use 'Se', but Karan denotes a medium/instrument (Kalm se likhta hai), whereas Apadan denotes separation (Ped se patta gira).
  • Sampradan vs Karma ('Ko' usage): When something is given permanently or done for someone's benefit, Sampradan ('Ko') is used; routine object reception takes Karma ('Ko').

Detailed Analysis of Remaining Four Karakas

Examining Apadan, Sambandh, Adhikaran, and Sambodhan with tricky grammatical rules.

  • Apadan Beyond Separation: Apadan is also used in cases of fear, shyness, learning, comparison, and source (e.g., 'Sher se darta hai', 'Himalaya se Ganga nikalti hai').
  • Sambandh Karak Markers: Markers include Ka, Ke, Ki, Ra, Re, Ri, Na, Ne, Ni (e.g., 'Raman ki kitaab', 'Apna ghoda'). It shows possession or relation.
  • Adhikaran Karak (Locative): Denotes the base, time, or place of action using 'Men' (in) and 'Par' (on) [e.g., 'Ped par bandar baitha hai', 'Vah samay par aayega'].
  • Sambodhan Karak: Used to call, address, or draw attention of someone using markers like He, O, Ajab, Are (e.g., 'He Ishwar! Raksha karo').
