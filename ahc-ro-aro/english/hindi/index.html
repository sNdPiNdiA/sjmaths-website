General English & Hindi: Hindi Grammar Fundamentals Core Study Notes

The Hindi section in the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO examination carries immense weight and is a decisive factor for merit. Mastery over foundational concepts such as Varnamala, Sandhi, Samas, and Karak ensures high accuracy and boosts overall scores.

Hindi Varnamala (Alphabet & Phonetics)

Understanding the classification of vowels, consonants, articulation points, and phonetic properties.

  • Total Letters in Hindi: Standard Hindi Varnamala consists of 52 letters: 13 vowels (Swara), 39 consonants (Vyanjan) including 4 ksh, tr, gy, shr (Samyukt Vyanjan) and two nukta letters.
  • Classification of Vowels (Swara): Vowels are divided based on utterance time into Hraswa (short: a, i, u, ri), Deergha (long: aa, ee, oo, e, ai, o, au), and Pluta (prolonged).
  • Consonant Categories (Vyanjan Varg): Classified into 5 principal vargas (Kavarg, Chavarg, Tavarg, Tavarg, Pavarg) containing 25 sparsh vyanjan, followed by antasth (y, r, l, v) and ushm (sh, sh, s, h).
  • Pranatva (Aspirated/Unaspirated): Alprana (Minimal breath) consists of 1st, 3rd, and 5th letters of each varg plus vowels/antasth. Mahaprana (Heavy breath) consists of 2nd and 4th letters plus ushm.
  • Ghoshosh (Voiced/Unvoiced): Aghosh (Unvoiced) sounds use 1st and 2nd letters of vargas plus sh, sh, s. Saghosh (Voiced) sounds use 3rd, 4th, 5th letters plus all vowels and antasth along with 'h'.

Hindi Sandhi (Rules of Combination)

Mastering the three primary categories of Sandhi: Swara, Vyanjan, and Visarga Sandhi for error-free spelling and word formation.

  • Swara Sandhi (Vowel Combination): Occurs when a vowel meets a vowel. Subdivided into 5 types: Deergha ( दीर्घ ), Gun ( गुण ), Vriddhi ( वृद्धि ), Yan ( यण ), and Ayadi ( अयादि ).
  • Deergha Swara Sandhi Rule: When hraswa or deergha (a, i, u) meets similar hraswa or deergha, they combine to form respective deergha (aa, ee, oo). Example: Him + Alaya = Himalaya.
  • Gun & Vriddhi Swara Sandhi: Gun: (a/aa) + (i/ee/u/oo/ri) becomes (e/o/ar). Example: Maha + Uday = Mahoday. Vriddhi: (a/aa) + (e/ai/o/au) becomes (ai/au). Example: Sada + Ev = Sadaiv.
  • Vyanjan Sandhi Rules: Combination of consonant with consonant or vowel. Example: Jagat + Eesh = Jagadeesh (1st letter turning into 3rd letter of the varg).
  • Visarga Sandhi Rules: Combination of visarga (:) with vowel or consonant resulting in transformation to 'o', 'sh', 's', or omission. Example: Manah + Rath = Manorath.

Hindi Samas (Compound Words)

Detailed analysis of word compounding, classification into six core types, and vigrah (dissociation) techniques.

  • Avyayibhava Samas: First member is an avyay (indeclinable/prefix) and acts as the dominant/primary constituent. Example: Yatha-shakti (according to power), Pratyek (each).
  • Tatpurusha Samas: Second member is dominant, and case markers (karak chinh) are elided during formation. Subdivided into 6 types based on cases (Karm, Karan, Sampradan, Apadan, Sambandh, Adhikaran).
  • Karmadharaya Samas: One member acts as an adjective (Visheshan) and the other as a noun/subject (Visheshya), or relationship of Upaman-Upamey. Example: Chandra-mukh (Moon-like face).
  • Dwigu Samas: First constituent is a numerical adjective indicating a group or collection (Samahara). Example: Tri-loka (three worlds), Nav-ratna (nine gems).
  • Dwandwa Samas: Both constituents are equal and dominant, generally joined by 'aur' (and) or 'ya' (or) in vigrah. Example: Mata-Pita (Mother and Father), Pap-Punya.
  • Bahuvrihi Samas: Neither constituent is dominant; both together point to a third, external entity or deity. Example: Dashanand (Ten-headed -> Ravan), Neelkanth (Blue-necked -> Lord Shiva).

Hindi Karak (Cases and Markers)

Study of grammatical relations between nouns/pronouns and verbs through explicit case endings (Karak Chinh / Vibhakti).

  • Definition and Total Karak: There are 8 distinct Karak categories in Hindi grammar that indicate the role of a noun in a sentence relative to the action.
  • Karta and Karm Karak: Karta (Subject) uses marker 'Ne' (ने). Karm (Object) uses marker 'Ko' (को), denoting the target of the action.
  • Karan and Sampradan Karak: Karan (Instrument/Means) uses 'Se / Ke dwara' (से / के द्वारा). Sampradan (Recipient/Beneficiary) uses 'Ko / Ke lie' (को / के लिए).
  • Apadan and Sambandh Karak: Apadan (Separation/Comparison) uses 'Se' (से). Sambandh (Possession/Relation) uses 'Ka, Ke, Ki, Ra, Re, Ri'.
  • Adhikaran and Sambodhan Karak: Adhikaran (Location/Time) uses 'Me, Par' (में, पर). Sambodhan (Addressing) uses 'He, O, Aje' (हे, अरे, अजी).

Shuddh-Ashuddh Vartani (Spellings & Sentence Correction)

Essential vocabulary corrections, common phonetic spelling mistakes, and structural errors tested in AHC RO/ARO exams.

  • Common Matra Errors: Aspirants often interchange short and long vowels. Example: Incorrect 'Ashirwad', Correct 'Ashirwad' (आशीर्वाद). Incorrect 'Kaviyatri', Correct 'Kavayitri' (कवयित्री).
  • Halant and Visarga Omissions: Omission of halant in words like 'Samrat' (सम्राट) instead of Samrat without halant, or writing 'Pratahkal' without correct sandhi rules.
  • Redundancy in Sentences: Avoid using synonymous words together, such as 'Wah vyakti wapas laut aaya' (He returned back) -> 'Wah wapas aaya' or 'Wah laut aaya'.
  • Ling and Vachan Agreement: Verbs must agree with the subject's gender and number. Example: 'Sita ne pustak padhi' is correct; 'Sita ne pustak padha' is incorrect.
