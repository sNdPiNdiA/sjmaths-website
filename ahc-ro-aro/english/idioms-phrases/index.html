Idioms and Phrases Core Study Notes

Idioms and Phrases form an integral part of the General English section in the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO examination, testing a candidate's grasp of figurative expression, lexical resource, and contextual comprehension. Mastering high-yield legal, administrative, and standard literary idioms ensures high scoring potential under negative marking.

Legal and Administrative Idioms

These expressions are frequently borrowed from judicial proceedings, court terminology, and bureaucratic functioning, making them vital for Allahabad High Court RO/ARO aspirants.

  • By and Large: Meaning: On the whole, generally, or considering everything. Contextual Usage: By and large, the writ petition was found maintainable.
  • Null and Void: Meaning: Having no legal force or binding effect. Contextual Usage: The lower court order was declared null and void.
  • Prima Facie: Meaning: Based on the first impression; accepted as correct until proved otherwise. Contextual Usage: There is a prima facie case of contempt.
  • Ultra Vires: Meaning: Beyond one's legal power or authority. Contextual Usage: The notification was struck down as ultra vires the parent Act.
  • In Lieu Of: Meaning: Instead of or in place of. Contextual Usage: Compensation was awarded in lieu of land acquisition.

Body Parts Based Idioms

A major chunk of questions in competitive exams tests idioms derived from human anatomy and physiological actions.

  • Apple of One's Eye: Meaning: A person or thing that is cherished above all others. Contextual Usage: The youngest child was the apple of his eye.
  • At Arm's Length: Meaning: Avoiding intimacy or close contact; keeping someone away. Contextual Usage: Public servants must keep corrupt contractors at arm's length.
  • Cold Feet: Meaning: Loss of nerve or confidence before undertaking a crucial task. Contextual Usage: He got cold feet right before presenting the high-profile case.
  • To Turn a Blind Eye: Meaning: Pretend not to notice or ignore something deliberately. Contextual Usage: Authorities cannot afford to turn a blind eye to procedural violations.
  • Slip of the Tongue: Meaning: An unintentional error in speaking. Contextual Usage: The disclosure was merely a slip of the tongue, not a deliberate leak.

Color and Animal Thematic Idioms

Examiners frequently test idioms containing colors and animals because their figurative meanings often differ drastically from literal interpretations.

  • Black Sheep: Meaning: An odd or disreputable member of a group or family. Contextual Usage: Every institution strives to weed out the black sheep.
  • Red Tape: Meaning: Excessive bureaucracy or adherence to official rules and formalities. Contextual Usage: The scheme was delayed due to administrative red tape.
  • White Elephant: Meaning: Something that costs a lot of money but has no useful purpose. Contextual Usage: The abandoned stadium turned into a white elephant.
  • Wild Goose Chase: Meaning: A foolish and hopeless pursuit of something unattainable. Contextual Usage: Searching for the missing file in the basement was a wild goose chase.

Verb-Action and Miscellaneous Idioms

Action-oriented idioms involving verbs like 'cut', 'break', 'fall', and 'run' test syntactic structure and grammatical collocation.

  • Burn the Midnight Oil: Meaning: To work or study late into the night. Contextual Usage: RO/ARO aspirants must burn the midnight oil to crack the exam.
  • Cut Corners: Meaning: To do something perfunctorily to save time or money, often compromising quality. Contextual Usage: Auditors must ensure no department cuts corners during procurement.
  • Add Fuel to the Fire: Meaning: To worsen an already tense or bad situation. Contextual Usage: His provocative statement only added fuel to the fire.
  • Spill the Beans: Meaning: To reveal a secret, often unintentionally. Contextual Usage: During interrogation, the accomplice spilled the beans.
