Sentence Correction for Allahabad High Court RO/ARO Core Study Notes

Sentence correction forms a vital component of the General English paper in the Allahabad High Court Review Officer and Assistant Review Officer examinations. Candidates are tested on their foundational command of English grammar, syntax accuracy, structural cohesion, and idiomatic precision.

Subject-Verb Agreement and Proximity Rules

Mastering the agreement between the subject and the verb is the cornerstone of resolving sentence correction queries in judicial exams.

  • Basic Rule: A singular subject takes a singular verb, and a plural subject takes a plural verb. Proximity to intervening words must be ignored.
  • Correlative Conjunctions: When subjects are joined by 'either...or' or 'neither...nor', the verb agrees with the subject closest to it.
  • Collective Nouns: Collective nouns like jury, committee, and flock take a singular verb when acting as a unit, and plural when acting individually.
  • Indefinite Pronouns: Words like everyone, somebody, anybody, and each are strictly singular and require singular verbs.
  • Fractional and Percentage Expressions: Expressions with fractions or percentages take a singular or plural verb depending on the noun that follows the preposition 'of'.
Incorrect Construction Correct Construction Grammatical Rule
Neither of the files are complete. Neither of the files is complete. 'Neither of' takes a singular verb.
The committee have submitted its report. The committee has submitted its report. Acting as a single unified body.
Bread and butter are my favorite breakfast. Bread and butter is my favorite breakfast. Compound nouns expressing a single idea.
Ten miles are a long distance to walk. Ten miles is a long distance to walk. Expressing a single quantity or unit.

Modifiers, Parallelism, and Diction

Precision in sentence structure relies heavily on placing modifiers correctly and maintaining parallel construction across coordinate clauses.

  • Misplaced Modifiers: A modifier must be placed as close as possible to the word it modifies to prevent ambiguous or absurd meanings.
  • Dangling Modifiers: Introductory participial phrases must clearly refer to the logical subject immediately following the comma.
  • Grammatical Parallelism: Elements in a series or comparison must share the same grammatical form (e.g., noun with noun, gerund with gerund).
  • Correlative Balance: Correlative conjunctions ('not only...but also', 'either...or') must be followed by identical parts of speech.

Pronoun Case and Antecedent Clarity

Pronouns must agree in number, gender, and person with their antecedents, and the correct subjective or objective case must be applied.

  • Case after Prepositions and Verbs: Prepositions and transitive verbs always take objective case pronouns (e.g., between you and me, not between you and I).
  • Pronouns with Gerunds: A noun or pronoun preceding a gerund must be in the possessive case (e.g., 'excuse my leaving' instead of 'excuse me leaving').
  • Ambiguous Reference: Pronouns like 'it', 'they', or 'which' must have a clear, singular antecedent to avoid vagueness.

Tense Consistency and Conditional Moods

Maintaining logical tense sequences and correctly formulating conditional statements are high-yield areas in AHC RO/ARO exams.

  • Sequence of Tenses: A past tense in the main clause is typically followed by a past tense in the subordinate clause, except for universal truths.
  • First Conditional: If + Simple Present, Future Simple (e.g., 'If it rains, we will cancel the session').
  • Second Conditional: If + Simple Past, Would + Base Verb (e.g., 'If I were a judge, I would ensure swift justice').
  • Third Conditional: If + Past Perfect, Would have + Past Participle (e.g., 'If she had studied, she would have cleared the exam').
