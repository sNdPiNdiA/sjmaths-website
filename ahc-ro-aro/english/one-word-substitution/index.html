One Word Substitution for AHC RO/ARO Core Study Notes

One word substitution is a high-scoring component of the General English section in the Allahabad High Court RO/ARO examination. It tests a candidate's precision in vocabulary and ability to express complex ideas concisely, reflecting the formal communication style required in judicial and administrative services.

Government, Governance & Legal Terminology

Essential one-word substitutions related to political systems, governance, and judicial proceedings frequently tested in competitive exams.

  • Autocracy / Dictatorship: A government by one person with absolute power. (Root: Auto = self, cracy = rule)
  • Bureaucracy: A system of government in which most of the important decisions are taken by state officials rather than by elected representatives.
  • Plutocracy: A society or a government ruled by the wealthy class.
  • Extradition: The action of extraditing a person accused or convicted of a crime interception to another country.
  • Amesty: An official pardon for people who have been convicted of political offenses.

Human Behaviour, States & Attributes

Vocabulary items describing human personality traits, habits, and psychological states.

  • Altruist: A disinterested and selfless concern for the well-being of others.
  • Misogynist: A person who dislikes, despises, or is strongly prejudiced against women.
  • Stoic: A person who can endure pain or hardship without showing their feelings or complaining.
  • Omniscient: Knowing everything; having infinite awareness.
  • Credulous: Showing too much readiness to believe things without proof.

Phobias, Manias and Scientific Classifications

Terms denoting irrational fears, obsessive desires, and specialized scientific or professional categories.

  • Claustrophobia: Extreme or irrational fear of confined places.
  • Kleptomania: A recurrent compulsive urge to steal, typically without regard for profit or need.
  • Numismatics: The study or collection of coins, medals, and paper money.
  • Etymology: The study of the origin of words and the way in which their meanings have changed throughout history.

Sound, Action & Specialized Locations

Categorized words for animal/object sounds, specialized killing terms, and institutional venues.

  • Apiary: A place where bees are kept; a collection of beehives.
  • Homicide: The deliberate and unlawful killing of one person by another; murder.
  • Cacophony: A harsh, discordant mixture of sounds.
  • Panacea: A solution or remedy for all difficulties or diseases.
