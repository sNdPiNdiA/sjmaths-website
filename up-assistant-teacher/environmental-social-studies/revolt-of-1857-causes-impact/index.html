Revolt of 1857: Causes & Impact - syllabus concepts, notes, and trackers for UP Assistant Teacher Exam.

Concepts and Theories

Political and Economic Causes

• Doctrine of Lapse: Introduced by Lord Dalhousie, annexing states like Satara (1848), Jhansi (1853), and Nagpur (1854) without natural heirs.

Annexation of Awadh: Annexed in 1856 on grounds of maladministration, alienating local sepoys and landlords (taluqdars).
Economic Drain: Destruction of traditional handicrafts, heavy land revenue settlements, and impoverishment of peasants.
Mnemonic for Dalhousie Annexations: Satara Jhansi Nagpur -> Some Jungle Ninjas.

Socio-Religious and Military Causes

• Social Discrimination: Racial arrogance ('White Man's Burden') and exclusion of Indians from high civil and military posts.

Religious Interference: Abolition of Sati (1829), Legalization of Widow Remarriage (1856), and the Religious Disabilities Act (1850) threatening Hindu-Muslim customs.
Military Grievances: Low pay, refusal of foreign service allowance (Bhatta), and the General Service Enlistment Act (1856) requiring sepoys to serve overseas.

Immediate Cause and Outbreak

• Enfield Rifle: Introduction of new rifles requiring cartridges greased with animal fat (beef and pork).

Mangal Pandey: Sepoy of the 34th Native Infantry at Barrackpore who refused to use the cartridge and attacked British officers on 29th March 1857.
Execution: Mangal Pandey was hanged on 8th April 1857.
Meerut Mutiny: On 9th May 1857, 85 sepoys rejected cartridges and were imprisoned; on 10th May, fellow sepoys broke open jails, killed officers, and marched to Delhi.

Key Centers and Leadership

• Delhi: Nominal leader Bahadur Shah II (Zafar); real military command by General Bakht Khan.

Kanpur: Led by Nana Saheb (adopted son of Peshwa Baji Rao II) assisted by Tantia Tope.
Lucknow (Awadh): Led by Begum Hazrat Mahal on behalf of her young son Birjis Qadir.
Jhansi and Gwalior: Led by Rani Lakshmibai, later joined by Tantia Tope.
Bihar (Jagdishpur): Led by the veteran octogenarian zamindar Kunwar Singh.
Bareilly: Led by Khan Bahadur Khan.

Suppression and British Officials

• Delhi Recaptured: By John Nicholson and Hudson in September 1857; Bahadur Shah II exiled to Rangoon.

Kanpur Recaptured: By Sir Colin Campbell; Nana Saheb escaped to Nepal.
Lucknow Relieved: By Henry Havelock, James Outram, and Sir Colin Campbell.
Jhansi Defeated: By Sir Hugh Rose, who famously remarked that Rani Lakshmibai was 'the best and bravest military leader of the rebels'.

Causes of Failure

• Lack of All-India Participation: Southern and Western India remained largely unaffected; many native rulers actively helped the British.

Poor Coordination: Absence of a central unified command and unified ideology among leaders.
Military Inferiority: Rebels lacked modern weapons, communication systems, and financial resources compared to the British.
Mnemonic for Failure Factors: Lack, Poor, Military -> Lions Provoke Mice.

Tricks to Remember

  • Trick 1: Centers and Leaders Acronym:

    Remember K-L-J-D for Kanpur (Nana Saheb), Lucknow (Begum Hazrat Mahal), Jhansi (Rani Lakshmibai), Delhi (Bakht Khan).

  • Trick 2: British Commanders Chronology:

    Use Nicholson (Delhi), Campbell (Kanpur), Rose (Jhansi) -> Remember phrase Never Cheat Rani.

  • Trick 3: Governor-General Recall:

    Think of Canning during Crisis of 1857 (C for Canning, C for Conflict).

  • Trick 4: Doctrine of Lapse States Order:

    Remember S-J-N-U for Satara (1848), Jhansi (1853), Nagpur (1854), Udaipur (1852).

  • Trick 5: Mangal Pandey Regiment:

    Mangal Pandey belonged to the 34th NI (Native Infantry) stationed at Barrackpore.

  • Trick 6: Date of Outbreak:

    Start date is 10th May at Meerut, exactly one month after Mangal Pandey's execution on 8th April.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mistake 1: Confusing Bahadur Shah II with Actual Military Leader:

    Students often think Bahadur Shah II led military operations; he was only the symbolic nominal head while General Bakht Khan commanded troops in Delhi.

  • Mistake 2: Mixing up British Suppressors:

    Do not confuse Sir Hugh Rose (Jhansi) with Sir Colin Campbell (Kanpur and Lucknow).

  • Mistake 3: Assuming All India Participation:

    The revolt was largely confined to North and Central India; South India, Punjab, and Bengal remained mostly quiet.

  • Mistake 4: Misinterpreting Awadh Annexation Year:

    Awadh was annexed in 1856, just a year before the revolt, not during Dalhousie's early years.

  • Mistake 5: Confusing Nana Saheb's Real Name:

    Nana Saheb's birth name was Dhondhu Pant; he was the adopted son of Peshwa Baji Rao II.

Point-wise Detailed Summary

  • Point 1: Nature of Revolt:

    Described as the First War of Independence by V.D. Savarkar and a Sepoy Mutiny by British writers.

  • Point 2: Economic Exploitation:

    Disrupted agrarian and handicraft systems, leading to deep resentment among peasants and artisans.

  • Point 3: Social Reforms Backlash:

    Interference in traditional customs created fear of forced religious conversion among orthodox Indians.

  • Point 4: Immediate Trigger:

    Introduction of the Enfield Rifle with cartridges greased with cow and pig fat.

  • Point 5: Outbreak Date:

    Began on 10th May 1857 at Meerut cantonment.

  • Point 6: Delhi Epicentre:

    Rebels proclaimed Bahadur Shah II as the Emperor of Hindustan.

  • Point 7: Kanpur Uprising:

    Led by Nana Saheb and his brilliant general Tantia Tope.

  • Point 8: Lucknow Resistance:

    Led by Begum Hazrat Mahal with massive support from local peasants and sepoys.

  • Point 9: Jhansi Valor:

    Led by Rani Lakshmibai, who fought valiantly against Sir Hugh Rose.

  • Point 10: Bihar Leadership:

    Spearheaded by Kunwar Singh at Jagdishpur despite his advanced age.

  • Point 11: Ultimate Failure:

    Failed due to lack of unity, poor arms, absence of a national vision, and betrayal by local rulers.

  • Point 12: Act of 1858:

    Led to the end of Company rule and transfer of power directly to the British Crown.

UPSC Notes & InsightsUPSC नोट्स और अंतर्दृष्टि

For the UP Assistant Teacher exam, focus heavily on matching leaders with their respective regions and the British officers who suppressed them.

Remember the date 10th May 1857 and Meerut as the starting point, as chronological questions frequently appear in Social Studies.

Do not mark Lord Dalhousie as Governor-General in 1857; Lord Canning was holding office during the outbreak.

Be careful with Awadh's annexation year (1856) vs Satara's annexation year (1848 under Doctrine of Lapse).

Key Takeawaysमुख्य बातें

  • The Revolt of 1857 began on 10th May 1857 at Meerut.
  • Mangal Pandey fired the first shot at Barrackpore on 29th March 1857.
  • Lord Canning served as the Governor-General during the revolt.
  • Key leaders included Rani Lakshmibai, Nana Saheb, Begum Hazrat Mahal, and Kunwar Singh.
  • The revolt resulted in the abolition of East India Company rule via the Government of India Act 1858.
  • Primary causes included Doctrine of Lapse, economic drain, religious interference, and Enfield Rifle cartridges.
