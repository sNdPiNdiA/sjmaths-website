Concepts and Theories

1. Core Concept Definition & The Rasa Sutra

• Rasa Sutra: Vibhavanubhava-vyabhichari-samyogad-rasa-nishpattih (From the union of Vibhava, Anubhava, and Vyabhichari Bhava, the accomplishment of Rasa takes place).

Rasa: The supreme aesthetic delight experienced by a sensitive reader (Sahrdaya) or spectator (Smajika).
Sthayi Bhava: The latent permanent emotions residing permanently in the human mind (Chitta), numbering nine.
Mnemonic: 'V-A-V Stha-Rasa' -> Vibhava + Anubhava + Vyabhichari = Sthayi Bhava transforming into Rasa.

2. Four Constituents of Rasa (Rasa Samgri)

• Sthayi Bhava: The principal permanent emotion that underlies the poetic situation (e.g., Rati for Shringara).

Vibhava (Determinants): Causes or stimuli that evoke the emotion. Divided into Alambana (primary object/person) and Uddipana (exciting environment/factors).
Anubhava (Consequents): Physical manifestations or voluntary/involuntary responses indicating the emotion (e.g., smiling, trembling, sweating).
Vyabhichari / Sanchari Bhava (Transitory/Accessory Emotions): Fleeting emotions that reinforce the primary emotion, numbering 33 (e.g., joy, suspicion, fatigue, shame).

3. Four Major Commentators on the Rasa Sutra

• Bhatta Lollata: Propounded Utpattivada (Theory of Production/Origin); Rasa is produced and located primarily in the historical character (Anukarya).

Shankuka: Propounded Anumitivada (Theory of Inference); Rasa is inferred by the spectator through artificial imitations acting as logical signs (Hetu).
Bhatta Nayaka: Propounded Bhuktivada (Theory of Enjoyment); Rasa is not produced or inferred, but universally enjoyed (Bhoga) through generalized poetry (Sadharanikarana).
Abhinavagupta: Propounded Abhivyaktivada (Theory of Manifestation); Rasa is permanently latent and gets manifested/suggested (Vyangya) in the sensitive reader's mind. Accepted as the definitive Siddhanta.

4. Classification of the Navarasa (Nine Rasas)

• Shringara (Erotic), Hasya (Comic), Karuna (Pathetic), Raudra (Furious), Veera (Heroic), Bhayanaka (Terrible), Bibhatsa (Odious), Adbhuta (Marvellous), and Shanta (Peaceful).

Bharata Muni originally recognized only eight Rasas, omitting Shanta in his dramatic theory.
Later theorists like Udbhata, Abhinavagupta, and Vishvanatha firmly established Shanta as the ninth Rasa with Shama (Equanimity) or Nirveda as its Sthayi Bhava.
Mnemonic: 'SH-H-K R-V-B B-A-Sh' -> Shringara, Hasya, Karuna, Raudra, Veera, Bhayanaka, Bibhatsa, Adbhuta, Shanta.

5. Identification Method & Analysis Approach for Poetry

• Step 1: Read the Sanskrit verse (Padyam) carefully to identify keywords indicating emotional tone.

Step 2: Locate the central character (Alambana) and the setting (Uddipana Vibhava).
Step 3: Note physical reactions (Anubhava) like weeping, shivering, or aggressive posture.
Step 4: Match the dominant underlying emotion with the corresponding Sthayi Bhava to name the exact Rasa.

Tricks to Remember

  • Trick 1: The Four Commentators' Order:

    Remember the sequence Lollata -> Shankuka -> Nayaka -> Abhinavagupta using the mnemonic 'L-S-N-A' (Like Sweet Nectar Always) matching Utpatti, Anumiti, Bhukti, Abhivyakti.

  • Trick 2: Sthayi Bhava Matching:

    Associate sound similarity: Shringara -> Rati, Hasya -> Hasa, Karuna -> Shoka, Raudra -> Krodha, Veera -> Utsaha, Bhayanaka -> Bhaya, Bibhatsa -> Jugupsa, Adbhuta -> Vismaya, Shanta -> Shama.

  • Trick 3: Bharata Muni's Rasa Count:

    Remember that Bharata Muni's Natyashastra lists Ashta Rasas (8 Rasas) explicitly in the primary verse, leaving out Shanta which was added later as the 9th.

  • Trick 4: Vibhava Subdivisions:

    Vibhava is split into two: Alambana (Foundation/Object - who is loved/feared) and Uddipana (Enhancer - moonlit night, jungle roar that boosts the emotion).

  • Trick 5: Abhinavagupta's Siddhanta:

    Abhinavagupta's Abhivyaktivada is considered the most authoritative (Siddhanta) in Sanskrit Poetics because it explains how latent emotions are awakened through Vyanjana (suggestion).

  • Trick 6: Vyabhichari Bhava Count:

    Always remember the exact number of transitory emotions (Vyabhichari / Sanchari Bhavas) is 33 (e.g., Nirveda, Glani, Shanka, Asuya, Mada, Shrama).

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mistake 1: Confusing Sthayi Bhava with Vyabhichari Bhava:

    Students often confuse Sthayi Bhava (permanent, lifelong latent emotion, 9 in total) with Vyabhichari Bhava (transitory, transient feelings that come and go, 33 in total).

  • Mistake 2: Assuming Bharata Muni Recognized 9 Rasas:

    Bharata Muni recognized only 8 Rasas in his Natyashastra. Shanta Rasa was added later by subsequent rhetoricians like Udbhata and Abhinavagupta.

  • Mistake 3: Mixing up Commentators and Theories:

    Never attribute Anumitivada to Bhatta Lollata; Shankuka propounded Anumitivada based on Nyaya, while Lollata propounded Utpattivada based on Mimamsa.

  • Mistake 4: Confusing Alambana and Uddipana Vibhava:

    Alambana is the principal subject/object supporting the emotion (e.g., Shakuntala for Dushyanta), whereas Uddipana consists of environmental stimulants (e.g., beautiful garden, spring breeze).

  • Mistake 5: Misinterpreting 'Sadharanikarana':

    Sadharanikarana (Generalization) is a key concept introduced by Bhatta Nayaka, not by Abhinavagupta, where the reader's personal ego dissolves during aesthetic experience.

  • Mistake 6: Wrong Deity or Color Assignment:

    Pay close attention to presiding deities (e.g., Yama for Karuna, Rudra for Raudra) and colors (e.g., Red for Raudra, White for Hasya) as direct matching questions appear in the UP exam.

Point-wise Detailed Summary

  • Foundation:

    Bharata Muni laid down the theory of Rasa in his monumental work Natyashastra.

  • The Rasa Sutra:

    Vibhavanubhava-vyabhichari-samyogad-rasa-nishpattih is the foundational formula for aesthetic realization.

  • Permanent Emotions:

    There are 9 Sthayi Bhavas corresponding to the Navarasa in classical Sanskrit poetics.

  • Four Elements:

    Rasa accomplishment relies on Sthayi Bhava, Vibhava, Anubhava, and Vyabhichari Bhava.

  • Transitory Feelings:

    There are exactly 33 Vyabhichari (Sanchari) Bhavas that support and nourish the main Sthayi Bhava.

  • Shanta Rasa Debate:

    Shanta Rasa (Sthayi: Shama/Nirveda) was later added to make the total number of Rasas nine (Navarasa).

  • Utpattivada:

    Propounded by Bhatta Lollata, stating Rasa is produced in the historical character.

  • Anumitivada:

    Propounded by Shankuka, stating Rasa is inferred by the spectator through artificial representation.

  • Bhuktivada:

    Propounded by Bhatta Nayaka, emphasizing aesthetic enjoyment (Bhoga) and generalization (Sadharanikarana).

  • Abhivyaktivada:

    Propounded by Abhinavagupta, establishing that Rasa is manifested through suggestion (Vyanjana).

  • Erotic Rasa:

    Shringara is regarded as Rasaraja (King of Rasas) with Rati as its Sthayi Bhava.

  • Pathos:

    Karuna Rasa (Sthayi: Shoka) is masterfully depicted by Bhavabhuti in Uttararamacharitam.

UPSC Notes & InsightsUPSC नोट्स और अंतर्दृष्टि

Memorize the exact pairing of all 9 Rasas with their Sthayi Bhavas, presiding deities, and colors, as matching-type questions are extremely common in the UP Assistant Teacher (Sanskrit) examination.

When analyzing a Sanskrit verse in an unseen passage, look for words denoting physical reactions (Anubhava) like 'ashru' (tears), 'pulaka' (thrill), or 'kampa' (trembling) to instantly identify Karuna or Bhayanaka Rasa.

Do not confuse Bharata Muni's original count of 8 Rasas with the later accepted Navarasa system which includes Shanta Rasa. Read question statements carefully regarding Bharata Muni's specific viewpoint.

Students often mix up the philosophical schools associated with the Rasa commentators. Remember: Lollata = Mimamsa, Shankuka = Nyaya, Nayaka = Sankhya-Vedanta, Abhinavagupta = Advaita Vedanta/Shaiva.

  • The Rasa Sutra explains Rasa Nishpatti through Vibhava, Anubhava, and Vyabhichari Bhava.
  • There are 9 Sthayi Bhavas and 9 Rasas in classical Sanskrit literature (Navarasa).
  • There are 33 transitory emotions known as Vyabhichari or Sanchari Bhavas.
  • Abhinavagupta's Abhivyaktivada (Theory of Manifestation) is the widely accepted Siddhanta.
