Concepts and Theories

1. Core Definition and Role of Upsarg (उपसर्गस्वरूपम्)

• Definition: Upsarg is a pre-positional particle attached to the beginning of verbal roots (Dhatu) or derived nouns.

Sanskrit Rule: उपसर्गेण धातुअर्थो बलाद् अन्यत्र नीयते। प्रहारहारसंहारविहारपरिहारवत्॥
Functions: They modify root meanings in four ways: (1) Vat-artha (keeping original meaning), (2) Vi-param-artha (reversing meaning), (3) Upajayanti (intensifying meaning), and (4) Samkuchanti (restricting meaning).
Mnemonic for 22 Upsargs: 'PRA-PARA-APAPA-SAM-ANU-AVA-NIS-NIR-DUS-DUR-VI-ANG-NI-ADH-API-ATI-SU-UT-ABHI-PRATI-PARI-UP'

2. Classification and Structural Formula of Krit Pratyay (कृत्प्रत्ययाः)

• Core Definition: Pratyays added to Dhatu (verbal roots) to form nouns, adjectives, and participles are called Krit (or Kridanta).

Structural Formula: Dhatu + Krit Pratyay = Kridanta Pad (Noun/Adjective/Participle).
Major Sub-types: Tavya/Aniyar (obligation - तव्यत्/अनिअर्च), Tumun (infinitive - तुमुन्), Ktya/Lyap (absolutive/gerund - क्त्व / ल्यप्), Shatri/Shanach (present participle - शतृ/शानच्), Kta/Ktavatu (past participle - क्त/क्तवतु).
Mnemonic for Krit Participles: 'Tavya-Aniyar-Tumun-Ktva-Lyap-Shatri-Shanach-Kta-Ktavatu' -> 'TATA LSS KK'

3. Classification and Structural Formula of Taddhit Pratyay (तद्धितप्रत्ययाः)

• Core Definition: Pratyays added to nominal stems (Pratipadika, nouns, pronouns, adjectives) instead of verbal roots are called Taddhit.

Structural Formula: Noun/Adjective + Taddhit Pratyay = Taddhitanta Pad.
Major Sub-types: Matup (possession - मतुप्), In/Vini/Thun (possession - इन्/विनि/ठन्), Thak (relation/nature - ठक्), Tarap/Tamam (comparative/superlative - तरप्/तमप्), Kan/Pash (diminutive/pejorative - कन्/पश).
Vriddhi Rule: First vowel of the base word often undergoes Vriddhi when adding certain Taddhit suffixes like Thak (अ/आ -> आ, इ/ई/ए/ऐ -> ऐ, उ/ऊ/ओ/औ -> औ).

4. Striking Features of Indeclinable Pratyays (अव्ययीभूताः प्रत्ययाः)

• Core Definition: Certain Krit and Taddhit suffixes result in words that do not decline across genders and cases, known as Avyay (Indeclinables).

Key Suffixes: Tumun (leaves -tum), Ktva (leaves -tva), Lyap (leaves -ya when prefixed with an Upsarg).
Crucial Distinction: Use Ktva when root has NO prefix (e.g., Gam + Ktvā = Gatvā); use Lyap when root HAS a prefix (e.g., A + Gam + Lyap = Āgatya).
Mnemonic for Lyap rule: 'Upsarg first, Lyap must; No Upsarg, Ktva thrust'.

5. Rules of Gender Retention and Pratyay Elimination

• Stri Pratyay Integration: Words formed via Krit and Taddhit often require feminine suffixes like Tapa (Ā) or Gheesha/Gheeni (Ī) to form feminine gender forms.

Anubandha Lop (Elision): Letters like Pa in Tumun or K in Thak are mute indicators (It) eliminated during final word formation via Tasya lopah.
Swara (Accent) Alteration: Krit suffixes generally preserve root accent or shift it to the suffix based on specific Paninian rules.

Tricks to Remember

  • Trick 1: The 'Tum' Sound for Infinitive:

    Whenever you see '-tum' at the end of a Sanskrit word (e.g., Gantum, Hantum), instantly identify it as Tumun Pratyay.

  • Trick 2: The 'Tva' vs 'Ya' Absolute Rule:

    If an indeclinable ends in '-tva', it is Ktva. If it starts with an Upsarg and ends in '-ya' (e.g., Viharya), it is Lyap.

  • Trick 3: The 'Tavy' / 'Aniy' Obligation Code:

    Words ending in -tavya, -tavya, or -aniya always represent modal/obligation meaning (Vidhi-ling sense) via Tavya/Aniyar.

  • Trick 4: The 'Vat' / 'Mat' Possession Trick:

    If a nominal base ends in 'a' or 'aa', add Matup (becomes -van/-vati). If it ends in 'i' or 'u', add Vatup (becomes -man/-mati).

  • Trick 5: Vriddhi Detection in Thak Pratyay:

    If the base noun's first vowel changes (e.g., Itihasa becomes Aitihasika), suspect Thak Pratyay immediately.

  • Trick 6: Participle Endings Identification:

    Past participles end in -ta (Kta) or -tavan (Ktavatu). Present participles end in -an/-at (Shatri) or -manah (Shanach).

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mistake 1: Confusing Lyap with Ktya:

    Students often call words with -ya as Ktva. Remember, Ktva never attaches to prefixed roots; prefixed roots take Lyap, which also leaves -ya.

  • Mistake 2: Forgetting Vriddhi in Taddhit Thak:

    Students derive Dharma + Thak as Dharmika instead of Dharmika with initial Vriddhi (Dharma -> Dhārmika).

  • Mistake 3: Confusing Krit and Taddhit Bases:

    Remember Krit is added ONLY to Dhatu (verbs), while Taddhit is added ONLY to Pratipadika (nouns/adjectives). Never apply Taddhit to a raw verbal root.

  • Mistake 4: Missing Anubandha Elisions:

    Do not treat mute letters like 'tu' in Tumun or 'k' in Thak as part of the suffix residue in the final derived word.

  • Mistake 5: Confusing Shatri and Shanach:

    Shatri is used for Parasmaipada roots (active endings like -at/-an), while Shanach is used for Atmanepada roots (endings like -māna).

Point-wise Detailed Summary

  • Upsarg Definition:

    Upsargs are 22 pre-verbal prefixes that modify or reverse the core meaning of Dhatu.

  • Krit Pratyay Scope:

    Krit affixes are attached to verbal roots (Dhatu) to form nouns, adjectives, and participles.

  • Taddhit Pratyay Scope:

    Taddhit affixes are attached to nominal stems (Pratipadika) to derive secondary nouns and adjectives.

  • Tumun Pratyay:

    Indicates purpose or infinitive meaning, leaving the suffix residue '-tum'.

  • Ktva Pratyay:

    Indicates prior action ('having done'), leaving the suffix residue '-tvā' on non-prefixed roots.

  • Lyap Pratyay:

    Equivalent to Ktva but mandatory when an Upsarg precedes the verbal root, leaving '-ya'.

  • Kta and Ktavatu:

    Kta denotes past passive sense (-ta), while Ktavatu denotes past active sense (-tavān).

  • Shatri and Shanach:

    Used for present continuous participles, corresponding to Parasmaipada and Atmanepada voices respectively.

  • Tavya and Aniyar:

    Krit suffixes used to express obligation, necessity, or potentiality (Karaniyam, Paṭhitavyam).

  • Matup Pratyay:

    Taddhit suffix denoting possession, transforming nouns into adjectives with -vān/-vatī/-vat.

  • Thak Pratyay:

    Taddhit suffix that triggers initial vowel Vriddhi and appends '-ika' to denote relationship or study.

  • Tarap and Tamam:

    Taddhit suffixes utilized for degree comparison between two (Tarap) and among many (Tamam).

UPSC Notes & InsightsUPSC नोट्स और अंतर्दृष्टि

In UP Assistant Teacher Exam, questions frequently ask to split a derived word into its base root and suffix (e.g., 'Gantum' = 'Gam + Tumun'). Memorize the standard residue forms like '-tum', '-tvā', and '-ya'.

Pay extra attention to roots that undergo internal changes (gun/vriddhi) when Krit suffixes like Kta or Tumun are attached, such as 'Gam' becoming 'Gata' or 'Bhu' becoming 'Bhutva'.

Trap: Examiners often give options with 'Ktva' for a word that has an Upsarg. Always check for an Upsarg first; if present, the correct suffix is 'Lyap', not 'Ktva'.

Trap: Confusing Taddhit suffixes with Krit suffixes. Remember that words like 'Dharmika' come from a noun ('Dharma') via Taddhit 'Thak', not from a verbal root.

Key Takeawaysमुख्य बातें

  • There are exactly 22 Upsargs in Sanskrit grammar that alter root meanings.
  • Krit Pratyay is applied to verbal roots (Dhatu), whereas Taddhit Pratyay is applied to nominal stems (Pratipadika).
  • Tumun denotes infinitives ('-tum'), Ktva denotes past absolutives ('-tvā'), and Lyap is used with prefixed roots ('-ya').
  • Kta and Ktavatu form past passive and active participles respectively.
  • Shatri and Shanach form present participles for active and middle voices.
  • Taddhit suffixes like Matup and Thak create possessive and relational adjectives with mandatory initial Vriddhi where applicable.
