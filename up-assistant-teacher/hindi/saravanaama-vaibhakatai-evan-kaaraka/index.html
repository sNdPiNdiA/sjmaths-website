Concepts and Theories

Purushvachak Sarvanam (Personal Pronoun)

• Refers to speaker, listener, or third party.

Subdivided into 3 categories: Uttam Purush (First Person - Main, Hum), Madhyam Purush (Second Person - Tu, Tum, Aap), and Anya Purush (Third Person - Yeh, Vah, Ve, Ye).
Combines with Karak markers directly: Mujh + Ko = Mujhko.
Does not include honorific nouns directly as base pronouns.
Mnemonic: U-M-A (Uttam, Madhyam, Anya) are the three personal pillars.

Nishchayavachak & Anishchayavachak Sarvanam

• Nishchayavachak points to definite objects/persons (Yeh, Vah, Ve, Ye).

Anishchayavachak indicates indefinite entities (Koi for living, Kuch for non-living).
When Nishchayavachak pronouns precede a noun directly, they act as Demonstrative Adjectives (Sarvanamik Visheshan), not pronouns.
Anishchayavachak pronouns take vibhakti easily: Kisi ko, Kuch ke liye.
Mnemonic: N-D (Nishchay = Definite), A-I (Anishchay = Indefinite).

Sambandhavachak & Nijvachak Sarvanam

• Sambandhavachak establishes correlation between clauses (Jo-So, Jaisi karni waisi bharni).

Nijvachak denotes self-reference without emphasis on other persons (Aap, Apne aap, Swayam).
Nijvachak 'Aap' must be distinguished from Madhyam Purush 'Aap' (Respectful 'You').
Nijvachak 'Aap' refers back to the subject (e.g., 'Main apne aap chala jaunga').
Mnemonic: S-S (Sambandh = Jo-So), N-A (Nij = Apne Aap).

Prashnavachak Sarvanam

• Used to ask questions about living or non-living entities.

Kaun is used for living beings (Pranivachak).
Kya is used for non-living objects or abstract concepts (A-pranivachak).
Takes vibhakti: Kiske liye, Kisne.
Mnemonic: K-L (Kaun = Living), K-NL (Kya = Non-Living).

Vibhakti and Karak Integration with Pronouns

• Pronouns transform when combined with Karak inflections (Karaka-chinhas like Ne, Ko, Se, Ke liye).

Example with 'Vah': Usne (Nominative/Karta), Usse (Instrumental/Karan), Uske liye (Dative/Sampradan).
Vocative case (Sambodhan) does not apply to pronouns.
Oblique forms (Tiryak roop) are formed before adding case endings.
Mnemonic: V-K-T (Vibhakti + Karak = Tiryak Roop).

Tricks to Remember

  • Pronoun vs Adjective Test:

    If a word like 'Yeh' or 'Vah' is followed immediately by a noun, it is a Sarvanamik Visheshan. If followed by a verb or auxiliary, it is a Sarvanam.

  • Pranivachak Question Trick:

    Use 'Kaun' if the answer is a person; use 'Kya' if the answer is an object or action.

  • Nijvachak 'Aap' Trick:

    If 'Aap' means 'yourself' or 'swayam', it is Nijvachak. If it means 'you' out of respect, it is Madhyam Purush.

  • Tiryak Roop Formation:

    Always convert 'Main' to 'Mujh', 'Hum' to 'Ham', and 'Vah' to 'Us' before adding Karak markers like 'ne', 'ko', 'se'.

  • Compound Pair Rule:

    Sambandhavachak pronouns always appear in correlated pairs like Jo-So, Jiska-Uska.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing Sarvanam with Visheshan:

    Marking 'Yeh' as a pronoun in 'Yeh ghar mera hai' (Incorrect: it modifies 'ghar', making it a demonstrative adjective).

  • Wrong Karak Fusion:

    Writing 'Main ne' instead of the correct fused oblique form 'Maine'.

  • Misidentifying Nijvachak:

    Treating the honorific 'Aap' as a Nijvachak pronoun when used to address someone directly.

  • Confusing Koi and Kuch:

    Using 'Koi' for inanimate objects (e.g., 'Glass mein koi gira hai' instead of 'kuch gira hai').

  • Counting Sambodhan in Pronouns:

    Assuming pronouns take all 8 Karak cases; they only take 7 (excluding Sambodhan).

Point-wise Detailed Summary

  • Definition Base:

    Pronouns prevent noun repetition and streamline sentence structure in Hindi.

  • Total Types:

    There are 6 principal types of Sarvanam in Hindi grammar.

  • Base Elements:

    There are exactly 11 fundamental base pronouns recognized in standard grammar.

  • Purushvachak Categories:

    Divided into Uttam, Madhyam, and Anya Purush based on speech roles.

  • Demonstrative Distinction:

    Nishchayavachak pronouns shift to adjectives if directly preceding a noun.

  • Indefinite Rules:

    Koi applies to animate entities; Kuch applies to inanimate entities.

  • Reflexive Identification:

    Nijvachak pronouns denote self-action without auxiliary noun support.

  • Karak Modification:

    Pronouns morph into oblique (tiryak) forms when merging with case markers.

  • Exclusion Rule:

    Vocative (Sambodhan) Karak does not apply to pronominal declension.

UPSC Notes & InsightsUPSC नोट्स और अंतर्दृष्टि

In UP teaching exams, examiners frequently test the boundary between Sarvanamik Visheshan and Nishchayavachak Sarvanam. Always check if a noun immediately follows the pronoun.

Remember the exact 11 base pronouns list as direct factual questions appear in preliminary screening exams.

Pay close attention to oblique case transformations (like 'Vah' changing to 'Us' before taking 'ne' or 'ko').

Trap: Assuming 'Aap' is always a Madhyam Purush pronoun. If the sentence implies doing something by oneself, it is Nijvachak.

Trap: Including Sambodhan in pronoun Karak declension tables. Pronouns never take vocative case markers.

Key Takeawaysमुख्य बातें

  • Sarvanam replaces nouns and has 6 distinct types in Hindi grammar.
  • There are 11 base pronouns from which all other pronoun variations derive.
  • Pronouns undergo distinct morphological changes (Tiryak roop) when combining with Karak vibhaktis.
  • Distinguishing between pronoun and demonstrative adjective depends strictly on word proximity to nouns.
  • Nijvachak and Purushvachak uses of 'Aap' must be contextually separated.
