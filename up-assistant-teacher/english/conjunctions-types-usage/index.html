Concepts and Theories

1. Coordinating Conjunctions & FANBOYS Mnemonic

Connects elements of equal grammatical weight (noun to noun, clause to clause).
FANBOYS Mnemonic: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So.
For: Indicates reason or cause (synonymous with 'because').
And: Adds similar ideas or affirmative statements.
Nor: Presents an additional negative alternative.
But: Shows contrast or exception.
Or: Presents an alternative or choice.
Yet: Shows unexpected contrast (similar to 'despite this').
So: Indicates consequence or result.

2. Subordinating Conjunctions & Hierarchy

Joins a dependent clause to an independent clause.
Creates complex sentences where one idea is subordinate to the main clause.
Categorized by function:
Time: When, while, before, after, since, until, as soon as.
Cause and Effect: Because, since, as, so that.
Condition: If, unless, provided that, in case.
Concession/Contrast: Although, even though, though, whereas, while.
Comparison: As...as, than, just as.
Mnemonic for Subordinators (AAABWEE): Although, As, After, Because, When, Even if, Even though.

3. Correlative Conjunctions & Parallelism

Always work in pairs to join grammatically equivalent elements.
Core Pairs: Either...or, neither...nor, not only...but also, both...and, whether...or, such...that, no sooner...than.
Golden Rule of Parallel Structure: The words following each part of the correlative conjunction must belong to the same part of speech.
Incorrect: She not only lost her bag but also her money was stolen.
Correct: She lost not only her bag but also her money.

4. Conjunctive Adverbs vs. Conjunctions

Conjunctive adverbs (e.g., however, therefore, meanwhile, consequently) modify clauses and show relationships but require a semicolon or period before them, and a comma after them.
Conjunctions directly join clauses within a single sentence structure.
Rule: You cannot join two independent clauses with only a comma and a conjunctive adverb (Comma Splice error).
Correct Structure: Clause 1 ; however, Clause 2.

5. Special Rules for 'Unless', 'Until', and 'lest'

Unless expresses a condition and is inherently negative (meaning 'if not').
Until expresses time up to a certain point and is also negative-leaning.
Strict Rule: Never use not in the same clause that contains unless or until.
Incorrect: Unless you do not work hard, you will fail.
Correct: Unless you work hard, you will fail.
Lest means 'for fear that' and is always followed by the modal auxiliary should (or bare subjunctive).
Correct Structure: Walk carefully lest you should fall.

Tricks to Remember

  • Trick 1: FANBOYS Acronym:

    Memorize the seven coordinating conjunctions using FANBOYS (For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So) to easily spot equal-rank joiners.

  • Trick 2: SCARCE-WHEN / NO-THAN Pairings:

    Remember Hardly/Scarcely take When/Before, while No Sooner takes Than. Recall: 'Scarcely When, No Sooner Than'.

  • Trick 3: UNLESS is UN-NEGATED:

    Remember that Unless and Until contain built-in negation. Never use not or never in their clauses.

  • Trick 4: NEAREST SUBJECT RULE:

    For neither...nor and either...or, look at the subject closest to the verb. The verb agrees with that nearest subject.

  • Trick 5: LEST SHOULD PAIR:

    Whenever you spot Lest in a fill-in-the-blank question, immediately search for should in the options.

  • Trick 6: PARALLEL BALANCE TEST:

    For correlative conjunctions, draw a line right after the conjunction word. Ensure the grammatical parts on both sides match identically.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mistake 1: Using 'Because' with 'Reason is':

    Writing 'The reason why he came was because...' is redundant. Correct form: 'The reason why he came was that...'

  • Mistake 2: Mixing 'Although' with 'But':

    Students often write 'Although he worked hard, but he failed.' Correct form: Drop 'but' and use a comma or yet.

  • Mistake 3: Wrong correlative pairing:

    Using 'Not only... as well as' instead of 'but also'. Always verify standard correlative pairs.

  • Mistake 4: Subject-Verb mismatch with 'As well as':

    Treating as well as, along with, or together with as coordinating conjunctions. The verb agrees with the first subject, not the second.

  • Mistake 5: 'No sooner' paired with 'When':

    Writing 'No sooner did I arrive when it started raining.' Correct form: Must use than.

  • Mistake 6: Double negative with 'Unless':

    Writing 'Unless you do not submit...' which creates a grammatical error. Correct form: 'Unless you submit...'.

Point-wise Detailed Summary

  • 1. Definition:

    Conjunctions link words, phrases, and clauses to establish logical relationships.

  • 2. Coordinating Conjunctions:

    Linked by FANBOYS; connect grammatical units of equal rank.

  • 3. Subordinating Conjunctions:

    Introduce dependent clauses expressing time, cause, condition, or concession.

  • 4. Correlative Conjunctions:

    Work in fixed pairs requiring strict parallelism in sentence construction.

  • 5. Rule of Proximity:

    In either...or / neither...nor, the verb matches the nearest subject.

  • 6. Inversion with Negative Adverbs:

    Sentences starting with Hardly, Scarcely, or No Sooner require subject-verb inversion.

  • 7. Unless & Until Restriction:

    Never pair unless or until with not in the same sub-clause.

  • 8. Lest + Should:

    Lest must always be followed by the modal auxiliary should.

  • 9. Although & Yet:

    Although pairs with yet or a comma, never with but.

  • 10. Neither...Nor vs Both...And:

    'Neither...nor' makes the verb singular/plural based on proximity; 'Both...and' always takes a plural verb.

Exam Notes & Insightsपरीक्षा नोट्स और अंतर्दृष्टि

In UP Assistant Teacher English exams, error-spotting questions frequently test the correct pairing of correlative conjunctions (e.g., No sooner...than, Hardly...when). Memorize these pairs to secure quick marks.

Examiners often trap candidates by placing a 'not' inside an 'unless' or 'until' clause. Always remember these words contain internal negation and reject additional negative particles.

Pay close attention to parallel structure errors where words following correlative conjunctions (not only... but also) belong to different parts of speech.

Do not confuse coordinating conjunctions with prepositions. Words like 'for' and 'as' can function as both depending on the context of the sentence.

Key Takeawaysमुख्य बातें

  • Conjunctions are essential glue words divided into Coordinating, Subordinating, and Correlative types.
  • The FANBOYS acronym covers all seven coordinating conjunctions.
  • Correlative conjunctions demand strict parallel grammatical structure immediately following each part of the pair.
  • Unless and Until are conditional/temporal words that forbid the use of 'not' in their clauses.
  • No sooner takes than, while Hardly/Scarcely take when/before.
  • The proximity rule applies to either...or and neither...nor, aligning the verb with the nearest subject.
