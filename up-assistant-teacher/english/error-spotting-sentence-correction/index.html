Concepts and Theories

1. Subject-Verb Agreement (Concord)

• The verb must agree with its subject in number and person.

Singular subjects take singular verbs; plural subjects take plural verbs.
Words joined by 'as well as', 'along with', 'with', 'together with' take the verb according to the first subject.
Correlative conjunctions like 'either...or', 'neither...nor' agree with the nearer subject.
Mnemonic: SANS (Subject And Number Sync).

2. Pronoun Case and Antecedent Agreement

• Pronouns must agree with their antecedents in gender, number, and person.

Nominative case is used after the verb 'to be' (e.g., It is I).
Objective case is used after transitive verbs and prepositions (e.g., between you and me).
Indefinite pronouns like 'everyone', 'somebody', 'none' usually take singular pronouns.
Mnemonic: GAP (Gender, Antecedent, Person match).

3. Tense Consistency and Sequence

• Past tense in the principal clause is usually followed by a past tense in the subordinate clause.

Universal truths, habitual actions, and historical presents remain in the simple present regardless of the main clause.
Avoid sudden shifts from past to present tense within the same narrative context.
Mnemonic: PAS (Past Always Sequenced).

4. Modifier Placement and Dangling Modifiers

• Modifying phrases or clauses must be placed as close as possible to the word they modify.

A dangling modifier occurs when the implicit subject of the modifier does not match the actual subject in the main clause.
Avoid split infinitives where an adverb splits 'to' and the verb stem unless emphasis requires it.
Mnemonic: MCM (Modifier Close Match).

5. Parallel Structure and Correlatives

• Elements in a series or joined by coordinate conjunctions must share the same grammatical form (nouns with nouns, infinitives with infinitives).

Correlative conjunctions (not only...but also, either...or, neither...nor) must precede parallel grammatical units.
Mnemonic: PAPS (Parallel Axis, Parallel Structure).

6. Articles, Determiners, and Prepositions

• Use 'a'/'an' for non-specific singular countable nouns; use 'the' for specific or previously mentioned nouns.

Avoid redundant articles before abstract nouns used in a general sense.
Prepositions must be idiomatically paired with correct verbs and adjectives (e.g., differ from, prohibit from).
Mnemonic: DAP (Determiner Article Precision).

Tricks to Remember

  • Trick 1: The 'Nearer Subject' Rule for Correlatives:

    For 'either...or', 'neither...nor', 'not only...but also', always look at the subject closest to the verb and match the verb number to that specific noun/pronoun.

  • Trick 2: The 'Preposition Trap' Check:

    Whenever you spot a preposition (in, on, at, by, for, with, from, between, among), immediately check if the following pronoun is in the objective case (me, him, her, us, them).

  • Trick 3: The 'Not Only... But Also' Parallelism Check:

    Ensure the words immediately following 'not only' mirror the exact grammatical category following 'but also' (e.g., Not only spoke but also wrote).

  • Trick 4: The 'Dangling Modifier' Test:

    Ask 'Who or what is performing the action?' right after an introductory modifying phrase. If the subject is missing immediately after the comma, it is a dangling modifier error.

  • Trick 5: The 'Hardly/Scarcely vs. No Sooner' Pair Check:

    Scan instantly for correlative pairings: Hardly/Scarcely must link with 'when'/'before', while No Sooner must link with 'than'.

  • Trick 6: The 'One of the' Rule:

    Remember the formula: 'One of the + Plural Noun + Singular Verb' (unless followed by a relative pronoun like 'who', which changes the verb to plural).

  • Trick 7: Uncountable Noun Verification:

    Check words like scenery, furniture, information, advice, machinery, luggage—they never take plural markers (-s/-es) and always take singular verbs.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mistake 1: Confusing 'Each' and 'Every' with Plural Verbs:

    Students often treat expressions like 'Each of the boys' as plural because of 'boys'. Correction: 'Each' and 'Every' are strictly singular and require singular verbs.

  • Mistake 2: Overusing Relative Pronoun Agreement Errors:

    In 'One of the books that was lost', students ignore the antecedent 'books'. Correction: The relative pronoun 'that' refers to 'books' (plural), so the verb must be 'were'.

  • Mistake 3: Mixing Up Adjectives and Adverbs:

    Using 'He performed good' instead of 'He performed well'. Correction: Verbs of action are modified by adverbs, not adjectives.

  • Mistake 4: Incorrect Use of 'As well as' Concord:

    Writing 'The captain along with his crew were drowned'. Correction: When joined by 'along with', the verb agrees with the first subject ('captain'), making it 'was'.

  • Mistake 5: Misplacing 'Only':

    Placing 'only' haphazardly in sentences alters meaning. Correction: Place 'only' immediately before the exact word or phrase it is intended to modify.

  • Mistake 6: Redundant Double Comparatives:

    Using phrases like 'more better' or 'most optimal'. Correction: Absolute adjectives and irregular comparatives do not accept more/most.

  • Mistake 7: Blundering Gerund and Infinitive Structures:

    Writing 'I look forward to meet you'. Correction: Phrasal verbs ending in 'to' (like look forward to, accustomed to) take a gerund (-ing): meeting.

Point-wise Detailed Summary

  • 1. Subject-Verb Concord:

    Verbs must strictly match subjects in number and person, unaffected by intervening prepositional phrases.

  • 2. Pronoun Case Integrity:

    Distinguish clearly between subjective, objective, and possessive cases of pronouns across all sentence structures.

  • 3. Tense Harmony:

    Maintain logical tense consistency between main and subordinate clauses unless universal truths dictate otherwise.

  • 4. Precision in Modifiers:

    Place modifiers adjacent to their targets to prevent ambiguity and dangling modifier errors.

  • 5. Strict Parallelism:

    Ensure coordinate and correlative elements maintain identical grammatical forms.

  • 6. Article and Determiner Accuracy:

    Apply definite ('the') and indefinite ('a/an') articles based on specificity and countability rules.

  • 7. Prepositional Collocations:

    Master fixed preposition-verb and preposition-adjective pairings tested frequently in exams.

  • 8. Conditional Moods:

    Use correct auxiliary verbs (were, had, would have) in hypothetical and conditional clauses.

  • 9. Eradication of Redundancies:

    Eliminate double negatives, redundant modifiers, and tautological expressions like 'return back'.

  • 10. Inversion and Conjunction Pairs:

    Memorize mandatory correlative pairings such as Hardly...when, Scarcely...before, No sooner...than.

  • 11. Uncountable Noun Rules:

    Treat abstract and mass nouns as singular entities requiring singular verb agreements.

  • 12. Comparative and Superlative Precision:

    Avoid double grading and ensure items being compared belong to the same logical class.

UPSC Notes & InsightsUPSC नोट्स और अंतर्दृष्टि

For the UP Assistant Teacher Exam, read the entire sentence twice before looking at options; first for general flow, and second for syntactic breakdown of parts of speech.

Pay extra attention to collective nouns and distance-measure nouns (e.g., 'Ten miles is a long distance'), as examiners frequently exploit these apparent plural traps.

Examiners often place long descriptive clauses between the subject and the verb to distract candidates from spotting concord mismatches.

Do not rely solely on 'what sounds right' (ear test), as colloquial spoken English often violates standard formal written grammar rules tested in competitive exams.

Key Takeawaysमुख्य बातें

  • Master Subject-Verb Agreement rules involving intervening phrases and correlative conjunctions.
  • Always check pronoun case after prepositions and conjunctions like 'than' or 'as'.
  • Identify and correct dangling modifiers by linking introductory clauses to the immediate grammatical subject.
  • Ensure strict parallel structure across series and correlative pairings (not only...but also).
  • Memorize fixed prepositional collocations and eliminate tautologies and redundancies.
  • Differentiate between singular uncountable nouns and plural countable nouns to maintain concord accuracy.
