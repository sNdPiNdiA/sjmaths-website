Concepts and Theories

1. Prepositions of Time (At, On, In) with Mnemonic

• AT: Used for exact clock times, specific points of time, and festivals (at 5 PM, at noon, at Diwali).

ON: Used for specific days, dates, and days of the week (on Monday, on 15th August).
IN: Used for longer periods like months, years, centuries, and seasons (in January, in 2024, in summer).
Mnemonic: 'A-O-I' stands for 'At is precise, On is for days, In is for length' (Pyramid Rule: Smallest time at top with AT, medium with ON, largest at base with IN).

2. Prepositions of Place and Position

• AT: Denotes an exact point or specific location (at the bus stop, at the door).

IN: Denotes enclosed spaces, boundaries, towns, countries, and larger areas (in the room, in India).
ON: Denotes a surface or a line boundary (on the table, on the wall, on the floor).
BETWEEN vs. AMONG: Between is used for two persons/things; Among is used for more than two persons/things.

3. Prepositions of Movement and Direction

• TO: Indicates movement towards a destination (go to school).

TOWARDS: Indicates direction without reaching the exact destination (walking towards the station).
INTO: Indicates movement inside an enclosed space or change of medium (jumped into the river).
THROUGH: Indicates passing from one side to another inside a medium (walked through the tunnel).
ACROSS: Indicates movement from one side to another on a flat surface (walked across the road).

4. Fixed Prepositions and Collocations with Mnemonic

• Certain words demand specific prepositions regardless of literal translation (afraid of, good at, differ from, comply with).

Die OF a disease, Die FROM a cause (like wounds or overwork), Die BY violence/suicide.
Angry WITH a person, Angry AT a thing/behavior.
Mnemonic: 'DAD' = Differs Among / Dependent On fixed patterns.

5. Gerunds and Infinitives after Prepositions

• When a verb immediately follows a preposition, it must take the -ing form (gerund).

Exception / Special Rule: Words ending in '-to' acting as prepositions (e.g., look forward to, accustomed to, habituated to, with a view to) must be followed by a gerund, not a base verb.
Example: I am looking forward to meeting you (not meet).

6. Omission and Redundancy of Prepositions

• Prepositions are omitted before expressions of time beginning with this, that, next, last, every (e.g., He came on last Monday is incorrect; He came last Monday is correct).

Avoid redundant prepositions such as 'return back' (use return), 'enter into a room' (use enter the room - unless entering an agreement/contract).

Tricks to Remember

  • Trick 1: The Pyramid Rule of Time (At, On, In):

    Visualize a pyramid from top to bottom: At sits at the sharp peak for exact time points; On sits on the middle wider layer for calendar days/dates; In sits at the massive base for months, years, and centuries.

  • Trick 2: The 'Surface vs Volume' Spatial Trick:

    Use On for flat surfaces (touching from above like on the desk), In for 3D containers (submerged or enclosed like in the box), and At for a dimensionless point on a map (like at the crossroads).

  • Trick 3: 'DIED' Cause Analysis Trick:

    Remember disease = OF, wounds/hunger = FROM, suicide/violence = BY. Acronym: D-O-F (Disease Of Fever).

  • Trick 4: The '-TO' Gerund Trap Trick:

    If a phrase ends in 'to' and you can replace it with a noun, the 'to' is a preposition requiring a gerund (looking forward to seeing, accustomed to working).

  • Trick 5: 'MARRY' Voice Trick:

    Active voice takes no preposition (A man marries a woman); Passive voice takes 'to' (A woman was married to a man).

  • Trick 6: 'ENTER' Agreement Exception:

    Physical entry takes no preposition (enter the hall); metaphorical entry into agreements takes 'into' (enter into a treaty).

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mistake 1: Using 'than' with words ending in '-ior':

    Students write superior than. Correct rule: words like superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior take 'to', not 'than'.

  • Mistake 2: Confusing 'Beside' and 'Besides':

    Students use beside meaning addition. Beside means by the side of; Besides means in addition to.

  • Mistake 3: Unnecessary Prepositions after Verbs:

    Writing order for, demand for (as verbs), pervade throughout. Verbs like order, demand, attack, resemble, comprise do not take prepositions in active voice.

  • Mistake 4: Wrong Preposition in Means of Transport:

    Using 'in' instead of 'on' for public transport. Rule: on a bus, on a train, on a plane; but in a car, in a taxi (where you cannot stand up).

  • Mistake 5: Misusing 'Since' and 'For' in Tenses:

    Students interchange them. Since is for a point of time (exact starting point); For is for a period of time (duration).

  • Mistake 6: Split Infinitives and Preposition Placement:

    Believing ending a sentence with a preposition is always grammatically incorrect. Modern English permits terminal prepositions in relative clauses and interrogatives (What are you looking at?).

Point-wise Detailed Summary

  • 1. Definition & Role:

    Prepositions link nouns/pronouns to other words, establishing spatial, temporal, or logical relations.

  • 2. Objective Case Rule:

    The noun or pronoun following a preposition must always be in the objective case (between you and me).

  • 3. Time Markers:

    At for exact times, On for days/dates, and In for months/years/seasons.

  • 4. Place Markers:

    At for specific points, In for enclosed areas, and On for surfaces.

  • 5. Group Division:

    Use Between strictly for two entities and Among for three or more entities.

  • 6. Motion Distinction:

    Into denotes movement inward; In denotes static enclosure; To shows directional destination.

  • 7. Gerund Governance:

    Verbs following prepositions must take the -ing (gerund) form, especially after phrasal prepositions ending in 'to'.

  • 8. Fixed Prepositions:

    Master high-frequency collocations such as afraid of, good at, differ from, comply with, superior to.

  • 9. Death Prepositions:

    Distinguish causes of death: die of (disease), die from (cause), die by (violence).

  • 10. Redundancy Elimination:

    Avoid redundant pairings like return back, enter into a room, and order for.

  • 11. Transport Rules:

    Use on for public transit where you can walk, and in for private enclosed vehicles.

  • 12. Exam Utility:

    Crucial for scoring high in spotting errors and sentence improvement sections of the UP Assistant Teacher exam.

UPSC Notes & InsightsUPSC नोट्स और अंतर्दृष्टि

For the UP Assistant Teacher exam, memorize fixed prepositions (e.g., 'abide by', 'accuse of', 'abstain from') as examiners frequently test verb-preposition and adjective-preposition collocations.

Watch out for questions testing 'superior than' or 'junior than'. These are classic traps; the correct preposition is always 'to' for adjectives ending in '-ior'.

Pay close attention to sentences containing 'look forward to', 'with a view to', and 'addicted to'. Examiners place base verbs after them, but they require gerunds (-ing).

Do not confuse 'beside' (by the side of) with 'besides' (in addition to). Sentence correction questions often exploit this minimal spelling difference.

Key Takeawaysमुख्य बातें

  • Prepositions govern the objective case of pronouns (e.g., between you and me).
  • Time hierarchy rule: At (clock time) > On (days) > In (months/years).
  • Use between for two and among for more than two entities.
  • Adjectives ending in '-ior' (superior, junior, senior) take to, never than.
  • Verbs following prepositions must be in the gerund form (-ing).
  • Physical entry takes no preposition, while contractual entry takes into.
