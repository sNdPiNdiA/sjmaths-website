Concepts and Theories

1. Fundamental Rule of Concord

• A singular subject requires a singular verb (e.g., adds -s/-es to present tense: he goes).

A plural subject requires a plural verb (e.g., base form in present tense: they go).
Agreement applies strictly to Person (1st, 2nd, 3rd) and Number (Singular, Plural).
Mnemonic: Singular Subject = Singular Verb (ends in 's'); Plural Subject = Plural Verb (no 's').

2. Compound Subjects with 'And'

• Two or more singular subjects joined by 'and' generally take a plural verb (e.g., Ram and Shyam are playing).

Exception 1 (Single Idea/Pair): If two nouns express a single composite idea or item, use a singular verb (e.g., Bread and butter is my favorite breakfast).
Exception 2 (Same Person/Title): If two nouns refer to the same person and have one article, use a singular verb (e.g., The novelist and poet is dead).

3. Proximity Rule & Intervening Phrases

• The verb must agree with the true subject, not with a noun or pronoun that comes between the subject and the verb.

Intervening phrases introduced by along with, together with, as well as, accompanied by, in addition to do not make the subject plural.
Example: The captain, along with his crew members, was drowned.
Mnemonic: As Well As = Attention to Word Alone (ignore the phrase after it).

4. Indefinite Pronouns and Quantifiers

• Pronouns like each, every, everyone, someone, anybody, nobody, nothing, either, neither always take a singular verb.

Expressions like none, all, some, any can take singular or plural verbs depending on whether the noun they refer to is countable or uncountable.
Example: Each of the boys has a prize vs None of the water is wasted.

5. Collective Nouns and Units of Measurement

• Collective nouns (jury, committee, family, audience) take a singular verb when acting as a single unit.

They take a plural verb when members are acting individually or divided in opinion (The committee are divided in their opinions).
Expressions of time, money, distance, and weight treated as a single quantity take a singular verb (Ten miles is a long walk).

Tricks to Remember

  • Trick 1: The 'S' Mirror Test:

    If the singular subject does not end in 's' (e.g., boy), the singular verb must end in 's' (e.g., runs). They mirror each other's 's'.

  • Trick 2: The Bracket Eraser:

    Mentally put brackets around descriptive phrases starting with as well as, along with, with, together with, and erase them before choosing the verb.

  • Trick 3: The 'Nearest Neighbor' Rule:

    For OR / NOR / BUT ALSO, ignore the first subject completely. Focus exclusively on the noun closest to the blank/verb to determine singular or plural.

  • Trick 4: The 'Each & Every' Magnet:

    Whenever you spot 'Each' or 'Every' in a sentence, magnetize your mind to a singular verb, no matter how many nouns follow.

  • Trick 5: The 'One Of' Divider:

    Spot the difference: 'One of + Plural Noun + WHO' takes a Plural Verb, but 'The ONLY one of + Plural Noun + WHO' takes a Singular Verb.

  • Trick 6: Uncountable Noun Shield:

    Words ending in -ery (scenery, machinery, stationery) and abstract nouns (advice, information, luggage) reject plural verbs under any circumstance.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mistake 1: Plural attraction in intervening phrases:

    Students incorrectly write: 'The quality of these mangoes are bad' instead of 'The quality of these mangoes is bad' because they look at 'mangoes' instead of the true subject 'quality'.

  • Mistake 2: Confusing 'Each of' with plural verbs:

    Students write: 'Each of the girls have a pen' instead of 'Each of the girls has a pen' due to the presence of the plural noun 'girls'.

  • Mistake 3: Treating collective nouns as always plural:

    Students assume words like 'jury' or 'committee' always take plural verbs. They must be singular when acting as a unified body.

  • Mistake 4: Misapplying the 'Either... Or' rule:

    Students match the verb with the first subject in 'Either he or I is...' instead of the correct 'Either he or I am...' based on the nearest subject.

  • Mistake 5: Treating 'Mathematics' or 'News' as plural:

    Students see the trailing 's' in mathematics, physics, news, or measles and mistakenly use a plural verb.

  • Mistake 6: Ignoring compound subjects expressing one unit:

    Students treat 'Rice and curry' or 'Slow and steady' as two separate entities taking a plural verb, ignoring the single composite concept rule.

Point-wise Detailed Summary

  • 1. Basic Concord Rule:

    A verb must agree with its subject in number and person.

  • 2. And Conjunction:

    Two singular subjects joined by 'and' take a plural verb, unless they form a single unit or refer to the same person.

  • 3. Prepositional Modifiers:

    Phrases like as well as, along with do not change the number of the primary subject.

  • 4. Indefinite Pronouns:

    Pronouns like each, every, everyone, someone always take singular verbs.

  • 5. Correlative Proximity:

    With either...or / neither...nor, the verb agrees with the nearest subject.

  • 6. Distance, Money, Time:

    Sums of money, periods of time, or distances considered as a whole take a singular verb.

  • 7. Relative Pronoun Agreement:

    Verbs in relative clauses agree with the antecedent of the relative pronoun.

  • 8. One of / The Only One of:

    Distinguish between 'one of + plural noun + plural verb' and 'the only one + singular verb'.

  • 9. Plural Forms with Singular Meaning:

    Certain diseases, subjects of study, and games ending in 's' take singular verbs.

  • 10. Nouns of Quantity:

    Fractions and percentages take singular or plural verbs depending on the noun following 'of'.

UPSC Notes & InsightsUPSC नोट्स और अंतर्दृष्टि

In the UP Assistant Teacher exam, always underline the true subject and cross out intervening prepositional phrases before determining the verb form.

Examiners frequently use distracting plural nouns right before the verb in sentences starting with 'Each of', 'One of', or 'Every one of'. Do not fall into this proximity trap.

Memorize exceptions like 'Bread and butter', 'Cart and horse', and 'Slow and steady' as they appear repeatedly in error-detection questions.

Do not confuse uncountable nouns like 'furniture', 'advice', and 'information' as plural just because they represent multiple items; they always require singular verbs.

Key Takeawaysमुख्य बातें

  • Subject-verb agreement requires strict matching of number and person between the subject and the verb.
  • Intervening phrases introduced by 'along with', 'together with', or 'as well as' do not affect the subject's number.
  • Correlative conjunctions like 'neither...nor' and 'either...or' dictate that the verb agrees with the nearest subject.
  • Collective nouns take singular verbs when unified and plural verbs when divided.
  • Words like 'each', 'every', and indefinite pronouns ('someone', 'anybody') strictly demand singular verbs.
  • Plural-looking nouns representing subjects (Physics), diseases (Measles), or news take singular verbs.
