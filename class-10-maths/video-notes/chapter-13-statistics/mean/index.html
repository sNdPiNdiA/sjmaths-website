Prerequisite 1 · Foundation Recap

What is the Mean?

In Class 9, you learned that the Mean (or Arithmetic Average) is the single numerical balance point that represents an entire set of data.

Mean (x̄) = Sum of all observationsTotal number of observations = Σ xn
  • If you have individual scores: 8, 12, 15, 25
  • Sum = 8 + 12 + 15 + 25 = 60  ·  Total count n = 4
  • Mean x̄ = 60 ÷ 4 = 15.
  • Important property: Mean is sensitive to extreme outliers!
Visual Balance Concept Center of Mass

15Observation 3
25Observation 4
Sum = 8 + 12 + 15 + 25 = 60
Divide by n = 4 ➔ Balance point x̄ = 15

When data grows into hundreds of observations, adding them one by one becomes impossible. That is why we organize data into frequency tables.

Prerequisite 2 · Foundation Recap

Ungrouped Frequency Table

When distinct observations repeat multiple times, we list each distinct value xᵢ along with its frequency fᵢ (how many times it occurs).

x̄ = Σ fᵢ xᵢΣ fᵢ = Σ fᵢ xᵢN
  • fᵢ (Frequency): Acts as the statistical weight of that observation.
  • N = Σ fᵢ: The total number of all students/observations.
  • Instead of adding 5 three times (5+5+5), we multiply: 3 × 5 = 15.

Core Transition · Class 9 to Class 10

Why Grouped Data?

If 100 students score marks between 0 and 100, a discrete table would need 100 separate rows! To make large data manageable, we group it into class intervals (e.g. 0–10, 10–20).

The Big Problem: Inside the interval [10 – 20], we do not know the exact score of any individual student!
  • The Solution: We assume all values cluster around the midpoint!
  • This midpoint is called the Class Mark (xᵢ).

Prerequisite 4 · Interval Preparation

Make Classes Continuous

Continuous classes (0–10, 10–20) share boundaries. If inclusive classes leave a gap (10–19, 20–29), convert them before proceeding.

Gap = 20 − 19 = 1 ➔ Half-Gap = 0.5
Rule: Subtract 0.5 from Lower Limit, Add 0.5 to Upper Limit!
  • 10–19 becomes 9.5 – 19.5 (Midpoint = 14.5).
  • 20–29 becomes 19.5 – 29.5 (Midpoint = 24.5).
  • Crucial: Class mark is identical whether computed from limits or continuous boundaries!

CBSE Method 1 of 3

Direct Method: Concept

The most straightforward method. Find midpoint xᵢ for every class, multiply directly by frequency fᵢ, sum all products, and divide by total N.

x̄ = Σ fᵢ xᵢΣ fᵢ = Σ fᵢ xᵢN
  • When to use: When class marks xᵢ and frequencies fᵢ are small integers.
  • Requires 4 standard columns in board exams: Class Interval, fᵢ, xᵢ, fᵢ xᵢ.
Direct Method Algorithm Execution Steps

3
Step 3: Sum and Divide by NFind Σfᵢ and Σfᵢxᵢ, then apply x̄ = Σfᵢxᵢ ÷ N

Motivation for Method 2

Why Not Direct Always?

Imagine if class intervals were daily wages: ₹200–₹250, ₹250–₹300. Then class marks are 225, 275, 325 and frequencies are 18, 24, 32.

Multiplying 275 × 24 by hand takes 5 minutes and invites multiplication slips during board exams!
  • The Genius Idea: Shift the Origin!
  • Pick a convenient middle number 'a' (Assumed Mean).
  • Subtract 'a' from all class marks: dᵢ = xᵢ − a. Large numbers shrink down to small manageable deviations!
Origin Shift Principle Shrinking Multi-Digit Numbers
xᵢ = 275Raw midpoint
a = 250Assumed origin
dᵢ = +25Tiny deviation!
fᵢ dᵢSuper fast to multiply
Instead of multiplying 275 × 24 = 6600,
we only multiply 25 × 24 = 600!
CBSE Method 2 of 3

Assumed Mean Method

Choose an assumed mean 'a' near the center of the xᵢ values. Compute signed deviations dᵢ = xᵢ − a, weight them by frequency, and add back 'a'.

dᵢ = xᵢ − a  ·  x̄ = a + Σ fᵢ dᵢΣ fᵢ
  • dᵢ = xᵢ − a: Signed deviation from assumed mean a.
  • Values below a give negative deviations. Keep the signs!
  • Average deviation (Σ fᵢ dᵢ ÷ N) shifts 'a' to the exact true mean.

CBSE Method 3 of 3

Step-Deviation Method

When all class intervals share a common width h, the deviations are all multiples of h. Divide by h to turn deviations into tiny single integers!

uᵢ = xᵢ − ah  ·  x̄ = a + h · Σ fᵢ uᵢΣ fᵢ
  • Step variable: uᵢ = dᵢ ÷ h. Turns −20, −10, 0, 10 into −2, −1, 0, 1.
  • Golden Rule: You divided by h, so you MUST multiply the correction bracket by h!
  • Do NOT use step-deviation if class widths are unequal.
Step Compression (h = 10) Single-Digit Arithmetic
d₁ = −20u₁ = −2
d₂ = −10u₂ = −1
d₃ = 0u₃ = 0
d₄ = +10u₄ = +1
Divide by h ➔ Multiply small single digits ➔ Multiply back by h at the end!

Decision Matrix

Which Method to Choose?

All three methods give the exact same answer. Choose the one that minimizes calculation risk in your exam paper.

  • Direct Method: When numbers are small (single/double digits). No extra columns needed.
  • Assumed Mean Method: When class marks are large, but interval widths are unequal.
  • Step-Deviation Method: Best for large numbers with uniform class width h.
Golden Rule: If the question specifies a method by name, you MUST use that method for full marks!

CBSE Examiner Insights

Full-Mark Presentation

CBSE step marking awards marks for formula, table construction, correct substitutions, and units.

  • Always state formula at the top (½ mark).
  • Construct columns with neat labels (1½ marks).
  • State assumptions clearly (a = 25, h = 10).
  • Box final answer with units (minutes, kg, ₹).
