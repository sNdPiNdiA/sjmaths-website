Class 10 Maths: Zeroes of Polynomial Previous Year Questions

Mastering the zeroes of polynomials is one of the highest-scoring topics in CBSE Class 10 Board Mathematics. Below are curated board exam questions with complete step-by-step marking scheme solutions.

CBSE 2020 • 1 Mark Question 1

If one zero of the quadratic polynomial \(x^2 + 3x + k\) is 2, find the value of \(k\).

Step-by-Step Solution:

Let \(p(x) = x^2 + 3x + k\). Since \(2\) is a zero of \(p(x)\), we have \(p(2) = 0\).
\((2)^2 + 3(2) + k = 0 \implies 4 + 6 + k = 0 \implies 10 + k = 0 \implies k = -10\).
Answer: The value of \(k\) is -10.

CBSE 2019 • 3 Marks Question 2

Find the zeroes of the quadratic polynomial \(6x^2 - 3 - 7x\) and verify the relationship between the zeroes and the coefficients.

Step-by-Step Solution:

First, arrange the polynomial in standard descending powers: \(p(x) = 6x^2 - 7x - 3\).
Split the middle term: product \(= 6 \times (-3) = -18\), sum \(= -7\). Factors are \(-9\) and \(+2\).
\(6x^2 - 9x + 2x - 3 = 3x(2x - 3) + 1(2x - 3) = (2x - 3)(3x + 1) = 0\).
Hence, the zeroes are \(\alpha = \frac{3}{2}\) and \(\beta = -\frac{1}{3}\).

Verification of Coefficients:
Here \(a = 6, b = -7, c = -3\).
1. Sum of zeroes: \(\alpha + \beta = \frac{3}{2} + \left(-\frac{1}{3}\right) = \frac{9 - 2}{6} = \frac{7}{6} = -\frac{b}{a} = -\frac{(-7)}{6} = \frac{7}{6}\). (Verified)
2. Product of zeroes: \(\alpha\beta = \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)\left(-\frac{1}{3}\right) = -\frac{3}{6} = -\frac{1}{2} = \frac{c}{a} = \frac{-3}{6} = -\frac{1}{2}\). (Verified)

CBSE 2022 • 3 Marks Question 3

If \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are zeroes of the polynomial \(x^2 - 5x + 6\), find the value of (i) \(\alpha^2 + \beta^2\) and (ii) \(\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta}\).

Step-by-Step Solution:

For \(x^2 - 5x + 6\), \(a = 1, b = -5, c = 6\).
Sum of zeroes \(\alpha + \beta = -b/a = 5\). Product of zeroes \(\alpha\beta = c/a = 6\).

(i) \(\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 2\alpha\beta = (5)^2 - 2(6) = 25 - 12 = 13\).
(ii) \(\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta} = \frac{\alpha + \beta}{\alpha\beta} = \frac{5}{6}\).
Answers: (i) 13, (ii) 5/6.
