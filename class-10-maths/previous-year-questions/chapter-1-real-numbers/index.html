CBSE Board Exam Blueprint & Topic Weightage: Real Numbers

In the Class 10 CBSE Board Examination, Real Numbers constitutes the entire Unit 1: Number Systems, contributing 6 marks to the 80-mark theory paper. Historically, board questions follow a consistent and dependable pattern:

  • Section A (1 Mark MCQ): Questions testing the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, prime factorisation exponents (such as finding the sum of exponents of prime factors), or applying the relationship \(\text{HCF}(a, b) \times \text{LCM}(a, b) = a \times b\).
  • Section B or C (2 or 3 Marks): Rigorous proof of irrationality for numbers like \(\sqrt{2}\), \(\sqrt{3}\), \(\sqrt{5}\), or composite forms such as \(3 + 2\sqrt{5}\) using proof by contradiction.
  • Word Problems (3 Marks): Real-life synchronization problems (e.g., bells tolling together, traffic lights, morning walks along circular paths) requiring the calculation of HCF or LCM.

Key Revision Tips for Board Examinations

When proving that a number is irrational, clearly state the initial assumption that \(p/q\) is in simplest form where \(p\) and \(q\) are co-prime positive integers with \(q \neq 0\). Showing that \(p\) and \(q\) share a common factor contradicts the assumption of co-primality. In prime factorisation questions, always express your final answers in standard canonical prime power format.
