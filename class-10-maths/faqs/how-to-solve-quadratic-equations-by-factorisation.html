How to Solve Quadratic Equations by Factorisation?

Last updated: January 2025 | Author: Sandeep Jaiswal, PGT Maths

Quick Answer: To solve by factorisation: 1) Write in standard form ax²+bx+c=0, 2) Split middle term into two terms whose product is ac and sum is b, 3) Factor by grouping, 4) Use zero product rule: if (x-α)(x-β)=0, then x=α or x=β.

Understanding Factorisation Method

The factorisation method is one of the simplest ways to solve quadratic equations. It works by expressing the quadratic expression as a product of two linear factors. When the product of two factors is zero, at least one of the factors must be zero.

Step-by-Step Procedure

1
Write in Standard Form

Ensure the equation is in the form ax² + bx + c = 0

2
Split the Middle Term

Find two numbers p and q such that p + q = b and p × q = a × c

3
Factor by Grouping

Group terms and factor out common factors from each group

4
Apply Zero Product Rule

Set each factor equal to zero and solve for x

Key Formula

If (x - α)(x - β) = 0, then x = α or x = β

Detailed Examples

Example 1: Simple Factorisation

Equation: x² - 5x + 6 = 0

Step 1: a=1, b=-5, c=6. Find p, q such that p+q=-5 and p×q=6

Step 2: p=-2, q=-3 (since -2 + (-3) = -5 and (-2) × (-3) = 6)

Step 3: x² - 2x - 3x + 6 = 0 → x(x-2) - 3(x-2) = 0 → (x-2)(x-3) = 0

Step 4: x - 2 = 0 or x - 3 = 0 → x = 2 or x = 3

Example 2: With Coefficient a ≠ 1

Equation: 2x² - 5x + 3 = 0

Step 1: a=2, b=-5, c=3. Find p, q such that p+q=-5 and p×q=2×3=6

Step 2: p=-2, q=-3 (since -2 + (-3) = -5 and (-2) × (-3) = 6)

Step 3: 2x² - 2x - 3x + 3 = 0 → 2x(x-1) - 3(x-1) = 0 → (2x-3)(x-1) = 0

Step 4: 2x - 3 = 0 or x - 1 = 0 → x = 3/2 or x = 1

Example 3: Identifying Quadratic Equations First

Problem: Is (x+1)² = 2(x-3) a quadratic equation?

Step 1: Expand: x² + 2x + 1 = 2x - 6

Step 2: Simplify: x² + 7 = 0

Answer: Yes, it's quadratic with a=1, b=0, c=7

Solving: x² = -7 → No real roots

Board Exam Tips

  • Always write the equation in standard form first
  • Check if a ≠ 0 before proceeding
  • Practice splitting middle term with different coefficient combinations
  • For equations with a=1, find factors of c that add up to b
  • For equations with a≠1, find factors of (a×c) that add up to b
  • Always verify your answers by substituting back

When Factorisation Works Best

Factorisation is most effective when:

  • The discriminant D = b² - 4ac is a perfect square
  • The roots are rational numbers
  • The coefficients are small integers

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Forgetting to Check a ≠ 0

If a = 0, the equation becomes linear, not quadratic.

Mistake 2: Wrong Splitting

Always verify: p + q = b AND p × q = a × c

Mistake 3: Sign Errors

Be careful with negative signs, especially when squaring negative numbers.
