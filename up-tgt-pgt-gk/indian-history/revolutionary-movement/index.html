Revolutionary Nationalism and INA: Kakori, Bhagat Singh, Azad and Bose

Revolutionary nationalism used secret organisations, political action and, in some phases, armed methods to challenge colonial rule.

UP TGT PGT GK6 concepts42 quiz questions10-question test

Topic overview

  • Revolutionary nationalism used secret organisations, political action and, in some phases, armed methods to challenge colonial rule.
  • The movement developed through different regional networks rather than one continuous organisation or single command.
  • The Kakori Action, the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association and the work of Bhagat Singh and Chandrashekhar Azad are central UP-linked themes.
  • Subhas Chandra Bose and the Indian National Army represented a distinct armed effort connected with the international conditions of the Second World War.
  • Revolutionaries influenced political consciousness, sacrifice, youth mobilisation and the wider debate about methods of the freedom struggle.

Concept 1

Origins and Revolutionary Organisations

Revolutionary nationalism emerged from dissatisfaction with limited constitutional politics, repression and the desire for immediate independence.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Early revolutionary networks developed in Bengal, Maharashtra, Punjab and other regions during the first two decades of the twentieth century.
  • Anushilan Samiti and Jugantar became important Bengal-based organisations with physical training and secret political networks.
  • Abhinav Bharat, associated with V. D. Savarkar, represented an important Maharashtra-based revolutionary organisation.
  • The Ghadar movement mobilised Indian expatriates, especially in North America, against colonial rule during the First World War period.
  • The First World War, the Defence of India Act and revolutionary trials intensified political repression and radicalisation.
  • The Hindustan Republican Association was formed in 1924 to organise an armed struggle for an independent Indian republic.
  • Later revolutionaries increasingly discussed socialism, workers, peasants and the social meaning of political freedom.

Key points

  • Anushilan Samiti and Jugantar—Bengal
  • Abhinav Bharat—Maharashtra
  • Ghadar movement—overseas revolutionary mobilisation
  • HRA—formed in 1924
  • Later organisations connected independence with republican and socialist ideas.
Worked examples
  • Ghadar activists returning to India during the First World War period
  • Hindustan Republican Association and its north Indian network

Additional examples

  • Ghadar activists returning to India during the First World War period
  • Hindustan Republican Association and its north Indian network
Important distinctions
  • Early secret societies focused on organisation and armed preparation, while later groups articulated wider republican or socialist goals.
  • The Ghadar movement worked through an overseas network, whereas HRA and HSRA operated mainly through Indian underground cells.
Common misconceptions
  • All revolutionary organisations were branches of one central body.
  • Revolutionary nationalism was limited to Bengal alone.
Exam focus
  • Match organisations with regions and leaders.
  • Remember HRA formation in 1924.
  • Distinguish Ghadar, HRA and HSRA.
Concept 2

Kakori Action of 1925

The Kakori Action was an HRA operation to obtain funds and became a major landmark of revolutionary activity in Uttar Pradesh.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The train action took place near Kakori, close to Lucknow, on 9 August 1925.
  • Revolutionaries targeted government treasury money being transported by train rather than private passenger property.
  • Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Rajendra Lahiri and Chandrashekhar Azad were among the associated revolutionaries.
  • The action was intended to finance revolutionary work and expose the vulnerability of colonial authority.
  • A wide investigation led to the Kakori Conspiracy Case and long prison sentences for several participants.
  • Rajendra Lahiri was executed on 17 December 1927; Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan and Roshan Singh were executed on 19 December 1927.
  • The case strengthened the public image of revolutionary sacrifice and Hindu–Muslim cooperation.

Key points

  • Date—9 August 1925
  • Place—near Kakori, Lucknow district
  • Organisation—Hindustan Republican Association
  • Important names—Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Rajendra Lahiri, Azad
  • Kakori case led to executions and long imprisonments.
Worked examples
  • Ram Prasad Bismil’s leadership and writings
  • Ashfaqulla Khan’s participation and execution

Additional examples

  • Ram Prasad Bismil’s leadership and writings
  • Ashfaqulla Khan’s participation and execution
Important distinctions
  • Kakori was primarily a revolutionary fund-raising action; the Assembly bombing was designed as a political act of protest and publicity.
  • The Kakori group belonged to HRA, while the later Bhagat Singh network operated through HSRA.
Common misconceptions
  • Kakori happened in 1928 with the Lahore Conspiracy Case.
  • The purpose was ordinary robbery rather than financing the revolutionary organisation.
Exam focus
  • Memorise 9 August 1925 and the Kakori–Lucknow connection.
  • Use correct leader–organisation matching.
  • Remember the execution dates as a chronology pair.
Concept 3

Bhagat Singh and HSRA

Bhagat Singh and his colleagues transformed revolutionary action into a highly public political message centred on anti-imperialism and social change.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Hindustan Republican Association was reorganised as the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association in 1928.
  • The reorganisation reflected a stronger commitment to socialism and a workers-and-peasants-oriented vision of freedom.
  • Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt threw low-intensity bombs in the Central Legislative Assembly at Delhi on 8 April 1929.
  • The Assembly action was intended to make the deaf hear and was followed by voluntary arrest rather than escape.
  • Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were tried in the Lahore Conspiracy Case.
  • Saunders was assassinated at Lahore on 17 December 1928 in an action connected with the response to the death of Lala Lajpat Rai.
  • Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev were executed on 23 March 1931 at Lahore.

Key points

  • HSRA—1928
  • Assembly action—8 April 1929
  • Saunders action—17 December 1928
  • Lahore Conspiracy Case
  • Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev—executed 23 March 1931
Worked examples
  • Assembly bombing by Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt
  • Hunger strike and prison rights campaign by revolutionary prisoners

Additional examples

  • Assembly bombing by Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt
  • Hunger strike and prison rights campaign by revolutionary prisoners
Important distinctions
  • The Assembly bombing aimed at political communication and arrest, while the Saunders action was a targeted retaliation.
  • Bhagat Singh’s writings connected political independence with social and economic transformation.
Common misconceptions
  • Bhagat Singh intended to cause mass casualties in the Assembly.
  • The Assembly action and Saunders action occurred in the same year and month.
Exam focus
  • Remember the 1928 HSRA reorganisation.
  • Distinguish Assembly bombing, Saunders action and Lahore case.
  • Use 23 March 1931 for the execution chronology.
Concept 4

Chandrashekhar Azad and Underground Networks

Chandrashekhar Azad helped sustain revolutionary networks after Kakori and became a senior organiser of HRA and HSRA activities.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Chandrashekhar Tiwari adopted the name Azad after his arrest during the Non-Cooperation movement in 1921.
  • His courtroom answers—Azad, freedom and prison—became part of his popular political image.
  • After the withdrawal of Non-Cooperation, he joined revolutionary networks and worked with Ram Prasad Bismil and other HRA members.
  • Azad helped reorganise the underground movement after the Kakori arrests and supported the HSRA network.
  • He was associated with Bhagat Singh and other revolutionaries in planning and organisational work.
  • Azad died at Alfred Park, Allahabad, on 27 February 1931 after fighting the police.
  • His death became a powerful symbol of refusing capture and accepting revolutionary sacrifice.

Key points

  • Original name—Chandrashekhar Tiwari
  • Public name—Chandrashekhar Azad
  • Connected with HRA and HSRA
  • Died at Alfred Park, Allahabad
  • Date of death—27 February 1931
Worked examples
  • Underground reorganisation after the Kakori arrests
  • Association with Bhagat Singh and the HSRA network

Additional examples

  • Underground reorganisation after the Kakori arrests
  • Association with Bhagat Singh and the HSRA network
Important distinctions
  • Azad was primarily an organiser and underground strategist, while Bhagat Singh became especially prominent for public political communication and writing.
  • Azad died in a police encounter; Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev were executed after trial.
Common misconceptions
  • Azad was a leader of the Indian National Army.
  • Alfred Park is unrelated to Chandrashekhar Azad’s last stand.
Exam focus
  • Remember the original name and adopted name.
  • Link Alfred Park with Allahabad and 27 February 1931.
  • Distinguish Azad’s HRA/HSRA role from Bose’s INA role.
Concept 5

Subhas Chandra Bose and the Indian National Army

Subhas Chandra Bose led an international military effort through the Indian National Army during the Second World War.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Subhas Chandra Bose resigned from the Congress presidency in 1939 after differences over strategy and leadership.
  • He later escaped from India and reached territories where Axis powers could support an armed anti-British campaign.
  • The first INA was formed in 1942 under Mohan Singh from Indian prisoners of war in Southeast Asia.
  • Bose took leadership of the reorganised INA in 1943 and gave it the political direction of Azad Hind.
  • He proclaimed the Provisional Government of Free India, or Azad Hind, on 21 October 1943.
  • The INA participated in the campaigns towards Imphal and Kohima in 1944, but the military advance failed.
  • The INA trials after the war at the Red Fort generated public sympathy and influenced opinion within the armed forces.

Key points

  • First INA phase—Mohan Singh, 1942
  • Bose assumed leadership—1943
  • Azad Hind government—21 October 1943
  • Imphal–Kohima campaign—1944
  • INA trials—Red Fort after the Second World War
Worked examples
  • Azad Hind government and its diplomatic claims
  • Public reaction to the INA trials of Shah Nawaz Khan, P. K. Sehgal and G. S. Dhillon

Additional examples

  • Azad Hind government and its diplomatic claims
  • Public reaction to the INA trials of Shah Nawaz Khan, P. K. Sehgal and G. S. Dhillon
Important distinctions
  • INA was an organised military force operating in a world-war context; HSRA was an underground revolutionary network inside colonial India.
  • Bose sought external military support and a regular army, while Bhagat Singh’s group relied on underground cells and political propaganda.
Common misconceptions
  • Bose founded the first INA in 1942 without an earlier Mohan Singh phase.
  • The INA captured Delhi and permanently defeated British forces.
Exam focus
  • Separate Mohan Singh’s first INA from Bose’s reorganisation.
  • Remember 21 October 1943 for Azad Hind.
  • Connect INA trials with post-war political impact.
Concept 6

Significance and Limitations

Revolutionary activities did not independently overthrow colonial rule but made sacrifice, youth mobilisation and anti-imperial ideas central to nationalist politics.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Revolutionaries challenged the claim that colonial rule was permanent or accepted by Indian youth.
  • Their trials, hunger strikes, writings and executions created wide public discussion about political freedom.
  • The movement encouraged courage, discipline, secular cooperation and a willingness to face state repression.
  • Bhagat Singh’s socialism widened the meaning of freedom beyond a transfer of political power.
  • The INA trials and military discontent after the war contributed to the weakening of colonial confidence.
  • Underground action suffered from limited mass organisation, secrecy, arrests, surveillance and inadequate resources.
  • The revolutionary stream worked alongside, and sometimes in tension with, mass civil-disobedience and constitutional politics.

Key points

  • Revolutionary action influenced political consciousness.
  • Public trials and sacrifice expanded nationalist mobilisation.
  • The movement had organisational and geographical limitations.
  • Revolutionary nationalism was one stream among several in the freedom struggle.
  • INA impact continued through the post-war public and military response.
Worked examples
  • Public response to Bhagat Singh’s execution
  • Impact of the INA trials on post-war nationalist opinion

Additional examples

  • Public response to Bhagat Singh’s execution
  • Impact of the INA trials on post-war nationalist opinion
Important distinctions
  • Revolutionary methods sought rapid disruption, while mass movements depended on broad participation and public mobilisation.
  • The INA’s wartime military campaign differed from the domestic underground actions of HRA and HSRA.
Common misconceptions
  • Revolutionaries had no political ideas beyond violence.
  • The INA alone forced the British to leave India.
Exam focus
  • Write balanced answers with contribution and limitation.
  • Compare revolutionary, mass and military streams.
  • Use public opinion and INA trials in significance questions.

  • Revolutionaries targeted government treasury money being transported by train rather than private passenger property.
  • Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Rajendra Lahiri and Chandrashekhar Azad were among the associated revolutionaries.

Must remember

  • Date—9 August 1925
  • Place—near Kakori, Lucknow district
  • Organisation—Hindustan Republican Association
  • Important names—Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Rajendra Lahiri, Azad
  • Kakori case led to executions and long imprisonments.

Concept-specific exam traps

  • Kakori happened in 1928 with the Lahore Conspiracy Case.
  • The purpose was ordinary robbery rather than financing the revolutionary organisation.
Concept 3

Bhagat Singh and HSRA

Definition and scope

  • The Hindustan Republican Association was reorganised as the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association in 1928.
  • The reorganisation reflected a stronger commitment to socialism and a workers-and-peasants-oriented vision of freedom.
  • Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt threw low-intensity bombs in the Central Legislative Assembly at Delhi on 8 April 1929.

Must remember

  • HSRA—1928
  • Assembly action—8 April 1929
  • Saunders action—17 December 1928
  • Lahore Conspiracy Case
  • Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev—executed 23 March 1931

Concept-specific exam traps

  • Bhagat Singh intended to cause mass casualties in the Assembly.
  • The Assembly action and Saunders action occurred in the same year and month.
Concept 4

Chandrashekhar Azad and Underground Networks

Definition and scope

  • Chandrashekhar Tiwari adopted the name Azad after his arrest during the Non-Cooperation movement in 1921.
  • His courtroom answers—Azad, freedom and prison—became part of his popular political image.
  • After the withdrawal of Non-Cooperation, he joined revolutionary networks and worked with Ram Prasad Bismil and other HRA members.

Must remember

  • Original name—Chandrashekhar Tiwari
  • Public name—Chandrashekhar Azad
  • Connected with HRA and HSRA
  • Died at Alfred Park, Allahabad
  • Date of death—27 February 1931

Concept-specific exam traps

  • Azad was a leader of the Indian National Army.
  • Alfred Park is unrelated to Chandrashekhar Azad’s last stand.
Concept 5

Subhas Chandra Bose and the Indian National Army

Definition and scope

  • Subhas Chandra Bose resigned from the Congress presidency in 1939 after differences over strategy and leadership.
  • He later escaped from India and reached territories where Axis powers could support an armed anti-British campaign.
  • The first INA was formed in 1942 under Mohan Singh from Indian prisoners of war in Southeast Asia.

Must remember

  • First INA phase—Mohan Singh, 1942
  • Bose assumed leadership—1943
  • Azad Hind government—21 October 1943
  • Imphal–Kohima campaign—1944
  • INA trials—Red Fort after the Second World War

Concept-specific exam traps

  • Bose founded the first INA in 1942 without an earlier Mohan Singh phase.
  • The INA captured Delhi and permanently defeated British forces.
Concept 6

Significance and Limitations

Definition and scope

  • Revolutionaries challenged the claim that colonial rule was permanent or accepted by Indian youth.
  • Their trials, hunger strikes, writings and executions created wide public discussion about political freedom.
  • The movement encouraged courage, discipline, secular cooperation and a willingness to face state repression.

Must remember

  • Revolutionary action influenced political consciousness.
  • Public trials and sacrifice expanded nationalist mobilisation.
  • The movement had organisational and geographical limitations.
  • Revolutionary nationalism was one stream among several in the freedom struggle.
  • INA impact continued through the post-war public and military response.

Concept-specific exam traps

  • Revolutionaries had no political ideas beyond violence.
  • The INA alone forced the British to leave India.

Quick facts

  • Anushilan Samiti and Jugantar—Bengal
  • Abhinav Bharat—Maharashtra
  • Ghadar movement—overseas revolutionary mobilisation
  • HRA—formed in 1924
  • Later organisations connected independence with republican and socialist ideas.

  • Place—near Kakori, Lucknow district
  • Organisation—Hindustan Republican Association
  • Important names—Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Rajendra Lahiri, Azad
  • Kakori case led to executions and long imprisonments.

Glossary

Important distinctions

Origins and Revolutionary OrganisationsvsKakori Action of 1925
  • Early secret societies focused on organisation and armed preparation, while later groups articulated wider republican or socialist goals.
  • The Ghadar movement worked through an overseas network, whereas HRA and HSRA operated mainly through Indian underground cells.
Kakori Action of 1925vsBhagat Singh and HSRA
  • Kakori was primarily a revolutionary fund-raising action; the Assembly bombing was designed as a political act of protest and publicity.
  • The Kakori group belonged to HRA, while the later Bhagat Singh network operated through HSRA.
