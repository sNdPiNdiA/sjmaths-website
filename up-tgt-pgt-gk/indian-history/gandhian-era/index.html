Gandhian Mass Movements: Satyagraha, Civil Disobedience and Quit India

Gandhian nationalism combined non-violent mass action, satyagraha, constructive work, social reform, local grievances and national political demands.

UP TGT PGT GK6 concepts42 quiz questions10-question test

Topic overview

  • The sequence moved from experiments in South Africa and local Indian struggles to all-India campaigns against colonial laws and authority.
  • Champaran, Ahmedabad and Kheda connected everyday economic grievances with a wider method of disciplined public mobilisation.
  • The Rowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh, Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience, the Salt March and Quit India were major turning points.
  • UP examinations test chronology, causes, methods, leaders, withdrawal decisions, constitutional negotiations, participation and limitations.

Concept 1

Satyagraha: Meaning, Roots and Local Struggles

Gandhi transformed non-violent resistance into a disciplined political method and tested it through local Indian movements.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Satyagraha means insistence on truth through non-violent action, self-discipline, willingness to suffer and persuasion rather than hatred or physical force.
  • Gandhi experiments in South Africa shaped ideas of civil resistance, community organisation, law-breaking under moral discipline and negotiation.
  • Champaran in 1917 addressed indigo cultivation and the tinkathia system; investigation, testimony and organised pressure helped cultivators.
  • Ahmedabad in 1918 was an industrial labour dispute in which Gandhi used a fast and negotiation to support workers seeking a fair settlement.
  • Kheda in 1918 linked crop failure with land revenue and encouraged peasants to withhold payment when relief or remission was justified.
  • These local struggles made nationalism relevant to peasants, workers, tenants and ordinary households rather than only educated petitioners.
  • The method combined demands, volunteers, publicity, constructive service, negotiation and readiness to suspend action if violence spread.
  • Local satyagrahas were not identical: their grievances, social groups, legal issues and outcomes differed by region.

Key points

  • Truth and non-violence
  • Champaran-1917
  • Ahmedabad-1918
  • Kheda-1918
  • Local grievance to national method.

Concept 2

Rowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh and Non-Cooperation

Post-war repression and the Punjab tragedy created conditions for a broad non-cooperation campaign.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Rowlatt Act of 1919 extended extraordinary powers of detention and trial and was opposed as a denial of civil liberties.
  • Gandhi called for a hartal and peaceful protest, but disturbances and violence occurred in several places, revealing the difficulty of controlling a large mobilisation.
  • On 13 April 1919 General Dyer ordered firing on an unarmed gathering at Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar.
  • The massacre and later official response damaged the legitimacy of British rule and strengthened the demand for national self-government.
  • The Khilafat issue brought Ali brothers and many Muslim leaders into a temporary alliance with Congress under Gandhi leadership.
  • The Non-Cooperation Movement from 1920 encouraged surrender of titles, boycott of schools, colleges, courts, councils and foreign cloth.
  • Khadi, spinning, volunteers, national education, public meetings and picketing made the campaign visible in towns and villages.
  • The movement expanded participation but faced repression, uneven organisation, social differences and the challenge of maintaining non-violence.

Key points

  • Rowlatt Act-1919
  • Jallianwala-13 April 1919
  • Khilafat alliance
  • Non-Cooperation-1920
  • Boycott and khadi.

Concept 3

Chauri Chaura, Withdrawal, Constructive Work and Swarajists

The suspension of Non-Cooperation showed the importance Gandhi gave to non-violent discipline and led to debates over the next political strategy.

Step-by-step explanation

  • On 5 February 1922 protesters at Chauri Chaura attacked a police station and killed policemen after confrontation and firing.
  • Gandhi suspended the Non-Cooperation Movement because a mass campaign based on non-violence could not continue when violence became widespread or uncontrolled.
  • The withdrawal disappointed many activists but reflected the distinction between political anger and disciplined satyagraha.
  • Constructive work included khadi, spinning, national education, village service, Hindu-Muslim unity, removal of untouchability and local organisation.
  • C. R. Das and Motilal Nehru formed the Swaraj Party in 1923 to enter legislatures and obstruct colonial governance from within.
  • No-changers preferred constructive work and organisational preparation outside legislatures, while Swarajists emphasised council entry; both remained connected to the national movement.
  • The debate showed that mass struggle, legislative obstruction and social reconstruction could be alternative or complementary strategies.
  • The period also exposed limits of unity, communal tension, repression, uneven participation and the need for stronger organisation.

Key points

  • Chauri Chaura-1922
  • Withdrawal for non-violence
  • Constructive programme
  • Swaraj Party-1923
  • Council entry debate.

Concept 4

Civil Disobedience, Dandi March and Constitutional Negotiations

Civil Disobedience directly challenged colonial laws, beginning with salt and expanding through coordinated public action.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Lahore Congress of 1929 adopted the goal of Purna Swaraj and authorised preparation for a new phase of struggle.
  • Gandhi selected salt because it affected rich and poor, represented colonial taxation and could unite coastal, rural and urban participants.
  • The Dandi March began from Sabarmati on 12 March 1930 and Gandhi broke the salt law at Dandi on 6 April 1930.
  • Civil Disobedience included salt-law violation, boycott, picketing, refusal of selected taxes, forest actions and local challenges to authority.
  • Women, students, peasants, workers, merchants and local leaders participated, although intensity and forms differed across regions.
  • The movement faced arrests, lathi charges, confiscation, censorship and repression, while volunteers were expected to maintain non-violence.
  • The Gandhi-Irwin Pact of 1931 suspended the movement and enabled Gandhi to attend the Second Round Table Conference.
  • The conference failed to resolve the constitutional conflict, and Civil Disobedience was resumed before later suspension and repression.

Key points

  • Purna Swaraj-1929
  • Dandi-12 March to 6 April 1930
  • Salt law
  • Gandhi-Irwin Pact-1931
  • Round Table Conference.

Concept 5

Poona Pact, Social Reform and Individual Satyagraha

The 1932–41 phase combined constitutional representation, anti-untouchability work, provincial politics and limited opposition to the Second World War.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Communal Award of 1932 proposed separate electorates for Depressed Classes, while Gandhi opposed that arrangement through a fast in prison.
  • The Poona Pact between Gandhi and Ambedkar representatives replaced separate electorates with reserved seats in joint electorates and expanded representation.
  • The Pact did not end caste discrimination; debates over political safeguards and social equality continued.
  • Gandhi linked national work with anti-untouchability campaigns, temple entry, sanitation, village service, khadi and social reform.
  • The Government of India Act 1935 introduced provincial autonomy, and Congress ministries formed in several provinces after the 1937 elections.
  • Congress ministries resigned in 1939 when the British declared India at war without consulting Indian political leadership.
  • Individual Satyagraha in 1940–41 allowed selected satyagrahis such as Vinoba Bhave and Jawaharlal Nehru to assert the right to oppose the war without an immediate mass uprising.
  • The phase shows that the freedom struggle included social justice, representation, provincial governance and strategic restraint alongside mass protest.

Key points

  • Communal Award-1932
  • Poona Pact-1932
  • Anti-untouchability work
  • Provincial ministries-1937
  • Individual Satyagraha-1940.

Concept 6

Quit India Movement and Gandhian Legacy

Quit India demanded immediate British withdrawal during the Second World War and became the final major mass upsurge led by Congress before independence.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The failure of the Cripps Mission in 1942 and wartime conditions increased pressure for immediate transfer of power.
  • The All India Congress Committee adopted the Quit India resolution at Bombay on 8 August 1942 with the call for Do or Die.
  • Gandhi, Congress leaders and thousands of activists were arrested soon after the movement began, creating a leadership vacuum.
  • Students, workers, women, peasants and local organisers continued protests, strikes, sabotage of communication and underground activity in several regions.
  • Parallel or alternative administrations appeared in places such as Ballia, Tamluk and Satara, though the movement was uneven and heavily suppressed.
  • The movement was not centrally controlled after the arrests and included both non-violent protest and militant underground actions that went beyond Gandhi stated method.
  • Repression, wartime censorship, arrests and violence limited immediate success, but the movement demonstrated that colonial rule lacked durable consent.
  • Gandhian politics left a legacy of mass participation, non-violent mobilisation, constructive work, ethical politics, negotiation and continuing debates over class, caste, communalism and state power.

Key points

  • Cripps failure-1942
  • Bombay resolution-8 August 1942
  • Do or Die
  • Arrests and underground networks
  • Parallel governments and final pressure.

Important distinctions

Satyagraha: Meaning, Roots and Local StrugglesvsRowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh and Non-Cooperation
  • Champaran was mainly an agrarian indigo issue; Ahmedabad was an urban industrial wage dispute.
  • Satyagraha sought moral and political pressure through non-violence, not passive inaction.
Rowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh and Non-CooperationvsChauri Chaura, Withdrawal, Constructive Work and Swarajists
  • Rowlatt protest opposed exceptional law; Non-Cooperation withdrew cooperation from colonial institutions.
  • Khilafat created a political alliance but the two issues were not identical.

Exam traps and cautions

  • Satyagraha means never disobeying a law.
  • Champaran, Ahmedabad and Kheda were one identical movement with one grievance.
  • Jallianwala Bagh occurred during the Quit India Movement.
  • Non-Cooperation meant armed rebellion or complete refusal of every public service.
  • Chauri Chaura ended the freedom struggle.
  • The Swaraj Party demanded loyalty to colonial rule.
  • The Dandi March ended British rule immediately.
  • Civil Disobedience was limited only to coastal salt making.
