Indian History

Road to Independence 1945-1947: Cabinet Mission, Mountbatten Plan and Partition

The final phase of British rule was shaped by post-war economic pressure, mass political demands and the weakening of imperial authority.

UP TGT PGT GK6 concepts42 quiz questions10-question test

Topic overview

  • The Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946 tried to preserve a united India through a limited Union and provincial groupings.
  • The Interim Government and Constituent Assembly began the transfer of political responsibility while Congress-League differences continued.
  • The Mountbatten Plan of 3 June 1947 accepted partition as the immediate route to transfer of power.
  • The Indian Independence Act created the two Dominions of India and Pakistan from 15 August 1947.

Concept 1

Post-war Pressure and the Last Wavell Initiative

The end of the Second World War weakened Britain and made a new constitutional settlement in India unavoidable.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Britain emerged from the war with severe economic and military pressure and could not indefinitely maintain colonial rule in India.
  • The Labour government elected in Britain in 1945 was more willing than the previous government to negotiate an Indian constitutional settlement.
  • Lord Wavell proposed a reconstituted Executive Council through the Wavell Plan of June 1945.
  • The Simla Conference was held in June-July 1945 to discuss the composition of the proposed Executive Council.
  • The central disagreement concerned Muslim representation and the Muslim League claim to nominate every Muslim member.
  • The failure of the conference showed that a power-sharing formula could not be agreed without resolving the Congress-League dispute.
  • The Labour Government under Clement Attlee, elected in July 1945, was more willing to negotiate a transfer of power.
  • The INA trials and the 1946 naval uprising further weakened the moral and political authority of British rule.

Key points

  • Wavell Plan—1945
  • Simla Conference—June-July 1945
  • Main dispute—Muslim representation
  • INA trials increased public mobilisation
  • Post-war Britain sought an exit strategy.
Worked examples
  • Failure of the Simla Conference
  • Public response to the INA trials

Additional examples

Important distinctions
  • The Wavell Plan tried to reconstitute the existing executive, whereas the Cabinet Mission later proposed a constitution-making process.
  • The INA trials created public pressure, while the Simla Conference was a formal political negotiation.
Common misconceptions
  • The Wavell Plan immediately granted India self-government.
  • The Simla Conference failed only because Congress rejected all negotiations.

Concept 2

Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946

The Cabinet Mission tried to preserve Indian unity through a weak Union and strong provincial groupings.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Cabinet Mission reached India in March 1946 to negotiate the transfer of power and the machinery for constitution-making.
  • Its members were Lord Pethick-Lawrence, Sir Stafford Cripps and A. V. Alexander.
  • The Plan of 16 May 1946 proposed a Union dealing mainly with defence, foreign affairs and communications.
  • All other subjects were to remain primarily with the provinces, while provincial groupings could frame group constitutions.
  • The proposed Constituent Assembly was to be formed through indirect election by provincial legislatures, with representation from princely states.
  • The Plan rejected the immediate creation of a sovereign Pakistan but tried to answer Muslim League concerns through grouping and provincial autonomy.
  • Elections to the Constituent Assembly followed the Plan in July-August 1946, with Congress strong in general seats and the League strong in Muslim-reserved seats.
  • Both parties initially accepted the Plan, but Congress-League disagreement over grouping led the League to withdraw acceptance on 29 July 1946.

Key points

  • Mission members—Pethick-Lawrence, Cripps and Alexander
  • Plan date—16 May 1946
  • Union subjects—defence, foreign affairs and communications
  • Three provincial sections were proposed
  • The Plan did not accept a separate Pakistan.
Worked examples
  • Grouping of provinces under the Plan
  • Proposed Constituent Assembly

Additional examples

  • Grouping of provinces under the Plan
  • Proposed Constituent Assembly
Important distinctions
  • The Cabinet Mission Plan proposed a united India; the Mountbatten Plan accepted partition.
  • The Union under the Plan had limited subjects, unlike a strongly centralised federation.
Common misconceptions
  • The Cabinet Mission Plan created Pakistan in 1946.
  • The Plan gave all powers to the Union government.

Concept 3

Interim Government and Constituent Assembly

Indian leaders began administering central government and constitution-making before complete independence.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Interim Government was formed on 2 September 1946 with Jawaharlal Nehru as its leading figure and Vice-President of the Executive Council.
  • The Muslim League initially stayed outside the Interim Government and later joined on 26 October 1946.
  • Liaquat Ali Khan held the Finance portfolio after the League joined, and disagreements continued inside the executive.
  • The Constituent Assembly met for the first time on 9 December 1946 in New Delhi.
  • Sachchidananda Sinha served as the temporary Chairman of the first sitting.
  • Dr Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent President of the Assembly on 11 December 1946.
  • Jawaharlal Nehru introduced the Objectives Resolution on 13 December 1946.
  • The Constituent Assembly adopted the Objectives Resolution on 22 January 1947, giving a broad statement of the future republic's principles.

Key points

  • Interim Government—2 September 1946
  • Constituent Assembly first met—9 December 1946
  • Temporary Chairman—Sachchidananda Sinha
  • Permanent President—Rajendra Prasad
  • Objectives Resolution—13 December 1946.
Worked examples
  • Nehru leading the Interim Government
  • Rajendra Prasad presiding over the Constituent Assembly

Additional examples

  • Nehru leading the Interim Government
  • Rajendra Prasad presiding over the Constituent Assembly
Important distinctions
  • The Interim Government handled administration, while the Constituent Assembly prepared the Constitution.
  • The Assembly began under the Cabinet Mission framework but later functioned as the sovereign constitution-making body for India.
Common misconceptions
  • The Constitution was framed immediately on 9 December 1946.
  • Rajendra Prasad was the temporary Chairman of the first sitting.

Concept 4

Direct Action, Violence and the Transfer Deadline

Congress-League deadlock and communal violence made a united constitutional settlement increasingly difficult.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Muslim League observed Direct Action Day on 16 August 1946 to press its demand for Pakistan.
  • Large-scale violence began in Calcutta and was followed by disturbances in Noakhali, Bihar, the United Provinces and Punjab.
  • The political dispute involved the future of provinces, minority safeguards, central authority and the demand for Pakistan.
  • The League and Congress interpreted the Cabinet Mission Plan differently, especially its provincial grouping provisions.
  • The League withdrew its acceptance of the Cabinet Mission Plan on 29 July 1946 and announced Direct Action.
  • The British government found that continued constitutional negotiations were not producing a stable interim settlement.
  • Prime Minister Clement Attlee announced on 20 February 1947 that power would be transferred not later than June 1948.
  • Lord Mountbatten replaced Lord Wavell as Viceroy and arrived in India in March 1947 to complete the transfer process.

Key points

  • Direct Action Day—16 August 1946
  • Attlee statement—20 February 1947
  • Mountbatten became Viceroy in March 1947
  • Communal violence affected the political negotiations
  • The transfer deadline was June 1948.
Worked examples
  • Calcutta violence after Direct Action Day
  • Mountbatten replacing Wavell

Additional examples

  • Calcutta violence after Direct Action Day
  • Mountbatten replacing Wavell
Important distinctions
  • Direct Action was a political mobilisation call; the Mountbatten Plan was a constitutional decision by the British government and Indian leaders.
  • The June 1948 deadline was later replaced by the earlier transfer date of August 1947.
Common misconceptions
  • Direct Action Day took place in 1947.
  • The British originally announced 15 August 1947 as the deadline.
Exam focus
  • Remember the 16 August 1946 date.
  • Connect the Attlee statement with June 1948.
  • Explain how violence affected negotiations.
Concept 5

Mountbatten Plan of 3 June 1947

The Mountbatten Plan accepted partition and established the immediate procedure for transferring power.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The 3 June Plan was announced by Lord Mountbatten after consultations with Congress, the Muslim League and Sikh representatives.
  • It accepted the division of British India into India and Pakistan rather than continuing the failed united constitutional arrangement.
  • The Punjab and Bengal legislatures were to vote on partition and, if divided, separate boundary procedures would follow.
  • A referendum was held in the North-West Frontier Province, while Sylhet held a referendum on joining East Bengal.
  • The plan advanced the transfer of power from the earlier June 1948 deadline to August 1947.
  • The princely states were no longer to be bound by British paramountcy after the transfer, creating a separate integration challenge.
  • Punjab and Bengal legislatures voted on division, while the North-West Frontier Province and Sylhet held referendums.
  • The plan was followed by the Boundary Commissions under Sir Cyril Radcliffe.

Key points

  • Plan date—3 June 1947
  • Partition of Punjab and Bengal
  • Referendums—NWFP and Sylhet
  • Transfer advanced to August 1947
  • Radcliffe Boundary Commission followed.
Worked examples
  • Punjab and Bengal legislative votes
  • Sylhet referendum

Additional examples

  • Punjab and Bengal legislative votes
  • Sylhet referendum
Important distinctions
  • The Mountbatten Plan was the political announcement; the Indian Independence Act supplied the legal framework.
  • The Radcliffe Commission drew boundaries; it did not itself create the two Dominions.
Common misconceptions
  • The Mountbatten Plan was announced on 15 August 1947.
  • The plan and the Indian Independence Act were the same document.
Exam focus
  • Remember 3 June 1947.
  • Match the referendums with NWFP and Sylhet.
  • Distinguish plan, Act and boundary award.
Concept 6

Indian Independence Act and Consequences

The Indian Independence Act legally created the two Dominions, while independence and partition produced immediate administrative and human consequences.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Indian Independence Act was passed by the British Parliament on 18 July 1947.
  • It provided for two independent Dominions, India and Pakistan, from the appointed day of 15 August 1947.
  • The Act ended the authority of the British Parliament to legislate for the new Dominions as part of the old imperial constitutional system.
  • The Constituent Assemblies of the two Dominions exercised legislative and constitution-making functions during the transition.
  • The Indian Independence Bill was introduced on 4 July 1947 and received royal assent as the Act on 18 July.
  • The Radcliffe boundary awards for Punjab and Bengal were published on 17 August 1947, after the formal independence ceremonies.
  • Partition caused mass migration, communal violence, loss of life and a large refugee rehabilitation challenge.
  • Independent India still had to integrate princely states and build a constitutional order after the transfer of power.

Key points

  • Act passed—18 July 1947
  • Independence—15 August 1947
  • Two Dominions—India and Pakistan
  • Boundary awards—17 August 1947
  • Partition produced migration, violence and rehabilitation challenges.
Worked examples
  • Legal difference between the Act and the 3 June Plan
  • Refugee rehabilitation after Partition

Additional examples

  • Legal difference between the Act and the 3 June Plan
  • Refugee rehabilitation after Partition
Important distinctions
  • The Act was legislation; Independence Day was the implementation of the transfer of power.
  • The formal creation of the Dominions occurred on 15 August, while the boundary details were announced on 17 August.
Common misconceptions
  • The Indian Independence Act was passed on 15 August.
  • The Radcliffe boundaries were announced before independence ceremonies.
Exam focus
  • Remember 18 July, 15 August and 17 August 1947 as a sequence.
  • State the two Dominions created by the Act.
  • Include both political transfer and human consequences in long answers.

Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946vsInterim Government and Constituent Assembly
  • The Cabinet Mission Plan proposed a united India; the Mountbatten Plan accepted partition.
  • The Union under the Plan had limited subjects, unlike a strongly centralised federation.

Exam traps and cautions

  • The Wavell Plan immediately granted India self-government.
  • The Simla Conference failed only because Congress rejected all negotiations.
  • The Cabinet Mission Plan created Pakistan in 1946.
  • The Plan gave all powers to the Union government.
  • The Constitution was framed immediately on 9 December 1946.
  • Rajendra Prasad was the temporary Chairman of the first sitting.
  • Direct Action Day took place in 1947.
  • The British originally announced 15 August 1947 as the deadline.

Why did the failure of the Simla Conference of 1945 matter?

The conference failed over the question of Muslim representation, showing that the Congress-League deadlock remained unresolved.

Post-war weakness made continued imperial rule difficult and increased pressure for a negotiated transfer.

Question 3Match the following

Which option correctly matches the event with its feature?

The Wavell Plan concerned the Executive Council, while the Simla Conference exposed the representation dispute.

Question 4Case-based

Case: A British Viceroy proposes an Executive Council with Indian members, but one party insists that it alone should nominate all Muslim members. What does this case describe?

The proposed Executive Council and the dispute over Muslim representation identify the Wavell Plan and Simla Conference.

Question 5MCQ

Which subjects were proposed for the limited Union under the Cabinet Mission Plan?

The proposed Union was limited mainly to defence, foreign affairs and communications, with provinces retaining wide autonomy.

Question 6Assertion–Reason

Assertion (A): The Cabinet Mission Plan tried to preserve Indian unity. Reason (R): It proposed a weak Union with provinces grouped into sections and substantial provincial autonomy.

A limited Union and provincial grouping were the Plan's main attempt to reconcile unity with autonomy.

Question 7Match the following

Which option correctly matches the Cabinet Mission feature?

The Plan proposed a limited Union and provincial groupings; the Assembly was not directly elected by adult franchise.

Question 8Case-based

Case: A constitutional proposal keeps India formally united but gives provinces grouped sections and a Union responsible for only three major subjects. Which proposal is being discussed?

A weak Union with provincial groupings is the distinctive design of the Cabinet Mission Plan.

Question 9MCQ

Who was the temporary Chairman at the first meeting of the Constituent Assembly?

Sachchidananda Sinha served as the temporary Chairman on 9 December 1946.

Question 10Assertion–Reason

Nehru introduced the Resolution in December 1946 and the Assembly adopted it in January 1947.
