Indian Polity and Constitution

Panchayati Raj and Municipalities: Local Self-Government

The 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments gave rural and urban local self-government a constitutional framework.

UP TGT PGT GK6 concepts42 quiz questions10-question test

Topic overview

  • Part IX covers Panchayats and the Eleventh Schedule lists 29 broad rural subjects; Part IXA covers Municipalities and the Twelfth Schedule lists 18 urban subjects.
  • Gram Sabha, the three-tier Panchayat structure, reservations, State Election Commission and State Finance Commission are core rural topics.
  • Nagar Panchayat, Municipal Council, Municipal Corporation, ward committees and district or metropolitan planning are core urban topics.
  • UP examinations also test the Balwant Rai Mehta, Ashok Mehta, G.V.K. Rao and L.M. Singhvi committee recommendations and constitutional exceptions.

Concept 1

Evolution and Constitutionalisation of Local Government

Committee recommendations prepared the ground for the 73rd and 74th Amendments.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) examined community development and recommended democratic decentralisation through a three-tier Panchayati Raj system.
  • Its classic structure was Gram Panchayat at village level, Panchayat Samiti at block or intermediate level and Zila Parishad at district level.
  • The Ashok Mehta Committee (1977-78) proposed a two-tier model of Mandal Panchayat and Zila Parishad and stronger political and planning participation.
  • The G.V.K. Rao Committee (1985) emphasised Panchayats as central institutions for rural development and strengthened district administration.
  • The L.M. Singhvi Committee (1986) recommended constitutional status for Panchayats, Gram Sabha as the foundation and local judicial tribunals.
  • The 73rd Amendment inserted Part IX and the Eleventh Schedule for Panchayats and came into effect on 24 April 1993.
  • The 74th Amendment inserted Part IXA and the Twelfth Schedule for Municipalities and came into effect on 1 June 1993.
  • The amendments created a constitutional framework, but actual devolution of powers, staff and finances depends substantially on State laws.

Key points

  • Balwant Rai Mehta - 1957, three-tier model
  • Ashok Mehta - two-tier proposal
  • G.V.K. Rao - district development focus
  • L.M. Singhvi - constitutional status
  • 73rd rural and 74th urban amendments.

Concept 2

Gram Sabha and Three-Tier Panchayat Structure

Gram Sabha provides the participatory foundation and Panchayats operate at village, intermediate and district levels.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Article 243(b) defines Gram Sabha as the body of persons registered in the electoral rolls relating to a village within the Panchayat area.
  • Gram Sabha is not the same as Gram Panchayat: Gram Sabha includes all registered voters, while Gram Panchayat is the elected village-level institution.
  • Article 243B provides Panchayats at village, intermediate and district levels, subject to constitutional exceptions.
  • A State with population not exceeding twenty lakhs may choose not to constitute Panchayats at the intermediate level.
  • The village Panchayat handles local civic and development functions assigned by State law and works with Gram Sabha participation.
  • The intermediate Panchayat coordinates blocks or groups of villages, while the Zila Parishad operates at district level.
  • The exact names, powers, functions and administrative arrangements may vary under State law within the constitutional framework.
  • Panchayat planning and implementation should connect local needs with district and State development programmes.

Key points

  • Gram Sabha - all registered village voters
  • Gram Panchayat - elected village body
  • Panchayat Samiti - intermediate level
  • Zila Parishad - district level
  • Intermediate tier may be omitted below 20 lakh population.

Concept 3

Reservations, Tenure and Panchayat Elections

The Constitution fixes representation, tenure and an independent State-level election machinery for Panchayats.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Article 243D reserves Panchayat seats for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in proportion to their population in the Panchayat area.
  • At least one-third of the total seats filled by direct election and at least one-third of chairperson offices are reserved for women.
  • Reservation for women includes women belonging to SC and ST reserved categories and operates through rotation as provided by law.
  • States may provide reservation for backward classes through legislation, subject to constitutional and judicial requirements.
  • Article 243E gives a Panchayat a five-year term from the date appointed for its first meeting unless dissolved earlier under law.
  • An election must ordinarily be completed before the term expires or within six months of dissolution, subject to the constitutional exception.
  • Article 243K vests superintendence, direction and control of Panchayat elections in the State Election Commission.
  • Disqualifications for membership are governed by State law subject to the constitutional framework and Article 243F.

Key points

  • Article 243D - SC, ST and women reservation
  • At least one-third women reservation
  • Five-year tenure
  • Election before expiry or within six months of dissolution
  • State Election Commission under Article 243K.

Concept 4

Panchayat Functions, Finance and Eleventh Schedule

Panchayat powers become meaningful through State devolution, local taxation, grants and planning.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Article 243G allows State Legislatures to endow Panchayats with powers and authority necessary to function as institutions of self-government.
  • The Eleventh Schedule contains 29 subjects that may be devolved to Panchayats, including agriculture, rural housing, drinking water, roads, health and poverty alleviation.
  • The Schedule is a guide to possible devolution; it does not by itself transfer every subject without State legislation.
  • Article 243H permits State law to authorise Panchayat taxes, duties, tolls, fees and grants-in-aid from the Consolidated Fund of the State.
  • Article 243I requires a State Finance Commission every five years to review local finances and recommend distribution of taxes, duties, tolls and grants.
  • Article 243J permits State law to provide for maintenance and audit of Panchayat accounts.
  • Panchayats depend on own-source revenue, assigned revenue, State grants, Central transfers and scheme-linked funds.
  • Local planning requires Gram Sabha participation and coordination with intermediate and district institutions.

Key points

  • Article 243G - powers and responsibilities
  • Eleventh Schedule - 29 subjects
  • Article 243H - local taxes and grants
  • Article 243I - State Finance Commission
  • Article 243J - accounts and audit.
Worked examples

  • State Finance Commission recommends local distribution; State Election Commission conducts local elections.

Concept 5

Municipalities and Twelfth Schedule

The 74th Amendment created a constitutional framework for transitional, smaller and larger urban areas.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Part IXA, inserted by the 74th Amendment, covers Municipalities through Articles 243P-243ZG.
  • Article 243Q provides Nagar Panchayat for a transitional area, Municipal Council for a smaller urban area and Municipal Corporation for a larger urban area.
  • Municipalities are composed mainly of directly elected ward representatives; the detailed composition is fixed by State law.
  • Article 243S requires ward committees in Municipalities with a population of three lakhs or more, subject to State law.
  • Article 243T provides reservations for SCs, STs and at least one-third women, including chairperson offices, with rotation.
  • Municipalities normally have a five-year term and elections are supervised by the State Election Commission under Article 243ZA.
  • The Twelfth Schedule contains 18 subjects including urban planning, land use, roads, water supply, sanitation, fire services and slum improvement.
  • Article 243W permits State Legislatures to devolve powers and responsibilities to Municipalities; the Schedule does not independently transfer all functions.

Key points

  • Part IXA - Municipalities
  • Article 243Q - three urban body types
  • Article 243S - ward committees above three lakh population
  • Article 243T - reservations
  • Twelfth Schedule - 18 subjects.

Concept 6

Committees, Exceptions and Local Planning

Local-government design includes planning bodies and constitutional exceptions for protected areas.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The District Planning Committee under Article 243ZD consolidates plans prepared by Panchayats and Municipalities and prepares a draft district development plan.
  • The Metropolitan Planning Committee under Article 243ZE prepares a draft development plan for a metropolitan area.
  • Both planning committees must consider common interests, spatial planning, water and other shared resources and available financial resources.
  • Article 243M excludes or modifies Part IX for Scheduled Areas, tribal areas, Nagaland, Meghalaya, Mizoram and certain hill areas and local arrangements.
  • Article 243ZC provides exceptions or modifications for specified areas under Part IXA.
  • The PESA framework extends self-government principles to Scheduled Areas with special protection for Gram Sabha and community resources.
  • Constitutional status does not eliminate State variation in local-body names, powers, staff, finances and election rules.
  • Local self-government is strengthened when planning, functions, funds and accountability reach the village or urban ward level.

Key points

  • Article 243ZD - District Planning Committee
  • Article 243ZE - Metropolitan Planning Committee
  • Articles 243M and 243ZC - exceptions
  • PESA for Scheduled Areas
  • Devolution depends on State law.

Important distinctions

Evolution and Constitutionalisation of Local GovernmentvsGram Sabha and Three-Tier Panchayat Structure
  • 73rd Amendment concerns rural Panchayats and Eleventh Schedule; 74th concerns urban Municipalities and Twelfth Schedule.
  • Balwant Rai Mehta recommended three tiers; Ashok Mehta recommended a two-tier structure.
Gram Sabha and Three-Tier Panchayat StructurevsReservations, Tenure and Panchayat Elections
  • Gram Sabha is a general body of voters; Gram Panchayat is an elected institution.
  • Panchayat Samiti coordinates at intermediate level; Zila Parishad coordinates at district level.

Memory hooks

  • Remember Gram Sabha and Three-Tier Panchayat Structure: Gram Sabha - all registered village voters
  • Remember Reservations, Tenure and Panchayat Elections: Article 243D - SC, ST and women reservation

Exam traps and cautions

  • The 73rd Amendment created Municipalities.
  • The constitutional amendments automatically transferred every listed subject to local bodies without State legislation.
  • Gram Sabha is composed only of elected Panchayat members.
  • The intermediate tier is compulsory in every State regardless of population.
  • Women reservation is limited only to general seats.
  • The Election Commission of India conducts every Panchayat election.
  • The Eleventh Schedule itself gives Panchayats independent control over all 29 subjects.
  • The State Finance Commission conducts Panchayat elections.

Who constitutes the Gram Sabha?

Article 243(b) defines Gram Sabha through electoral-roll registration.
