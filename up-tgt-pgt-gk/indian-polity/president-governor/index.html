Indian Polity and Constitution

President, Vice-President and Governor: Election and Powers

The President is the constitutional head of the Union, the Vice-President is ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha and the Governor is the constitutional head of a State.

UP TGT PGT GK6 concepts42 quiz questions10-question test

Topic overview

  • Their offices, elections or appointments, qualifications, tenure, removal and powers are distributed across Articles 52-78 and 153-167.
  • Executive power is exercised formally by the President or Governor but normally on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers.
  • The President and Governor have legislative, financial, judicial and emergency-related powers with different constitutional limits.
  • UP examinations frequently test election methods, vetoes, ordinances, pardoning powers, tenure and the discretionary role of the Governor.

Concept 1

President: Election, Qualification, Tenure and Impeachment

The President is an indirectly elected constitutional head whose removal follows a special impeachment process.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Article 52 provides for the office of the President of India, and Article 53 vests Union executive power in the President.
  • The President is elected by an Electoral College consisting of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States.
  • For this election, the expression State includes the National Capital Territory of Delhi and the Union Territory of Puducherry as constitutionally provided.
  • The election uses proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote and secret ballot.
  • A candidate must be a citizen of India, at least 35 years old, qualified for election to the Lok Sabha and not hold an office of profit under the Government, subject to constitutional exceptions.
  • The President holds office for five years and may be re-elected; the President continues until the successor enters office.
  • Impeachment for violation of the Constitution may be initiated by either House, requires notice and special majorities, and the other House investigates the charge.
  • The President's oath is administered by the Chief Justice of India or, in their absence, the senior-most available Supreme Court judge.

Key points

  • Electoral College - elected MPs and elected State MLAs
  • Voting - proportional representation, STV and secret ballot
  • Minimum age - 35
  • Term - five years and re-election possible
  • Removal - impeachment for violation of the Constitution.

Concept 2

President: Executive, Legislative and Financial Powers

The President formally exercises Union powers while the parliamentary system places day-to-day responsibility with the Council of Ministers.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The President appoints the Prime Minister and appoints other ministers on the Prime Minister's advice; the Council is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
  • The President appoints constitutional authorities and officials as provided by the Constitution, including Governors, the Attorney-General and members of the Union Public Service Commission.
  • The President summons and prorogues Parliament and may dissolve the Lok Sabha on the advice of the Council of Ministers.
  • The President may address Parliament, send messages and cause reports and statements to be laid before it as constitutionally required.
  • A Bill passed by Parliament generally requires Presidential assent; the President may return an ordinary Bill once for reconsideration but cannot return a Money Bill.
  • A Constitution Amendment Bill duly passed under Article 368 requires Presidential assent and cannot be returned for reconsideration.
  • The Union Budget is caused to be laid before Parliament in the President's name, and Money Bills require prior recommendation.
  • The President may promulgate an Ordinance under Article 123 when both Houses of Parliament are not in session and immediate action is necessary; it must be placed before Parliament and is temporary.

Key points

  • Prime Minister appointed by President
  • Parliament summoned, prorogued and Lok Sabha dissolved under constitutional practice
  • Ordinary Bill may be returned once
  • Money Bill needs prior recommendation and cannot be returned
  • Article 123 Ordinance is temporary.

Concept 3

President: Pardoning Power, Emergencies and Vetoes

The President has special constitutional powers in criminal justice, emergencies and legislation, each with a defined field and procedure.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Article 72 permits the President to grant pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions of punishment and to suspend, remit or commute sentences in specified cases.
  • The President's Article 72 power extends to court-martial cases, offences relating to matters within Union executive power and all cases involving a death sentence.
  • A pardon removes both the sentence and certain legal effects of conviction; remission reduces the sentence without changing its character, while commutation substitutes a lighter form of punishment.
  • The President may return an ordinary Bill once, creating a suspensive veto; withholding assent in limited situations is described as an absolute veto, and inaction may operate as a pocket veto because no constitutional time limit is prescribed.
  • The President cannot exercise a veto to reject a Constitution Amendment Bill duly passed under Article 368.
  • Under Article 352, a National Emergency may be proclaimed for war, external aggression or armed rebellion on written Cabinet advice.
  • Article 356 concerns failure of constitutional machinery in a State, and Article 360 concerns Financial Emergency.
  • Emergency powers affect the federal system and Fundamental Rights only within the limits and safeguards expressly provided by the Constitution.

Key points

  • Article 72 - President pardon
  • Court martial and death sentence included
  • Ordinary Bill - suspensive return once
  • Article 352 - war, external aggression, armed rebellion
  • Articles 356 and 360 - State breakdown and Financial Emergency.

Concept 4

Vice-President: Election, Role, Tenure and Removal

The Vice-President links the Union executive with the Rajya Sabha and acts as President when the office is vacant or the President is unable to function.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Article 63 provides for the office of the Vice-President of India.
  • The Vice-President is elected by members of both Houses of Parliament, including nominated members, through proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote and secret ballot.
  • State Legislative Assembly members do not participate in the Vice-Presidential election.
  • A candidate must be a citizen, at least 35 years old, qualified for election as a member of the Rajya Sabha and not hold an office of profit under the Government, subject to constitutional exceptions.
  • The Vice-President is ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha and performs the functions of the President during a vacancy or when the President is unable to discharge duties.
  • The Vice-President has a five-year term and may continue until a successor enters office; re-election is constitutionally permitted.
  • The Vice-President may resign to the President and is removed by a Rajya Sabha resolution passed by a majority of all the then members and agreed to by the Lok Sabha after notice.
  • The Vice-President is not removed by impeachment in the same manner as the President.

Key points

  • Election - both Houses of Parliament
  • Nominated MPs vote; State MLAs do not
  • Ex officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha
  • Qualification - 35 and Rajya Sabha eligibility
  • Removal - Rajya Sabha resolution agreed by Lok Sabha.

Concept 5

Governor: Appointment, Qualification, Tenure and Role

The Governor is the constitutional head of a State, appointed by the President and normally guided by the State Council of Ministers.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Article 153 provides for a Governor for each State; the same person may be appointed Governor of two or more States.
  • The Governor is appointed by the President under Article 155 and holds office during the pleasure of the President under Article 156.
  • The normal term is five years, but the Governor may resign to the President or be removed earlier; the Governor continues until a successor enters office.
  • A candidate must be a citizen of India and at least 35 years old; the Governor cannot be a member of Parliament or a State Legislature while holding office.
  • The Governor takes an oath to preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and to devote themselves to the service and well-being of the people.
  • The Governor appoints the Chief Minister and appoints other ministers on the Chief Minister's advice; the Council is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly.
  • The Governor normally acts on aid and advice but has limited discretion in situations such as a hung Assembly, reservation of Bills and reporting constitutional breakdown.
  • The Governor is not an agent with unlimited personal power; discretionary action remains subject to the Constitution and judicial review.

Key points

  • Appointment - President
  • Pleasure - President; normal term five years
  • Minimum age - 35
  • Chief Minister appointed by Governor
  • Limited discretion, not unlimited personal rule.

Concept 6

Governor: Legislative, Financial, Judicial and Emergency Powers

The Governor exercises State-level constitutional powers in legislation, finance, clemency and constitutional breakdown.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Governor summons, prorogues and may dissolve the State Legislative Assembly, subject to constitutional practice and ministerial advice.
  • The Governor may address the Legislature, assent to Bills, withhold assent, return an ordinary Bill for reconsideration or reserve a Bill for the President under Article 200.
  • A Money Bill in the State Legislature requires the Governor's recommendation, and the annual financial statement is laid before the Legislature in the Governor's name.
  • Article 213 permits the Governor to promulgate Ordinances when the Legislature is not in session and immediate action is necessary; the Ordinance is temporary and subject to constitutional limits.
  • Article 161 gives the Governor power to grant pardons, reprieves, respites, remissions or commutations for offences within the State executive field.
  • The Governor cannot pardon a death sentence in the same broad constitutional field as the President, though the Governor may suspend, remit or commute sentences under the applicable law.
  • The Governor may reserve certain Bills for the President, especially where constitutional provisions require or permit reservation.
  • Under Article 356, the Governor may send a report that the State government cannot be carried on according to the Constitution, but the President decides the proclamation.

Key points

  • Article 200 - assent, return or reservation of Bills
  • Article 201 - Bills reserved for President
  • Article 213 - State Ordinance
  • Article 161 - Governor pardon
  • Article 356 - Governor report, President decision.
Worked examples
  • Comparing Articles 123 and 213

  • Article 200 concerns State Bills and permits reservation; Article 111 concerns Presidential assent to Parliamentary Bills.
  • The Governor reports constitutional breakdown under Article 356, but the President issues the proclamation after constitutional consideration.
Common misconceptions
  • A Governor's Article 356 report automatically imposes President's Rule.
  • A State Ordinance is permanent law without legislative approval.
  • The Governor can reserve every Bill for the President without constitutional reason.

  • A Money Bill in the State Legislature requires the Governor's recommendation, and the annual financial statement is laid before the Legislature in the Governor's name.

Must remember

  • Article 200 - assent, return or reservation of Bills
  • Article 201 - Bills reserved for President
  • Article 213 - State Ordinance
  • Article 161 - Governor pardon
  • Article 356 - Governor report, President decision.

Concept-specific exam traps

  • A Governor's Article 356 report automatically imposes President's Rule.
  • A State Ordinance is permanent law without legislative approval.

Quick facts

  • Electoral College - elected MPs and elected State MLAs
  • Voting - proportional representation, STV and secret ballot
  • Minimum age - 35
  • Term - five years and re-election possible
  • Removal - impeachment for violation of the Constitution.
  • Prime Minister appointed by President
  • Parliament summoned, prorogued and Lok Sabha dissolved under constitutional practice
  • Ordinary Bill may be returned once
  • Money Bill needs prior recommendation and cannot be returned
  • Article 123 Ordinance is temporary.

Glossary

President: Election, Qualification, Tenure and ImpeachmentThe President is an indirectly elected constitutional head whose removal follows a special impeachment process.
President: Executive, Legislative and Financial PowersThe President formally exercises Union powers while the parliamentary system places day-to-day responsibility with the Council of Ministers.
President: Pardoning Power, Emergencies and VetoesThe President has special constitutional powers in criminal justice, emergencies and legislation, each with a defined field and procedure.
Vice-President: Election, Role, Tenure and RemovalThe Vice-President links the Union executive with the Rajya Sabha and acts as President when the office is vacant or the President is unable to function.
Governor: Appointment, Qualification, Tenure and RoleThe Governor is the constitutional head of a State, appointed by the President and normally guided by the State Council of Ministers.
Governor: Legislative, Financial, Judicial and Emergency PowersThe Governor exercises State-level constitutional powers in legislation, finance, clemency and constitutional breakdown.

Important distinctions

President: Election, Qualification, Tenure and ImpeachmentvsPresident: Executive, Legislative and Financial Powers
  • The President is elected by elected MPs and elected MLAs; the Vice-President is elected by members of both Houses of Parliament only.
  • Impeachment is a constitutional removal process for the President; a Governor is not impeached and is removed by the President.
President: Executive, Legislative and Financial PowersvsPresident: Pardoning Power, Emergencies and Vetoes
  • The President may return an ordinary Bill once but not a Money Bill; a Constitution Amendment Bill cannot be returned under Article 368.
  • An Ordinance has the force of an Act temporarily but must be laid before Parliament and ceases as constitutionally prescribed.

Memory hooks

  • Remember President: Election, Qualification, Tenure and Impeachment: Electoral College - elected MPs and elected State MLAs
  • Remember President: Executive, Legislative and Financial Powers: Prime Minister appointed by President
  • Remember President: Pardoning Power, Emergencies and Vetoes: Article 72 - President pardon

Exam traps and cautions

  • The President is directly elected by all citizens.
  • Nominated MPs vote in the Presidential election.
  • The President can be elected only once.
  • The President can permanently veto every Bill without constitutional limits.
  • An Ordinance remains permanently valid without Parliamentary approval.
  • The President independently chooses the Council of Ministers in a majority government.
  • A Governor can pardon a death sentence in the same way as the President.
  • National Emergency can be proclaimed for any law-and-order problem.
