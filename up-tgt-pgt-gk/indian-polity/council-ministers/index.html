Indian Polity and Constitution

Prime Minister and Council of Ministers: Real Executive

The Prime Minister is the head of the Union Council of Ministers and the central link between the President, the Council and Parliament.

UP TGT PGT GK6 concepts42 quiz questions10-question test

Topic overview

  • Articles 74 and 75 establish the aid-and-advice relationship, appointment of ministers, collective responsibility and parliamentary responsibility.
  • The Council of Ministers is the wider constitutional body; the Cabinet is its smaller core that takes the principal policy decisions.
  • The Prime Minister allocates portfolios, coordinates departments, leads the Cabinet and communicates major decisions to the President.
  • UP examinations frequently test appointment, tenure, collective responsibility, Cabinet committees, caretaker conventions and the difference between Council and Cabinet.

Concept 1

Prime Minister: Appointment and Constitutional Role

The Prime Minister leads the Union executive and coordinates the parliamentary government.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Article 75 provides that the President appoints the Prime Minister; in a majority House, the leader of the majority party or coalition is normally appointed.
  • When no party has a clear majority, the President may appoint the person most likely to command Lok Sabha confidence, subject to a floor test.
  • The Prime Minister advises the President on the appointment of other ministers and on allocation or reshuffling of portfolios.
  • The Prime Minister chairs Cabinet meetings, coordinates ministries and provides political direction to the Union Government.
  • The Prime Minister is the principal channel between the Council of Ministers and the President under Article 78.
  • The Prime Minister advises on summoning, prorogation and dissolution of Lok Sabha within the constitutional system.
  • The Prime Minister leads the government in Parliament, explains policy and maintains the confidence of Lok Sabha.
  • The Prime Minister may resign personally; because the ministry is collectively responsible, the resignation normally affects the whole Council.

Key points

  • President appoints Prime Minister
  • Majority support is tested in Lok Sabha
  • Advises on ministerial appointments and portfolios
  • Chairs Cabinet and coordinates government
  • Article 78 - communication with President.
Worked examples
  • Appointment after a clear majority
  • Floor test after a hung House

Additional examples

  • Appointment after a clear majority
  • Floor test after a hung House
Important distinctions
  • President is the constitutional head; Prime Minister is the head of the Council and the real political executive.
  • A majority Prime Minister has stronger practical discretion than one heading a fragile coalition, but both remain responsible to Lok Sabha.
Common misconceptions
  • The President can choose any Prime Minister permanently without majority support.
  • The Prime Minister is directly elected by all citizens as a separate national office.

Concept 2

Council Composition, Appointment and Membership

The Council includes ministers of different ranks who share responsibility for Union administration.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Council of Ministers consists of the Prime Minister and other ministers appointed by the President on the Prime Minister's advice.
  • The 91st Amendment limits the total number of Union ministers, including the Prime Minister, to 15 percent of Lok Sabha strength.
  • The Council may include Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State with independent charge and Ministers of State; these are political ranks rather than separate constitutional bodies.
  • A minister may come from either House of Parliament, but must satisfy the relevant membership and qualification rules.
  • A person who is not a member of either House may remain a minister for a maximum of six consecutive months; otherwise the office ends.
  • The President administers the oath of office and secrecy to ministers on the forms in the Third Schedule.
  • Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President, but in practice the Prime Minister determines whether a minister continues in a majority government.
  • Ministers answer for their departments individually and participate in the collective responsibility of the whole Council.

Key points

  • President appoints on Prime Minister's advice
  • 91st Amendment - 15 percent ceiling
  • Cabinet, MoS independent charge and MoS ranks
  • Six-month rule for non-member minister
  • Oath of office and secrecy.

Concept 3

Collective and Individual Ministerial Responsibility

Parliamentary government combines shared Cabinet solidarity with responsibility for individual departments.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Article 75(3) makes the Council of Ministers collectively responsible to Lok Sabha.
  • Collective responsibility means the ministry stands or falls together on major policy and confidence questions.
  • A minister who publicly rejects a Cabinet decision is normally expected to resign or accept collective Cabinet discipline.
  • Individual responsibility means a minister answers for the administration and policy of the assigned department in Parliament.
  • The President appoints ministers and ministers formally hold office during presidential pleasure, but the parliamentary relationship operates through the Prime Minister.
  • A no-confidence motion is directed at the ministry as a whole; a censure or departmental criticism may focus on a particular minister or policy.
  • Cabinet confidentiality and oath of secrecy support collective decision-making, while parliamentary questions support individual scrutiny.
  • Coalition governments may use coordination agreements, but constitutional collective responsibility to Lok Sabha remains.

Key points

  • Article 75(3) - collective responsibility
  • Ministry stands or falls together
  • Individual minister answers for department
  • No-confidence concerns whole ministry
  • Secrecy supports Cabinet solidarity.

Concept 4

Cabinet, Council of Ministers and Cabinet Committees

The Cabinet is the smaller core through which the wider Council takes coordinated executive decisions.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Council of Ministers is the constitutional and larger body; the Cabinet is the inner group of senior ministers that directs policy.
  • The 44th Amendment gave constitutional recognition to the Cabinet for the written advice required for a National Emergency under Article 352.
  • Cabinet meetings settle major legislative, economic, security, administrative and foreign-policy questions.
  • The Prime Minister chairs the Cabinet and determines the agenda, coordination and allocation of work.
  • Cabinet committees are smaller groups of ministers created for focused coordination and decision preparation.
  • Some committees are standing and others may be ad hoc; their composition and names can change through executive business rules.
  • The Cabinet Secretariat supports coordination, agenda management, inter-ministerial communication and follow-up.
  • Cabinet committees do not replace Parliament; their decisions remain subject to constitutional authority, law and parliamentary accountability.

Key points

  • Cabinet is smaller core of Council
  • 44th Amendment recognises Cabinet advice for Article 352
  • Prime Minister chairs Cabinet
  • Committees coordinate specialised subjects
  • Cabinet Secretariat supports business.

Concept 5

Prime Minister, President and Parliament

The Prime Minister connects formal presidential authority with responsible government in Parliament.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The President is the constitutional head, while the Prime Minister and Council exercise real executive leadership within the aid-and-advice framework.
  • Article 74 requires a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the President.
  • The President may ask the Council to reconsider advice once, but the reconsidered advice is binding under the constitutional arrangement.
  • Article 78 requires the Prime Minister to communicate Council decisions and legislative proposals to the President.
  • The Prime Minister advises the President on ministerial appointments, portfolio allocation and important parliamentary actions.
  • The Prime Minister leads the government in Lok Sabha, responds to debates and coordinates legislative business with the whips and ministers.
  • The President addresses Parliament and acts formally on Bills, while the ministry supplies the political and legislative programme.
  • The relationship is not personal rule: it is structured by the Constitution, parliamentary majority, conventions and judicial interpretation.

Key points

  • Article 74 - aid and advice
  • President may request reconsideration once
  • Article 78 - information to President
  • Prime Minister leads Parliament and government
  • Formal head and real executive distinction.

Concept 6

Tenure, Resignation and Caretaker Government

Ministerial tenure is linked to presidential appointment, Prime Ministerial leadership and the confidence of Lok Sabha.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Ministers formally hold office during the pleasure of the President, but the President normally acts on the Prime Minister's advice in a parliamentary system.
  • The Council continues while it enjoys the confidence of Lok Sabha and until a successor government is appointed after an election or resignation.
  • The Prime Minister may ask an individual minister to resign or advise the President to remove the minister from office.
  • If the Prime Minister resigns, the Council of Ministers normally goes out with the Prime Minister because the Council is headed by that office.
  • A ministry that loses a no-confidence motion normally resigns or seeks another constitutionally valid course; it cannot govern as if it retained majority support.
  • A caretaker government performs necessary administration during transition but conventionally avoids major irreversible policy decisions unless unavoidable.
  • The six-month rule for a non-member minister concerns membership, not a general extension of ministerial tenure without parliamentary support.
  • The President may invite another leader to form a government when the incumbent loses majority and an alternative can demonstrate support.

Key points

  • Tenure during President's pleasure in constitutional form
  • Prime Minister controls ministerial continuation in practice
  • Prime Minister resignation normally ends Council
  • No-confidence loss affects whole ministry
  • Caretaker limits major new policy.
