Indian Polity and Constitution

Constitutional and Statutory Bodies: ECI, UPSC, CAG and Finance Commission

Constitutional bodies derive their authority directly from the Constitution, while statutory bodies are created by legislation and executive bodies by government resolution or executive action.

UP TGT PGT GK6 concepts42 quiz questions10-question test

Topic overview

  • The Election Commission of India supervises elections to Parliament, State Legislatures and the offices of President and Vice-President under Article 324.
  • UPSC and State Public Service Commissions conduct or advise on public-service recruitment under Articles 315-323; UPPSC is the State commission for Uttar Pradesh.
  • The Comptroller and Auditor General audits Union and State accounts and reports to the relevant legislature through the President or Governor.
  • The Finance Commission recommends distribution of financial resources between Union and States, while NITI Aayog is an executive policy and cooperative-federalism body, not a constitutional commission.

Concept 1

Election Commission and Electoral Principles

The Election Commission of India administers the constitutional election framework for major Union and State offices.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Article 324 vests superintendence, direction and control of electoral rolls and elections in the Election Commission of India.
  • Its constitutional election field covers Parliament, State Legislatures and the offices of President and Vice-President.
  • The Commission consists of the Chief Election Commissioner and such number of other Election Commissioners as the President may fix, with Regional Commissioners when required.
  • The President appoints the CEC and Election Commissioners under the constitutional and statutory framework in force.
  • Article 325 provides one general electoral roll for each territorial constituency and prohibits exclusion on religion, race, caste or sex.
  • Article 326 provides adult suffrage for Lok Sabha and State Assembly elections, subject to disqualifications prescribed by law.
  • Article 329 limits court interference in electoral matters during the election process and directs challenges through election petitions as provided by law.
  • The Commission conducts delimitation-related and electoral-roll work under constitutional, statutory and judicially interpreted powers.

Key points

  • Article 324 - ECI control
  • Covers Parliament, State Legislatures, President and Vice-President elections
  • Article 325 - one general roll
  • Article 326 - adult suffrage
  • Article 329 - election disputes through prescribed process.

Concept 2

UPSC, State PSCs and UPPSC

Public Service Commissions provide merit-based recruitment and independent advice on service matters.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Article 315 provides a Union Public Service Commission and a Public Service Commission for each State, with provision for a Joint Commission where Parliament provides by law.
  • UPSC serves the Union; the Uttar Pradesh Public Service Commission performs the State commission role for Uttar Pradesh.
  • The President appoints UPSC members and the Governor appoints State PSC members, including the Chairman, under Article 316.
  • The normal term is six years or until the age of 65 for UPSC members and 62 for State PSC members, whichever is earlier.
  • Article 317 provides removal and suspension rules; removal for proved misbehaviour involves the Supreme Court inquiry route prescribed by the Constitution.
  • Article 320 includes conducting examinations, advising on recruitment methods and consultation on appointments, promotions, transfers and disciplinary matters.
  • The advice of a Public Service Commission is constitutionally important but is not automatically a judicial order or an absolute veto over government action.
  • Annual reports are submitted to the President or Governor and are placed before the relevant legislature with a memorandum explaining non-acceptance of advice.

Key points

  • Article 315 - UPSC and SPSC
  • UPPSC is Uttar Pradesh's State PSC
  • Six-year term
  • Age 65 UPSC and 62 SPSC
  • Article 320 - examinations and advice.

Concept 3

Comptroller and Auditor General and Public Accounts

The CAG audits public money and reports findings to legislatures for financial accountability.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Article 148 establishes the office of the Comptroller and Auditor General of India, appointed by the President.
  • The CAG can be removed in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court judge, protecting institutional independence.
  • The CAG audits accounts of the Union and States and other authorities or bodies as provided by the Constitution and law.
  • CAG reports relating to Union accounts are submitted to the President, who causes them to be laid before Parliament.
  • Reports relating to State accounts are submitted to the Governor, who causes them to be laid before the State Legislature under Article 151.
  • The Public Accounts Committee examines CAG reports and whether expenditure was authorised by Parliament or the State Legislature.
  • The CAG does not itself impose every recovery or punishment; legislative committees and executive departments act on findings under law.
  • Audit supports legislative control over the Consolidated Fund, appropriation and public expenditure.

Key points

  • Article 148 - CAG
  • President appoints; difficult removal
  • Audits Union and State accounts
  • Article 151 - reports laid before legislature
  • PAC examines CAG reports.

Concept 4

Finance Commission and Fiscal Federalism

The Finance Commission recommends how public revenues and grants should support Union-State balance.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Article 280 requires the President to constitute a Finance Commission every five years or earlier if necessary.
  • The Commission consists of a Chairman and other members whose qualifications and selection are governed by law.
  • It recommends distribution between the Union and States of net proceeds of taxes and allocation among States of their shares.
  • It recommends principles governing grants-in-aid from the Consolidated Fund of India to States under constitutional purposes.
  • It suggests measures to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State to supplement resources of Panchayats and Municipalities on the basis of State Finance Commission recommendations.
  • The President lays the recommendations and an explanatory memorandum before each House of Parliament under Article 281.
  • The Finance Commission is a recommending body; implementation occurs through constitutional, statutory and budgetary action by governments.
  • It supports fiscal equalisation and cooperative federalism but is distinct from the GST Council and State Finance Commissions.

Key points

  • Article 280 - Finance Commission
  • Constituted every five years or earlier
  • Tax devolution and grants-in-aid
  • Local-body resource augmentation
  • Article 281 - report before Parliament.
Worked examples

Concept 5

NITI Aayog: Status and Functions

NITI Aayog is an executive policy institution created to support cooperative and competitive federalism.

Step-by-step explanation

  • NITI Aayog was created in 2015 by an executive resolution of the Union Government and replaced the Planning Commission.
  • It is neither a constitutional body created by an Article nor a statutory commission created by an Act of Parliament.
  • The Prime Minister chairs the Governing Council, which includes Chief Ministers of States and representatives of Union Territories as provided by the resolution.
  • Its structure includes a Vice-Chairperson, full-time members, ex officio members, special invitees and a Chief Executive Officer.
  • It serves as a policy think tank, promotes innovation, monitors programmes, supports evidence-based planning and facilitates Centre-State cooperation.
  • NITI Aayog does not have the Finance Commission's constitutional power to recommend tax devolution or grants-in-aid.
  • Its cooperative-federalism platform allows States to participate in development priorities, while competitive-federalism initiatives compare performance indicators.
  • Because it is executive, its composition and working arrangements can be changed by government decision more easily than a constitutional body.

Key points

  • Created in 2015 by executive resolution
  • Replaced Planning Commission
  • Not constitutional or statutory
  • PM chairs Governing Council
  • Policy think tank and cooperative federalism.

Concept 6

Comparison, Independence and Accountability

Exam questions often require classification by source, appointment, tenure, function and reporting route.

Step-by-step explanation

  • ECI, UPSC, CAG and Finance Commission are constitutional bodies because their offices and functions are created by constitutional provisions.
  • UPPSC is a State Public Service Commission under the same constitutional Part XIV framework, not a merely departmental recruitment office.
  • NITI Aayog is an executive institution; its authority comes from government resolution and policy decisions rather than a specific constitutional Article or Parliamentary statute.
  • Removal safeguards vary: CEC and CAG receive strong protection, while PSC members have Article 317 procedures and Finance Commission members follow statutory rules.
  • Reporting routes matter: CAG reports go to the legislature through President or Governor; PSC reports go to President or Governor and then legislature; Finance Commission report goes to Parliament.
  • Election administration, recruitment, audit, fiscal devolution and policy coordination are distinct functions and should not be merged in answers.
  • Parliamentary committees, judicial review, statutory rules and public reporting create accountability without converting each body into a court.
  • A strong comparative answer states legal status, constitutional Article, appointment authority, tenure, principal function and reporting or removal rule.

Key points

  • Source of authority classifies bodies
  • Appointment and removal safeguards differ
  • Reporting chain is exam-relevant
  • Functions must not be confused
  • NITI Aayog is executive, not constitutional.
Worked examples

Additional examples

Important distinctions
  • ECI manages elections; UPSC or UPPSC recruits and advises; CAG audits; Finance Commission recommends fiscal sharing; NITI Aayog coordinates policy.
  • Constitutional status gives direct constitutional foundation; executive status gives flexibility but not the same constitutional guarantees.
Common misconceptions
  • All independent bodies have identical appointment and removal rules.
  • A body becomes constitutional merely because it performs an important national function.
Exam focus

  • Always state whether NITI Aayog is executive.
