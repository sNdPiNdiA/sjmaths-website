Indian Polity and Constitution

Making of the Indian Constitution and the Preamble

India's Constitution was made through the Constituent Assembly, detailed committee work and clause-by-clause debate.

UP TGT PGT GK6 concepts42 quiz questions10-question test

Topic overview

  • The Constituent Assembly was created under the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946 and first met on 9 December 1946.
  • The Drafting Committee, chaired by Dr B. R. Ambedkar, converted the Assembly's decisions into a complete constitutional draft.
  • The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949, signed on 24 January 1950 and came into force on 26 January 1950.
  • The Preamble states the source, nature, objectives and constitutional identity of the Indian Republic.

Concept 1

Demand for a Constitution-Making Body

The Constituent Assembly was the result of a long national demand for a Constitution made by Indians themselves.

Step-by-step explanation

  • M. N. Roy is associated with the first clear proposal for a Constituent Assembly in 1934.
  • The Indian National Congress formally accepted the demand for a Constituent Assembly in 1935.
  • Jawaharlal Nehru stated in 1938 that the Constitution should be framed by a Constituent Assembly elected on the basis of adult franchise.
  • The August Offer of 1940 recognised, in principle, the right of Indians to frame their Constitution after the war.
  • The Cripps proposals of 1942 offered a constitution-making arrangement but were rejected by Indian political parties for several reasons.
  • The Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946 finally provided the practical framework for forming the Constituent Assembly.
  • The Assembly was not elected through direct adult franchise; provincial representatives were chosen indirectly by provincial legislatures.
  • The demand connected national self-government with popular participation in constitution-making.

Key points

  • M. N. Roy - proposal in 1934
  • Congress accepted the demand in 1935
  • Nehru restated the demand in 1938
  • Cabinet Mission Plan - 1946
  • The Assembly was indirectly elected.
Worked examples
  • Comparing the August Offer, Cripps proposals and Cabinet Mission Plan
  • Explaining why a Constituent Assembly was linked with self-government

Additional examples

  • Comparing the August Offer, Cripps proposals and Cabinet Mission Plan
  • Explaining why a Constituent Assembly was linked with self-government
Important distinctions
  • The August Offer accepted the principle, while the Cabinet Mission Plan supplied the operational method.
  • A Constituent Assembly makes a Constitution; a legislature primarily makes ordinary laws, although the Indian Assembly performed both functions during the transition.
Common misconceptions
  • M. N. Roy personally formed the Constituent Assembly.
  • The Constituent Assembly was elected through universal adult franchise in 1946.
Exam focus
  • Remember the 1934, 1935, 1938 and 1946 sequence.
  • Identify the Cabinet Mission Plan as the immediate basis of the Assembly.
  • Distinguish direct election from indirect election.
Concept 2

Constituent Assembly: Composition and Working

The Assembly combined elected provincial representatives with representatives of princely states and worked through plenary debates and committees.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Cabinet Mission Plan originally provided for a Constituent Assembly of 389 members.
  • The original plan included representatives from British Indian provinces, Chief Commissioners' Provinces and princely states.
  • Provincial representatives were elected indirectly by provincial legislative assemblies through proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote.
  • Representatives of princely states were nominated by the rulers rather than elected by a common adult-franchise vote.
  • After Partition, the membership of the Indian Constituent Assembly was reduced to 299 members, including 229 representatives from provinces and 70 from princely states.
  • The Assembly first met on 9 December 1946 in the Constitution Hall, now the Central Hall of Parliament House.
  • Sachchidananda Sinha was the temporary Chairman, and Dr Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent President on 11 December 1946.
  • After 15 August 1947, the Assembly functioned as the sovereign constitution-making body and also as the Dominion legislature.
  • The Assembly debated constitutional provisions in committees before considering them in the full House.

Key points

  • Original strength - 389
  • Post-Partition strength - 299
  • Election - indirect and proportional
  • First meeting - 9 December 1946
  • President - Rajendra Prasad.
Worked examples
  • Tracing a provincial representative from election to Assembly debate
  • Distinguishing elected provincial members from nominated princely-state representatives

Additional examples

  • The Assembly worked as a constitution-making body and, after independence, also as the Dominion legislature.

Concept 3

Leadership, Committees and Constitutional Adviser

Much of the detailed Constitution was prepared through specialised committees supported by constitutional research and drafting.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Dr Rajendra Prasad was the President of the Constituent Assembly and presided over its major proceedings.
  • H. C. Mookherjee served as Vice-President of the Assembly.
  • B. N. Rau served as the Constitutional Adviser and prepared a draft that helped organise the Assembly's decisions.
  • Jawaharlal Nehru chaired the Union Powers Committee and Union Constitution Committee.
  • Vallabhbhai Patel chaired the Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities and Tribal and Excluded Areas and the Provincial Constitution Committee.
  • A Steering Committee helped coordinate the Assembly's business and programme of work.
  • The committee system allowed technical and political questions to be examined before final debate in the Assembly.
  • The Drafting Committee converted the Assembly's decisions and committee reports into legal constitutional language.

Key points

  • Constitutional Adviser - B. N. Rau
  • Assembly President - Rajendra Prasad
  • Union committees - Jawaharlal Nehru
  • Advisory and Provincial committees - Vallabhbhai Patel
  • Drafting Committee - B. R. Ambedkar.
Worked examples
  • Matching a committee with its chairperson
  • Explaining why committee reports were necessary before clause-by-clause debate

Additional examples

  • Matching a committee with its chairperson
  • Explaining why committee reports were necessary before clause-by-clause debate
Important distinctions
  • B. N. Rau advised and prepared a working draft; the Drafting Committee revised and presented the draft to the Assembly.
  • The Union Powers Committee dealt with Union authority, while the Advisory Committee dealt with rights, minorities and excluded areas.
Common misconceptions
  • B. N. Rau was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee.
  • The Drafting Committee alone decided every constitutional provision.
Exam focus

  • Do not confuse the Constitutional Adviser with the Drafting Committee Chairman.
  • Remember that the Assembly retained final authority.
Concept 4

Drafting Committee and the Constitution-Making Process

The drafting process moved from committee decisions to a working draft, public discussion, amendments and final clause-by-clause adoption.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Drafting Committee was appointed on 29 August 1947 with Dr B. R. Ambedkar as its Chairman.
  • Its task was to prepare a constitutional text in accordance with the Assembly's decisions and committee reports.
  • B. N. Rau's draft and the reports of Assembly committees formed important working materials for the Drafting Committee.
  • The Draft Constitution was submitted to the President of the Constituent Assembly on 21 February 1948.
  • Dr Ambedkar presented the Draft Constitution to the Assembly on 4 November 1948 for detailed consideration.
  • The draft was circulated and discussed before the Assembly considered its provisions in detail.
  • Members proposed amendments and debated questions of rights, federalism, executive power, minorities and social policy.
  • The Constitution was not simply copied from one foreign model; the Assembly adapted several comparative sources to Indian conditions.
  • Dr Ambedkar presented and defended the Drafting Committee's work, but the Assembly made the final decisions.

Key points

  • Drafting Committee date - 29 August 1947
  • Chairman - B. R. Ambedkar
  • Draft submitted - 21 February 1948
  • Process - reports, draft, amendments and debate
  • Final authority - Constituent Assembly.
Worked examples
  • Following a provision from committee report to final Constitution
  • Distinguishing drafting from adoption

Additional examples

  • Following a provision from committee report to final Constitution
  • Distinguishing drafting from adoption
Important distinctions
  • Drafting is the preparation of legal text; adoption is the formal decision of the Constituent Assembly.
  • The Drafting Committee coordinated the text, while the full Assembly debated and approved the provisions.
Common misconceptions
  • The Constitution was written by Ambedkar alone.
  • The Draft Constitution of 1948 was already the final Constitution.
Exam focus

  • State the role of Ambedkar without erasing the Assembly's collective work.
  • Use the sequence: committees, draft, amendments, adoption.
Concept 5

Adoption, Enactment, Commencement and Sources

The Constitution has separate dates for adoption, signature and commencement, and it draws institutional ideas from several constitutional traditions.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Constitution was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 26 November 1949.
  • Members signed the Constitution on 24 January 1950.
  • The Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950.
  • 26 January was selected to honour the Purna Swaraj declaration and Independence Day observance of 1930.
  • The original Constitution contained 395 Articles, 22 Parts and 8 Schedules; later amendments changed its structure.
  • The Government of India Act, 1935 supplied important administrative and federal provisions, but the Constitution was not merely a copy of that Act.
  • The United Kingdom influenced parliamentary government, cabinet responsibility, rule of law and legislative procedure.
  • The United States influenced Fundamental Rights, judicial review, an independent judiciary and impeachment provisions.
  • Ireland influenced the Directive Principles of State Policy and the method of nominating members to the Rajya Sabha.
  • Canada influenced a federation with a strong Centre and the distribution of residuary powers.
  • Australia influenced the Concurrent List, freedom of trade and commerce and joint sittings of the two Houses.
  • Germany influenced emergency provisions, South Africa influenced amendment and election methods, and the USSR influenced Fundamental Duties.
  • The Constitution is a single integrated document made for India's social, political and administrative conditions.

Key points

  • Adopted - 26 November 1949
  • Signed - 24 January 1950
  • Commenced - 26 January 1950
  • Original text - 395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules
  • Sources included the Government of India Act 1935 and several foreign constitutions.
Worked examples
  • Arranging 26 November, 24 January and 26 January in order
  • Comparing adoption with commencement

Additional examples

  • Arranging 26 November, 24 January and 26 January in order
  • Comparing adoption with commencement
Important distinctions
  • Adoption is the Assembly's approval; commencement is the date the Constitution became operative.
  • The Government of India Act, 1935 was a major source, but the Indian Constitution combines Indian debates with comparative constitutional ideas.
Common misconceptions
  • The Constitution was adopted and commenced on the same date.
  • 26 January was chosen because the Constitution was signed on that day.
Exam focus

  • Remember why 26 January was selected.

Concept 6

Preamble: Meaning, Words and Constitutional Role

The Preamble summarises the source, nature and objectives of the Constitution and guides its interpretation.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Preamble begins with We, the People of India, showing that popular sovereignty is the source of constitutional authority.
  • It describes India as a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic.
  • It promises Justice - social, economic and political; Liberty of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship; and Equality of status and opportunity.
  • It seeks Fraternity assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation.
  • The words Socialist, Secular and Integrity were inserted by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976.
  • The Preamble is part of the Constitution and can be used as an interpretive guide, but it does not by itself confer independent governmental power.
  • The Preamble reflects the ideals of the Objectives Resolution adopted by the Constituent Assembly.
  • The Preamble can be amended, subject to the basic-structure limitation on constitutional amendment power recognised in Kesavananda Bharati (1973).

Key points

  • Source - We, the People of India
  • Nature - Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic
  • Objectives - Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity
  • 42nd Amendment - 1976
  • Preamble is part of the Constitution; Kesavananda Bharati limits amendments.
Worked examples
  • Mapping Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity to constitutional goals
  • Identifying words added by the 42nd Amendment

Additional examples

  • Mapping Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity to constitutional goals
  • Identifying words added by the 42nd Amendment
Important distinctions
  • The Preamble states constitutional ideals; Fundamental Rights provide enforceable rights and remedies.
  • The Preamble guides interpretation but is not a substitute for the operative Articles of the Constitution.
Common misconceptions
  • Socialist and Secular were present in the original 1949 Preamble.
  • The Preamble is not part of the Constitution because it is placed before the Articles.
Exam focus
  • Revise the four objectives and the five identity terms.
  • Remember the 42nd Amendment additions.
  • Connect the Preamble with the Objectives Resolution and basic structure doctrine.

Important distinctions

Demand for a Constitution-Making BodyvsConstituent Assembly: Composition and Working
  • The August Offer accepted the principle, while the Cabinet Mission Plan supplied the operational method.
  • A Constituent Assembly makes a Constitution; a legislature primarily makes ordinary laws, although the Indian Assembly performed both functions during the transition.
Constituent Assembly: Composition and WorkingvsLeadership, Committees and Constitutional Adviser
  • Provincial members were elected, whereas princely-state members were nominated.
  • The Assembly worked as a constitution-making body and, after independence, also as the Dominion legislature.
