Indian Polity and Constitution

Parliament: Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha and Legislative Procedure

Parliament is the Union legislature and consists of the President, the Rajya Sabha and the Lok Sabha under Article 79.

UP TGT PGT GK6 concepts42 quiz questions10-question test

Topic overview

  • The Lok Sabha represents the people directly, while the Rajya Sabha represents the States and Union Territories and provides continuity.
  • Questions commonly test composition, qualifications, sessions, presiding officers, privileges, Bills, Money Bills, budget procedure and parliamentary control.
  • The two Houses have broadly equal legislative powers but differ in financial powers, confidence responsibility and some special constitutional functions.
  • Parliamentary committees and devices such as Question Hour, motions and debates make the executive accountable to the legislature.

Concept 1

Composition, Representation and Membership

Parliament combines a directly elected popular House with a permanent federal House.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Article 79 defines Parliament as the President and two Houses: the Council of States and the House of the People.
  • Rajya Sabha represents States and Union Territories and includes up to 12 nominated members with special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art or social service.
  • Rajya Sabha is a continuing House; approximately one-third of its members retire every second year and its normal maximum strength is 250.
  • Lok Sabha is directly elected from territorial constituencies and is the House on which the Council of Ministers depends for confidence.
  • The constitutional maximum for Lok Sabha is 550 elected members from States and Union Territories under Article 81; actual seats are fixed by law and delimitation.
  • Article 84 requires citizenship, oath or affirmation and age qualifications: 30 years for Rajya Sabha and 25 years for Lok Sabha.
  • Article 102 and related statutes provide disqualifications such as office of profit, insolvency, unsound mind, loss of citizenship and specified electoral offences.
  • The President is a component of Parliament but is not a member of either House; assent is needed for Bills to become law.

Key points

  • Article 79 - President plus two Houses
  • Rajya Sabha permanent; one-third retire every two years
  • Lok Sabha directly elected and normally five years
  • Minimum age: 30 Rajya Sabha, 25 Lok Sabha
  • President is part of Parliament, not a member of either House.

Concept 2

Sessions, Presiding Officers and Privileges

Parliamentary work depends on constitutionally defined sessions, presiding officers and legislative privileges.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The President summons Parliament and may prorogue either House; the gap between two sessions cannot exceed six months.
  • Adjournment pauses a sitting and is ordered by the presiding officer; prorogation ends a session and is ordered by the President.
  • The President may dissolve Lok Sabha, but Rajya Sabha as a continuing House is not dissolved.
  • The Vice-President is the ex officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha and does not vote in the first instance, but may exercise a casting vote in a tie.
  • Rajya Sabha elects a Deputy Chairman from among its members, while Lok Sabha elects a Speaker and Deputy Speaker.
  • The Speaker presides over Lok Sabha, maintains order, interprets rules and certifies a Money Bill under Article 110.
  • Article 105 protects freedom of speech in Parliament and parliamentary publication, subject to constitutional provisions and House rules.
  • Privileges belong to the Houses, their committees and members where necessary for independent legislative functioning; they are not a general immunity from law.

Key points

  • Article 85 - summons and prorogation
  • Maximum six-month gap between sessions
  • Speaker certifies Money Bills
  • Vice-President is Rajya Sabha Chairman
  • Article 105 - parliamentary privileges.

Concept 3

Legislative Procedure and Joint Sitting

A Bill becomes law through passage by the Houses, constitutional presentation and Presidential action.

Step-by-step explanation

  • An ordinary Bill may originate in either House and may be introduced by a minister or a private member.
  • A Bill must be passed by both Houses in the same form; disagreements can be resolved by a joint sitting in the constitutionally permitted cases.
  • The President summons a joint sitting under Article 108; the Speaker normally presides, followed by the Deputy Speaker or Deputy Chairman according to the constitutional arrangement.
  • Joint sitting is not available for Money Bills because Rajya Sabha has only recommendatory power, nor for Constitution Amendment Bills because Article 368 requires separate passage.
  • After passage, the President may assent or, for an ordinary Bill, return it once for reconsideration; if passed again, assent cannot be withheld on that ground.
  • A Bill pending in Rajya Sabha does not lapse on dissolution of Lok Sabha, while a Bill pending in Lok Sabha generally lapses unless it has already passed Lok Sabha and is pending in Rajya Sabha.
  • The legislative process includes introduction, readings, committee scrutiny, debate, voting and transmission between the Houses.
  • The President may address Parliament and send messages; the Houses then consider the legislative business through their rules and procedures.

Key points

  • Ordinary Bill may begin in either House
  • Both Houses must pass the same text
  • Article 108 joint sitting for specified deadlocks
  • No joint sitting for Money or Constitution Amendment Bills
  • President may return an ordinary Bill once.

Concept 4

Money Bill, Budget and Financial Procedure

Financial control is weighted toward Lok Sabha because the elected House controls taxation and grants.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Article 110 defines a Money Bill by matters such as taxation, government borrowing, Consolidated Fund custody and appropriation of money.
  • A Money Bill can be introduced only in Lok Sabha and only on the President's recommendation.
  • The Lok Sabha Speaker certifies whether a Bill is a Money Bill; the certification is recorded on the Bill and the Speaker's decision has constitutional significance.
  • Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend a Money Bill; it may make recommendations and must return it within 14 days.
  • The Annual Financial Statement, commonly called the Union Budget, is laid before both Houses under Article 112.
  • Demands for grants are voted by Lok Sabha; charged expenditure is discussed but not submitted to vote.
  • The Appropriation Bill authorises withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund, while the Finance Bill gives effect to taxation proposals.
  • Parliamentary financial committees, especially the Public Accounts Committee and Estimates Committee, scrutinise public expenditure and estimates.

Key points

  • Article 110 - Money Bill definition
  • Only Lok Sabha introduces and votes Money Bills
  • Rajya Sabha has 14 days and recommendatory role
  • Article 112 - Annual Financial Statement
  • Appropriation authorises withdrawal; Finance Bill implements taxation.
Worked examples

Concept 5

Parliamentary Control and Executive Accountability

The Council of Ministers remains answerable to Parliament, especially the Lok Sabha, through confidence and scrutiny mechanisms.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Article 75(3) makes the Council of Ministers collectively responsible to Lok Sabha.
  • A no-confidence motion tests whether the ministry commands the confidence of the House; loss of majority normally requires resignation or another constitutional course.
  • A confidence motion is used by a ministry to establish majority support, while a censure motion expresses disapproval of a policy or action.
  • Question Hour allows members to seek information from ministers and may expose administrative failures or policy gaps.
  • Zero Hour is a parliamentary practice for raising urgent matters and is not expressly named in the Constitution.
  • Adjournment motions draw attention to a definite urgent matter involving government responsibility and interrupt normal business when admitted.
  • The President's address and parliamentary debates provide occasions for discussing government policy, but day-to-day accountability is exercised through the Houses.
  • The anti-defection law seeks to discourage unstable floor-crossing but does not remove the need for political responsibility to the elected House.

Key points

  • Article 75(3) - collective responsibility to Lok Sabha
  • No-confidence tests majority support
  • Question Hour seeks ministerial information
  • Zero Hour is a parliamentary practice
  • Censure targets a policy or action.

Concept 6

Committees, Devices and Anti-Defection

Much parliamentary scrutiny happens through committees, procedural devices and party-discipline rules.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Parliamentary committees allow detailed scrutiny of Bills, expenditure, assurances, delegated legislation and departmental work that cannot be completed on the floor alone.
  • The Public Accounts Committee examines the CAG reports and checks whether public money was spent according to parliamentary authorisation.
  • The Estimates Committee examines estimates and suggests economies, efficiency and administrative improvements; it is traditionally a Lok Sabha committee.
  • The Committee on Public Undertakings examines reports and accounts of public enterprises and the CAG reports relating to them.
  • Department-related Standing Committees examine demands for grants, Bills, annual reports and policy documents of assigned ministries.
  • The Tenth Schedule provides disqualification for defection, including voluntarily giving up party membership or voting contrary to a party direction without permitted protection.
  • The Speaker or Chairman decides defection petitions under the Tenth Schedule, subject to judicial review after the Kihoto Hollohan decision.
  • Parliamentary questions, motions, petitions and committee reports create a continuing record of scrutiny and public accountability.

Key points

  • PAC examines CAG reports
  • Estimates Committee examines estimates and economy
  • Public Undertakings Committee examines PSUs
  • Tenth Schedule - anti-defection
  • Kihoto Hollohan recognised judicial review of decisions.
Worked examples
  • Matching a committee to its function
  • Applying the Tenth Schedule to a defection situation

Additional examples

Important distinctions
  • PAC checks expenditure already incurred; Estimates Committee examines proposed expenditure and economies.
  • A committee report recommends or scrutinises; a House vote or constitutional authority produces the formal decision.
Common misconceptions

  • The anti-defection law is contained in Article 105 rather than the Tenth Schedule.

Important distinctions

Composition, Representation and MembershipvsSessions, Presiding Officers and Privileges
  • Rajya Sabha is permanent and indirectly elected; Lok Sabha is subject to dissolution and directly elected.
  • The President is a constitutional component of Parliament, whereas MPs are members of one of the two Houses.
Sessions, Presiding Officers and PrivilegesvsLegislative Procedure and Joint Sitting
  • Adjournment ends a sitting temporarily; prorogation ends a session; dissolution ends Lok Sabha.
  • Chairman presides over Rajya Sabha as its ex officio officer; Speaker presides over Lok Sabha and has Money Bill certification authority.

Memory hooks

  • Remember Composition, Representation and Membership: Article 79 - President plus two Houses
  • Remember Sessions, Presiding Officers and Privileges: Article 85 - summons and prorogation
  • Remember Legislative Procedure and Joint Sitting: Ordinary Bill may begin in either House

Exam traps and cautions

  • Rajya Sabha can be dissolved like Lok Sabha.
  • The President is a member of the Rajya Sabha because the President is part of Parliament.
  • The President directly presides over daily House sittings.
  • Prorogation and dissolution mean the same thing for both Houses.
  • Every disagreement between the Houses automatically leads to a joint sitting.
  • The President can return a Constitution Amendment Bill under Article 111.
  • Rajya Sabha can veto a Money Bill by refusing to pass it.
  • Every Bill involving money is automatically a Money Bill under Article 110.

Question 1MCQ

Which description of Parliament is constitutionally correct?

Article 79 includes the President and the two Houses in Parliament.

Question 2Assertion–Reason

Assertion (A): Rajya Sabha is a continuing House. Reason (R): One-third of its members retire every second year and the House is not dissolved.

The staggered retirement system preserves continuity in Rajya Sabha.

Question 3Match the following

Which option correctly matches the House with its feature?

Rajya Sabha is the continuing House; Lok Sabha is directly elected and normally has a five-year term.

Question 4Case-based

Case: A candidate is 27 years old and otherwise qualified for Parliament. Which House can the candidate seek under the age rule?

The minimum age is 25 for Lok Sabha and 30 for Rajya Sabha.

Question 5MCQ

Who certifies whether a Bill is a Money Bill?

The Lok Sabha Speaker certifies a Money Bill under the constitutional procedure.

Question 6Assertion–Reason

Assertion (A): The gap between two sessions of Parliament cannot exceed six months. Reason (R): The President summons Parliament under Article 85.

Both statements are true, but the power to summon does not itself explain the constitutional maximum gap.

Question 7Match the following

Which option correctly matches the officer with the House or role?

The Vice-President is the ex officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha.

Question 8Case-based

Case: A question asks who may order adjournment of a House sitting and who ends a session. Which answer is correct?

Adjournment is a House procedure, while prorogation is ordered by the President.

Question 9MCQ

For which situation can a joint sitting of Parliament be used?

Article 108 provides a joint sitting for specified deadlocks on ordinary Bills.

Question 10Assertion–Reason

Assertion (A): A Constitution Amendment Bill cannot be passed through a joint sitting. Reason (R): Article 368 requires each House to pass it separately with the prescribed majority.

The separate voting requirement under Article 368 excludes joint sitting.
