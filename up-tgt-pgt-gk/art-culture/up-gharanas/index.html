Music Gharanas of Uttar Pradesh: Lucknow, Banaras, Kirana, and Agra Gharanas for UP TGT and PGT

Uttar Pradesh is the cradle of Hindustani classical music, housing some of the most influential Gharanas (stylistic lineages) in vocal music, instrumental music, and dance.

UP TGT PGT GK5 concepts35 quiz questions10-question test

Topic overview

  • Uttar Pradesh is the cradle of Hindustani classical music, housing some of the most influential Gharanas (stylistic lineages) in vocal music, instrumental music, and dance.
  • A Gharana represents a lineage or systemic tradition of musicians tied to a particular stylistic approach, pedigree, and geographical origin.
  • The major Gharanas deeply rooted in UP include the Lucknow, Banaras, Kirana, and Agra Gharanas, each possessing distinct aesthetics regarding swara application, laya, and repertoire.
  • Understanding these traditions, their founding masters, notable exponents, and stylistic features is a high-scoring component for UP TGT and PGT examinations in Music and General Studies.

Concept 1 · foundation

Lucknow Gharana: Vocal, Thumri, and Kathak Traditions

The Lucknow Gharana flourished under the patronage of the Nawabs of Awadh, specializing in emotional expression, lyrical semi-classical forms, and the Kathak dance idiom.

Prerequisites

  • Basic understanding of Hindustani classical music aesthetics
  • Familiarity with semi-classical genres like Thumri, Dadra, and Ghazal
  • Geographical location of Awadh and Lucknow in Uttar Pradesh

Definitions and scope

  • Gharana: A system of social organization linking musicians or dancers by lineage or apprenticeship.
  • Ang: The stylistic body or branch of a tradition (e.g., Lucknow Ang of Kathak and Thumri).
  • Thumri: A semi-classical romantic vocal genre heavily cultivated in Lucknow.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Lucknow vocal and instrumental tradition was established by brothers Bakhshu Khan and Maltu Khan in the late 18th century.
  • They shifted their base to Lucknow under the generous patronage of Nawab Wajid Ali Shah, a noted patron of arts and a composer himself.
  • The Gharana places supreme emphasis on 'Bhava' (expression), subtlety, and romantic aesthetic appeal rather than heavy, mathematical rhythmic acrobatics.
  • In vocal music, it is renowned for its stylized rendering of Thumri, Dadra, Chaiti, Hori, and Tappa, bringing out the poetic nuances of the text.
  • In dance, the Lucknow Gharana of Kathak was shaped by Ishwari Prasad and later advanced by Kalka Prasad and Bindadin Maharaj.
  • Bindadin Maharaj composed numerous thumris specifically for abhinaya (mime and expression) in Kathak, fusing music and dance seamlessly.
  • Instrumental masters of Lucknow excelled in Sarangi and Sitar, maintaining the lyrical singing quality (Gayaki Ang) in instrumental play.
  • Legendary exponents like Begum Akhtar popularized the Lucknow style of Dadra and Ghazal across the Indian subcontinent.

Key points

  • Founding masters: Bakhshu Khan and Maltu Khan.
  • Patron ruler: Nawab Wajid Ali Shah (Qadar Piya).
  • Core genres: Thumri, Dadra, Ghazal, and Kathak dance.
  • Primary aesthetic: Lyrical grace, emotional depth, and poetic expression.
  • Key Kathak icons: Kalka Prasad, Bindadin Maharaj, Birju Maharaj.
  • Vocal/Semi-classical icon: Begum Akhtar (Mallika-e-Ghazal).
Worked examples
  • Example 1: A vocalist singing a Thumri in Raag Khamaj in the Lucknow style will emphasize delicate meends (glides) and romantic word-painting over fast taans.
  • Example 2: A Kathak dancer executing 'Gat Bhav' to a Thumri composed by Bindadin Maharaj showcases the signature Lucknow repertoire.

Applications and connections

  • Crucial for UP TGT/PGT Music questions regarding patron Nawabs and founders.
  • Essential for understanding the evolution of semi-classical vocal forms in North India.
  • Forms the foundational syllabus for Kathak and vocal pedagogy in UP universities.

Advanced points, exceptions and limitations

  • The Lucknow instrumental tradition maintains strong links with the Senia tradition through court migrations.
  • Unlike rhythmic-heavy Gharanas, Lucknow prioritizes textual interpretation over complex layakari.
  • The blending of Thumri singing with footwork in Kathak is a unique hallmark of this tradition.

Additional examples

  • Nawab Wajid Ali Shah's pen name as a composer was 'Akhtar Piya'.
  • Begum Akhtar trained under classical masters like Ustad Imdad Khan and Ata Mohammad Khan.
  • The Thumri 'Babul Mora Naihar Chhooto Hi Jaaye' is famously attributed to Nawab Wajid Ali Shah.
Important distinctions
  • Lucknow vs Banaras Thumri: Lucknow focuses on leisurely, soft romanticism, whereas Banaras incorporates bolder folk influences and quicker emotional shifts.
  • Lucknow vs Jaipur Vocal: Lucknow is lighter and semi-classical dominant, while Jaipur is purely Khayal-centric and scholarly.
  • Lucknow vs Agra: Lucknow emphasizes lyrical beauty; Agra emphasizes robust, Dhrupad-derived vocal mechanics.
Common misconceptions
  • Misconception: Lucknow Gharana only pertains to dance. Fact: It has a rich, independent vocal and semi-classical Thumri/Dadra heritage.
  • Misconception: Wajid Ali Shah founded the Gharana. Fact: Bakhshu Khan and Maltu Khan founded it; Wajid Ali Shah was its supreme royal patron.
Exam focus
  • Remember founders Bakhshu Khan and Maltu Khan for TGT/PGT.
  • Identify Nawab Wajid Ali Shah's contribution and pen name 'Akhtar Piya'.
  • Associate Begum Akhtar with Lucknow Gharana's vocal/Dadra/Ghazal tradition.
  • Link Kalka-Bindadin Maharaj to Lucknow Kathak.
Concept 2 · core

Banaras Gharana: Temple Roots, Vocal Mastery, and Tabla Purbi Ang

The Banaras Gharana is uniquely rooted in the spiritual ethos of Kashi (Varanasi), excelling in heavy Khayal, rich semi-classical genres, and the distinct Purbi Ang Tabla tradition.

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of Varanasi's cultural and religious milieu
  • Basic knowledge of Hindustani percussion (Tabla) and vocal genres
  • Familiarity with Poorab Ang folk and semi-classical music

Definitions and scope

  • Purbi Ang: The eastern style of Tabla playing originating in Banaras and Lucknow.
  • Khayal-numa: Traditional vocal forms influenced by folk and temple music in Banaras.
  • Thappa/Tappa: A semi-classical genre mastered by Banaras vocalists.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Banaras Gharana grew organically out of the temple traditions, temple courtesans, and public festivals of Varanasi.
  • In vocal music, Pandit Bade Ram Das Ji and his descendants established a powerful vocal lineage known for rich Khayal, Thumri, Chaiti, Hori, and Kajari.
  • The vocal style effortlessly bridges classical rigor with folk elements native to eastern Uttar Pradesh (Purvanchal).
  • In instrumental percussion, Pandit Ram Sahai founded the Banaras Tabla Gharana in the late 18th century after training under Pandit Salku Maharaj and later Lucknow masters.
  • The Banaras Tabla Gharana (Purbi Ang) is characterized by open-hand resonant strokes (like 'Na' and 'Ge'), heavy sound production, and unique compositions like Uthans and Gat-Kayedas.
  • Legendary Tabla maestros like Pandit Kishan Maharaj and Pandit Anokhe Lal Mishra elevated Banaras Tabla to global renown.
  • Vocal legends like Siddheshwari Devi, Rasoolan Bai, and Padma Vibhushan Girija Devi immortalized the Banaras Thumri and Tappa style.
  • The Gharana places high value on 'Bol-Banao' Thumri, where the lyrics are embellished with delicate emotional variations.

Key points

  • Vocal founders/masters: Bade Ram Das Ji, Mahadev Mishra.
  • Tabla founder: Pandit Ram Sahai (creator of Banaras Baaj).
  • Key semi-classical forms: Thumri, Tappa, Chaiti, Hori, Kajari, Dadra.
  • Iconic vocalists: Girija Devi, Siddheshwari Devi, Rasoolan Bai.
  • Iconic Tabla maestros: Pt. Kishan Maharaj, Pt. Anokhe Lal Mishra, Pt. Samta Prasad.
  • Aesthetic trait: Fusion of classical depth with Poorab folk vibrancy.
Worked examples
  • Example 1: A performance of Raag Bhairav by a Banaras vocalist incorporating traditional Poorab bandishes.
  • Example 2: Pandit Kishan Maharaj executing a complex solo in Teental utilizing traditional Banaras Kayedas and Relas with high-speed mathematical precision.

Applications and connections

  • High-frequency topic in UP TGT/PGT music examinations regarding Tabla bols and gharana origin.
  • Essential for understanding the geographical classification of North Indian musical styles (Uttari vs Poorbi).
  • Study of semi-classical seasonal songs (Chaiti, Kajari) anchored in Banaras traditions.

Advanced points, exceptions and limitations

  • The Banaras Tabla style relies heavily on the 'Nat' and 'Kat' syllables, making it physically demanding and auditorily powerful.
  • Banaras Thumri differs from Punjab Thumri by placing greater emphasis on raga purity and slower tempo elaboration.
  • The integration of Dhrupad elements into Banaras vocal styles occurred through cross-training with visiting Senia musicians.

Additional examples

  • Pandit Ram Sahai established the Banaras Tabla Gharana with his famous 6-day open challenge (Peshkar) in Lucknow court.
  • Girija Devi was affectionately known as the 'Queen of Thumri'.
  • Chaiti and Kajari are semi-classical seasonal festival songs quintessential to the Banaras repertoire.
Important distinctions
  • Banaras Tabla vs Delhi Tabla: Banaras uses open, resonant strokes (Purbi Ang); Delhi uses closed, crisp, finger-based strokes (Delhi Baaj).
  • Banaras Vocal vs Kirana Vocal: Banaras embraces semi-classical folk-infused forms readily; Kirana focuses strictly on meditative Khayal expansion.
  • Banaras Thumri vs Lucknow Thumri: Banaras is more robust and grounded in earthy Poorab roots; Lucknow is softer and courtly.
Common misconceptions
  • Misconception: Banaras Gharana is solely famous for Tabla. Fact: It has an equally formidable and ancient vocal lineage.
  • Misconception: Tappa originated in Punjab only. Fact: Banaras masters developed a distinct Poorab Tappa style characterized by intricate oscillations.
Exam focus
  • Remember Pandit Ram Sahai as the founder of Banaras Tabla Gharana.
  • Identify Girija Devi, Siddheshwari Devi, and Rasoolan Bai with Banaras vocal/Thumri.
  • Associate Pt. Kishan Maharaj and Pt. Anokhe Lal with Banaras Tabla Baaj.
  • Note the regional link to Purvanchal folk forms (Chaiti, Kajari).
Concept 3 · core

Kirana Gharana: Swara Purity, Slow Alap, and Meditative Khayal

Founded by Ustad Abdul Karim Khan and Ustad Abdul Wahid Khan, the Kirana Gharana originates from Kairana town in the Shamli district of Uttar Pradesh and is renowned for its sublime focus on swara intonation.

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of Shruti, Swara, and micro-tonal variations in Hindustani music
  • Geographical awareness of Shamli / Muzaffarnagar belt in western UP
  • Concept of slow Vilambit Khayal expansion

Definitions and scope

  • Swara Sadhana: Intensive practice devoted to the purity and emotional resonance of individual musical notes.
  • Meend: Continuous sliding note transition characteristic of Kirana style.
  • Vilambit Khayal: Slow-tempo vocal exposition.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Kirana Gharana derives its name from Kairana (historically Kirana), a small town in the Shamli district of western Uttar Pradesh.
  • The tradition was shaped and popularized by cousins Ustad Abdul Karim Khan and Ustad Abdul Wahid Khan in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.
  • Ustad Abdul Karim Khan introduced a deeply emotional, soulful rendering of ragas influenced by Carnatic music and intuitive swara accuracy.
  • Ustad Abdul Wahid Khan focused on the intellectual and structural depth of ragas, specializing in slow, meditative alap development.
  • The hallmark of Kirana vocalism is the extreme slow-tempo (Vilambit) development of the raga, where each note is treated as an independent musical universe.
  • Kirana vocalists use delicate meends and microtonal pitch bending (Shruti) rather than aggressive or rapid taans.
  • The style places immense stress on exact pitch tuning (Sur), making vocalists sound almost instrumental in their precision.
  • Legendary exponents like Pandit Bhimsen Joshi, Gangubai Hangal, Prabha Atre, and Roshanara Begum carried this tradition to legendary heights.

Key points

  • Origin: Kairana (Shamli district, Uttar Pradesh).
  • Founders: Ustad Abdul Karim Khan and Ustad Abdul Wahid Khan.
  • Core aesthetic: Meditative slow alap, unmatched swara purity, emotional depth.
  • Key exponents: Pt. Bhimsen Joshi, Gangubai Hangal, Prabha Atre, Begum Akhtar (early training).
  • Vocal focus: Khayal and devotional Bhajans/Abhangs.
  • Instrumental adaptation: Mastered on Sarangi and Sitar (e.g., Ustad Bundu Khan).
Worked examples
  • Example 1: Pandit Bhimsen Joshi rendering a Vilambit Khayal in Raag Todi, sustaining the Gandhar and Nishad notes with immaculate pitch stability for minutes before introducing rhythm.
  • Example 2: Abdul Karim Khan's historic 78rpm recording of Raag Darbari Kanada, showcasing ethereal meends and devotional vocal delivery.

Applications and connections

  • Essential for UP TGT/PGT questions linking western UP towns to classical music lineages.
  • Important for musicology questions regarding swara-centric aesthetics vs laya-centric styles.
  • Core study module for advanced vocal pedagogy in Indian universities.

Advanced points, exceptions and limitations

  • Kirana vocalists frequently incorporate Carnatic raga structures (e.g., Abhogi, Shankarabharanam) into Hindustani repertoire due to Abdul Karim Khan's stay in South India.
  • The style demands exceptional breath control to sustain long, uninterrupted meends across octaves.
  • Unlike Agra Gharana, Kirana minimizes heavy rhythmic permutations (Layakari) in favor of melodic immersion.

Additional examples

  • Pandit Bhimsen Joshi popularized patriotic and devotional compositions like 'Mile Sur Mera Tumhara' and Kannada Dasavani while maintaining Kirana purity.
  • Ustad Abdul Karim Khan founded the Arya Music Circle in Pune to bridge classical music access across communities.
  • The Khayal rendering in Kirana starts directly from the sthayi note after a brief contemplative alap.
Important distinctions
  • Kirana vs Agra: Kirana focuses on meditative swara purity and slow alap; Agra focuses on robust Dhrupad-ang rhythm and bandish delivery.
  • Kirana vs Gwalior: Kirana is known for emotional, introspective slow vilambit; Gwalior is known for balanced, structured, robust khayal with direct rhythmic play.
  • Kirana vs Banaras: Kirana is strictly classical/meditative; Banaras seamlessly integrates semi-classical folk flavors.
Common misconceptions
  • Misconception: Kirana Gharana originated in Pakistan. Fact: It originated in Kairana, a town in the Shamli district of Uttar Pradesh, India.
  • Misconception: Kirana singers cannot sing fast taans. Fact: While they prioritize slow alap, masters like Bhimsen Joshi possessed formidable, lightning-fast taan agility.
Exam focus
  • Remember Kairana / Shamli district (UP) as the geographical origin.
  • Identify Ustad Abdul Karim Khan and Ustad Abdul Wahid Khan as founders.
  • Associate Pandit Bhimsen Joshi and Gangubai Hangal with Kirana Gharana.
  • Highlight 'Swara Purity' and 'Slow Alap' as core Kirana identifiers.
Concept 4 · advanced

Agra Gharana: Dhrupad Heritage, Robust Khayal, and Nom Thom Alap

The Agra Gharana, anchored in the historical city of Agra, traces its lineage directly to the ancient Dhrupad tradition, featuring a robust, voluminous, and rhythmically complex vocal style.

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of Dhrupad and Dhamar vocal genres
  • Knowledge of historical Mughal court music centers in Agra and Fatehpur Sikri
  • Familiarity with Bol-Bant and Laya-kari in Khayal

Definitions and scope

  • Nom Thom Alap: The non-lexical vowel-based exposition derived from ancient Dhrupad singing.
  • Dagarvani: The pristine Dhrupad lineage associated with Agra's foundational roots.
  • Bol-Bant: Rhythmic play with the words of the composition.

Step-by-step explanation

  • The Agra Gharana traces its lineage back to Haji Sujan Khan and Khuda Baksh in the 16th-17th centuries during the Mughal era in Agra.
  • It absorbed the powerful Dhrupad and Dhamar singing traditions, notably through the descendents of Alakh Das and later Ustad Ghagra Khan.
  • The revival and modern structuring of the Agra Gharana in the late 19th century were spearheaded by Ustad Wazir Khan and legendary Ustad Faiyaz Khan.
  • Ustad Faiyaz Khan (often called 'Aftab-e-Mousiqui' or Sun of Music) was the most prominent colossus of the Agra Gharana, blending scholarly depth with emotional fervor.
  • The vocal signature of Agra Gharana is full-throated, resonant voice production (Akaran gaaiki), ensuring projection across large open-air auditoriums.
  • Unlike Kirana's microtonal slurs, Agra emphasizes bold, assertive note placements, complex rhythmic variations (layakari), and comprehensive raga exposition.
  • Agra vocalists give equal importance to the composition (Bandish), singing it with robust bol-bant and rhythmic interplay with the percussionist.
  • Notable masters include Vilayat Hussain Khan, Latafat Hussain Khan, Sharafat Hussain Khan, and Dinkar Kaikini.

Key points

  • Geographical root: Agra, Uttar Pradesh.
  • Founders: Haji Sujan Khan, Khuda Baksh.
  • Supreme Icon: Ustad Faiyaz Khan (Aftab-e-Mousiqui).
  • Stylistic hallmark: Dhrupad-ang Khayal, robust voice, complex layakari, bol-bant.
  • Core aesthetic: Masculine, voluminous, intellectual, and rhythmically rigorous.
  • Other stalwarts: Vilayat Hussain Khan, Latafat Hussain Khan.
Worked examples
  • Example 1: Ustad Faiyaz Khan rendering a majestic Vilambit Khayal in Raag Ramkali, opening with a dignified Nom-Thom alap followed by powerful bandish delivery.
  • Example 2: An Agra Gharana vocalist executing intricate bol-bant across the cycle of Jhaptal (10 beats), demonstrating precise rhythmic sync with the Tabla.

Applications and connections

  • Crucial for UP TGT/PGT questions on Ustad Faiyaz Khan and Agra music history.
  • Essential for understanding the transition of Dhrupad aesthetics into Khayal performance.
  • Important for musicology exams testing knowledge of voice production and acoustic resonance.

Advanced points, exceptions and limitations

  • The Agra Gharana preserves the 'Rangila' and 'Sarangiwale' sub-lineages originating from court families.
  • Agra vocalists are trained to sing across three octaves with equal volume and clarity, avoiding breathy tones.
  • The integration of 'Nom-Thom' syllables before starting the Khayal bandish is a direct retention of medieval temple/court Dhrupad protocols.

Additional examples

  • Ustad Faiyaz Khan served long as the court musician of Baroda while maintaining deep ties to Agra.
  • The composition 'Sakhi Eri' or traditional bandishes in Raag Yaman taught in Agra gharana reflect rich poetic content.
  • The title 'Aftab-e-Mousiqui' was bestowed upon Faiyaz Khan at a music conference in Allahabad in 1915.
Important distinctions
  • Agra vs Kirana: Agra is bold, full-throated, rhythmically complex, and Dhrupad-derived; Kirana is soft, introspective, swara-focused, and slow-alap driven.
  • Agra vs Gwalior: Both are robust and Khayal-centric, but Agra retains stronger Dhrupad-ang elements and complex bol-bant, whereas Gwalior is known for directness and clear bandish presentation.
  • Agra vs Banaras: Agra is more structured and academic in Khayal execution; Banaras leans heavily into folk-infused semi-classical genres.
Common misconceptions
  • Misconception: Agra Gharana is related to the Taj Mahal construction era only. Fact: While tied to the city of Agra, its musical systematization evolved across the late Mughal and British colonial periods through master practitioners.
  • Misconception: Agra singers only sing loud without emotion. Fact: Despite robust voice production, masters like Faiyaz Khan brought profound emotional devotion and spiritual gravity (Bhakti and Sufi rasa) to their renditions.
Exam focus
  • Remember Ustad Faiyaz Khan ('Aftab-e-Mousiqui') as the definitive master of Agra Gharana.
  • Note the Dhrupad-ang and Nom-Thom alap roots of Agra.
  • Associate the geographical city of Agra with robust voice production and bol-bant.
  • Identify Vilayat Hussain Khan and Latafat Hussain Khan as Agra stalwarts.
Concept 5 · exam-synthesis

Comparative Synthesis of UP Gharanas for UP TGT/PGT Music Examinations

A holistic comparative synthesis of Lucknow, Banaras, Kirana, and Agra Gharanas enables aspirants to master cross-questioning, matching lists, and analytical MCQs in UP TGT/PGT examinations.

Prerequisites

  • Thorough understanding of concepts 1 through 4
  • Familiarity with historical founders, UP districts, and stylistic hallmarks
  • Competency in objective question elimination techniques

Definitions and scope

  • Comparative Matrix: A structured evaluation of musical lineages based on geography, founders, and aesthetics.
  • Exam Synthesis: The application of isolated facts into high-yield conceptual recall.

Step-by-step explanation

  • Lucknow Gharana: Centered in Lucknow (Awadh); founded by Bakhshu Khan & Maltu Khan; excels in Thumri, Dadra, and Kathak; patronized by Wajid Ali Shah.
  • Banaras Gharana: Centered in Varanasi; vocal founders include Bade Ram Das Ji; Tabla founder Pt. Ram Sahai; excels in Purbi Ang Tabla, Thumri, and Poorab folk forms.
  • Kirana Gharana: Centered in Kairana (Shamli district, western UP); founded by Abdul Karim Khan & Abdul Wahid Khan; excels in slow alap, swara purity, and meditative Khayal.
  • Agra Gharana: Centered in Agra; founded by Haji Sujan Khan & Khuda Baksh; championed by Ustad Faiyaz Khan; excels in Dhrupad-ang Khayal, robust voice, and bol-bant.
  • Geographical distribution in UP: Western UP hosts Kirana (Shamli); Central UP hosts Lucknow; Eastern UP (Purvanchal) hosts Banaras; South-Western UP hosts Agra.
  • Vocal aesthetic spectrum: Kirana is the most meditative and swara-focused; Agra is the most robust and rhythm-interplay (bol-bant) focused; Banaras merges folk and semi-classical; Lucknow centers on lyrical romanticism (Bhava).
  • Exam questions frequently test matching founders to Gharanas, districts to Gharanas, and iconic artists to their respective traditions.
  • Mastering these distinctions prevents common overlaps and guarantees high scores in both Music subject papers and General Studies UP-specific sections.

Key points

  • Lucknow = Lyrical, Thumri/Kathak, Wajid Ali Shah, Bakhshu-Maltu.
  • Banaras = Purbi Ang Tabla (Ram Sahai), Thumri/Tappa, Girija Devi.
  • Kirana = Swara Purity, Shamli (Kairana), Abdul Karim Khan, Bhimsen Joshi.
  • Agra = Dhrupad-ang, Faiyaz Khan, Bol-Bant, robust voice.
  • UP regional span covers Western, Central, Eastern, and Southern cultural zones.
Worked examples
  • Example 1 (Matching Question): Match Gharana (A. Kirana, B. Agra, C. Lucknow, D. Banaras) with Founder (1. Ram Sahai, 2. Bakhshu Khan, 3. Abdul Karim Khan, 4. Sujan Khan). Solution: A-3, B-4, C-2, D-1.
  • Example 2 (Assertion-Reason): Assertion: Kirana Gharana places extreme emphasis on slow alap and swara intonation. Reason: Founders Abdul Karim Khan and Abdul Wahid Khan prioritized microtonal precision over fast rhythmic acrobatics. Solution: Both are true and R is the correct explanation of A.

Applications and connections

  • Direct application in solving matching, statement-based, and assertion-reason questions in UP TGT/PGT Music and Art & Culture exams.
  • Useful for viva voce and interview preparation for teaching positions in Uttar Pradesh educational institutions.
  • Helps cultural historians map the patron-artist migration networks across North Indian princely states.

Advanced points, exceptions and limitations

  • Many master musicians trained across multiple Gharanas (e.g., Begum Akhtar learning from Kirana and Lucknow masters), illustrating the porous boundaries of these traditions.
  • The evolution of Gharanas reflects the transition of music from royal courts (Durbar) to public sabhas and modern institutions.
  • Understanding regional dialects (Awadhi, Braj Bhasha, Bhojpuri) illuminates the lyrical content of regional Gharana compositions.

Additional examples

  • A typical UP PGT question asks for the exact district of the Kirana Gharana (Shamli/Muzaffarnagar belt).
  • Identifying Ustad Faiyaz Khan's title 'Aftab-e-Mousiqui' is a recurring objective question.
  • Recognizing Pandit Kishan Maharaj as a doyen of Banaras Tabla Baaj.
Important distinctions
  • Gharana vs Dagar: Gharanas are multi-branched vocal/instrumental traditions; Dagar is a specific, ancient family lineage strictly preserving Dhrupad.
  • North UP vs South UP traditions: Kirana in the north-west vs Agra in the south-west show distinct stylistic adaptations.
  • Court vs Temple patronages: Lucknow and Agra reflect courtly nawabi/mughal aesthetics; Banaras reflects temple-courtesan-public festival ecosystems.
Common misconceptions
  • Misconception: All Gharanas originated in royal courts. Fact: Banaras Gharana and parts of Kirana grew out of indigenous community, temple, and local town settings.
  • Misconception: A Gharana restricts an artist from singing other styles. Fact: Mature artists frequently incorporate elements from allied traditions while maintaining their core gharana identity.
Exam focus
  • Memorize the 4 primary UP Gharanas, their founders, and headquarters.
  • Be prepared for district-level geographical tagging (Shamli for Kirana, Varanasi for Banaras, Agra for Agra, Lucknow for Lucknow).
  • Revise iconic exponents: Faiyaz Khan (Agra), Bhimsen Joshi (Kirana), Girija Devi (Banaras), Begum Akhtar (Lucknow).
  • Practice matching lists of musical instruments/genres to their respective UP Gharanas.

Rapid revision: Music Gharanas of UP

Review every concept point-wise before attempting the quiz and timed test.

Concept 1

Lucknow Gharana: Vocal, Thumri, and Kathak Traditions

Definition and scope

  • Founded by Bakhshu Khan and Maltu Khan in the late 18th century.
  • Thrived under the royal patronage of Nawab Wajid Ali Shah (Akhtar Piya).
  • Specializes in Thumri, Dadra, Ghazal, and Kathak dance with an emphasis on emotional expression (Bhava).

Must remember

  • Founders: Bakhshu Khan & Maltu Khan.
  • Patron: Nawab Wajid Ali Shah.
  • Dance icons: Kalka Prasad and Bindadin Maharaj.
  • Vocal icon: Begum Akhtar (Mallika-e-Ghazal).
  • Aesthetic: Lyrical grace and romanticism.

Concept-specific exam traps

  • Confusing Wajid Ali Shah as the musical founder instead of royal patron.
  • Assuming Lucknow Gharana is exclusively a dance tradition ignoring its rich vocal Thumri heritage.
Concept 2

Banaras Gharana: Temple Roots, Vocal Mastery, and Tabla Purbi Ang

Definition and scope

  • Rooted in the spiritual and cultural milieu of Varanasi (Kashi).
  • Excels in both vocal (Thumri, Tappa, Khayal) and percussion (Purbi Ang Tabla).
  • Founded in Tabla by Pandit Ram Sahai in the late 18th century.

Must remember

  • Tabla Founder: Pandit Ram Sahai.
  • Tabla Baaj: Purbi Ang (open, resonant strokes).
  • Vocal Masters: Bade Ram Das Ji, Girija Devi, Siddheshwari Devi, Rasoolan Bai.
  • Tabla Masters: Pt. Kishan Maharaj, Pt. Anokhe Lal Mishra.
  • Repertoire: Rich in seasonal folk-classical forms like Chaiti, Kajari, Hori.

Concept-specific exam traps

  • Thinking Banaras Gharana is only a Tabla tradition.
  • Confusing Delhi Baaj (closed strokes) with Banaras Purbi Ang (open resonant strokes).
Concept 3

Kirana Gharana: Swara Purity, Slow Alap, and Meditative Khayal

Definition and scope

  • Originates from Kairana town in the Shamli district of western Uttar Pradesh.
  • Founded by cousins Ustad Abdul Karim Khan and Ustad Abdul Wahid Khan.
  • Celebrated for absolute swara purity, slow vilambit alap, and emotive meends.

Must remember

  • Geographical origin: Kairana, Shamli district, UP.
  • Founders: Ustad Abdul Karim Khan and Ustad Abdul Wahid Khan.
  • Stalwart: Pandit Bhimsen Joshi, Gangubai Hangal, Prabha Atre.
  • Aesthetic hallmark: Meditative slow alap and microtonal pitch accuracy.
  • Vocal form: Vilambit Khayal and devotional bhajans.

Concept-specific exam traps

  • Mistakenly placing Kairana in Pakistan or another state instead of western UP.
  • Assuming Kirana singers lack rhythmic agility because of their slow alap focus.
Concept 4

Agra Gharana: Dhrupad Heritage, Robust Khayal, and Nom Thom Alap

Definition and scope

  • Anchored in Agra, tracing roots to Haji Sujan Khan and Khuda Baksh.
  • Deeply connected to ancient Dhrupad-Dhamar vocal mechanics.
  • Championed by Ustad Faiyaz Khan ('Aftab-e-Mousiqui').

Must remember

  • Founders: Haji Sujan Khan, Khuda Baksh.
  • Supreme Icon: Ustad Faiyaz Khan (Aftab-e-Mousiqui).
  • Stylistic feature: Full-throated robust voice, Nom-Thom alap, bol-bant.
  • Other masters: Vilayat Hussain Khan, Latafat Hussain Khan.
  • Aesthetic: Intellectual, masculine, and rhythmically rigorous Khayal.

Concept-specific exam traps

  • Confusing Agra Gharana with soft semi-classical genres; Agra is fundamentally robust and Khayal/Dhrupad oriented.
  • Forgetting Ustad Faiyaz Khan's honorary title 'Aftab-e-Mousiqui'.
Concept 5

Comparative Synthesis of UP Gharanas for UP TGT/PGT Music Examinations

Definition and scope

  • Synthesizes geographic distribution across UP: West (Kirana), Central (Lucknow), South (Agra), East (Banaras).
  • Compares stylistic spectrum from meditative swara (Kirana) to robust rhythm/Dhrupad (Agra), folk-classical fusion (Banaras), and lyrical romanticism (Lucknow).
  • Equips aspirants for objective matching and analytical exam questions.

Must remember

  • Kirana = Shamli / Abdul Karim Khan / Swara purity.
  • Lucknow = Awadh / Bakhshu-Maltu / Thumri & Kathak.
  • Banaras = Varanasi / Ram Sahai / Purbi Ang & Girija Devi.
  • Agra = Agra / Faiyaz Khan / Dhrupad-ang & Bol-bant.
  • All four are premier pillars of Uttar Pradesh's musical heritage.

Concept-specific exam traps

  • Mixing up founders and geographical headquarters in matching questions.
  • Failing to distinguish between Agra's robust Dhrupad-ang and Kirana's meditative swara-ang.

Quick facts

  • Ustad Faiyaz Khan of Agra Gharana was bestowed with the title 'Aftab-e-Mousiqui' (Sun of Music).
  • The Kirana Gharana originates from Kairana, located in the Shamli district of Uttar Pradesh.
  • Pandit Ram Sahai founded the Banaras Tabla Gharana in the late 18th century.
  • Nawab Wajid Ali Shah of Lucknow was a noted patron of arts and composed under the pen name 'Akhtar Piya'.
  • Girija Devi, legendary exponent of Banaras Thumri, was affectionately known as the 'Queen of Thumri'.
  • Begum Akhtar popularized the Lucknow style of Dadra and Ghazal across India.

Glossary

GharanaA system of musical lineage and stylistic tradition transmitted from master to disciple.
Vilambit KhayalA slow-tempo classical vocal composition forming the centerpiece of Khayal performance.
Purbi AngThe eastern style of Tabla playing native to Banaras and Lucknow, known for open resonant strokes.
Bol-BantRhythmic improvisation and permutation using the words of a vocal composition.
MeendA smooth, continuous glide from one musical note to another without a break.
Nom-Thom AlapA non-lexical, vowel-based introductory exposition derived from ancient Dhrupad singing.

Important distinctions

Kirana GharanavsAgra Gharana
  • Kirana focuses on microtonal swara purity and slow meditative alap; Agra focuses on robust, full-throated voice and complex rhythmic bol-bant.
  • Kirana originated in Kairana (Shamli, UP); Agra originated in the historical city of Agra.
Lucknow GharanavsBanaras Gharana
  • Lucknow Thumri is soft, courtly, and romantically leisurely; Banaras Thumri is earthy, bold, and infused with Purvanchal folk flavor.
  • Lucknow was nurtured in the royal Nawabi court; Banaras grew out of temple traditions, festivals, and courtesan ecosystems.

Memory hooks

  • Mnemonic for UP Gharana Towns: 'K'irana in Shamli (West), 'L'ucknow in Center, 'A'gra in South, 'B'anaras in East (KLAB: Kirana, Lucknow, Agra, Banaras).
  • Founder Hook: Bakhshu-Maltu (Lucknow), Ram Sahai (Banaras Tabla), Abdul Karim-Wahid (Kirana), Sujan-Khuda Baksh (Agra).
  • Vocal Icon Hook: Faiyaz Khan = Agra, Bhimsen Joshi = Kirana, Girija Devi = Banaras, Begum Akhtar = Lucknow.

Exam traps and cautions

  • Do not confuse the founders of Kirana (Abdul Karim Khan) with Agra (Sujan Khan).
  • Remember that Banaras is famous for both vocal Thumri and Purbi Ang Tabla, not just Tabla.
  • Do not assume Wajid Ali Shah founded Lucknow Gharana; he was its royal patron.
  • Check whether a question asks for the district of Kirana (Shamli) versus its musical style.
