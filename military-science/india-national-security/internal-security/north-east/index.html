India's Internal Security: Kashmir and the North-East

Internal Security in the Context of the North-East India Military Science

North-East India comprises eight states connected strategically to the rest of the country through the narrow Siliguri Corridor.

Topic overview

  • North-East India comprises eight states connected strategically to the rest of the country through the narrow Siliguri Corridor.
  • The region shares extensive and porous international borders with Myanmar, China, Bhutan, Bangladesh, and Nepal.
  • Internal security challenges stem from ethnic diversity, historical alienation, cross-border infiltration, and demands for autonomy.
  • Counter-insurgency doctrine in the North-East balances kinetic military operations with political dialogue and developmental initiatives.
Concept 1

Geostrategic Significance and Vulnerabilities of the North-East

The unique geographic layout and international boundaries of North-East India create distinct strategic imperatives and security vulnerabilities.

Explanation

  • The region acts as India's strategic gateway to Southeast Asia under the active Act East Policy.
  • The Siliguri Corridor, popularly known as the Chicken's Neck, is a vital vulnerability linking the region to mainland India.
  • Porous borders with neighbouring countries facilitate illegal migration, drug trafficking, and safe havens for insurgent groups.
  • Complex terrain featuring dense tropical forests, rugged hills, and major river systems complicates conventional military operations.
  • Geographic isolation from the national capital historically fueled economic backwardness and socio-political alienation among local populations.

Key points

  • Siliguri Corridor is the singular land bridge connecting North-East India to the rest of the nation.
  • International borders span over 5,000 kilometres, presenting immense management and surveillance challenges.
  • Proximity to the Golden Triangle makes the region vulnerable to narcotics and small arms smuggling networks.
  • Terrain topography necessitates specialised jungle warfare training and air-mobile logistical capabilities for security forces.
Examples / applications:
  • Vulnerability of the Siliguri Corridor during strategic blockades or foreign military threats.
  • Cross-border movement of insurgent cadres across the unfenced Indo-Myanmar border.
Compare:
  • Unlike the militarised Line of Control in Jammu and Kashmir, North-East borders are largely civilian-populated and porous.
  • Unlike desert borders in Western India, the North-East features dense forests and high-precipitation tropical terrain.
Common misconceptions:
  • Believing that North-East security issues are purely internal without external geopolitical influences.
  • Assuming that geographic isolation is the sole reason for underdevelopment without acknowledging governance gaps.
Exam focus:
  • Identify the geopolitical importance of the Siliguri Corridor in national defence planning.
  • Assess the security implications of porous international borders in the North-East.
  • Evaluate the strategic linkages between the Act East Policy and regional stability.
Concept 2

Evolution of Insurgency and Tribal Dynamics

Ethno-national aspirations and tribal identities formed the bedrock of protracted armed insurgencies in post-independence North-East India.

Explanation

  • Post-independence integration of diverse tribal principalities sparked identity fears and demands for absolute sovereignty.
  • Naga nationalism emerged as the earliest organised armed movement demanding independence under the leadership of A.Z. Phizo.
  • Subsequent fragmentation led to the birth of multiple militant groups representing Mizo, Meitei, ULFA, and other ethnic factions.
  • Inter-ethnic clashes frequently complicate the security landscape alongside direct conflicts with Indian security forces.
  • Insurgent groups historically established external sanctuaries in neighbouring countries like Burma, East Pakistan, and Bhutan.

Key points

  • Naga insurgency began in the 1950s, representing the longest-running internal security conflict in independent India.
  • Ethno-linguistic diversity prevented a unified pan-North-East insurgent front, often resulting in intra-tribal warfare.
  • Clandestine support from external adversaries aggravated violence during the Cold War era.
  • Extortion, taxation, and parallel governments became primary funding mechanisms for underground outfits.
Examples / applications:
  • Naga National Council declaration of independence on August 14, 1947.
  • Formation of the United Liberation Front of Assam in 1979 demanding a sovereign socialist Assam.
Compare:
  • Unlike religious fundamentalist terrorism in other regions, North-East insurgencies are primarily anchored in ethno-nationalism and tribal identity.
  • Unlike Kashmir, which involves bilateral interstate disputes, North-East insurgencies are largely domestic identity movements with regional spillovers.
Common misconceptions:
  • Treating all North-East militant groups as a single homogeneous anti-India coalition.
  • Confusing economic grievances as the primary driver when ethnic identity preservation is often paramount.
Exam focus:
  • Trace the historical roots of the Naga and Mizo national movements.
  • Examine the socio-cultural factors driving tribal identity politics in the North-East.
  • Analyze the transformation of political grievances into violent separatist insurgencies.
Concept 3

AFSPA and the Special Legal Framework

The Armed Forces Special Powers Act provides the necessary legal umbrella for security forces operating in disturbed areas of the North-East.

Explanation

  • Enacted originally as an ordinance in 1958, AFSPA grants special enforcement powers to military and paramilitary personnel in disturbed areas.
  • The legislation permits the use of force, including lethal force, for the maintenance of public order after due declaration.
  • It provides legal immunity to armed forces personnel from prosecution without prior sanction from the Central Government.
  • Civil rights activists and regional bodies have consistently opposed AFSPA, citing instances of human rights violations and abuse of power.
  • Recent years have witnessed a phased withdrawal of AFSPA from several districts due to improved security conditions.

Key points

  • Application of AFSPA requires an area to be officially notified as a disturbed area by central or state authorities.
  • The act empowers commissioned officers, warrant officers, and non-commissioned officers to arrest without warrant.
  • Legal immunity provisions aim to protect soldiers from malicious litigation while performing counter-insurgency duties.
  • Supreme Court judgments have upheld the constitutional validity of AFSPA while issuing guidelines to prevent misuse.
Examples / applications:
  • Declaration of disturbed areas across Nagaland, Manipur, and Assam during peak insurgency decades.
  • Phased revocation of AFSPA zones in Tripura, Meghalaya, and parts of Assam and Nagaland in recent years.
Compare:
  • Unlike standard criminal procedure codes, AFSPA shifts primary law enforcement and security maintenance directly to the armed forces.
  • Unlike emergency provisions under Article 352, AFSPA operates specifically within targeted geographical zones designated as disturbed.
Common misconceptions:
  • Assuming AFSPA grants absolute license for arbitrary killings without any regulatory oversight or legal framework.
  • Believing that the Indian Army prefers operating under AFSPA rather than focusing on conventional external defence.
Exam focus:
  • Critically evaluate the necessity versus human rights concerns surrounding AFSPA.
  • Understand the statutory provisions and criteria for declaring a disturbed area.
  • Analyze recent trends regarding the progressive withdrawal of AFSPA from North-Eastern states.
Concept 4

Counter-Insurgency Strategy and Peace Accords

India's security strategy in the North-East integrates kinetic counter-insurgency operations with progressive political settlements and peace accords.

Explanation

  • Military operations like Operation Golden Bird and coordinated actions with Myanmar successfully disrupted insurgent supply lines.
  • The Suspension of Operations framework allows militant groups to reside in designated camps while engaging in political talks.
  • Numerous peace accords have been signed over decades to integrate alienated groups into the constitutional mainstream.
  • The Sixth Schedule of the Constitution provides autonomous district councils to safeguard tribal autonomy and local governance.
  • Developmental packages, infrastructure expansion, and economic integration serve as key pillars to win hearts and minds.

Key points

  • The dual-track approach combines proactive security operations with open doors for unconditional peace negotiations.
  • Assam Accord of 1985 and the Mizo Accord of 1986 serve as landmark examples of successful political settlements.
  • Border fencing and technological surveillance along international boundaries restrict cross-border movement of insurgents.
  • Integration of surrendered militants through rehabilitation schemes prevents them from relapsing into criminality.
Examples / applications:
  • Mizo Accord of 1986 bringing peace and statehood to Mizoram after decades of insurgency.
  • Bodo Peace Accord signed in 2020 restructuring the Bodoland Territorial Region.
Compare:
  • Unlike purely kinetic warfare against foreign invaders, North-East counter-insurgency prioritises reconciliation and assimilation of misguided citizens.
  • Unlike conventional military doctrines, COIN doctrine places heavy emphasis on psychological operations and civic action programs.
Common misconceptions:
  • Believing that military force alone can permanently resolve ethnic and identity-based conflicts.
  • Assuming that signing a peace accord immediately erases all underlying ethnic and tribal tensions.
Exam focus:
  • Analyze the strategic components of India's Counter-Insurgency doctrine in the North-East.
  • Evaluate the effectiveness of historical peace accords in restoring democratic governance.
  • Assess the role of the Sixth Schedule in mitigating regional autonomy demands.
Concept 5

External Linkages and Regional Geopolitics

External state and non-state actors historically exploited the vulnerabilities of North-East India, making regional security a vital foreign policy concern.

Explanation

  • China's historical and contemporary interest in the North-East creates serious strategic anxieties for Indian national security planners.
  • Proximity to the Golden Triangle makes the region a transit route for arms proliferation and narcotics funding.
  • Cooperation with Myanmar's military junta is critical for neutralizing insurgent camps operating in the contiguous Sagaing Region.
  • Bangladesh under friendly administrations has cooperated closely to deny sanctuary to anti-India groups operating from its soil.
  • Geopolitical connectivity initiatives seek to transform the North-East from a security liability into an economic bridge to ASEAN.

Key points

  • External intelligence agencies historically provided sanctuary, weapons training, and financial backing to underground North-Eastern outfits.
  • Myanmar military operations like Operation Sunshine demonstrated successful bilateral security cooperation against insurgent bases.
  • Border infrastructure development under the Border Area Development Programme strengthens strategic depth against external threats.
  • Economic engagement with Southeast Asia dilutes the strategic isolation of the landlocked North-Eastern states.
Examples / applications:
  • Operation Sunshine conducted jointly by Indian and Myanmar armies against insurgent camps in 2015 and 2019.
  • Expulsion of top ULFA leadership from Bangladesh following diplomatic engagements in the late 2000s.
Compare:
  • Unlike the western border where Pakistan is the primary overt adversary, the eastern border involves complex multilateral security dynamics.
  • Unlike traditional border defence, North-East external security requires close diplomatic coordination with friendly neighbours for internal stabilization.
Common misconceptions:
  • Assuming foreign support for North-East insurgents remains as active today as it was during the Cold War.
  • Overlooking the economic dimension of external policy in favour of purely military defense postures.
Exam focus:
  • Evaluate the impact of foreign sanctuaries on the longevity of North-East insurgencies.
  • Examine the role of Indo-Myanmar and Indo-Bangladesh bilateral diplomacy in internal security.
  • Analyze China's strategic calculus regarding India's North-Eastern frontier.
