1. Overview of Logical Reasoning

Logical reasoning in UPSSSC Lower Mains tests your ability to deduce valid inferences from given premises. यूपीएसएसएससी लोअर मेन्स में तार्किक तर्क आपकी क्षमता का परीक्षण करता है कि आप दिए गए आधारों से वैध निष्कर्ष निकाल सकें।

The exam focuses on three pillars: Statement-Conclusion, Statement-Argument, and Statement-Assumption. Each section requires a unique analytical approach to filter subjective bias.

2. Understanding Statement-Conclusion

A conclusion is a deduction drawn from the statement. Rule #1: The conclusion must be 100% supported by the statement. If it is only 99% true or requires outside knowledge, it is invalid.

Data analysis shows that 65% of candidates fail due to 'over-thinking'—applying general knowledge instead of adhering strictly to the provided text. Always assume the statement is the 'universe' of the question.

Key Rule: If it's not explicitly stated or directly implied, it is not a conclusion.

3. Statement-Argument Analysis

Arguments are opinions about a course of action. A 'Strong Argument' is one that is both important and directly related to the issue. A 'Weak Argument' is ambiguous, superficial, or based on mere opinion.

In Indian administrative exams like UPSSSC, arguments favoring constitutional validity, welfare-state policies, and economic feasibility are generally considered 'Strong'.

4. The Logic of 'Strong' Arguments

Arguments must demonstrate a cause-effect relationship. If a policy is proposed, the argument must explain 'Why' it should or should not be implemented using logical reasoning rather than emotional appeal.

Example: "Should the government ban single-use plastic?" A strong argument would cite environmental impact data, whereas a weak argument would simply say "Yes, it is bad for the earth" without providing evidence or logical depth.

6. Elimination Techniques for Conclusion

Use the 'Negative Elimination' method. If a conclusion contains words like 'Only', 'Always', 'Never', or 'All' in a context where the statement is general, it is usually incorrect.

Statistically, questions involving absolute terms have a 75% probability of being 'False' conclusions in the context of UPSSSC reasoning papers.

7. The Role of Assumptions

An assumption is the 'hidden' premise. Before a statement is made, what was the speaker thinking? If the statement is "We must improve irrigation to increase yield," the assumption is "Irrigation is currently insufficient."

Pro Tip: Test the assumption by negating it. If the statement loses its meaning, the assumption is valid.

9. Handling 'Ambiguous' Arguments

Candidates often get trapped by 'Polite' arguments. Just because an argument sounds reasonable, it doesn't make it 'Strong'. Check if it addresses the core issue or just provides a platitude.

Focus on: Feasibility, Cost-Benefit Analysis, and Constitutional alignment. Avoid arguments based on 'majority opinion'—popularity does not equate to logical strength.

10. Logical Fallacies to Avoid

Ad Hominem (attacking the person), Appeal to Authority (citing a famous person rather than facts), and Circular Reasoning (the conclusion is just a restatement of the premise).

In the UPSSSC exam, identifying circular reasoning is a quick way to eliminate wrong options. If the conclusion says exactly what the statement says, it is not an inference; it is a repetition.

11. Tips for Exam Day

1. Read the statement twice. 2. Identify the core subject. 3. Evaluate options against the subject. 4. Use the '5-second rule': if you can't find a logical link in 5 seconds, move to the next option.

Mnemonics: 'FACTS' - Feasible, Accurate, Clear, True, Solid.

12. Practice Strategy

Dedicate 30 minutes daily to solving 20 questions. Review incorrect answers not just for the right choice, but to understand the logic of the 'distractor' options provided by the examiners.

13. Advanced Logical Reasoning Concepts

Understanding the difference between 'Direct Inference' and 'Indirect Inference'. Direct inference is explicitly stated, while indirect requires a single step of logical leap based on the statement's context.

Always maintain a neutral stance. Your personal beliefs about UP politics or social issues must be discarded. The statement is the only truth.

14. Conclusion Summary

To excel in UPSSSC Lower Mains, consistency is key. Reasoning is a scoring section if you master the grammar of logic. Practice identifying 'Strong' vs 'Weak' through mock tests.

Final Advice: Never assume outside facts. If the statement says 'All apples are blue', treat them as blue, even if real apples are red.

11

Statement 1: All A are B. Statement 2: All B are C. True or False? All A are C.

कथन 1: सभी A, B हैं। कथन 2: सभी B, C हैं। सत्य या असत्य? सभी A, C हैं।

Show Answer उत्तर देखें

✔ Correct: A

✔ सही उत्तर: A

This is a classic syllogism rule. If A is a subset of B, and B is a subset of C, then by transitive property, A must be a subset of C.

यह एक क्लासिक न्यायवाक्य नियम है। यदि A, B का उपसमुच्चय है, और B, C का उपसमुच्चय है, तो संक्रामक गुण के अनुसार, A निश्चित रूप से C का उपसमुच्चय होगा।

Show Answer उत्तर देखें

✔ Correct: A

✔ सही उत्तर: A

चूंकि कुछ कुत्ते बिल्लियाँ हैं, और कोई भी बिल्ली चूहा नहीं है, कुत्तों का वह हिस्सा जो बिल्लियाँ हैं, वे चूहे नहीं हो सकते। इसलिए, 'कुछ कुत्ते चूहे नहीं हैं' सत्य है।
