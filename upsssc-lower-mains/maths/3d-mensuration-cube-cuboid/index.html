2. Overview & Foundation

3D Mensuration deals with solid objects having length, breadth, and height. Cube and cuboid are the most fundamental structures in geometry, often appearing in UPSSSC exams. 3D क्षेत्रमिति उन ठोस वस्तुओं से संबंधित है जिनमें लंबाई, चौड़ाई और ऊंचाई होती है। घन और घनाभ ज्यामिति में सबसे बुनियादी संरचनाएं हैं, जो अक्सर UPSSSC परीक्षाओं में पूछे जाते हैं।

3. The Geometry of a Cube (घन की ज्यामिति)

A cube is a special type of cuboid where all sides (length, breadth, and height) are equal. Let the side be 'a'. It has 6 faces, 12 edges, and 8 vertices. Every face is a square.

Volume (V) = a³, Total Surface Area (TSA) = 6a², Lateral Surface Area (LSA) = 4a², Diagonal = a√3.

Shortcut: If side increases by k%, volume increases by ((1+k/100)³ - 1) * 100%.

4. The Geometry of a Cuboid (घनाभ की ज्यामिति)

A cuboid is a 3D box-shaped object. Length (l), breadth (b), and height (h) are distinct. It has 6 rectangular faces.

Volume (V) = l × b × h, TSA = 2(lb + bh + lh), Diagonal = √(l² + b² + h²).

5. Surface Area Comparison

Understanding the difference between Total Surface Area (TSA) and Lateral Surface Area (LSA) is crucial. LSA excludes the top and bottom faces (only 4 walls).

Formula Table: - Cube LSA = 4a² - Cuboid LSA = 2h(l+b)

6. Advanced Diagonal Concepts

The longest rod that can be placed inside a cuboid is its diagonal. For a room of dimensions 10m x 8m x 6m, the diagonal = √(100+64+36) = √200 = 14.14m.

7. Volume and Capacity

Volume is the space occupied. 1 cubic meter = 1000 liters. This is a common conversion question in UPSSSC Lower Mains.

9. Mnemonics for Formulas

Use 'LBH' for Volume (Length, Breadth, Height). Remember '2(lb+bh+lh)' as the 'Pairwise Sum' for TSA.

10. Exam Tips for UPSSSC

1. Always check units. 2. Look for 'k' factor changes. 3. Use approximation for square roots.

11. Complex Geometry Problems

When two cubes are joined, the length doubles, but breadth and height remain the same. This creates a new cuboid.

12. Ratio-based Problems

If sides are in ratio 3:4:5, let sides be 3x, 4x, 5x. Volume = 60x³. Useful for quick ratio calculations.

13. Painting Problems

Total cost = (Surface Area to be painted) × (Rate per unit area). Always subtract the door/window area if specified.

14. Volume vs Surface Area

Small cubes cut from a large cube: n³ = V_large / V_small. This is a recurring pattern in competitive exams.
