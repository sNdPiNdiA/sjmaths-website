2. Overview of Human Systems

Human physiology is the study of mechanical, physical, and biochemical functions of humans. The digestive system processes nutrients, the respiratory system handles gaseous exchange, and the circulatory system acts as the transport highway.मानव शरीर क्रिया विज्ञान मनुष्यों के यांत्रिक, भौतिक और जैव रासायनिक कार्यों का अध्ययन है। पाचन तंत्र पोषक तत्वों को संसाधित करता है, श्वसन तंत्र गैसीय विनिमय को संभालता है, और संचार प्रणाली परिवहन राजमार्ग के रूप में कार्य करती है।

3. The Digestive System: Anatomy & Enzymes

The process begins in the mouth with salivary amylase (ptyalin) breaking down starch into maltose. The esophagus uses peristalsis to move the bolus. In the stomach, Gastric glands secrete HCl (pH 1.5-2.5) and pepsinogen, which converts to pepsin to digest proteins. The small intestine is the site of maximum absorption via villi.

Fact: The liver produces bile, which emulsifies fats, while the pancreas secretes lipase, trypsin, and amylase.

4. The Respiratory System: Gas Exchange Mechanics

Respiration involves ventilation (breathing) and cellular respiration. Air travels through the pharynx, larynx, trachea, bronchi, and bronchioles to reach the alveoli. The partial pressure of oxygen in alveoli (104 mmHg) is higher than in blood (40 mmHg), facilitating diffusion.

Hemoglobin carries 97% of oxygen as oxyhemoglobin. CO2 is transported primarily as bicarbonate ions (70%) in the plasma.

Clinical Note: Vital capacity is the maximum amount of air a person can expel from the lungs after maximum inhalation (approx 4.5-5 liters).

5. Circulatory System: The Heart and Vessels

The human heart is myogenic, consisting of four chambers. The right atrium receives deoxygenated blood from the vena cava, which passes to the right ventricle and then to the lungs via the pulmonary artery. Oxygenated blood returns via pulmonary veins to the left atrium and is pumped to the body by the left ventricle.

6. Blood Composition & Lymph

Blood consists of plasma (55%) and formed elements (45%). Plasma is mostly water with proteins like albumin and globulins. Red blood cells (Erythrocytes) contain hemoglobin, White blood cells (Leukocytes) provide immunity, and Platelets (Thrombocytes) aid in clotting.

Key Data: Average blood volume in an adult male is 5-6 liters, while in a female, it is 4-5 liters.

7. The Digestive Tract: Detailed Breakdown

The alimentary canal is roughly 9 meters long. The large intestine is responsible for water absorption and vitamin K synthesis by commensal bacteria. The liver is the largest internal organ, storing glycogen and detoxifying chemicals.

8. Respiratory Volumes & Capacities

Tidal volume is the volume of air inspired or expired during normal breathing (500ml). Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV) is the additional volume of air that can be inspired by a forceful effort (2500-3000ml).

Exam Focus: Residual volume (1100-1200ml) ensures lungs do not collapse after forced expiration.

9. Cardiac Cycle & Regulation

The cardiac cycle lasts 0.8 seconds. Systole (contraction) takes 0.3s, and diastole (relaxation) takes 0.5s. The SAN (Sinoatrial node) acts as the natural pacemaker, initiating the electrical impulse for contraction.

Blood pressure (BP) is measured in mmHg. Normal BP is 120/80 mmHg. Hypertension is defined as sustained pressure exceeding 140/90 mmHg.

10. Blood Groups & Rh Factor

Karl Landsteiner discovered ABO blood groups. Type O is the universal donor, and AB is the universal recipient. The Rh factor (discovered in Rhesus monkeys) determines the positive or negative status of blood.

11. Tips, Tricks, and Mnemonics

Mnemonic for Digestive Enzymes: "P-A-L-T" (Pepsin, Amylase, Lipase, Trypsin). Remember: Pepsin starts protein digestion in the stomach, Trypsin continues it in the intestine.

Trick for Circulatory System: Arteries carry blood 'Away' (A for A). Veins 'Visit' the heart back.
