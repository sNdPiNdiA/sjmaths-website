Semantic Classification (Word Odd-One-Out)

Comprehensive guide to Semantic Classification for SSC CGL Tier-1 and Tier-2 General Intelligence. Master word relations, taxonomic categories, and 10 high-probability practice MCQs.

1. Concept & Core Principles

In Semantic Classification, candidates are presented with a group of four or five words. Three of them share a well-defined common semantic attribute, property, origin, or category, while one does not belong to that group. The objective is to isolate the odd word out.

The 4-Step Solving Algorithm:

  1. Scan All Options: Never pick an option until you have examined all four words simultaneously.
  2. Formulate the Defining Trait: State the commonality explicitly (e.g., "These three are primary endocrine glands", not just "These are in the body").
  3. Test the Anomaly: Verify that the odd one clearly violates that specific trait, rather than just being different in an arbitrary way.
  4. Beware of Superficial Traps: SSC questions rarely rely on spelling length or vowel counts when a deeper real-world or semantic classification exists.

2. High-Yield Semantic Categories for SSC CGL

Most SSC CGL questions fall into these standard taxonomic and contextual relationships:

Semantic Domain Standard Associations Common SSC Exam Traps
Worker & Tool Carpenter : Saw • Sculptor : Chisel • Surgeon : Scalpel • Mason : Plumb Line • Tailor : Needle Mixing a tool with a raw material (e.g., Potter : Clay vs Potter : Wheel).
Physical Quantities & Units Force : Newton • Pressure : Pascal • Power : Watt • Resistance : Ohm • Work/Energy : Joule Pairing derived units with base SI units or mixing scalar with vector quantities.
Country & Capital Australia : Canberra • Canada : Ottawa • Japan : Tokyo • Egypt : Cairo • Brazil : Brasilia Giving the largest/famous city instead of the capital (e.g., Sydney vs Canberra, Toronto vs Ottawa).
Animals, Sounds & Young Lion : Cub / Roar • Dog : Puppy / Bark • Horse : Foal / Neigh • Frog : Tadpole / Croak Mixing sound with offspring (e.g., three pairs show young ones, one shows vocal sound).
Biological Taxonomy Mammals (Whale, Dolphin, Bat, Kangaroo) • Reptiles (Crocodile, Lizard, Turtle) • Arachnids (Spider, Scorpion) Whales and Dolphins are mammals, not fish! Spiders are arachnids (8 legs), not insects (6 legs).

3. 10 Interactive Practice MCQs (With Instant Explanations)

Select the odd word out in each of the following questions:

Q1. Three of the following four words are alike in a certain way and one is different. Pick the odd one out:
(A) Canberra
(B) Sydney
(C) Ottawa
(D) Wellington
View Solution

Correct Option: (B) Sydney

Explanation: Canberra (Australia), Ottawa (Canada), and Wellington (New Zealand) are national capitals. Sydney is a major city in Australia, but the capital is Canberra. Therefore, Sydney is the odd one out.
Q2. Select the odd one out from the given options:
(A) Whale
(B) Shark
(C) Dolphin
(D) Seal
View Solution

Correct Option: (B) Shark

Explanation: Whale, Dolphin, and Seal are warm-blooded marine mammals (Class Mammalia) that give birth to live young and breathe through lungs. Shark is a cartilaginous fish (Class Chondrichthyes) that breathes via gills.
Q3. Find the odd word-pair out:
(A) Carpenter : Saw
(B) Tailor : Needle
(C) Sculptor : Chisel
(D) Chef : Food
View Solution

Correct Option: (D) Chef : Food

Explanation: In pairs (A), (B), and (C), the relationship is Worker : Tool used. In pair (D), Food is the product/end result prepared by a chef, not the tool (the tool would be a Knife or Pan).
Q4. Three of the following four are alike based on a physical property. Choose the odd one:
(A) Mercury
(B) Iron
(C) Copper
(D) Aluminium
View Solution

Correct Option: (A) Mercury

Explanation: All four elements are metals, but Mercury is the only metal that is in a liquid state at room temperature. Iron, Copper, and Aluminium are solid metals.
Q5. Select the option that does not belong to the group:
(A) Pascal : Pressure
(B) Joule : Energy
(C) Watt : Power
(D) Ampere : Resistance
View Solution

Correct Option: (D) Ampere : Resistance

Explanation: In options (A), (B), and (C), the first term is the SI unit of the second term (Pascal for Pressure, Joule for Energy, Watt for Power). The SI unit of Resistance is Ohm, while Ampere is the unit of Electric Current.
Q6. Three of the following four organs are alike in a biological system. Identify the odd one:
(A) Kidney
(B) Ureter
(C) Urinary Bladder
(D) Pancreas
View Solution

Correct Option: (D) Pancreas

Explanation: Kidney, Ureter, and Urinary Bladder are organs of the human Excretory / Urinary System. Pancreas belongs to the Digestive and Endocrine systems.
Q7. Choose the word that does not belong with the others:
(A) Diamond
(B) Graphite
(C) Fullerenes
(D) Brass
View Solution

Correct Option: (D) Brass

Explanation: Diamond, Graphite, and Fullerenes are allotropes of pure Carbon (a non-metal element). Brass is an alloy made of Copper and Zinc.
Q8. Select the odd pair among the following:
(A) Lion : Pride
(B) Fish : School
(C) Wolf : Pack
(D) Cow : Den
View Solution

Correct Option: (D) Cow : Den

Explanation: In pairs (A), (B), and (C), the second word is the collective noun for a group of that animal (a pride of lions, a school of fish, a pack of wolves). In (D), Den is a shelter (and for wild animals like lions or bears, not cows). A group of cows is called a Herd.
Q9. Pick the odd one out based on Indian geography:
(A) Godavari
(B) Krishna
(C) Narmada
(D) Mahanadi
View Solution

Correct Option: (C) Narmada

Explanation: Godavari, Krishna, and Mahanadi are east-flowing peninsular rivers that drain into the Bay of Bengal and form deltas. Narmada is a west-flowing rift valley river that drains into the Arabian Sea and forms an estuary.
Q10. Four words are given below. Which one does not share the same grammatical or functional class?
(A) Happy : Delighted
(B) Sluggish : Lethargic
(C) Benevolent : Kind
(D) Wealthy : Poverty
View Solution

Correct Option: (D) Wealthy : Poverty

Explanation: Pairs (A), (B), and (C) consist of synonyms (words having identical or closely related meanings). Pair (D) contains an antonym pair with mismatched grammatical forms (Wealthy is an adjective while Poverty is a noun).
