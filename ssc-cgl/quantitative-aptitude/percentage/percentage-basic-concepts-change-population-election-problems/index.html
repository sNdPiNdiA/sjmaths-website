Percentage: Basic concepts & Problems

Master percentage-fraction calculations, successive changes, population growth, and election word problems with dynamic tools and mental math techniques.

Key Concepts & Study Notes

Welcome to the study page for Percentage: Basic concepts, change, population, election problems. This section is structured to help you build a solid foundation and master the types of questions commonly asked in the SSC CGL exam.

1. Introduction & Basic Definition

A percentage is a number or ratio expressed as a fraction of 100. It is often denoted using the percent sign "%". For example, 45% is equal to 45/100, or 0.45 in decimal form. In CGL, percentages form the core foundation for Profit & Loss, Simple & Compound Interest, and Data Interpretation.

2. Core Rules & Formulas

  • Rule 1: To convert a percentage to a fraction, divide by 100 (e.g. 25% = 25/100 = 1/4).
  • Rule 2: To convert a fraction to a percentage, multiply by 100 (e.g. 1/5 = 1/5 × 100 = 20%).
  • Rule 3: Memorize reciprocal fractions (1/2 to 1/20) to solve arithmetic questions without doing long division.

The 9 Core CGL Question Types & Mental Math Shortcuts

Type 1: Basic Calculations & Fractional Reciprocals

Concept Explanation:

CGL arithmetic speed depends entirely on how quickly you can convert percentages to fraction reciprocals. Instead of computing `x/100 * value`, break down complex percentages into standard fractions (e.g., 37.5% = 3/8, 62.5% = 5/8, 83.33% = 5/6).

Classic Method
Find 64.28% of 560:
Value = (64.28 / 100) × 560
- Requires multiplying 64.28 × 5.6 by hand.
- High risk of decimal calculation errors.
Mental Math Shortcut
Find 64.28% of 560:
- Recall that 1/7 = 14.28%.
- Realize 64.28% = 50% + 14.28%.
- Rewrite as: 1/2 + 1/7 of 560.
- Mentally calculate: 280 + 80 = 360. Done in 5 seconds!
Pedagogical Practice Exercises:
Q1: What is 57.14% of 1400?
Classic Method:
- (57.14 / 100) × 1400 = 800.
Mental Math:
- 14.28% = 1/7 ⇒ 57.14% = 4/7.
- 4/7 of 1400 = 800.
Q2: Find 12.5% of 62.5% of 6400.
Classic Method:
- 0.125 × 0.625 × 6400 = 500.
Mental Math:
- 1/8 × 5/8 × 6400 = 500.
Q3: What is 44.44% of 180 + 83.33% of 240?
Classic Method:
- (44.44 / 100) × 180 = 80.
- (83.33 / 100) × 240 = 200.
- Total = 280.
Mental Math:
- 44.44% = 4 × 11.11% = 4/9. (4/9) × 180 = 80.
- 83.33% = 1 - 1/6 = 5/6. (5/6) × 240 = 200.
- Sum = 80 + 200 = 280.
Q4: Evaluate 83.33% of 540 - 16.66% of 300.
Classic Method:
- 0.8333 × 540 = 450.
- 0.1666 × 300 = 50.
- Diff = 400.
Mental Math:
- 83.33% = 5/6. 5/6 of 540 = 450.
- 16.66% = 1/6. 1/6 of 300 = 50.
- 450 - 50 = 400.
Q5: If 45% of A = 36% of B, find (A+B)/(A-B).
Classic Method:
- 0.45A = 0.36B ⇒ A/B = 36/45 = 4/5. (4+5)/(4-5) = -9.
Mental Math:
- 5A = 4B ⇒ A=4, B=5. (4+5)/(4-5) = -9.
Q6: If 62.5% of A is equal to 87.5% of B, find B : A.
Classic Method:
- 0.625 A = 0.875 B ⇒ B / A = 0.625 / 0.875 = 5/7.
Mental Math:
- 62.5% = 5/8 and 87.5% = 7/8.
- 5/8 A = 7/8 B ⇒ 5A = 7B ⇒ B : A = 5 : 7.
Q7: A is 16.66% more than B. B is 37.5% less than C. A is what percent of C?
Classic Method:
- A = 1.1666 B, B = 0.625 C. A = 1.1666 × 0.625 C = 0.7291 C ⇒ 72.91%.
Mental Math:
- B = 5/8 C. A = 7/6 B = 7/6 × 5/8 C = 35/48 C = 72.91%.
Q8: Evaluate 58.33% of 480 + 37.5% of 640.
Classic Method:
- Calculate 58.33/100 × 480 = 280.
- Calculate 37.5/100 × 640 = 240.
- Sum = 280 + 240 = 520.
Mental Math:
- 58.33% = 1/2 + 1/12 = 7/12 ⇒ 7/12 × 480 = 280.
- 37.5% = 3/8 ⇒ 3/8 × 640 = 240. Total = 520.
Q9: Evaluate 87.5% of 800 + 41.66% of 240.
Classic Method:
- 0.875 × 800 = 700.
- 0.4166 × 240 = 100. Total = 800.
Mental Math:
- 87.5% = 7/8 ⇒ 7/8 × 800 = 700.
- 41.66% = 1/2 - 1/12 = 5/12 ⇒ 5/12 of 240 = 100. Total = 800.
Q10: Evaluate 90.9% of 220 + 8.33% of 288 + 14.28% of 350.
Classic Method:
- 0.909 × 220 = 200.
- 0.0833 × 288 = 24.
- 0.1428 × 350 = 50. Sum = 274.
Mental Math:
- 90.9% = 10/11 ⇒ 10/11 × 220 = 200.
- 8.33% = 1/12 ⇒ 1/12 × 288 = 24.
- 14.28% = 1/7 ⇒ 1/7 × 350 = 50. Sum = 274.
Type 2: Successive Percentage Changes (Area/Volume)

Concept Explanation:

Successive changes occur when a value increases or decreases, and the next change applies to the newly updated value. For 2D shapes (Area ∝ x2) and 3D shapes (Volume ∝ x3), changes chain successively. Use `A + B + AB/100` or multiplier factors.

Classic Method
If radius of circle is increased by 20%, find area change:
Use formula: Net change = A + B + (AB / 100)
- Substitute: 20 + 20 + (20 × 20 / 100)
- Net change = 40 + 4 = 44%.
Mental Math Shortcut
If radius of circle is increased by 20%, find area change:
- 20% increase means a scale factor of 6/5.
- Since Area is proportional to radius squared (r2), Area changes by: (6/5)2 = 36 / 25.
- Net change = 11 units increase over base of 25.
- Mentally: 11/25 = 44% (since 1/25 = 4%).
Pedagogical Practice Exercises:
Q1: If radius of circle increases by 10%, find change in area.
Classic Method:
- 10 + 10 + (10*10)/100 = 21% increase.
Mental Math:
- factor = 1.1. Squared = 1.21 ⇒ 21% increase.
Q2: A rectangle's length is increased by 30% and breadth decreased by 20%. Area change?
Classic Method:
- 30 - 20 - (30*20)/100 = 4% increase.
Mental Math:
- 1.3 × 0.8 = 1.04 ⇒ 4% increase.
Q3: Side of an equilateral triangle increases by 10%. Area change?
Classic Method:
- 10 + 10 + 1 = 21% increase.
Mental Math:
- Area ∝ s^2. 1.1^2 = 1.21 ⇒ 21% increase.
Q4: A circle's radius decreases by 30%. Find change in its circumference.
Classic Method:
- Circumference ∝ r. Circumference decreases by 30%.
Mental Math:
- Linear scale factor is directly proportional: 30% decrease.
Q5: If length of rectangle increases by 15% and breadth increases by 10%. Area change?
Classic Method:
- 15 + 10 + 1.5 = 26.5% increase.
Mental Math:
- 1.15 × 1.10 = 1.265 ⇒ 26.5% increase.
Q6: Side of a cube increases by 20%. Surface area change?
Classic Method:
- Area ∝ s^2. (1.2)^2 = 1.44 ⇒ 44% increase.
Mental Math:
- 20% = 1/5 ⇒ 6/5 factor. Area ratio = 36/25 ⇒ 11/25 = 44% increase.
Q7: If length of a cuboid increases by 10%, breadth by 20% and height decreases by 30%, what is net volume change?
Classic Method:
- V_new = 1.10 × 1.20 × 0.70 = 0.924 ⇒ 7.6% decrease.
Mental Math:
- Ratios: 11/10 × 6/5 × 7/10 = 462/500 = 924/1000 ⇒ 7.6% decrease.
Q8: If radius of cylinder increases by 20% and height decreases by 15%, find change in volume.
Classic Method:
- V ∝ r^2 * h. (1.2)^2 × 0.85 = 1.224 ⇒ 22.4% increase.
Mental Math:
- 6/5 × 6/5 × 17/20 = 612/500 = 1.224 ⇒ 22.4% increase.
Q9: Radius of a cone decreases by 20% and height increases by 50%. Volume change?
Classic Method:
- V ∝ r^2 * h. (0.8)^2 × 1.5 = 0.96 ⇒ 4% decrease.
Mental Math:
- 4/5 × 4/5 × 3/2 = 48/50 = 96% ⇒ 4% decrease.
Q10: If the radius of a sphere increases by 50%, what is the percentage change in its volume?
Classic Method:
- V ∝ r^3. (1.5)^3 = 3.375. Net increase = 237.5%.
- 50% = +1/2 ⇒ factor 3/2. Cube: 27/8 ⇒ (27-8)/8 = 19/8 = 237.5%.
Type 3: Product-Constancy Rule

Concept Explanation:

When the product of two variables is constant (e.g., `Price * Consumption = Expenditure`), any percentage change in one requires a counter-change in the other to maintain constancy. If one increases by `x/y`, the other must decrease by `x/(x+y)` (Abrule).

Classic Method
Sugar price rises by 25%. Consumption cut %:
Formula: Decrease % = [P / (100 + P)] × 100
- Substitute: [25 / 125] × 100
- Net decrease = 1/5 × 100 = 20%.
Mental Math Shortcut
Sugar price rises by 25%. Consumption cut %:
- 25% increase is a fraction increase of +1/4.
- By the constancy rule, the counteraction must be a decrease of -1 / (4 + 1) = -1/5.
- Mentally translate: -1/5 = 20% decrease. Solved in 2 seconds!
Pedagogical Practice Exercises:
Q1: Breadth of a rectangle decreases by 20%. To keep area constant, length must increase by?
Classic Method:
- 0.8 × L = 1 ⇒ L = 1.25 ⇒ 25% increase.
Mental Math:
- Decrease = -1/5 ⇒ Increase = +1/(5-1) = +1/4 = 25%.
Q2: Speed of a train is increased by 25%. Time taken to travel the same distance decreases by?
Classic Method:
- S_new = 1.25S ⇒ T_new = T/1.25 = 0.8T ⇒ 20% decrease.
Mental Math:
- Speed increases by +1/4 ⇒ Time decreases by -1/(4+1) = -1/5 = 20%.
Q3: Tax on an article decreases by 10% and consumption increases by 10%. Revenue effect?
Classic Method:
- 0.90 × 1.10 = 0.99 ⇒ 1% decrease.
Mental Math:
- Loss of x^2/100 % = 10^2/100 = 1% decrease.
Q4: Cinema ticket price increases by 25%. Sales decrease by 10%. Revenue change?
Classic Method:
- 1.25 × 0.9 = 1.125 ⇒ 12.5% increase.
- 5/4 × 9/10 = 45/40 = 9/8 ⇒ 1/8 = 12.5% increase.
Q5: Tax on salary increases by 15%. Net income decreases by? (Assuming tax rate is 20%).
Classic Method:
- Old Tax = 20%, Net = 80%. New Tax = 23%, Net = 77%. Decrease = 3/80 = 3.75%.
- Net decrease = Old Tax% × Tax Increase% = 20% × 15% = 3%. (Percent change on Net base = 3/80 = 3.75%).
Q6: A man's working hours per day increased by 15% and his hourly wage increased by 20%. Salary change?
Classic Method:
- 1.15 × 1.20 = 1.38 ⇒ 38% increase.
Mental Math:
- Successive: 15 + 20 + (15*20)/100 = 38% increase.
Q7: Price of entry tickets decreases by 30%. Attendance increases by 40%. Revenue change?
Classic Method:
- 0.70 × 1.40 = 0.98 ⇒ 2% decrease.
Mental Math:
- Successive: -30 + 40 - 12 = -2% (2% decrease).
Q8: Price of rice increases by 30%. A family reduces consumption so expenditure increases by only 10%. Find percentage reduction in consumption.
Classic Method:
- Exp = Price × Cons ⇒ 1.10 = 1.30 × Cons ⇒ Cons = 11/13 ⇒ 2/13 = 15.38%.
Mental Math:
- Base exp 100. Price hike makes it 130. New exp limit 110. Reduce by 20/130 = 2/13 = 15.38%.
Q9: Price of sugar is reduced by 25%. Now a person gets 10 kg more sugar for $600. Find reduced price per kg.
Classic Method:
- Savings = 25% of 600 = $150. Reduced price = 150/10 = $15/kg.
Mental Math:
- Reduced price = (Total Expense × reduction%) / Quantity = (600 × 0.25) / 10 = $15/kg.
Q10: Due to a 20% reduction in sugar price, a man can buy 5 kg more for $320. Find original price per kg.
Classic Method:
- Savings = 20% of 320 = $64. This buys 5 kg ⇒ New price = 64/5 = $12.8/kg. Original price = 12.8 / 0.8 = $16/kg.
- Price ratio = 4:5 ⇒ Cons. ratio = 5:4. Cons. increase = 1 unit = 5 kg.
- Original cons = 20 kg. Original Price = 320/20 = $16/kg.
Type 4: Income, Expenditure & Savings

Concept Explanation:

These problems rely on the basic identity: `Income = Expenditure + Savings`. When multi-variable increases occur, solve using weighted average deviation equations (`I_part * I_change = E_part * E_change + S_part * S_change`) to avoid heavy decimal calculations.

Classic Method
Man spends 75%. Income increases 20%, Exp increases 10%. Sav change:
Assume Income = 100, Exp = 75, Savings = 25.
- New Income = 120.
- New Exp = 75 × 1.1 = 82.5.
- New Savings = 120 - 82.5 = 37.5.
- Savings increase = (12.5 / 25) × 100 = 50%.
Mental Math Shortcut
Man spends 75%. Income increases 20%, Exp increases 10%. Sav change:
- Ratio of Expenditure : Savings = 75 : 25 = 3 : 1.
- Write the weighted average deviation balance equation:
(3 × 10% Exp) + (1 × S% Savings) = (4 × 20% Income)
30% + S% = 80% ⇒ S = 50%.
Pedagogical Practice Exercises:
Q1: B spends 70% of salary. Savings increase by 10%, salary constant. Expenditure change?
Classic Method:
- Income = 100. Exp = 70, Sav = 30. New Sav = 33 ⇒ New Exp = 67. Change = -3/70 = 4.28% decrease.
Mental Math:
- Exp:Sav = 7:3. 7 × E% + 3 × 10% = 0 ⇒ 7E = -30% ⇒ E = -4.28%.
Q2: A spends 90% of income. If income increases by 20% and savings increase by 80%, exp increases by?
Classic Method:
- Income = 100, Exp = 90, Sav = 10. New Income = 120, New Sav = 18 ⇒ Exp = 102. Change = 12/90 = 13.33% increase.
Mental Math:
- Ratio Exp:Sav = 9:1. 9 × E% + 1 × 80% = 10 × 20% ⇒ 9E = 120% ⇒ E = 13.33%.
Q3: A spends 75% of his income. If income increases by 20% and savings increase by 20%, expenditure increases by?
Classic Method:
- Income = 100, Exp = 75, Sav = 25. New Income = 120, New Sav = 30 ⇒ Exp = 90 ⇒ 20% increase.
Mental Math:
- If income and savings increase by same %, expenditure must also increase by that same %: 20%.
Q4: Salary of X is 40% more than Y. If X spends 70% and Y spends 80%, find ratio of their savings.
Classic Method:
- Let Y = 100 ⇒ X = 140. Y_sav = 20. X_sav = 140 × 0.3 = 42. Ratio = 42 : 20 = 21 : 10.
Mental Math:
- X_sav : Y_sav = (1.4 × 30%) : (1 × 20%) = 42 : 20 = 21 : 10.
Q5: C spends 85% of income. Salary increases by 10%. If he saves same absolute amount, find % increase in exp.
Classic Method:
- Income = 100. Exp = 85. Sav = 15. New income = 110, New Sav = 15 ⇒ New Exp = 95. Change = 10/85 = 11.76% increase.
- Savings increase% = 0. Ratio Exp:Sav = 17:3. 17 × E% + 3 × 0% = 20 × 10% ⇒ E = 200/17 = 11.76%.
Q6: Salary ratio of A & B is 8 : 9. If salary of A increases by 50% and B's salary decreases by 25%, find new ratio.
Classic Method:
- A_new = 8 × 1.5 = 12. B_new = 9 × 0.75 = 6.75 ⇒ Ratio = 12 : 6.75 = 16 : 9.
Mental Math:
- New ratio = (8 × 3/2) : (9 × 3/4) = 12 : 27/4 = 16 : 9.
Q7: A man's expenditure and savings are in the ratio 5 : 3. If his income increases by 12% and expenditure by 15%, by how much does savings increase?
Classic Method:
- Income = 800, Exp = 500, Sav = 300. New Income = 896, New Exp = 575 ⇒ New Sav = 321. Change = 21/300 = 7%.
Mental Math:
- Deviation: 5 × 15% + 3 × S% = 8 × 12% ⇒ 75 + 3S = 96 ⇒ S = 7%.
Q8: A spent 80% of his income. If his income increases by 12% and savings decrease by 10%, find the percentage increase in expenditure.
Classic Method:
- Income = 100, Exp = 80, Sav = 20. New Income = 112, New Sav = 18 ⇒ New Exp = 94. Change = 14/80 = 17.5%.
- Exp:Sav = 4:1. 4 × E% + 1 × (-10%) = 5 × 12% ⇒ 4E - 10 = 60 ⇒ E = 17.5%.
Q9: A spends 65% of his income. If income increases by 20.1% and expenditure increases by 20%, savings increase by?
Classic Method:
- 100 × 1.201 - 65 × 1.20 = 42.1. Savings change = (42.1-35)/35 = 20.28%.
- Ratio Exp:Sav = 13:7. Balance: 13 × 20% + 7 × S% = 20 × 20.1% ⇒ 260 + 7S = 402 ⇒ S = 20.28%.
Q10: Expenditure of A is 120% of B's expenditure. Savings of A is 80% of B's savings. If B spends 60% of his income, A's income is what % of B's?
Classic Method:
- Let B's Exp = 60, B's Sav = 40. B's Income = 100.
- A's Exp = 60 × 1.20 = 72. A's Sav = 40 × 0.80 = 32. A's Income = 72 + 32 = 104. A/B = 104%.
Mental Math:
- A_income = 1.2 × 60% + 0.8 × 40% = 72% + 32% = 104% of B's income.
Type 5: Election Problems

Concept Explanation:

Election equations relate registered voters, turnout%, valid/invalid votes, and winning margins. Chain backward using fractions: `Total * Turnout% * Valid% * Margin% = margin_votes` to find the total voter base quickly.

Classic Method
Winner got 55% valid. 20% invalid. Winner wins by 800. Total votes:
Let total votes be x.
- Valid votes = 0.80x.
- Winner gets 0.55 of valid, loser gets 0.45 of valid.
- Difference = 10% of valid votes = 800.
- 0.10 × (0.80x) = 800 ⇒ 0.08x = 800 ⇒ x = 10,000.
Mental Math Shortcut
Winner got 55% valid. 20% invalid. Winner wins by 800. Total votes:
- Winner gets 55% ⇒ Loser gets 45%. Margin is 10% (= 1/10).
- Invalid is 20% ⇒ Valid is 80% (= 4/5).
- Mentally chain backward:
Total × 4/5 × 1/10 = 800 ⇒ Total × 4/50 = 800
- 800 / 4 = 200, and 200 × 50 = 10,000.
Pedagogical Practice Exercises:
Q1: In a two-candidate election, winner gets 62% of votes and wins by 1440 votes. Find total votes.
Classic Method:
- 62% - 38% = 24%. 24% of x = 1440 ⇒ x = 6000.
Mental Math:
- Margin = 24% = 6/25. 6 units = 1440 ⇒ 1 unit = 240. Total = 25 × 240 = 6000.
Q2: Winning margin is 10% of total votes. Winner gets 55% votes. Find loser's vote share.
Classic Method:
- Winner = 55%. Loser = 100 - 55 = 45%. Margin = 10% (verified). Loser's share = 45%.
Mental Math:
- Winner (55) - Loser (L) = 10 ⇒ L = 45%.
Q3: 10% votes were invalid. Candidate A got 60% of valid votes and won by 720 votes. Total votes?
Classic Method:
- Valid = 0.9x. Winner margin = 20% of valid = 0.18x = 720 ⇒ x = 4000.
Mental Math:
- Total × 9/10 × 1/5 = 720 ⇒ Total × 9/50 = 720 ⇒ Total = 4000.
Q4: In a three-candidate election, A got 40%, B got 36% and C got the rest. C got 1200 votes. What was winner's margin over nearest rival?
- C = 100 - 40 - 36 = 24%. 24% of x = 1200 ⇒ Total = 5000. Margin A - B = 4% of 5000 = 200.
- C = 24% (1200 votes). A - B margin = 40% - 36% = 4%. Ratio 24% : 4% = 6 : 1. If 6 units = 1200, 1 unit = 200.
Q5: In a voter list, 10% did not vote. A won by 300 votes against B. If A got 52% of total voters on list, find total list.
- Total = x. Voted = 0.9x. Winner A = 0.52x. Loser B = 0.9x - 0.52x = 0.38x. Margin = 0.14x = 300 ⇒ x = 2142.
- Margin = 52% - (90% - 52%) = 14%. 14% of total = 300 ⇒ Total = 2142.
Q6: In an election, winner gets 53% of valid votes. 15% votes were invalid. If total votes cast was 12,000, find valid votes got by loser.
- Valid = 12000 × 0.85 = 10200. Loser gets 47% of 10200 = 4794.
- Loser gets 47% of 85% of 12000 = 120 × 85 × 0.47 = 4794.
Q7: In an election, 8% of voters did not vote. There were only 2 candidates. Winner secured 48% of total votes on list and defeated rival by 1100 votes. Find total voters.
- Total = x. Voted = 0.92x. Loser = 0.92x - 0.48x = 0.44x. Margin = 0.04x = 1100 ⇒ x = 27,500.
- Margin = Winner (48%) - Loser (92 - 48 = 44%) = 4% of total. 4% = 1100 ⇒ Total = 27,500.
Q8: In an election, 20% voters did not vote. 120 votes were invalid. Winner got 41% of total votes on the voter list and won by 200 votes. Total votes on list?
- Total = x. Voted = 0.8x. Valid = 0.8x - 120. Winner A = 0.41x. Loser B = 0.39x - 120. Margin = 0.02x + 120 = 200 ⇒ 0.02x = 80 ⇒ x = 4000.
- Winner margin % = 2% of total. 2% of total - 120 invalid = 200 ⇒ 2% of total = 320 ⇒ Total = 16,000 (Adjusted logic details: check variable baseline: 80 - 120 votes = 200 margin ⇒ Total = 4000).
Q9: In an election, candidate A got 30% of total votes. Candidate B got 40% of the rest. Winner C won by 840 votes over B. Find total votes.
- Total = x. A = 0.3x. Remainder = 0.7x. B = 0.28x. C = 0.42x. Margin C - B = 0.14x = 840 ⇒ x = 6000.
- A = 30%. Remainder = 70%. B = 40% of 70% = 28%. C = 60% of 70% = 42%. Diff C - B = 14%. 14% = 840 ⇒ 100% = 6000.
Q10: In an election, 10% of voters did not cast their votes. 10% of cast votes were declared invalid. Winner got 54% of valid votes and won by 1620 votes. Find the total number of voters.
- Let Total = T. Cast = 0.9T. Valid = 0.81T. Winner margin = 8% of valid ⇒ 0.08 × 0.81T = 1620 ⇒ T = 25,000.
- Winner 54%, Loser 46% ⇒ Margin = 8% = 2/25. Turnout = 9/10, Valid = 9/10.
- Chain: T × 9/10 × 9/10 × 2/25 = 1620 ⇒ T × 162/2500 = 1620 ⇒ T = 25,000.
Type 6: Population & Depreciation

Concept Explanation:

These calculations represent geometric compounding. Population growth adds `P * (1 + R/100)^n`, while machinery depreciation subtracts `P * (1 - R/100)^n`. Master the multiplier methods for clean execution.

Classic Method
Pop increases 10% Year 1, decreases 10% Year 2. Present is 9900. Original pop:
Formula: P = P_old × (1 + R1/100) × (1 - R2/100)
- 9900 = P_old × (110/100) × (90/100)
- 9900 = P_old × 1.1 × 0.9
- 9900 = P_old × 0.99 ⇒ P_old = 9900 / 0.99 = 10,000.
Mental Math Shortcut
Pop increases 10% Year 1, decreases 10% Year 2. Present is 9900. Original pop:
- An increase of 10% and subsequent decrease of 10% always results in a net decrease of: (10)2 / 100 = 1% decrease.
- Thus, 99% of the original population = 9900.
- Mentally solve: 1% is 100, so 100% must be 10,000.
Pedagogical Practice Exercises:
Q1: Depreciation rate is 15%. If present is $17,000, value in 1 year?
Classic Method:
- 17000 × 0.85 = 14,450.
Mental Math:
- 17000 - 1700 (10%) - 850 (5%) = 14,450.
Q2: Population of a village is 5000. It increases by 10% in Year 1, decreases by 20% in Year 2. Final population?
Classic Method:
- 5000 × 1.1 × 0.8 = 4400.
Mental Math:
- growth = +10 - 20 - 2 = -12% ⇒ 5000 - 600 = 4400.
Q3: Depreciation of car is 10% in Year 1 and 10% in Year 2. Net depreciation?
Classic Method:
- 10 + 10 - 1 = 19% decrease.
Mental Math:
- 0.9 × 0.9 = 0.81 ⇒ 19% depreciation.
Q4: Population increases by 10% in Year 1 and 20% in Year 2. Find net growth percentage.
Classic Method:
- 10 + 20 + 2 = 32% increase.
Mental Math:
- 1.1 × 1.2 = 1.32 ⇒ 32% growth.
Q5: Value of a bike depreciates by 10% per year. If current value is $121,500, find value 2 years ago.
Classic Method:
- P * 0.9 * 0.9 = 121500 ⇒ 0.81P = 121500 ⇒ P = 150,000.
Mental Math:
- 81 parts = 121500 ⇒ 1 part = 1500 ⇒ Total (100 parts) = 150,000.
Q6: Town population increases by 5% annually. If present is 9261, find population 3 years ago.

Type 7: Exam Marks / Pass-Fail

Concept Explanation:

These systems evaluate differences on linear lines. Solve by establishing relations between passing margins and total percentages: `Percentage Difference = Mark Difference` to obtain the maximum mark scale.

Classic Method
Gets 30% fails by 10. Gets 40% gets 15 above. Max marks:
Let maximum marks be M.
- Passing marks = 0.30M + 10.
- Passing marks = 0.40M - 15.
- Equate: 0.30M + 10 = 0.40M - 15 ⇒ 0.10M = 25 ⇒ M = 250.
Mental Math Shortcut
Gets 30% fails by 10. Gets 40% gets 15 above. Max marks:
- Percentage gap between candidates = 40% - 30% = 10%.
- Total marks gap between candidates = 15 (above pass) - (-10 below pass) = 25 marks.
- Therefore, 10% of total marks = 25.
- Instantly multiply by 10 to get maximum marks: 250.

Type 8: Venn Diagram Problems

Concept Explanation:

Venn diagrams resolve overlapping groups. For 2 sets: `Total = Set_A + Set_B - Both + Neither`. The excess over the sum of separate passed groups represents the double intersection overlap.

Classic Method
70% pass English, 80% pass Maths, 10% fail both. 144 pass both. Total:
- Students who passed in at least one subject = 100% - 10% (failed in both) = 90%.
- Formula: P(A U B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A n B)
- 90% = 70% + 80% - Both% ⇒ Both% = 150% - 90% = 60%.
- 60% of total = 144 ⇒ Total = 144 / 0.60 = 240.
Mental Math Shortcut
70% pass English, 80% pass Maths, 10% fail both. 144 pass both. Total:
- Total passed = 90%.
- Add separate pass percents: 70% + 80% = 150%.
- The excess over total passed is the intersection: 150% - 90% = 60%.
- Since 60% = 3/5, then 3 units = 144 ⇒ 1 unit = 48.
- Total (5 units) = 48 × 5 = 240.

Type 9: Salary / Expenditure Tree Fractions

Concept Explanation:

Tree allocations occur when successive percentages apply to the remaining balance. Chain remainder fractions together: `Salary * (1 - r1) * (1 - r2) * (1 - r3) = final_savings` to find the initial value easily.

Classic Method
Spends 20% on rent, 50% of remaining on food, saves $6000. Salary:
Let salary be S.
- Rent = 0.20S. Remaining = 0.80S.
- Food = 50% of 0.80S = 0.40S.
- Left savings = 0.80S - 0.40S = 0.40S.
- 0.40S = 6000 ⇒ S = 6000 / 0.40 = 15,000.
Mental Math Shortcut
Spends 20% on rent, 50% of remaining on food, saves $6000. Salary:
- Spend 20% (1/5) ⇒ Remainder factor is 4/5.
- Spend 50% (1/2) ⇒ Remainder factor is 1/2.
- Write the direct chain equation in your head:
Salary × 4/5 × 1/2 = 6000 ⇒ Salary × 2/5 = 6000
- 2 units = 6000 ⇒ 1 unit = 3000.
- Total (5 units) = 3000 × 5 = 15,000.
