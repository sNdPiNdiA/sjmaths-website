Key Concepts & Worked Archetypes

Concept 1

Concept 1: Systems of Quadratic and Linear Equations

A system containing a linear equation and a quadratic equation can be solved by substitution and analyzed by examining the discriminant of the resulting quadratic equation.

  • If a line intersects a parabola, the system yields $0$, $1$, or $2$ real solutions.
  • Substitute the linear expression for $y$ from $y = mx + b$ into the quadratic equation $y = ax^2 + bx + c$.
  • Set the resulting quadratic equation to zero in the form $Ax^2 + Bx + C = 0$.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Equate the two expressions, collect all terms on one side, and apply the quadratic formula or factor.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Type both equations directly into Desmos and click the intersection points to instantly read off the coordinate pairs.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: Which of the following is the $x$-coordinate of a solution to the system: $y = 2x^2 - 3x + 1$ and $y = x + 1$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Set the expressions for $y$ equal to each other: $2x^2 - 3x + 1 = x + 1$
Step 2
Subtract $x$ and $1$ from both sides: $2x^2 - 4x = 0$
Step 3
Factor out the greatest common factor: $2x(x - 2) = 0$
Step 4
Solve for $x$ to get the solutions: $x = 0$ and $x = 2$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Enter $y = 2x^2 - 3x + 1$ and $y = x + 1$ into Desmos.
Step 2
Click the intersection points on the graph.
Step 3
Read the $x$-coordinates: $0$ and $2$.
Concept 2

Concept 2: Rational Equations & Domain Restrictions

Rational equations contain algebraic fractions where variables appear in the denominator, requiring careful attention to excluded values that make denominators zero.

  • Always identify domain restrictions before clearing denominators by setting each denominator unequal to $0$.
  • Clear denominators by multiplying the entire equation by the least common denominator (LCD).
  • Check all derived solutions against the initial domain restrictions to eliminate extraneous roots.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Factor denominators, find the LCD, multiply across all terms, solve the resulting polynomial equation, and check for extraneous solutions.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Graph both sides of the equation as separate functions $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ in Desmos and find the $x$-coordinate of their intersection.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: What is the solution to the equation $\frac{3}{x-2} + \frac{1}{x} = \frac{4}{x-2}$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify domain restrictions: $x \neq 2$ and $x \neq 0$
Step 2
Subtract $\frac{3}{x-2}$ from both sides: $\frac{1}{x} = \frac{1}{x-2}$
Step 3
Cross-multiply to solve for $x$: $1(x - 2) = 1(x)$
Step 4
Simplify the equation: $x - 2 = x \implies -2 = 0$, which yields no solution.
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Type $y = \frac{3}{x-2} + \frac{1}{x}$ as line 1.
Step 2
Type $y = \frac{4}{x-2}$ as line 2.
Step 3
Observe parallel lines or lack of intersection to determine no solution.
Concept 3

Concept 3: Radical Equations & Extraneous Solutions

Radical equations contain variables under a root sign, and solving them typically requires isolating the radical and raising both sides to a power.

  • Isolate the radical expression on one side of the equation before applying powers.
  • Square (or cube) both sides to eliminate the radical symbol: $(\sqrt{f(x)})^2 = (g(x))^2$.
  • Always substitute final answers back into the original radical equation to verify validity.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Isolate the radical, square both sides, solve the resulting polynomial, and plug answers back in to reject extraneous solutions.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Graph the left side as $f(x)$ and the right side as $g(x)$ in Desmos. The valid intersection is the true solution; extraneous roots will not appear.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: What is the real solution to the equation $\sqrt{x+2} + 4 = x$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Isolate the radical term: $\sqrt{x+2} = x - 4$
Step 2
Square both sides: $x + 2 = (x - 4)^2$
Step 3
Expand the right side: $x + 2 = x^2 - 8x + 16$
Step 4
Set the quadratic to zero: $x^2 - 9x + 14 = 0$
Step 5
Factor and solve: $(x - 7)(x - 2) = 0 \implies x = 7$ or $x = 2$ (Extraneous)
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Enter $y = \sqrt{x+2} + 4$ into Desmos.
Step 2
Enter $y = x$ into Desmos.
Step 3
Click the single intersection point to find $x = 7$.
Concept 4

Concept 4: Exponential & Logarithmic Nonlinear Forms

Equations where the variable appears in the exponent or argument of a logarithm require rewriting using inverse operations and logarithmic properties.

  • Definition of logarithms: If $b^y = x$, then $\log_b(x) = y$.
  • Power property of logs: $\log(a^b) = b \cdot \log(a)$.
  • Common base rule: If $b^x = b^y$, then $x = y$ (for $b > 0, b \neq 1$).
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Take the logarithm of both sides for exponential equations, or convert logarithmic equations into exponential form.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Define functions in Desmos and use the intersection feature or numerical evaluation to find the exact root.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: If $2^{3x-1} = 32$, what is the value of $x$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Rewrite $32$ with base $2$: $32 = 2^5$
Step 2
Rewrite the equation with matching bases: $2^{3x-1} = 2^5$
Step 3
Equate the exponents: $3x - 1 = 5$
Step 4
Solve for $x$: $3x = 6 \implies x = 2$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Enter $y = 2^{3x-1}$ into Desmos.
Step 2
Enter $y = 32$ into Desmos.
Step 3
Click the intersection to read $x = 2$.

What is the positive solution to the equation $x^2 - 5x - 14 = 0$?

Step 3
Set each factor equal to zero: $x - 7 = 0$ or $x + 2 = 0$
Step 4
Solve for $x$: $x = 7$ or $x = -2$. The positive solution is $7$.

Step 2
Look at the x-intercepts on the graph.
Step 3
The x-intercepts are at x = -2 and x = 7, so the positive solution is 7.
Question 2 Quadratic Equation Solving
Easy

If $2x^2 + 8x - 10 = 0$, what is the sum of all possible values of $x$?

Hint: You can either use Vieta's formulas for a quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, where the sum of roots is $-b/a$, or factor the equation.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Write the given equation: $2x^2 + 8x - 10 = 0$
Step 2
Divide the entire equation by $2$ to simplify: $x^2 + 4x - 5 = 0$
Step 3
Factor the simplified equation: $(x + 5)(x - 1) = 0$
Step 4
Find the roots: $x = -5$ and $x = 1$. The sum is $-5 + 1 = -4$.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
Use Vieta's formula for the sum of roots, which is $-\frac{b}{a}$.
Step 2
Identify $a = 2$ and $b = 8$ from $2x^2 + 8x - 10 = 0$.
Step 3
Calculate $-\frac{8}{2} = -4$.
Question 3 Quadratic Equation Solving
Medium

The equation $3x^2 - kx + 12 = 0$, where $k$ is a constant, has exactly one real solution. What is a possible value of $k$?

Hint: An equation has exactly one real solution when its discriminant ($b^2 - 4ac$) is equal to zero.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Identify the coefficients $a$, $b$, and $c$ in the quadratic equation $3x^2 - kx + 12 = 0$: $a = 3$, $b = -k$, $c = 12$
Step 2
Set the discriminant equal to zero for one real solution: $b^2 - 4ac = 0$
Step 3
Substitute the coefficients into the discriminant formula: $(-k)^2 - 4(3)(12) = 0$
Step 4
Simplify and solve for $k$: $k^2 - 144 = 0 \implies k^2 = 144 \implies k = 12$ or $k = -12$.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
Test the options for $k$ in Desmos by graphing $y = 3x^2 - kx + 12$.
Step 2
Adjust $k$ until the vertex of the parabola touches the x-axis exactly once.
Step 3
For $k = 12$, the vertex is at $(2, 0)$, confirming it gives one real solution.

Revision, Traps & Desmos Syntax

Quadratic Formula

$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$

Used to find roots of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ when factoring fails.

Discriminant

$D = b^2 - 4ac$

Determines number of real intersections: $D > 0$ (2), $D = 0$ (1), $D < 0$ (0).

Logarithmic Rule of Powers

$\log_b(x^k) = k \cdot \log_b(x)$

Brings exponents out of logarithmic arguments for easier algebraic isolation.

🚨 Top SAT Traps & Misconceptions

⚠️ SAT Trap: Forgetting Extraneous Solutions
Squaring both sides of a radical equation or multiplying by a variable denominator can introduce false roots that fail the check in the original equation.
⚠️ SAT Trap: Domain Blindness in Rational Expressions
Canceling a factor like $(x-2)$ from numerator and denominator without noting that $x = 2$ is an excluded value in the original domain.

Explanation:
Step 1
For an involution $f(f(x)) = x$ with a fractional linear transformation, we must have $d = -a$.
Step 2
Substitute $d = -a$ into the general form $f(x) = \frac{ax + b}{cx - a}$.
Step 3
Use the given condition $f(2) = 5$: $\frac{2a + b}{2c - a} = 5 \implies 2a + b = 10c - 5a \implies 7a + b = 10c$.
Step 4
