Key Concepts & Worked Archetypes

Concept 1

Concept 1: Product, Quotient, and Power Rules

Exponents represent repeated multiplication, and their manipulation relies on combining terms with common bases through addition, subtraction, or multiplication of exponents.

  • Product Rule: $x^a \cdot x^b = x^{a+b}$
  • Quotient Rule: $\frac{x^a}{x^b} = x^{a-b}$
  • Power Rule: $(x^a)^b = x^{a \cdot b}$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Rewrite all terms to share a common base, apply exponent rules sequentially, and equate the exponents if the bases are equal.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use the Desmos expression evaluator to test equivalent expressions or substitute numerical values for variables to instantly eliminate wrong choices.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: If $2^{3x} \cdot 4^{x} = 32$, what is the value of $x$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Express all terms with a common base of 2.
Step 2
$2^{3x} \cdot (2^2)^x = 2^5$
Step 3
$2^{3x} \cdot 2^{2x} = 2^5$
Step 4
$2^{5x} = 2^5$
Step 5
$5x = 5 \implies x = 1$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Type y = 2^{3x} * 4^x into Desmos.
Step 2
Type y = 32 on the next line.
Step 3
Find the intersection point where x = 1.
Concept 2

Concept 2: Rational Exponents and Radicals

Fractional exponents represent roots, where the numerator is the power and the denominator is the index of the radical.

  • Fractional Exponent Rule: $x^{\frac{m}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{x^m} = (\sqrt[n]{x})^m$
  • Negative Exponent Rule: $x^{-a} = \frac{1}{x^a}$
  • Zero Exponent Rule: $x^0 = 1$ (for $x \neq 0$)
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Convert radical expressions to fractional exponents before combining or simplifying terms algebraically.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Plug in a prime number for the base (e.g., let $x = 8$) into both the question and the answer choices to find an exact numerical match.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Concept 3

Concept 3: Exponential Equations with Variable Bases

Equations where variables appear in the base require isolating the base and applying reciprocal powers to solve.

  • Even roots yield both positive and negative solutions: $x^{2n} = c \implies x = \pm \sqrt[2n]{c}$
  • Odd roots yield a single unique solution: $x^{2n+1} = c \implies x = \sqrt[2n+1]{c}$
  • Reciprocal Power Property: $x^{\frac{a}{b}} = c \implies x = c^{\frac{b}{a}}$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Isolate the term with the fractional exponent, then raise both sides of the equation to the reciprocal power.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Graph both sides of the equation as functions $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ in Desmos and click the intersection point.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: If $x^{\frac{3}{2}} = 27$, what is the value of $x$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify the given equation: $x^{\frac{3}{2}} = 27$
Step 2
Raise both sides to the reciprocal power $\frac{2}{3}$: $(x^{\frac{3}{2}})^{\frac{2}{3}} = 27^{\frac{2}{3}}$
Step 3
Simplify the left side: $x^1 = (\sqrt[3]{27})^2$
Step 4
Evaluate the right side: $x = (3)^2 = 9$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Type y = x^{\frac{3}{2}} into Desmos.
Step 2
Type y = 27 on the second line.
Step 3
Click the intersection to read the x-coordinate: 9.
Concept 4

Concept 4: Exponential Growth and Decay Modeling

Exponential word problems model real-world phenomena via growth factors and percentage changes over time intervals.

  • Growth Model: $P(t) = P_0(1 + r)^t$
  • Decay Model: $P(t) = P_0(1 - r)^t$
  • Compound Interest / General Base Form: $f(t) = a \cdot b^{\frac{t}{k}}$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Identify initial value $P_0$, growth/decay rate $r$, and time scaling factor $k$, then substitute into the standard formula.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Set up the function in Desmos, use a table to evaluate specific time intervals, or find intercepts rapidly.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: A population of bacteria triples every 4 hours. If the initial population is 500, which function represents the population $p$ after $t$ hours?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Use the general formula $P(t) = P_0 \cdot b^{\frac{t}{k}}$
Step 2
Substitute initial population $P_0 = 500$
Step 3
Substitute growth multiplier $b = 3$
Step 4
Substitute time period for tripling $k = 4$
Step 5
$P(t) = 500 \cdot 3^{\frac{t}{4}}$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Test t = 4 hours in the problem statement (population should be 500 * 3 = 1500).
Step 2
Substitute t = 4 into the options to check which yields 1500.

Which of the following expressions is equivalent to $(3x^2 y^3)^4$?

Multiply the exponents of $x$: $(x^2)^4 = x^{2 \cdot 4} = x^8$

Question 4 Equivalent Exponential Expressions
Medium

If $\frac{(2^3 x^y)^2}{2^4 x^2} = 2^a x^6$, what is the value of $y$?

Hint: Simplify the numerator first by distributing the exponent 2, then apply the quotient rule.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Expand the numerator: $(2^3 x^y)^2 = 2^6 x^{2y}$
Step 2
Write the full fraction: $\frac{2^6 x^{2y}}{2^4 x^2} = 2^{6-4} x^{2y-2} = 2^2 x^{2y-2}$
Step 3
Equate the exponent of $x$ to the right-hand side: $2y - 2 = 6$
Step 4
Solve for $y$: $2y = 8 \implies y = 4$.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1

Step 2
Test options for $y$ until the left-hand side matches $2^a x^6$ for some integer $a$.
Question 5 Equivalent Exponential Expressions
Hard

Which of the following is equivalent to $\frac{\sqrt{x^3 \sqrt{x}}}{x}$ for $x > 0$?

Hint: Convert all radicals to fractional exponents starting from the innermost radical outward.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Rewrite the inner radical: $\sqrt{x} = x^{\frac{1}{2}}$
Step 2
Combine terms inside the outer radical: $x^3 \cdot x^{\frac{1}{2}} = x^{\frac{7}{2}}$
Step 3
Apply the outer square root: $\left(x^{\frac{7}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}} = x^{\frac{7}{4}}$
Step 4
Divide by $x$: $\frac{x^{\frac{7}{4}}}{x^1} = x^{\frac{7}{4} - 1} = x^{\frac{3}{4}}$.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
Plug in a clean value for $x$, such as $x = 16$, into the original expression.
Step 2
Compute the numerical value: $\frac{\sqrt{16^3 \sqrt{16}}}{16} = \frac{\sqrt{4096 \cdot 4}}{16} = \frac{128}{16} = 8$.
Step 3
Test each option with $x = 16$ to see which evaluates to $8$ ($16^{3/4} = 8$).

Question 10 Exponential Equations & Solving for Variables
Hard

If $2^{2x} - 5(2^x) + 4 = 0$, what is the sum of all possible real values of $x$?

Hint: Use substitution: let $u = 2^x$, which transforms the equation into a quadratic in terms of $u$.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Let $u = 2^x$. The equation becomes $u^2 - 5u + 4 = 0$.
Step 2
Factor the quadratic: $(u - 1)(u - 4) = 0$.
Step 3
Solve for $u$: $u = 1$ or $u = 4$.
Step 4
Substitute back $2^x = 1 \implies x = 0$, and $2^x = 4 \implies x = 2$. Sum = $0 + 2 = 2$.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
Type $2^{2x} - 5(2^x) + 4 = 0$ into Desmos.
Step 2
Click on the roots on the graph to see $x = 0$ and $x = 2$.
Step 3
Add them together to get $2$.

Question 20 Structure of Exponential Expressions
Hard

Which of the following is equivalent to $\frac{2^{x+1} + 2^x}{2^{x-1} + 2^x}$?

Hint: Factor out $2^x$ from both the numerator and the denominator.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Factor $2^x$ out of the numerator: $2^x(2^1 + 1) = 2^x(2 + 1) = 3(2^x)$
Step 2
Factor $2^x$ out of the denominator: $2^x(2^{-1} + 1) = 2^x(\frac{1}{2} + 1) = \frac{3}{2}(2^x)$
Step 3
Divide the numerator by the denominator: $\frac{3(2^x)}{\frac{3}{2}(2^x)}$
Step 4
Cancel $2^x$ and simplify: $\frac{3}{\frac{3}{2}} = 2$.
