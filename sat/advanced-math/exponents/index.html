Key Concepts & Worked Archetypes

Concept 1

Concept 1: Product, Quotient, and Power Rules

Exponents represent repeated multiplication, and their manipulation relies on combining terms with common bases through addition, subtraction, or multiplication of exponents.

  • Product Rule: $x^a · x^b = xa+b$
  • Quotient Rule: $x^ax^b = xa-b$
  • Power Rule: $(x^a)^b = xa · b$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Rewrite all terms to share a common base, apply exponent rules sequentially, and equate the exponents if the bases are equal.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use the Desmos expression evaluator to test equivalent expressions or substitute numerical values for variables to instantly eliminate wrong choices.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: If $23x · 4x = 32$, what is the value of $x$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Express all terms with a common base of 2.
Step 2
$23x · (2^2)^x = 2^5$
Step 3
$23x · 22x = 2^5$
Step 4
$25x = 2^5$
Step 5
$5x = 5 \implies x = 1$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:

Type y = 23x * 4^x into Desmos.
Step 2
Type y = 32 on the next line.
Step 3
Find the intersection point where x = 1.
Concept 2

Concept 2: Rational Exponents and Radicals

Fractional exponents represent roots, where the numerator is the power and the denominator is the index of the radical.

  • Fractional Exponent Rule: $xmn = \sqrt[n]{x^m} = (\sqrt[n]{x})^m$
  • Negative Exponent Rule: $x-a = 1x^a$
  • Zero Exponent Rule: $x^0 = 1$ (for $x \neq 0$)
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Convert radical expressions to fractional exponents before combining or simplifying terms algebraically.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Plug in a prime number for the base (e.g., let $x = 8$) into both the question and the answer choices to find an exact numerical match.

Concept 3

Concept 3: Exponential Equations with Variable Bases

Equations where variables appear in the base require isolating the base and applying reciprocal powers to solve.

  • Even roots yield both positive and negative solutions: $x2n = c \implies x = ± \sqrt[2n]{c}$
  • Odd roots yield a single unique solution: $x2n+1 = c \implies x = \sqrt[2n+1]{c}$
  • Reciprocal Power Property: $xab = c \implies x = cba$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Isolate the term with the fractional exponent, then raise both sides of the equation to the reciprocal power.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Graph both sides of the equation as functions $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ in Desmos and click the intersection point.

Concept 4

Concept 4: Exponential Growth and Decay Modeling

Exponential word problems model real-world phenomena via growth factors and percentage changes over time intervals.

  • Growth Model: $P(t) = P_0(1 + r)^t$
  • Decay Model: $P(t) = P_0(1 - r)^t$
  • Compound Interest / General Base Form: $f(t) = a · btk$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Identify initial value $P_0$, growth/decay rate $r$, and time scaling factor $k$, then substitute into the standard formula.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Set up the function in Desmos, use a table to evaluate specific time intervals, or find intercepts rapidly.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Substitute t = 4 into the options to check which yields 1500.
