Key Concepts & Worked Archetypes

Concept 1

Concept 1: Quadratic Functions & Vertex Form

A quadratic function is a polynomial of degree 2, characterized by a parabolic graph with a maximum or minimum vertex.

  • Vertex form of a parabola is given by $f(x) = a(x - h)^2 + k$, where $(h, k)$ is the vertex.
  • If $a > 0$, the parabola opens upward and yields a minimum value.
  • If $a < 0$, the parabola opens downward and yields a maximum value.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Expand the quadratic or complete the square to identify the vertex coordinates and optimal values.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Type the quadratic equation directly into Desmos and click the vertex point to instantly read off $(h, k)$.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: What is the minimum value of the function $f(x) = 2x^2 - 12x + 19$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Factor out the leading coefficient from the x terms: $f(x) = 2(x^2 - 6x) + 19$
Step 2
Complete the square inside the parenthesis: $f(x) = 2(x^2 - 6x + 9 - 9) + 19$
Step 3
Simplify and distribute: $f(x) = 2(x - 3)^2 - 18 + 19$
Step 4
Write the final vertex form: $f(x) = 2(x - 3)^2 + 1$
Step 5
Identify the minimum value $k = 1$.
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Input $f(x) = 2x^2 - 12x + 19$ into Desmos.
Step 2
Click on the lowest point (vertex) of the plotted parabola.
Step 3
Read the y-coordinate directly as $1$.
Concept 2

Concept 2: Exponential Growth & Decay

Exponential functions model rapid growth or decay where the variable appears in the exponent.

  • Standard exponential form is $f(x) = a \cdot b^x$, where $a$ is the initial value and $b$ is the base multiplier.
  • When $b > 1$, the function represents exponential growth.
  • When $0 < b < 1$, the function represents exponential decay.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Set up equations using given coordinate points $(x, y)$ to solve for initial value $a$ and base $b$.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Create a regression table in Desmos by inputting data points as $(x_1, y_1)$ and run $y_1 \sim a \cdot b^{x_1}$.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: An insect population triples every 4 days. If the initial population is 50, which equation models the population $P$ after $d$ days?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify the base growth factor per time unit: $P(d) = a \cdot b^d$
Step 2
Incorporate the tripling rate over 4 days: $b^4 = 3 \implies b = 3^{\frac{1}{4}}$
Step 3
Substitute initial value $a = 50$: $P(d) = 50(3^{\frac{1}{4}})^d$
Step 4
Simplify the exponent: $P(d) = 50(3)^{\frac{d}{4}}$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Test $d = 0$, giving $P(0) = 50(3)^0 = 50$.
Step 2
Test $d = 4$, giving $P(4) = 50(3)^1 = 150$.
Step 3
Match against option formulas to verify the correct exponent denominator.
Concept 3

Concept 3: Polynomial Roots & Factored Form

Polynomial functions connect algebraic factors directly to x-intercepts and graphical behaviors.

  • If $(x - r)$ is a factor of polynomial $P(x)$, then $r$ is a root, zero, and x-intercept.
  • Even multiplicity roots cause the graph to touch and turn around the x-axis.
  • Odd multiplicity roots cause the graph to cross straight through the x-axis.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Use synthetic division, grouping, or the Rational Root Theorem to factor high-degree polynomials.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Plot the polynomial in Desmos to instantly inspect all x-intercepts and local extrema.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: What are all the real solutions to the equation $x^3 - 4x^2 - 5x = 0$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Factor out the greatest common factor $x$: $x(x^2 - 4x - 5) = 0$
Step 2
Factor the remaining quadratic trinomial: $x(x - 5)(x + 1) = 0$
Step 3
Set each factor equal to zero: $x = 0$, $x - 5 = 0$, $x + 1 = 0$
Step 4
Solve for all roots: $x = -1, 0, 5$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Enter $y = x^3 - 4x^2 - 5x$ into Desmos.
Step 2
Click on the x-intercepts on the graph.
Step 3
Read off coordinates $(-1, 0)$, $(0, 0)$, and $(5, 0)$.
Concept 4

Concept 4: Radical & Rational Functions

Functions involving roots or variables in denominators require careful attention to domain restrictions.

  • For even roots $\sqrt[n]{f(x)}$, the radicand must be non-negative: $f(x) \geq 0$.
  • For rational functions $\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$, denominators cannot equal zero: $Q(x) eq 0$.
  • Extraneous solutions frequently appear when squaring both sides of radical equations.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Isolate the radical or denominator, apply inverse operations, and check all solutions against domain constraints.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Graph both sides of the equation as separate functions in Desmos and find their intersection point.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: What is the solution to $\sqrt{x + 7} + 1 = x$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Isolate the radical term: $\sqrt{x + 7} = x - 1$
Step 2
Square both sides of the equation: $x + 7 = (x - 1)^2$
Step 3
Expand and set to zero: $x + 7 = x^2 - 2x + 1 \implies x^2 - 3x - 6 = 0$
Step 4
Use quadratic formula: $x = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{9 - 4(1)(-6)}}{2} = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{33}}{2}$
Step 5
Check extraneous solutions by substitution: $x = \frac{3 - \sqrt{33}}{2}$ is invalid since $x - 1 < 0$.
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Enter $y = \sqrt{x + 7} + 1$ and $y = x$ into Desmos.
Step 2
Click the intersection point in the first quadrant.
Step 3
Read the valid decimal intersection value.

Revision, Traps & Desmos Syntax

Quadratic Vertex Form

$f(x) = a(x - h)^2 + k$

Vertex is located at $(h, k)$. Axis of symmetry is $x = h$.

Exponential Growth/Decay

$f(x) = a(1 \pm r)^t$

Used when growth or decay is given as a percentage rate $r$ over time $t$.

Difference of Squares

$a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$

Quick factorization for solving nonlinear systems or simplifying expressions.

🚨 Top SAT Traps & Misconceptions

⚠️ SAT Trap: Extraneous Solutions in Radicals
Squaring both sides of an equation introduces false roots. Always plug back or check Desmos.
⚠️ SAT Trap: Confusing Growth Factor with Rate
In $y = 100(1.05)^t$, $1.05$ is the growth factor, but $0.05$ (or $5\%$) is the growth rate.
