Key Concepts & Worked Archetypes

Concept 1

Concept 1: Simplifying Rational Expressions

A rational expression is a quotient of two polynomials. Simplifying involves factoring numerators and denominators completely and canceling common factors.

  • Factor completely before canceling: $\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} = \frac{A(x) \cdot C(x)}{B(x) \cdot C(x)}$
  • Cancel common factors only, never terms: $\frac{x+2}{x+3} \neq \frac{2}{3}$
  • State domain restrictions where the denominator equals zero: $Q(x) \neq 0$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Factor the numerator and denominator into binomials, identify the greatest common factors, and divide out matching terms.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Define $f(x)$ as the original expression and $g(x)$ as the simplified option in Desmos, then check for identical tables or graphs.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: Which of the following is equivalent to $\frac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 - 5x + 6}$ for $x > 3$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Factor the numerator into difference of squares: $(x-3)(x+3)$
Step 2
Factor the denominator into two binomials: $(x-3)(x-2)$
Step 3
Rewrite the expression: $\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{(x-3)(x-2)}$
Step 4
Cancel the common factor $(x-3)$ to get the result: $\frac{x+3}{x-2}$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Type $\frac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 - 5x + 6}$ into Desmos line 1.
Step 2
Type each multiple choice option into subsequent lines.
Step 3
Compare tables to find the expression that produces identical outputs.
Concept 2

Concept 2: Adding and Subtracting Rational Expressions

Combining rational expressions requires finding a common denominator, expanding numerators, and combining like terms.

  • Find the Least Common Denominator (LCD) of all denominators.
  • Convert each fraction: $\frac{A}{B} + \frac{C}{D} = \frac{AD + BC}{BD}$
  • Distribute negative signs carefully during subtraction in numerators.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Determine the LCD, scale each numerator by the missing factor, combine over the single LCD, and simplify the resulting polynomial.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Substitute a non-zero test value for $x$ (e.g., $x = 4$) into the problem expression, then plug the same value into the answer choices.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: What is the sum of $\frac{2}{x-1} + \frac{3}{x+2}$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify the LCD as $(x-1)(x+2)$
Step 2
Rewrite fractions with the LCD: $\frac{2(x+2)}{(x-1)(x+2)} + \frac{3(x-1)}{(x-1)(x+2)}$
Step 3
Expand numerators: $\frac{2x + 4 + 3x - 3}{(x-1)(x+2)}$
Step 4
Combine like terms to get the final expression: $\frac{5x + 1}{(x-1)(x+2)}$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Evaluate original expression at $x = 4$: $\frac{2}{3} + \frac{3}{6} = 1.166...$
Step 2
Substitute $x = 4$ into option choices.
Step 3
Match the numeric result to identify the correct choice.
Concept 3

Concept 3: Solving Rational Equations

Equations containing rational expressions are solved by clearing denominators and checking for extraneous solutions.

  • Multiply the entire equation by the LCD to eliminate denominators.
  • Solve the resulting linear or quadratic equation.
  • Check all solutions against domain restrictions to discard extraneous roots.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Factor all denominators, multiply every term by the LCD, solve the polynomial equation, and verify roots.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use Desmos to graph both sides of the equation as $y_1$ and $y_2$, then click the intersection point.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: What is the solution to $\frac{5}{x} + \frac{2}{3} = \frac{7}{x}$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify the LCD of the terms as $3x$
Step 2
Multiply every term by $3x$: $3x\left(\frac{5}{x}\right) + 3x\left(\frac{2}{3}\right) = 3x\left(\frac{7}{x}\right)$
Step 3
Simplify the equation: $15 + 2x = 21$
Step 4
Solve for $x$: $2x = 6$
Step 5
Final solution: $x = 3$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Enter $y_1 = \frac{5}{x} + \frac{2}{3}$ in Desmos.
Step 2
Enter $y_2 = \frac{7}{x}$ in Desmos.
Step 3
Click the intersection point on the graph to read $x = 3$.
Concept 4

Concept 4: Equivalent Rational Forms & Constants

Digital SAT frequently tests matching equivalent algebraic forms or finding unknown constants within numerators and denominators.

  • Use polynomial long division or equating coefficients for improper rational expressions.
  • Rewrite $\frac{ax+b}{cx+d}$ in mixed quotient form: $Q + \frac{R}{cx+d}$
  • Ensure degree of numerator is less than denominator before final matching.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Perform polynomial division or combine fractions on the right side and equate corresponding coefficients of powers of $x$.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use Desmos sliders for unknown constants or evaluate both expressions at specific numerical values.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: If $\frac{3x - 5}{x - 2} = A + \frac{B}{x - 2}$, what is the value of $A + B$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Rewrite numerator to match denominator term: $\frac{3(x - 2) + 1}{x - 2}$
Step 2
Split the fraction: $\frac{3(x-2)}{x-2} + \frac{1}{x-2}$
Step 3
Simplify to identify constants: $3 + \frac{1}{x-2}$
Step 4
Determine values: $A = 3$ and $B = 1$
Step 5
Calculate sum: $A + B = 4$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Enter expression with a slider for $A$ and $B$ in Desmos.
Step 2
Adjust sliders until the difference graph equals zero identically.
Step 3
Read values of $A$ and $B$ directly.

Confirm option C.

Calc

Identify $a = \frac{1}{2}$ and $b = 1$, but wait! Let's re-check cancellation: numerator has $(x+2)$ twice? Let's verify: $2x(x^2-4) = 2x(x-2)(x+2)$. Question 15 Solving Rational Equations
Hard

If $\frac{x}{x - 2} + \frac{1}{x - 4} = \frac{2}{x^2 - 6x + 8}$, what is the product of all distinct real solutions?

Hint: Check the denominators after clearing fractions; ensure solutions do not make any denominator zero.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Factor the right denominator: $x^2 - 6x + 8 = (x - 2)(x - 4)$.
Step 2
Multiply the entire equation by $(x - 2)(x - 4)$ to clear fractions: $x(x - 4) + 1(x - 2) = 2$.
Step 3
Expand and simplify: $x^2 - 4x + x - 2 = 2 \implies x^2 - 3x - 4 = 0$.
Step 4
Factor: $(x - 4)(x + 1) = 0$, giving potential solutions $x = 4$ and $x = -1$. But $x = 4$ makes $x - 4 = 0$ in the denominator, so it is extraneous. Only $x = -1$ works, wait! The product of distinct real solutions when there is only one solution $-1$ is $-1$, but let's check if $x=-1$ works: $\frac{-1}{-3} + \frac{-1}{-5} = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{5} = \frac{8}{15}$, while RHS is $\frac{2}{1+6+8} = \frac{2}{15}$, not equal! Thus no real solutions exist.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1

Step 2
Observe that the graphs never intersect (or intersection is at a vertical asymptote/hole).
Step 3
Conclude there are no real solutions.
