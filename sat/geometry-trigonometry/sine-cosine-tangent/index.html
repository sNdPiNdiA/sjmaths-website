Key Concepts & Worked Archetypes

Concept 1

Concept 1: Right Triangle Trigonometric Ratios (SOHCAHTOA)

Trigonometric ratios relate the acute angles of a right triangle to the lengths of its sides using Sine, Cosine, and Tangent.

  • $\sin(\theta) = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}$
  • $\cos(\theta) = \frac{\text{Adjacent}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}$
  • $\tan(\theta) = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Adjacent}}$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Identify the reference angle, label the opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse sides relative to the angle, and set up the appropriate trigonometric equation to solve for the missing side length.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Define the angle variable directly in Desmos (e.g., $A=30$) and type out trigonometric functions to quickly evaluate side lengths or ratios without manual radical simplification.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: In right triangle $ABC$, the length of hypotenuse $AC$ is $10$ and $\sin(A) = 0.6$. What is the length of side $BC$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify that side $BC$ is opposite to angle $A$.
Step 2
Use the sine definition: $\sin(A) = \frac{BC}{AC}$
Step 3
Substitute the known values: $0.6 = \frac{BC}{10}$
Step 4
Solve for $BC$: $BC = 10 \times 0.6 = 6$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Open Desmos and type the equation: $x/10 = 0.6$
Step 2
Read the value of $x$ directly from the output.
Concept 2

Concept 2: Cofunction Identities for Complementary Angles

The sine of an acute angle is always equal to the cosine of its complementary angle.

  • If angles $A$ and $B$ are complementary, then $A + B = 90^\circ$ (or $\frac{\pi}{2}$ radians).
  • $\sin(A) = \cos(90^\circ - A)$
  • $\cos(A) = \sin(90^\circ - A)$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Recognize that the sum of the two acute angles in any right triangle equals $90^\circ$, then equate the sine of one angle directly to the cosine of the other.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Type both given expressions into Desmos or use an equation like $\sin(x) = \cos(70)$ in degree mode to instantly find $x = 20$.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: In right triangle $XYZ$, angle $Y$ is a right angle. If $\sin(X) = \frac{5}{13}$, what is the value of $\cos(Z)$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify that angles $X$ and $Z$ are complementary in right triangle $XYZ$.
Step 2
Apply the cofunction identity: $\sin(X) = \cos(90^\circ - X)$
Step 3
Note that $90^\circ - X$ is equal to angle $Z$.
Step 4
Conclude that $\cos(Z) = \sin(X) = \frac{5}{13}$.
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Recall that acute angles in a right triangle sum to $90^\circ$.
Step 2
Immediately equate $\cos(Z)$ to the given value of $\sin(X)$.
Concept 3

Concept 3: Special Right Triangles ($30^\circ-60^\circ-90^\circ$ and $45^\circ-45^\circ-90^\circ$)

Exact trigonometric values can be derived without a calculator using standard geometric ratios for special angles.

  • For $30^\circ-60^\circ-90^\circ$: side lengths are in the ratio $1 : \sqrt{3} : 2$.
  • For $45^\circ-45^\circ-90^\circ$: side lengths are in the ratio $1 : 1 : \sqrt{2}$.
  • Exact values: $\sin(30^\circ) = \frac{1}{2}$, $\cos(30^\circ) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $\tan(45^\circ) = 1$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Scale the base triangle ratios by multiplying or dividing the given side length by the corresponding radical multiplier.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Set Desmos to degree mode and evaluate exact expressions like $\sin(30)$ to get $0.5$ or convert options into decimals to match.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: An equilateral triangle has a side length of $6$. What is the height of the triangle?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Draw an altitude splitting the equilateral triangle into two congruent $30^\circ-60^\circ-90^\circ$ triangles.
Step 2
Identify the base of the resulting right triangle as half of $6$, which is $3$.
Step 3
Use the $30^\circ-60^\circ-90^\circ$ ratio where the height is $3 \times \sqrt{3}$.
Step 4
Final height is $3\sqrt{3}$.
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Use the height formula for an equilateral triangle: $h = \frac{s\sqrt{3}}{2}$
Step 2
Substitute $s = 6$: $h = \frac{6\sqrt{3}}{2} = 3\sqrt{3}$
Concept 4

Concept 4: Applications to Similar Right Triangles

Trigonometric ratios remain invariant for a given angle regardless of the size of the similar right triangle.

  • Similar triangles have equal corresponding acute angles.
  • $\sin(A)$ in a small triangle equals $\sin(A)$ in a scaled larger triangle.
  • Ratio of corresponding sides is constant: $\frac{a_1}{b_1} = \frac{a_2}{b_2}$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Set up proportions between corresponding sides of similar right triangles created by drawing altitudes to the hypotenuse.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use Desmos to test scaling factors or set up algebraic equality between two slope ratios.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: Triangle $ABC$ is similar to triangle $DEF$, where angle $A$ corresponds to angle $D$ and angle $B$ is a right angle. If $\tan(A) = \frac{4}{3}$ and $DE = 9$, what is the length of $EF$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Use the property that similar triangles share the same trigonometric ratios: $\tan(D) = \tan(A) = \frac{4}{3}$.
Step 2
Set up the tangent ratio for triangle $DEF$: $\tan(D) = \frac{EF}{DE}$
Step 3
Substitute the known values: $\frac{4}{3} = \frac{EF}{9}$
Step 4
Solve for $EF$: $EF = 9 \times \frac{4}{3} = 12$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Type the proportion into Desmos: $x/9 = 4/3$
Step 2
Read the value of $x$ as $12$ instantly.

Question 2 Right Triangle Side Lengths
Easy

In right triangle $DEF$, angle $E = 90^\circ$, $DE = 9$, and $EF = 12$. What is the value of $\cos(D)$?

Hint: First, find the hypotenuse $DF$ using the Pythagorean theorem.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Use the Pythagorean theorem to find hypotenuse $DF$: $DF^2 = DE^2 + EF^2 = 9^2 + 12^2 = 81 + 144 = 225$.
Step 2
Take the square root to find $DF = 15$.
Step 3
Recall that $\cos(D) = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{DE}{DF}$.
Step 4
Substitute the lengths: $\cos(D) = \frac{9}{15} = \frac{3}{5}$.

Recognize the 9-12-15 right triangle as a scaled 3-4-5 triangle.
Step 2
$\cos(D) = \text{adjacent} / \text{hypotenuse} = 9/15 = 3/5$... Wait, adjacent to $D$ is $DE=9$, hypotenuse is 15. $9/15 = 3/5$. Let's check options: Option B is 4/5, C is 3/5. Correct index is 2.

Question 13 Word Problems & Applications
Medium

From a point $P$ on the ground, the angle of elevation to the top of a monument is $30^\circ$. Moving $40$ meters closer to the monument, the angle of elevation becomes $60^\circ$. What is the height of the monument?

Hint: Let the height be $h$. Use $\tan(30^\circ)$ and $\tan(60^\circ)$ to create a system or equation.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Let $h$ be the height of the monument and $x$ be the distance from the new position to the base. Thus $\tan(60^\circ) = \frac{h}{x}$, so $x = \frac{h}{\sqrt{3}}$.
Step 2
From the first position, total distance is $x + 40$, so $\tan(30^\circ) = \frac{h}{x + 40}$.
Step 3
Substitute $x = \frac{h}{\sqrt{3}}$ into $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{h}{\frac{h}{\sqrt{3}} + 40}$.
Step 4
Solve for $h$: $\frac{h}{\sqrt{3}} + 40 = h\sqrt{3} \implies 40 = h\sqrt{3} - \frac{h}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{2h}{\sqrt{3}} \implies h = 20\sqrt{3}$.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
For a 30-60-90 triangle split, height $h = \frac{\text{distance moved}}{\sqrt{3} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}} = \frac{40}{\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}} = 20\sqrt{3}$.
Step 2
Verify $20\sqrt{3} \approx 34.64$ in Desmos.

Question 18 Advanced Trigonometric Manipulations

If $\cos(x) = \frac{4}{5}$ and $0 < x < \frac{\pi}{2}$, what is the value of $\sin(x) \cdot \tan(x)$?

Step 3
Substitute into the expression: $\frac{\frac{9}{25}}{\frac{4}{5}}$.
Step 4
Calculate: $\frac{9}{25} \cdot \frac{5}{4} = \frac{9}{20}$.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
Store $x = \arccos(4/5)$ in Desmos.
Step 2
Type $\sin(x) * \tan(x)$ and press fraction conversion to get $9/20$.
