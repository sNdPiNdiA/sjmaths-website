Key Concepts & Worked Archetypes

Concept 1

Concept 1: Equation of a Circle

A circle is the set of all points equidistant from a fixed center $(h, k)$, defined by the standard coordinate equation.

  • Standard form: $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$
  • Center of the circle is given by the coordinate pair $(h, k)$
  • Radius is equal to the positive square root of the right-hand side constant: $r = \sqrt{r^2}$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Expand the equation, group like terms, and use completing the square for both $x$ and $y$ variables to transform general form into standard form.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Type the general equation directly into Desmos. Click on the center point to read coordinates and measure the radius along the axes.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: What are the coordinates of the center of the circle defined by the equation $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 8y = 0$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Group the $x$ terms and $y$ terms together: $(x^2 - 6x) + (y^2 + 8y) = 0$
Step 2
Complete the square for $x$ by adding $(-6/2)^2 = 9$ to both sides
Step 3
Complete the square for $y$ by adding $(8/2)^2 = 16$ to both sides
Step 4
Rewrite the equation in standard form: $(x - 3)^2 + (y + 4)^2 = 25$
Step 5
Identify the center $(h, k)$ as $(3, -4)$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Open Desmos and type the equation $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 8y = 0$
Step 2
Click on the graphical representation of the circle's center
Step 3
Read the coordinates $(3, -4)$ directly from the popup
Concept 2

Concept 2: Completing the Square

The process of converting a general second-degree quadratic equation into the standard binomial circle format.

  • General form: $x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0$
  • Coefficient of squared terms must be $1$ before completing the square
  • Radius squared formula from coefficients: $r^2 = \left(\frac{D}{2}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{E}{2}\right)^2 - F$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Isolate constant terms, take half of the linear coefficients $D$ and $E$, square them, and add them to both sides of the equation.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use Desmos to instantly graph the general form equation and inspect the center and intercepts without doing manual algebra.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: Find the radius of the circle given by $2x^2 + 2y^2 + 4x - 12y - 10 = 0$.

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Divide the entire equation by $2$ to make the leading coefficients $1$: $x^2 + y^2 + 2x - 6y - 5 = 0$
Step 2
Isolate the constant on the right side: $x^2 + 2x + y^2 - 6y = 5$
Step 3
Complete the square by adding $(2/2)^2 = 1$ and $(-6/2)^2 = 9$ to both sides: $(x^2 + 2x + 1) + (y^2 - 6y + 9) = 5 + 1 + 9$
Step 4
Factor the trinomials: $(x + 1)^2 + (y - 3)^2 = 15$
Step 5
Extract the radius: $r = \sqrt{15}$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Enter $2x^2 + 2y^2 + 4x - 12y - 10 = 0$ into Desmos
Step 2
Find the leftmost and rightmost points on the circle, or use a point on the circumference to calculate distance from center
Step 3
Confirm $r = \sqrt{15} \approx 3.873$
Concept 3

Concept 3: Arc Length & Sector Area

Calculations involving fractional parts of a circle's circumference and total area based on central angle measures.

  • Arc length formula: $s = \frac{\theta}{360^{\circ}} \times 2\pi r$ (with $\theta$ in degrees) or $s = r\theta$ (with $\theta$ in radians)
  • Sector area formula: $A = \frac{\theta}{360^{\circ}} \times \pi r^2$ (with $\theta$ in degrees)
  • Proportionality rule: $\frac{\text{Arc Length}}{\text{Circumference}} = \frac{\text{Central Angle}}{360^{\circ}}
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Find the total circumference or area first, then multiply by the ratio of the given central angle to $360^{\circ}$.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Set up a direct proportion in Desmos using fraction templates and solve numerical expressions instantly.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: A circle has a radius of $6$ units. What is the length of an arc intercepted by a central angle of $60^{\circ}$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Recall the total circumference formula: $C = 2\pi r$
Step 2
Substitute the given radius: $C = 2\pi(6) = 12\pi$
Step 3
Set up the arc length fraction: $s = \frac{60^{\circ}}{360^{\circ}} \times 12\pi$
Step 4
Simplify the fraction: $s = \frac{1}{6} \times 12\pi$
Step 5
Compute final answer: $s = 2\pi$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Type (60/360) * 2 * pi * 6 directly into the Desmos calculator line
Step 2
Read the decimal value or convert to exact fraction form
Step 3
Match with $2\pi$
Concept 4

Concept 4: Tangents and Chords

Geometric relationships between lines intersecting circles, specifically perpendicularity at the point of tangency.

  • A tangent line to a circle is always perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency: $\text{Radius} \perp \text{Tangent}$
  • Perpendicular lines from the center of a circle to a chord bisect the chord
  • Inscribed angles subtended by the same arc are equal; an inscribed angle is half of its corresponding central angle
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Apply the Pythagorean theorem to right triangles formed by the radius, tangent, and secant lines.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Sketch or visualize the right triangle formed by radius and tangent, then use Desmos distance formula if coordinates are provided.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: Line $AB$ is tangent to a circle with center $O$ and radius $5$ at point $B$. If the distance from $A$ to the center $O$ is $13$, what is the length of segment $AB$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify that radius $OB$ is perpendicular to tangent $AB$, creating right triangle $OBA$ with right angle at $B$
Step 2
State the Pythagorean theorem for the triangle: $OB^2 + AB^2 = OA^2$
Step 3
Substitute known values into the equation: $5^2 + AB^2 = 13^2$
Step 4
Simplify exponents: $25 + AB^2 = 169$
Step 5
Solve for $AB$: $AB = \sqrt{144} = 12$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Recognize the classic Pythagorean triple $5 - 12 - 13$
Step 2
Immediately select leg length $12$

Question 4 Equation of a Circle
Medium

What are the coordinates of the center of the circle given by the equation $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 8y - 11 = 0$?

Hint: Complete the square for both the $x$ and $y$ terms, or use the formulas $h = -\frac{D}{2}$ and $k = -\frac{E}{2}$ for $x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0$.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Group the $x$ terms and $y$ terms: $(x^2 - 6x) + (y^2 + 8y) = 11$.
Step 2
Complete the square for $x$: add $\left(\frac{-6}{2}\right)^2 = 9$. Complete the square for $y$: add $\left(\frac{8}{2}\right)^2 = 16$.
Step 3
Rewrite the equation: $(x^2 - 6x + 9) + (y^2 + 8y + 16) = 11 + 9 + 16$.
Step 4
Simplify: $(x - 3)^2 + (y + 4)^2 = 36$, so the center is $(3, -4)$.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
Type the equation $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 8y - 11 = 0$ into Desmos.
Step 2
Click on the center of the circle on the graph.
Step 3
Read the coordinates directly as $(3, -4)$.
Question 5 Equation of a Circle
Hard

A circle is tangent to the $x$-axis and has its center at $(5, 6)$. Which of the following is the equation of the circle?

Hint: If a circle is tangent to the $x$-axis, its radius is equal to the absolute value of the $y$-coordinate of its center.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Identify the center of the circle as $(h, k) = (5, 6)$.
Step 2
Determine the radius. Since the circle is tangent to the $x$-axis, the distance from the center $(5, 6)$ to the $x$-axis is the $y$-coordinate, which is $6$.
Step 3
Calculate $r^2 = 6^2 = 36$.
Step 4
Write the equation: $(x - 5)^2 + (y - 6)^2 = 36$.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
Use Desmos to test options. Graph each option with center $(5, 6)$.
Step 2
Check which option touches (is tangent to) the line $y = 0$ (the $x$-axis).
Step 3
$(x - 5)^2 + (y - 6)^2 = 36$ touches the $x$-axis perfectly at $(5, 0)$.

Step 4

Question 7 Arc Length and Sector Area
Easy

In a circle with radius $8$, a sector has an area of $16\pi$. What is the measure of the central angle of this sector in degrees?

Hint: Use the sector area formula: $\text{Area} = \pi r^2 \times \left(\frac{\theta}{360^{\circ}}\right)$.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Identify $r = 8$ and Sector Area $= 16\pi$.
Step 2
Set up the equation: $16\pi = \pi (8^2) \times \left(\frac{\theta}{360}\right)$.
Step 3
Simplify: $16\pi = 64\pi \times \left(\frac{\theta}{360}\right)$, which gives $\frac{16}{64} = \frac{\theta}{360}$.
Step 4
Solve for $\theta$: $\frac{1}{4} = \frac{\theta}{360} \implies \theta = 90^{\circ}$.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
In Desmos, solve the equation for $\theta$: $16\pi = \pi * 8^2 * (x / 360)$.
Step 2
Desmos will automatically output $x = 90$.
Step 3
Select $90^{\circ}$.

Question 10 Arc Length and Sector Area
Hard

The perimeter of a sector of a circle is $20$ cm, and its radius is $6$ cm. What is the area of this sector?

Hint: The perimeter of a sector consists of two radii and the arc length ($P = 2r + s$). Once you find the arc length $s$, use the sector area formula $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}rs$.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Use the perimeter formula for a sector: $P = r + r + s$, where $s$ is the arc length.
Step 2
Substitute $P = 20$ and $r = 6$: $20 = 6 + 6 + s \implies 20 = 12 + s \implies s = 8$.
Step 3
Use the sector area formula involving radius and arc length: $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}rs$.
Step 4
Calculate: $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(6)(8) = 24$.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
In Desmos, define $r = 6$ and $s = 20 - 2 * 6$.
Step 2
Calculate area using $0.5 * r * s$.
Step 3
Desmos immediately outputs $24$.

Question 12 Circle Theorems and Angles
Easy

Triangle $ABC$ is inscribed in a circle such that side $AC$ is a diameter of the circle. If $\angle BCA = 40^{\circ}$, what is the measure of $\angle BAC$?

Hint: An angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle ($90^{\circ}$). The sum of angles in a triangle is $180^{\circ}$.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Recognize that since $AC$ is a diameter, the inscribed angle opposite to it, $\angle ABC$, is $90^{\circ}$ (Thales's Theorem).
Step 2
Use the sum of angles in $\triangle ABC$: $\angle BAC + \angle BCA + \angle ABC = 180^{\circ}$.
Step 3
Substitute the known values: $\angle BAC + 40^{\circ} + 90^{\circ} = 180^{\circ}$.
Step 4
Solve for $\angle BAC$: $\angle BAC = 180^{\circ} - 130^{\circ} = 50^{\circ}$.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
Since $AC$ is the diameter, $\angle B = 90^{\circ}$.
Step 2
The acute angles of a right triangle are complementary: $90 - 40 = 50^{\circ}$.
Step 3
Select $50^{\circ}$.
Question 13 Circle Theorems and Angles
Medium

A quadrilateral $ABCD$ is inscribed in a circle. If $\angle A = 70^{\circ}$ and $\angle B = 80^{\circ}$, what is the measure of $\angle C$?

Hint: Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral (quadrilateral inscribed in a circle) are supplementary (add up to $180^{\circ}$).
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Identify that $ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral.
Step 2
Note that opposite angles $\angle A$ and $\angle C$ are supplementary: $\angle A + \angle C = 180^{\circ}$.
Step 3
Substitute $\angle A = 70^{\circ}$: $70^{\circ} + \angle C = 180^{\circ}$.
Step 4
Solve for $\angle C$: $\angle C = 180^{\circ} - 70^{\circ} = 110^{\circ}$.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
Look at the opposite angle pair: $\angle A$ is opposite to $\angle C$.
Step 2
Subtract $\angle A$ from $180^{\circ}$: $180 - 70 = 110^{\circ}$.
Step 3
Select $110^{\circ}$.
Question 14 Circle Theorems and Angles
Medium

A tangent line $PT$ touches a circle at point $T$. Point $P$ is outside the circle, and a secant line through $P$ intersects the circle at points $A$ and $B$. If $PT = 6$ and $PA = 4$, what is the length of chord $AB$?

Hint: Use the Tangent-Secant Theorem: $PT^2 = PA \times PB$, where $PB$ is the total length of the secant segment from $P$.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Apply the Tangent-Secant Theorem: $PT^2 = PA \times PB$.
Step 2
Substitute $PT = 6$ and $PA = 4$: $6^2 = 4 \times PB \implies 36 = 4 \times PB$.
Step 3
Solve for $PB$: $PB = \frac{36}{4} = 9$.
Step 4
Find $AB$: $AB = PB - PA = 9 - 4 = 5$.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
In Desmos, set up the equation $6^2 = 4 * (4 + x)$ where $x = AB$.
Step 2
Solve for $x$: $36 = 16 + 4x \implies 20 = 4x \implies x = 5$.
Step 3
Select $5$.

Question 17 Advanced Circle Modeling

A circular garden has a diameter of $20$ meters. A uniform pathway of width $2$ meters is built around the garden. What is the area of the pathway?

Hint: Subtract the area of the inner circle (garden) from the total area of the outer circle (garden + pathway).

Step 3

Question 18 Advanced Circle Modeling
Medium

A chord of length $12$ cm is at a distance of $8$ cm from the center of a circle. What is the radius of the circle?

Hint: The perpendicular from the center of a circle to a chord bisects the chord. Use the Pythagorean theorem with the half-chord, distance to center, and radius.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Draw a perpendicular from the center to the chord, which bisects the chord into two segments of length $\frac{12}{2} = 6$ cm.
Step 2
Form a right triangle where the legs are the distance from the center ($8$) and the half-chord ($6$), and the hypotenuse is the radius $r$.
Step 3
Apply the Pythagorean theorem: $r^2 = 6^2 + 8^2$.
Step 4
Calculate: $r^2 = 36 + 64 = 100 \implies r = 10$.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
Recognize the $6-8-10$ right triangle Pythagorean triple.
Step 2
The radius is the hypotenuse, which is $10$.
Step 3
Select $10$.

Standard Circle Equation

C = 2\pi r, \quad A = \pi r^2

Calculates the linear distance along the arc for a central angle $\theta$.

🚨 Top SAT Traps & Misconceptions

⚠️ SAT Trap: Radius Squared Confusion
Forgetting that the right side of the circle equation is $r^2$, not $r$. Always take the square root to find the radius.
⚠️ SAT Trap: Coordinate Sign Flips
Mistaking $(x - 3)^2$ as having a center at $x = -3$ instead of $x = 3$. Remember the formula subtracts the coordinate.
