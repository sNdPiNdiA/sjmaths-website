Key Concepts & Worked Archetypes

Concept 1

Concept 1: Rectangles, Squares, and Composite Shapes

Perimeter is the total external boundary length of a 2D shape, whereas area measures the interior surface covered by standard square units.

  • Perimeter of a rectangle: $P = 2l + 2w$
  • Area of a rectangle: $A = l \cdot w$
  • Area of a square with side length $s$: $A = s^2$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Decompose complex composite shapes into standard non-overlapping rectangles or triangles, calculate their individual areas or boundary lengths, and sum or subtract them accordingly.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use Desmos to define custom polygon boundary equations or evaluate algebraic area functions instantly by entering the function and substituting given parameter values.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: A rectangular garden has a length that is 3 meters greater than its width. If the total perimeter is 38 meters, what is the area of the garden in square meters?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Let the width be $w$ and the length be $w + 3$.
Step 2
Set up the perimeter equation: $2(w + (w + 3)) = 38$
Step 3
Simplify and solve for $w$: $2(2w + 3) = 38$, $4w + 6 = 38$, $4w = 32$, $w = 8$.
Step 4
Calculate the length: $l = 8 + 3 = 11$.
Step 5
Compute the area: $A = 8 \cdot 11 = 88$.
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Open Desmos and type the equation: $2(x + x + 3) = 38$
Step 2
Click or tap the intersection or root line to find $x = 8$.
Step 3
Evaluate $8(8 + 3)$ directly to yield 88.
Concept 2

Concept 2: Triangles and Special Right Triangles

Triangles have an area determined by base and height perpendicular to that base, with special ratios applying to $45^{\circ}-45^{\circ}-90^{\circ}$ and $30^{\circ}-60^{\circ}-90^{\circ}$ triangles.

  • Area of any triangle: $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$
  • Equilateral triangle area with side $s$: $A = \frac{s^2\sqrt{3}}{4}$
  • Pythagorean theorem for right triangles: $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Identify the base and perpendicular height, apply the standard area formula, or utilize trigonometric ratios ($A = \frac{1}{2}ab \sin C$) when side-angle-side is given.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Store triangle side lengths as variables in Desmos and immediately calculate area using Heron's formula or trigonometric area functions.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: What is the area of a right triangle whose hypotenuse is 13 and one of its legs is 5?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Use the Pythagorean theorem to find the missing leg $b$: $5^2 + b^2 = 13^2$
Step 2
Expand the squares: $25 + b^2 = 169$
Step 3
Isolate $b^2$: $b^2 = 144$, yielding $b = 12$.
Step 4
Compute the triangle area: $A = \frac{1}{2}(5)(12) = 30$.
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Enter the equation in Desmos: $5^2 + x^2 = 13^2$ with $x > 0$.
Step 2
Identify the positive root $x = 12$.
Step 3
Calculate $\frac{1}{2} \cdot 5 \cdot 12 = 30$.
Concept 3

Concept 3: Circles, Arcs, and Sectors

Circles are defined entirely by their radius, with circumference acting as the perimeter and area measuring the circular disk's interior region.

  • Circumference of a circle: $C = 2\pi r$ or $C = \pi d$
  • Area of a circle: $A = \pi r^2$
  • Sector area with central angle $\theta$ (in degrees): $A = \pi r^2 \cdot \frac{\theta}{360^{\circ}}$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Substitute the given diameter or radius into the exact formula, keeping terms in terms of $\pi$ unless a decimal approximation is requested.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Type $\pi$ directly into Desmos and store radius values as variables (e.g., $r = 4$) to instantly evaluate complex sector and annulus problems.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: A circle has a circumference of $16\pi$. What is the area of the circle?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Use the circumference formula: $2\pi r = 16\pi$
Step 2
Divide both sides by $2\pi$: $r = 8$
Step 3
Substitute the radius into the area formula: $A = \pi(8)^2$
Step 4
Compute the final area: $A = 64\pi$.
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Define $r = \frac{16\pi}{2\pi}$ in Desmos to find $r = 8$.
Step 2
Evaluate $\pi r^2$ by typing \pi \cdot 8^2 to get $64\pi$.
Concept 4

Concept 4: Coordinate Geometry Perimeter & Area

Polygons plotted on the Cartesian plane have areas and perimeters calculated using coordinate distance formulas or the Shoelace formula.

  • Distance between two points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$: $d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$
  • Shoelace formula for polygon area with vertices $(x_1, y_1), (x_2, y_2), \dots$: $A = \frac{1}{2}|x_1y_2 + x_2y_3 + \dots + x_ny_1 - (y_1x_2 + y_2x_3 + \dots + y_nx_1)|$
  • Horizontal/Vertical segment length: $|x_2 - x_1|$ or $|y_2 - y_1|$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Find the lengths of all sides using the distance formula and sum them for perimeter, or enclose the figure in a bounding rectangle and subtract outer right triangles for area.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use Desmos to plot the polygon points or set up the Shoelace determinant matrix structure to instantly find exact coordinate polygon areas.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: What is the area of the triangle in the xy-plane with vertices at $(0,0)$, $(4,0)$, and $(0,3)$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify that the vertices form a right triangle on the coordinate axes.
Step 2
Determine the base length along the x-axis: $b = 4 - 0 = 4$.
Step 3
Determine the height length along the y-axis: $h = 3 - 0 = 3$.
Step 4
Calculate the area: $A = \frac{1}{2}(4)(3) = 6$.
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Recognize standard right triangle legs on coordinate axes.
Step 2
Compute $\frac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot 3 = 6$ instantly.

Question 1 Rectangle and Square Foundations
Easy

A rectangular garden has a length of $12\text{ meters}$ and a perimeter of $40\text{ meters}$. What is the area of the garden, in square meters?

Hint: Use the perimeter formula $P = 2l + 2w$ to find the width, then multiply length by width to find the area.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Write down the perimeter formula for a rectangle: $P = 2l + 2w$.
Step 2
Substitute the known values $P = 40$ and $l = 12$ into the equation: $40 = 2(12) + 2w$.
Step 3
Simplify and solve for $w$: $40 = 24 + 2w \implies 16 = 2w \implies w = 8$.
Step 4
Calculate the area using $A = l \times w$: $A = 12 \times 8 = 96$.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
Notice that semi-perimeter is half of the total perimeter: $40 / 2 = 20$.
Step 2
Since length is $12$, the width must be $20 - 12 = 8$.
Step 3
Area is $12 \times 8 = 96$.

Hint: Set up a quadratic equation using $A = l \cdot w$ where $l = 2w - 3$, solve for $w$, then calculate the perimeter.

Step 4
Factor the quadratic equation: $(2w + 9)(w - 6) = 0$, giving valid width $w = 6$.
Step 5
Find length: $l = 2(6) - 3 = 9$.
Step 6
Calculate perimeter: $P = 2(9 + 6) = 30$.

Question 5 Rectangle and Square Foundations
Hard

A rectangular plot of land is partitioned into three smaller congruent rectangles by two parallel interior fences, each running parallel to the shorter side of the plot. If the total length of fencing used for the perimeter and the two interior partitions is $360\text{ meters}$, and the area of the plot is maximized, what is the maximum area in square meters?

Hint: Express total fencing in terms of width $w$ and length $l$ where 2 interior partitions add $2w$ or $2l$ to the fence total, then use vertex of a parabola.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Let the width of each of the 3 smaller rectangles be $x$ and the common length be $y$. The total plot dimensions are $3x$ by $y$.
Step 2
Total fencing includes 2 outer lengths, 2 outer widths, and 2 interior partitions of length $y$: $P_{fence} = 2(3x) + 2y + 2y = 6x + 4y$.
Step 3
Set total fence equal to 360: $6x + 4y = 360 \implies 3x + 2y = 180 \implies y = 90 - 1.5x$.
Step 4
Express Area $A = (3x)(y) = 3x(90 - 1.5x) = 270x - 4.5x^2$.
Step 5
Find the vertex $x = -b / (2a) = -270 / (2(-4.5)) = 30$.
Step 6
Calculate maximum area: $A = 270(30) - 4.5(30)^2 = 8100 - 4050 = 4050$. Wait, let's check: $3x = 90$, $y = 45$, Area $= 90 \times 45 = 4050$ (Option B).
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
Graph $A = 3x(180 - 3x)/2$ or similar in Desmos.
Step 2
Find the maximum point on the parabola using vertex cursor.
Step 3
Read maximum area value $4050$ directly.

A circle has a circumference of $16\pi\text{ centimeters}$. What is the area of the circle, in square centimeters?

Hint: Use the circumference formula $C = 2\pi r$ to find the radius $r$, then substitute $r$ into the area formula $A = \pi r^2$.

Substitute the given circumference: $16\pi = 2\pi r$.
Step 3
Solve for radius $r$: $r = \frac{16\pi}{2\pi} = 8$.
Step 4
Calculate the area: $A = \pi r^2 = \pi (8^2) = 64\pi$.

Step 2
Square the radius and attach $\pi$: $8^2 = 64$, giving $64\pi$.

Step 3
Simplify the fraction: $\frac{60}{360} = \frac{1}{6}$.
Step 4
Compute final area: $A = \frac{1}{6} \times 36\pi = 6\pi$.

🚨 Top SAT Traps & Misconceptions

⚠️ SAT Trap: Radius vs. Diameter Confusion
College Board frequently provides the diameter in the stem while formulas require the radius. Always divide diameter by 2 before calculating area.
⚠️ SAT Trap: Scale Factor Area Distortion
When the linear dimensions of a 2D shape are scaled by a factor $k$, the perimeter scales by $k$, but the area scales by $k^2$.

What is the perimeter of a rectangle with a length of $12\text{ cm}$ and a width of $5\text{ cm}$?
