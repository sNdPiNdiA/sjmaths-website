Key Concepts & Worked Archetypes

Concept 1

Concept 1: Triangle Congruence Criteria

Two triangles are congruent if all corresponding sides and angles are equal, determined efficiently through criteria like SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, and HL.

  • Side-Side-Side (SSS): All three corresponding side lengths are equal, such that $AB = DE$, $BC = EF$, and $AC = DF$.
  • Side-Angle-Side (SAS): Two sides and the included angle are equal, such that $AB = DE$, $\angle B = \angle E$, and $BC = EF$.
  • Angle-Angle-Side (AAS): Two angles and a non-included side are equal, guaranteeing total triangle congruence.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Identify matching vertices based on coordinate geometry or given geometric markings, set up corresponding equal side equations, and solve for unknowns.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use Desmos polygon coordinate plotting or quick visual scale matching when figures are drawn to scale in the digital interface.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: In triangle $ABC$ and triangle $DEF$, $\angle A = \angle D$, $\angle B = \angle E$, and $AB = DE$. Which postulate or theorem proves $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify that $\angle A$ and $\angle B$ are two angles in $\triangle ABC$ and $\angle D$ and $\angle E$ are the corresponding angles in $\triangle DEF$.
Step 2
Recognize that side $AB$ is the side included between $\angle A$ and $\angle B$, and side $DE$ is the side included between $\angle D$ and $\angle E$.
Step 3
Conclude that the congruence criterion is Angle-Side-Angle (ASA).
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Check the letter order in the given statement: $A$ matches $D$, $B$ matches $E$, and segment $AB$ connects the first two letters.
Step 2
Conclude that the side is between the two known angles.
Step 3
Select ASA instantly.
Concept 2

Concept 2: Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles (CPCTC)

Once two triangles are proven congruent, all corresponding pairs of sides and angles are strictly equal in measure.

  • If $\triangle PQR \cong \triangle STU$, then corresponding segments are equal: $PQ = ST$, $QR = TU$, and $PR = SU$.
  • Corresponding angles are equal: $\angle P = \angle S$, $\angle Q = \angle T$, and $\angle R = \angle U$.
  • Congruence statements must preserve vertex correspondence order: $\triangle PQR \cong \triangle STU$ means vertex $P$ maps to vertex $S$.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Write out the congruence statement, map corresponding algebraic expressions for sides or angles, equate them, and solve.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Look directly at the index positions of the letters in the congruence statement to instantly pair unknown lengths or angles without redrawing.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Step 2

Concept 3

Concept 3: Rigid Transformations & Congruence

Two geometric figures are congruent if and only if one can be mapped onto the other using a sequence of rigid transformations: translations, rotations, and reflections.

  • Translations shift every point of a figure by the same distance in a given direction: $(x, y) \to (x + h, y + k)$.
  • Rotations turn a figure around a fixed center point by a specified angle without altering size or shape.
  • Reflections flip a figure across a line of reflection, creating a mirror image that remains congruent to the original.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Apply coordinate transformation rules sequentially to test if coordinates map directly onto the target figure.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use the Desmos graphing calculator to plot points and visually verify reflections or rotations when coordinate rules are complex.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Concept 4

Concept 4: Congruence in Circles and Regular Polygons

Congruent circles have equal radii, and congruent central angles or arcs within the same circle or congruent circles intercept congruent chords.

  • In congruent circles, if two central angles are equal, their corresponding chords are equal in length.
  • Equal chords within congruent circles subtend equal arcs and equal central angles.
  • Regular polygons with an equal number of sides and equal side lengths are congruent.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Set arc measures or chord lengths equal to each other, form algebraic equations, and solve for the unknown variables.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Recognize symmetry properties in circle diagrams to immediately equate parallel or chord lengths without formal proofs.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

🚨 Top SAT Traps & Misconceptions

⚠️ SAT Trap: AAA Does Not Prove Congruence
Equating three angles (AAA) proves similarity, not congruence. Triangles can be the exact same shape but different sizes.
⚠️ SAT Trap: Incorrect Vertex Letter Mapping
Assuming sides match based on visual orientation rather than the explicit vertex order in the congruence statement ($\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$).
⚠️ SAT Trap: SSA Ambiguous Case
Two sides and a non-included angle (SSA or ASS) do not uniquely determine a triangle, meaning congruence is not guaranteed.
