Sector Area

Key Concepts & Worked Archetypes

Concept 1

Concept 1: Sector Area in Degrees

A sector is a portion of a circle enclosed by two radii and an arc. The area is proportional to the central angle relative to the full circle.

  • Area of a circle: $A = \pi r^2$
  • Sector Area formula: $A_{sector} = \frac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2$
  • Ratio property: $\frac{A_{sector}}{A_{circle}} = \frac{\theta}{360}$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Identify the radius and central angle, plug them into the formula, and simplify the fraction before multiplying by $\pi$.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Define the radius 'r' and angle 'theta' in Desmos, then type the formula directly to get the decimal or exact value.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: A circle has a radius of 6 and a central angle of 60 degrees. What is the area of the sector?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify $r = 6$ and $\theta = 60$.
Step 2
Set up the equation: $A = \frac{60}{360} \times \pi(6)^2$
Step 3
Simplify the fraction: $\frac{1}{6} \times 36\pi$
Step 4
Calculate final result: $6\pi$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Define r = 6 in Desmos.
Step 2
Define theta = 60 in Desmos.
Step 3
Type (theta/360) * pi * r^2 to get the result.
Concept 2

Concept 2: Sector Area in Radians

When the central angle is given in radians, the formula simplifies significantly because the full circle is $2\pi$ radians.

  • Full circle in radians: $2\pi$
  • Sector Area formula: $A = \frac{1}{2} r^2 \theta$
  • Conversion: $\text{degrees} \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \text{radians}$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Ensure the angle is in radians, then multiply half the square of the radius by the angle.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Set Desmos to 'Radians' mode (wrench icon) to avoid manual conversion errors.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: Find the area of a sector with radius 4 and central angle $\frac{\pi}{3}$ radians.

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify $r = 4$ and $\theta = \frac{\pi}{3}$.
Step 2
Set up the equation: $A = \frac{1}{2} (4)^2 (\frac{\pi}{3})$
Step 3
Square the radius: $A = \frac{1}{2} (16) (\frac{\pi}{3})$
Step 4
Final result: $A = \frac{8\pi}{3}$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Set Desmos to Radians mode.
Step 2
Type 0.5 * 4^2 * (pi/3).
Step 3
Convert to fraction if necessary.
Concept 3

Concept 3: Arc Length vs Sector Area

Arc length is the distance along the edge, while sector area is the 2D space inside. They share the same proportional relationship to the circle.

  • Arc Length $s = r\theta$ (where $\theta$ is in radians)
  • Sector Area $A = \frac{1}{2} rs$
  • Relationship: $A = \frac{s \times r}{2}$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Calculate the arc length first if given, then use the triangle-like area formula $A = \frac{1}{2}rs$.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use the ratio of arc length to circumference to find the fraction of the circle, then multiply by total area.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: A sector has an arc length of $4\pi$ and a radius of 6. Find the area.

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify $s = 4\pi$ and $r = 6$.
Step 2
Use the formula: $A = \frac{1}{2} r s$
Step 3
Substitute values: $A = \frac{1}{2} (6) (4\pi)$
Step 4
Final result: $A = 12\pi$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Type 0.5 * 6 * 4 * pi in Desmos.
Step 2
Read the result.

Question 4 Basic Sector Area Calculation
Medium

A sector of a circle has an area of $15\pi$ and a central angle of $135^{\circ}$. What is the radius of the circle?

Hint: Rearrange the sector area formula to solve for $r^2$, then take the square root.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Start with the formula: $\text{Area} = \frac{\theta}{360^{\circ}} \times \pi r^2$
Step 2
Substitute the given values: $15\pi = \frac{135}{360} \times \pi r^2$
Step 3
Reduce the fraction $\frac{135}{360}$ to $\frac{3}{8}$: $15\pi = \frac{3}{8} \pi r^2$
Step 4
Multiply both sides by $\frac{8}{3\pi}$: $r^2 = 15 \times \frac{8}{3} = 40$
Step 5
Solve for $r$: $r = \sqrt{40} = 2\sqrt{10}$

🚨 Top SAT Traps & Misconceptions

Using the degree formula with a radian measure (or vice versa) is the #1 error. Always check the units of the angle.

Questions often provide the diameter. Always divide by 2 before plugging into the area formula.
