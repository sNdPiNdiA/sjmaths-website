Key Concepts & Worked Archetypes

Concept 1

Concept 1: Linear Scale Factor

Linear scale factor ($k$) multiplies all 1D dimensions (side lengths, perimeters, radii, heights) of a geometric figure during a dilation.

  • If a figure is dilated by a scale factor $k$, every side length $s$ becomes $s' = k \cdot s$.
  • The perimeter $P$ of the dilated figure scales linearly: $P' = k \cdot P$.
  • If $k > 1$, the figure undergoes an enlargement; if $0 < k < 1$, it undergoes a reduction.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Identify the corresponding sides of the original and dilated figures, set up a ratio $k = \frac{\text{new length}}{\text{original length}}$, and solve for the unknown dimension using cross-multiplication.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use Desmos to define the original equation or ratio and evaluate numerical options instantly by testing scale factors.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: A triangle with side lengths $5$, $12$, and $13$ is dilated by a scale factor of $3$. What is the perimeter of the new triangle?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Calculate the perimeter of the original triangle by summing its side lengths: $P = 5 + 12 + 13$.
Step 2
Simplify the perimeter equation: $P = 30$.
Step 3
Multiply the original perimeter by the linear scale factor $k = 3$: $P' = 30 \times 3$.
Step 4
State the final calculated perimeter: $P' = 90$.
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1

Step 2
Multiply the result directly by $3$ in the next line: $30 \times 3$.
Step 3
Read final answer $90$ instantly.
Concept 2

Concept 2: Area Scale Factor

When a two-dimensional figure is dilated by a scale factor $k$, its area scales quadratically by a factor of $k^2$.

  • If the linear scale factor is $k$, the area scale factor is $k^2$.
  • Area of the new figure $A' = k^2 \cdot A$.
  • To find the linear scale factor $k$ from two known areas, take the square root of the area ratio: $k = \sqrt{\frac{A'}{A}}$.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Square the given linear scale factor $k$ to find the area multiplier, then multiply the original area by $k^2$.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Store $k$ as a variable in Desmos (e.g., $k = 2.5$) and evaluate $k^2 \times \text{Area}$ in a single keystroke.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: A rectangle has an area of $18\text{ cm}^2$. If it is dilated by a scale factor of $4$, what is the area of the new rectangle?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify the linear scale factor $k = 4$.
Step 2
Square the linear scale factor to find the area multiplier: $k^2 = 4^2$.
Step 3
Evaluate the squared scale factor: $k^2 = 16$.
Step 4
Multiply the original area by $16$: $A' = 18 \times 16 = 288$.
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Type the expression directly into Desmos: $18 \times 4^2$.
Step 2
Read the evaluated output of $288$ immediately.
Concept 3

Concept 3: Volume Scale Factor

When a three-dimensional solid is dilated by a scale factor $k$, its volume scales cubically by a factor of $k^3$.

  • If the linear scale factor is $k$, the volume scale factor is $k^3$.
  • Volume of the new solid $V' = k^3 \cdot V$.
  • To find the linear scale factor from two known volumes, take the cube root of the volume ratio: $k = \sqrt[3]{\frac{V'}{V}}$.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Cube the linear scale factor $k$ to determine the volume multiplier, then multiply the original volume by $k^3$.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: Two similar cylinders have heights in the ratio $2:3$. If the volume of the smaller cylinder is $40\text{ cm}^3$, what is the volume of the larger cylinder?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify the linear scale factor from the smaller to larger cylinder as $k = \frac{3}{2}$.
Step 2
Cube the linear scale factor to find the volume ratio: $k^3 = \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^3$.
Step 3
Simplify the cubed ratio: $k^3 = \frac{27}{8}$.
Step 4
Multiply the smaller volume by $\frac{27}{8}$: $V' = 40 \times \frac{27}{8} = 135$.
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Enter the calculation directly in Desmos: $40 \times (3/2)^3$.
Step 2
Read output $135$ instantly.
Concept 4

Concept 4: Coordinate Dilations & Centers

Dilating a point $(x, y)$ in the coordinate plane by scale factor $k$ from a center of dilation $(a, b)$ shifts both coordinates proportionally.

  • If the center of dilation is the origin $(0, 0)$, the rule is $(x, y) \rightarrow (kx, ky)$.
  • If the center is a point $(a, b)$, the rule is $(x, y) \rightarrow (a + k(x - a), b + k(y - b))$.
  • Distance from the center of dilation to any point on the figure is multiplied by $k$.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Subtract the center coordinates $(a, b)$ from the point, multiply the differences by $k$, and add the center coordinates back.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Define the center as variables $a$ and $b$ and point as $x$ and $y$ in Desmos to test transformations instantly.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: Point $P(3, 4)$ is dilated by a scale factor of $2$ with respect to the center $(1, 1)$. What are the coordinates of the image $P'$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Compute the horizontal and vertical distances from the center: $\Delta x = 3 - 1 = 2$ and $\Delta y = 4 - 1 = 3$.
Step 2
Multiply each distance by the scale factor $k = 2$: $\Delta x' = 2 \times 2 = 4$ and $\Delta y' = 3 \times 2 = 6$.
Step 3
Add the scaled distances back to the center coordinates: $x' = 1 + 4 = 5$ and $y' = 1 + 6 = 7$.
Step 4
Express the final coordinate pair: $(5, 7)$.
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Enter point coordinates in Desmos: $(1 + 2(3-1), 1 + 2(4-1))$.
Step 2
Read coordinate pair $(5, 7)$ immediately.

Revision, Traps & Desmos Syntax

Linear Scale Factor

$k = \frac{\text{new dimension}}{\text{original dimension}}$

Used for side lengths, perimeters, radii, and heights.

Area Scale Factor

$A' = k^2 \cdot A$

Used when converting or comparing 2D surface areas of similar figures.

Volume Scale Factor

$V' = k^3 \cdot V$

Used when converting or comparing 3D volumes of similar solids.

🚨 Top SAT Traps & Misconceptions

⚠️ SAT Trap: Squaring Volume Ratios
Students frequently apply $k^2$ instead of $k^3$ when scaling 3D volumes, or apply $k$ to area instead of $k^2$. Always match the exponent to the dimension of the measurement (1 for length, 2 for area, 3 for volume).
⚠️ SAT Trap: Non-Origin Centers of Dilation
Assuming a dilation always scales from the origin $(0, 0)$. Always check if a custom center point is specified in coordinate geometry questions.
