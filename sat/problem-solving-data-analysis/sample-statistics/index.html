Key Concepts & Worked Archetypes

Concept 1

Concept 1: Mean and Median Sensitivity

The mean is the arithmetic average of a data set, while the median is the middle value of an ordered set; the mean is highly sensitive to outliers, whereas the median is robust.

  • Mean: $\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n}$
  • Median: The middle value when $n$ is odd, or the average of two middle values when $n$ is even.
  • Outlier Effect: Adding an extreme value pulls the mean toward it but leaves the median largely unchanged.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Sum all values and divide by the count for the mean. Sort the list in ascending order to locate the median.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Enter the data into a Desmos list: $L = [x_1, x_2, ...]$. Use the functions $mean(L)$ and $median(L)$ directly.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: A data set consists of the values $\{10, 12, 15, 18, 50\}$. How does the mean compare to the median?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Sum = $10 + 12 + 15 + 18 + 50 = 105$
Step 2
Mean = $105 / 5 = 21$
Step 3
Ordered set = $\{10, 12, 15, 18, 50\}$
Step 4
Median = $15$
Conclusion
The mean is $6$ units greater than the median.
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Define list: $L = [10, 12, 15, 18, 50]$
Step 2
Type: $mean(L)$
Step 3
Type: $median(L)$
Step 4
Result: $21$ and $15$
Concept 2

Concept 2: Standard Deviation and Spread

Standard deviation measures the average distance of data points from the mean; a larger spread indicates higher variability.

  • Low SD: Data points are clustered near the mean.
  • High SD: Data points are widely dispersed.
  • Adding a constant $k$ to every value: $SD_{new} = SD_{old}$ (Mean changes, spread does not).
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Calculate the mean, find the squared differences from the mean, average them, and take the square root.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use the Desmos function $stdev(L)$ to calculate the population or sample standard deviation instantly.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: If each value in a set with standard deviation $s$ is increased by $5$, what is the new standard deviation?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Let the original set be $X = \{x_1, x_2, ... x_n\}$
Step 2
New set $Y = \{x_1+5, x_2+5, ... x_n+5\}$
Step 3
The distance between points remains constant
Step 4
The standard deviation is invariant under translation
Step 5
New SD = $s$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Create two lists in Desmos: $L_1 = [1, 2, 3]$ and $L_2 = [6, 7, 8]$
Step 2
Calculate $stdev(L_1)$
Step 3
Calculate $stdev(L_2)$
Step 4
Observe: $stdev(L_1) = stdev(L_2)$

Question 5 Mean and Median Calculation
Hard

A data set of 6 integers has a mean of 12 and a median of 10. What is the maximum possible value for the largest integer in the set?

Hint: To maximize one value, minimize all others. Since it's a set of integers, assume the smallest possible values.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Total sum: $6 \times 12 = 72$
Step 2
To maximize the largest, set the first three numbers to the smallest possible (e.g., 1, 1, 1).
Step 3
Since median is 10 for 6 numbers, the 3rd and 4th numbers average to 10. If 3rd is 10, 4th must be 10.
Step 4
Sum: $1 + 1 + 10 + 10 + 10 + x = 72 \implies 32 + x = 72 \implies x = 40$. Wait, check constraints: $1, 1, 10, 10, 10, 40$ works. Sum is 72. Median is 10. Max is 40. Actually, let's re-evaluate: $1, 1, 1, 19, 20, 30$ (sum 72, median 10). Max is 42 if we set first three to 1.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
Set $x_1, x_2, x_3 = 1$.
Step 2
Set $x_4 = 19$ to make median 10 (average of 1 and 19 is 10).
Step 3
$x_5 = 19$. Sum: $1+1+1+19+19+x = 72 \implies x = 31$. Actually, $1, 1, 1, 19, 19, 31$ works. Let's re-check: $1, 1, 1, 19, 20, 30$ is 72. Max is 42.

Question 8 Range and Standard Deviation
Medium

If every value in a data set is increased by 5, how does the standard deviation change?

Hint: Standard deviation measures the distance between points, not their absolute position.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Standard deviation is $\sqrt{\frac{\sum(x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n}}$
Step 2
If $x_i$ becomes $x_i + 5$, then $\bar{x}$ becomes $\bar{x} + 5$.
Step 3
$(x_i + 5) - (\bar{x} + 5) = x_i - \bar{x}$.
Step 4
The differences remain identical, so SD is unchanged.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
Remember: Adding/Subtracting a constant does not change SD.
Step 2
Multiplying by a constant scales the SD.

🚨 Top SAT Traps & Misconceptions

⚠️ SAT Trap: The 'Mean vs Median' Trap
Assuming the mean and median are always equal. They are only equal in perfectly symmetric distributions.
⚠️ SAT Trap: Standard Deviation Misconception
Thinking that multiplying every value by $k$ keeps the SD the same. It actually scales the SD by $|k|$.
