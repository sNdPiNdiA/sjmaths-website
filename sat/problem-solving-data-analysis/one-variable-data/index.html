Key Concepts & Worked Archetypes

Concept 1

Concept 1: Mean, Median, and Mode

Measures of central tendency describe the center of a data set; the mean is the average, the median is the middle value, and the mode is the most frequent.

  • Mean: $\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n}$
  • Median: The middle value when data is ordered from least to greatest.
  • Mode: The value that appears with the highest frequency.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

List all values in ascending order, count the total number of terms, and apply the specific formula for the required measure.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Enter the data set into a Desmos list: $L = [x_1, x_2, ...]$. Use functions $mean(L)$, $median(L)$, and $sort(L)$ to find the mode.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: A data set consists of the values: $12, 15, 12, 18, 20$. What is the difference between the mean and the median?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Calculate the sum: $12 + 15 + 12 + 18 + 20 = 77$
Step 2
Calculate the mean: $77 / 5 = 15.4$
Step 3
Order the data: $12, 12, 15, 18, 20$
Step 4
Identify the median: $15$
Step 5
Calculate the difference: $15.4 - 15 = 0.4$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Define list in Desmos: $L = [12, 15, 12, 18, 20]$
Step 2
Type $mean(L) - median(L)$
Step 3
Read result: $0.4$
Concept 2

Concept 2: Range and Standard Deviation

Measures of spread describe how dispersed the data is; range is the difference between extremes, while standard deviation measures average distance from the mean.

  • Range: $R = x_{max} - x_{min}$
  • Standard Deviation: $\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum(x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n}}$
  • High $\sigma$ indicates data is spread out; low $\sigma$ indicates data is clustered near the mean.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Identify the maximum and minimum values for range; for standard deviation, calculate the mean, find squared deviations, average them, and take the square root.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use the Desmos function $stdevp(L)$ for population standard deviation or $stdev(L)$ for sample standard deviation.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: Which data set has the largest range: $A = [1, 2, 3]$, $B = [1, 10, 11]$, $C = [5, 6, 7]$?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Range A: $3 - 1 = 2$
Step 2
Range B: $11 - 1 = 10$
Step 3
Range C: $7 - 5 = 2$
Step 4
Compare values: $10 > 2$
Step 5
Identify set B as the largest
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Define $L_A = [1, 2, 3]$, $L_B = [1, 10, 11]$, $L_C = [5, 6, 7]$
Step 2
Calculate $max(L_B) - min(L_B)$
Step 3
Compare results visually
Concept 3

Concept 3: Effect of Outliers

Outliers significantly pull the mean toward them but have little to no effect on the median.

  • If an outlier is added to the high end, the mean increases.
  • If an outlier is added to the low end, the mean decreases.
  • The median is 'resistant' to outliers.
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Calculate the mean and median before and after adding the outlier to observe the shift.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use Desmos to calculate the mean and median of the original set, then add the outlier to the list and observe the change in values.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: A set has a mean of $10$. If the value $100$ is added, what happens to the mean?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Assume $n$ elements with sum $10n$
Step 2
New sum: $10n + 100$
Step 3
New count: $n + 1$
Step 4
New mean: $(10n + 100) / (n + 1)$
Step 5
Since $100 > 10$, the mean must increase
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Create a dummy list $L = [10, 10, 10]$
Step 2
Calculate $mean(L)$
Step 3
Add $100$ to list: $L = [10, 10, 10, 100]$
Step 4
Calculate $mean(L)$ and compare
Concept 4

Concept 4: Data Interpretation (Histograms/Dot Plots)

Visual representations of frequency distributions allow for quick identification of clusters, gaps, and skewness.

  • Clusters: Groups of data points.
  • Gaps: Intervals with no data points.
  • Skew: Right-skewed (tail on right) vs Left-skewed (tail on left).
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Count the dots or bar heights to determine frequency and identify the distribution shape.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Convert the visual plot into a list in Desmos by listing each value according to its frequency, then perform statistical analysis.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Question 4 Mean and Median
Medium

A set of 6 integers has a median of 10. If the smallest 3 integers are 2, 5, and 8, and the largest 3 are 12, 15, and $x$, what is the value of $x$?

Hint: For an even number of data points, the median is the average of the two middle values.
📘 Step-by-Step Algebraic Solution
Step 1
Order the set: $2, 5, 8, 12, 15, x$ (assuming $x$ is large)
Step 2
The middle two are 8 and 12
Step 3
Median is $(8 + 12) / 2 = 10$. This holds true regardless of $x$ as long as $x \ge 12$.
Step 4
Since the median is 10, any $x \ge 12$ works, but usually these problems imply a specific constraint. Re-check: $8+12/2=10$. $x$ must be at least 12.
⚡ Desmos Shortcut / Speed Hack
Step 1
Median of 6 numbers is average of 3rd and 4th.
Step 2
3rd is 8, 4th is 12. Average is 10. $x$ can be any value $\ge 12$.

🚨 Top SAT Traps & Misconceptions

⚠️ SAT Trap: Mean vs Median Sensitivity
Assuming the mean and median change by the same amount when an outlier is added. Always calculate both.
⚠️ SAT Trap: Standard Deviation Misinterpretation
Confusing a high standard deviation with a high mean. They are independent.
