Key Concepts & Worked Archetypes

Concept 1

Concept 1: Population vs. Sample Inference

Statistical inference involves drawing conclusions about a large population based on data collected from a smaller, representative sample.

  • Representative samples must be randomly selected to avoid bias: $Sample \approx Population$
  • Margin of error decreases as sample size increases: $MOE \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$
  • Confidence intervals estimate the population parameter: $Parameter = Sample Statistic \pm MOE$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Identify the sample statistic, calculate the margin of error if provided, and apply the interval bounds to estimate the population range.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use Desmos to plot the confidence interval bounds as vertical lines to visualize the range and eliminate options that fall outside the calculated interval.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: A survey of 400 randomly selected voters shows 52% support Candidate A with a margin of error of 4%. What is the plausible range for the population support?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify the sample proportion: $p = 0.52$
Step 2
Identify the margin of error: $MOE = 0.04$
Step 3
Calculate the lower bound: $0.52 - 0.04 = 0.48$
Step 4
Calculate the upper bound: $0.52 + 0.04 = 0.56$
Step 5
The range is $0.48$ to $0.56$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Type '0.52 - 0.04' into Desmos
Step 2
Type '0.52 + 0.04' into Desmos
Step 3
Read the values 0.48 and 0.56 directly from the output
Concept 2

Concept 2: Margin of Error and Sample Size

The margin of error (MOE) quantifies the uncertainty in a sample statistic; larger samples yield more precise estimates.

  • Inverse relationship between sample size and MOE: $MOE_2 = MOE_1 \times \sqrt{\frac{n_1}{n_2}}$
  • Doubling the sample size does not halve the MOE: $MOE_{new} = \frac{MOE_{old}}{\sqrt{2}}$
  • To halve the MOE, the sample size must be quadrupled: $n_{new} = 4 \times n_{old}$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Set up a ratio comparing the two sample sizes and their corresponding margins of error to solve for the unknown variable.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use the Desmos calculator to compute the square root factors immediately rather than performing manual radical simplification.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: If a sample size of 100 results in a MOE of 10%, what is the MOE for a sample size of 400?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Set up the ratio: $\frac{MOE_2}{0.10} = \sqrt{\frac{100}{400}}$
Step 2
Simplify the fraction: $\frac{100}{400} = 0.25$
Step 3
Calculate the square root: $\sqrt{0.25} = 0.5$
Step 4
Solve for $MOE_2$: $0.10 \times 0.5 = 0.05$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Enter '0.10 * sqrt(100/400)' into Desmos
Step 2
Observe the result 0.05
