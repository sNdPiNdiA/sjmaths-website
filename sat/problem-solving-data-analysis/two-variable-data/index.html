Key Concepts & Worked Archetypes

Concept 1

Concept 1: Scatterplots and Line of Best Fit

Two-variable data represents relationships between two quantitative variables, often modeled by a linear equation $y = mx + b$.

  • Slope $m$ represents the rate of change: $m = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$
  • Y-intercept $b$ represents the initial value when $x = 0$
  • Residuals are defined as: $Residual = Actual - Predicted$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Identify two points on the line of best fit, calculate the slope, and solve for the intercept using $y = mx + b$.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use the Desmos regression feature by typing 'y1 ~ mx1 + b' to get exact values for m and b instantly.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: A scatterplot shows the relationship between study hours ($x$) and test scores ($y$). The line of best fit is $y = 5x + 60$. What is the predicted score for 4 hours of study?

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify the equation: $y = 5x + 60$
Step 2
Substitute $x = 4$ into the equation: $y = 5(4) + 60$
Step 3
Calculate the product: $y = 20 + 60$
Step 4
Final result: $y = 80$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Type 'y = 5x + 60' into Desmos
Step 2
Click the gear icon or use the table feature to find $x = 4$
Step 3
Read the corresponding $y$ value as 80
Concept 2

Concept 2: Interpreting Slope and Intercept

Contextualizing the mathematical components of a linear model within real-world scenarios.

  • Slope $m$ is the change in $y$ per unit increase in $x$
  • Intercept $b$ is the value of $y$ when the independent variable is zero
  • Units of slope are always $\frac{units of y}{units of x}$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Read the problem statement carefully to identify the 'starting amount' (intercept) and the 'per unit' cost or rate (slope).

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Look for keywords: 'per', 'each', 'every' for slope; 'initial', 'starting', 'flat fee' for intercept.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: A car rental company charges a flat fee of $50 plus $0.25 per mile. Write the linear model.

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify the flat fee as the intercept: $b = 50$
Step 2
Identify the rate per mile as the slope: $m = 0.25$
Step 3
Construct the equation: $y = 0.25x + 50$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Scan for the constant value: 50
Step 2
Scan for the rate value: 0.25
Step 3
Assemble as $y = mx + b$
Concept 3

Concept 3: Residuals and Model Accuracy

A residual is the vertical distance between an observed data point and the predicted value on the line of best fit.

  • Residual = $y_{observed} - y_{predicted}$
  • Positive residual: The model underestimated the value
  • Negative residual: The model overestimated the value
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Find the predicted $y$ value using the line equation for a given $x$, then subtract it from the actual $y$ value provided in the scatterplot.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Use Desmos to plot the point $(x, y)$ and the line $y = mx + b$, then visually measure the vertical gap.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

Problem: For $x = 2$, the actual $y$ is 15. The line of best fit predicts $y = 12$. Find the residual.

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:
Step 1
Identify observed value: $y_{obs} = 15$
Step 2
Identify predicted value: $y_{pred} = 12$
Step 3
Calculate residual: $15 - 12$
Step 4
Result: $3$
⚡ Speed / Desmos Tactic:
Step 1
Input $15 - 12$ into the Desmos calculator
Step 2
Result: 3
Concept 4

Concept 4: Two-Way Tables

Categorical data organized into rows and columns to show frequencies or relative frequencies.

  • Total frequency is the sum of all cells
  • Conditional probability: $P(A|B) = \frac{Count(A \cap B)}{Count(B)}$
  • Relative frequency: $\frac{Part}{Whole}$
📘 Traditional Algebraic Method

Sum the relevant row or column to find the denominator, then identify the specific cell for the numerator.

⚡ SAT Speed Trick & Desmos Hack

Convert the table into a matrix in Desmos or simply use the calculator to perform the division of the specific cell by the row/column total.

💡 Worked SAT Archetype Example

📘 Step-by-Step Textbook Solution:

Identify the part: $40$
Step 2
Identify the total: $100$
Step 3
Calculate probability: $\frac{40}{100}$
Step 4

Step 2
Result: 0.4

Always check if the question asks for the rate of change (slope) or the starting value (intercept).
⚠️ SAT Trap: Residual Sign
Remember that $y - \hat{y}$ can be negative if the point is below the line.
