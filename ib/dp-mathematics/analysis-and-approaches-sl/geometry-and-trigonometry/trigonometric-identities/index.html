  1. 3.6Identities and relationships

Learn the ideas

Key ideas and methods

  • The fundamental identity is sin²x+cos²x=1.
  • Dividing by cos²x gives 1+tan²x=sec²x when cos x≠0; dividing by sin²x gives 1+cot²x=cosec²x when sin x≠0.
  • To verify an identity, simplify one side using valid identities and algebra. Do not cancel terms across addition as if they were factors.

Follow the reasoning

Worked example

This original example shows the method step by step; it is not copied from an IB examination paper.

Worked example 1

Simplify using the fundamental identity

Question. Simplify (1−cos²x)/sin x where the expression is defined.

  1. Use 1−cos²x=sin²x.
  2. Then sin²x/sin x=sin x, provided sin x≠0.

Answer: sin x for sin x≠0.

Try it yourself

Practice with worked answers

  1. Rewrite 1−sin²x in terms of cosine.

    Show worked answer

    1−sin²x=cos²x.

  2. Simplify tan x·cos x where cos x≠0.

    Show worked answer

    tan x·cos x=(sin x/cos x)cos x=sin x.

Avoid these slips

Common mistakes

  • Dividing by a trigonometric expression without noting when it is zero.
  • Treating 1−cos²x as (1−cos x)².
  • Changing only one side of an identity and calling the result a proof.
