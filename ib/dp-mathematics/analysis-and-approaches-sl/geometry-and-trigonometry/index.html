Learn the ideas

Key ideas and methods

Diagrams, triangles and circle measure

  • A clear diagram distinguishes known lengths and angles from unknowns. In right triangles, apply SOH CAH TOA or Pythagoras. In non-right triangles, apply the Sine Rule $\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}$ or the Cosine Rule $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$.
  • Angle bearings are measured clockwise from True North ($000^\circ$ to $360^\circ$) using 3 digits.
  • Radian measure is defined by $\theta = \frac{s}{r}$. In radians, arc length is $s = r\theta$, sector area is $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$, and segment area is $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2(\theta - \sin\theta)$.

Exact trigonometric values and identities

  • Recall exact values: $\sin\frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$, $\cos\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{1}{2}$, $\sin\frac{\pi}{4} = \cos\frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, $\tan\frac{\pi}{4} = 1$. Quadrant signs follow ASTC (All, Sine, Tangent, Cosine).
  • The fundamental Pythagorean identity is $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$. The double-angle formulas are $\sin 2\theta = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta$ and $\cos 2\theta = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta = 2\cos^2\theta - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta$.
  • The ambiguous sine rule case (SSA) arises when $a < b$ and $a > b\sin A$, yielding two valid angles: acute $B_1$ and obtuse $B_2 = 180^\circ - B_1$ (provided $A + B_2 < 180^\circ$).

Trigonometric graphs and equations

  • For $y = a\sin(b(x - c)) + d$, amplitude is $|a|$, period is $\frac{2\pi}{|b|}$ (or $\frac{\pi}{|b|}$ for tangent), principal axis is $y = d$, and horizontal phase shift is $c$.
  • When solving trigonometric equations over finite domains $[0, 2\pi]$, identify reference angles, locate active quadrants, and adjust intervals when dealing with multi-arguments (e.g. $2x$ requires checking $[0, 4\pi]$).

Follow the reasoning

Worked examples

These original examples show common reasoning steps and standard IB notation.

Worked example 1

Find an arc length and sector area

Question. A circle has radius $r = 6\text{ cm}$ and a sector angle of $\theta = \frac{5\pi}{6}\text{ rad}$. Calculate the arc length and the area of the sector in exact form.

  1. $s = r\theta = 6 \cdot \frac{5\pi}{6} = 5\pi\text{ cm}$.
  2. $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta = \frac{1}{2}(36)\left(\frac{5\pi}{6}\right) = 18 \cdot \frac{5\pi}{6} = 15\pi\text{ cm}^2$.

Answer: Arc length $5\pi\text{ cm}$; area $15\pi\text{ cm}^2$.

Worked example 2

Check the ambiguous case of the sine rule

Question. In $\triangle ABC$, $a = 8\text{ cm}$, $A = 30^\circ$, and $b = 12\text{ cm}$. Find the possible values of angle $B$.

  1. Apply the Sine Rule: $\frac{\sin B}{b} = \frac{\sin A}{a} \implies \sin B = \frac{12\sin 30^\circ}{8} = \frac{12(0.5)}{8} = 0.75$.
  2. Acute solution: $B_1 = \arcsin(0.75) \approx 48.6^\circ$.
  3. Obtuse candidate: $B_2 = 180^\circ - 48.6^\circ = 131.4^\circ$.
  4. Verification: $A + B_2 = 30^\circ + 131.4^\circ = 161.4^\circ < 180^\circ$, so both triangles are geometrically valid.

Answer: $B \approx 48.6^\circ$ or $B \approx 131.4^\circ$.

Worked example 3

Solve a trigonometric equation on an interval

Question. Solve $2\sin x = \sqrt{3}$ for $0 \le x < 2\pi$.

  1. Isolate the trig ratio: $\sin x = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
  2. The reference angle is $x_{\text{ref}} = \frac{\pi}{3}$.
  3. Since $\sin x > 0$, solutions lie in Quadrants I and II:
    • Quadrant I: $x = \frac{\pi}{3}$
    • Quadrant II: $x = \pi - \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{2\pi}{3}$

Answer: $x = \frac{\pi}{3}$ or $x = \frac{2\pi}{3}$.

Try it yourself

Practice with worked answers

  1. Question 1. A sector of a circle of radius $4\text{ cm}$ subtends an angle of $1.2\text{ rad}$. Calculate its perimeter and area.

    Show worked answer

    Arc length $s = r\theta = 4(1.2) = 4.8\text{ cm}$. Total perimeter $P = 2r + s = 8 + 4.8 = 12.8\text{ cm}$. Area $A = \frac{1}{2}(4^2)(1.2) = 9.6\text{ cm}^2$.

  2. Question 2. Solve $\cos x = \frac{1}{2}$ for $0 \le x < 2\pi$.

    Show worked answer

    Reference angle is $\frac{\pi}{3}$. Cosine is positive in Quadrants I and IV: $x = \frac{\pi}{3}$ and $x = 2\pi - \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{5\pi}{3}$.

  3. Question 3. In right-angled triangle $PQR$, the hypotenuse is $13\text{ cm}$ and one leg is $5\text{ cm}$. Find the exact length of the other leg and the acute angle $\theta$ opposite the $5\text{ cm}$ leg to 1 decimal place.

    Show worked answer

    Pythagorean theorem: $\text{leg} = \sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{144} = 12\text{ cm}$. Angle $\sin\theta = \frac{5}{13} \implies \theta = \arcsin\left(\frac{5}{13}\right) \approx 22.6^\circ$.

Avoid these slips

Common mistakes

  • Using degree values directly in $s = r\theta$ or $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$ without converting to radians first.
  • Assuming the Ambiguous Case always produces two triangles without verifying $A + B_2 < 180^\circ$.
  • Forgetting that $\cos 2x = 0$ over $[0, 2\pi]$ doubles the number of solutions because $2x \in [0, 4\pi]$.
  • Using the Sine Rule when the Cosine Rule is required (e.g., given three sides SSS or two sides and included angle SAS).

Before you move on

Revision checklist

  • Can I calculate distances, midpoints, and angles in 3D cuboids and pyramids?
  • Can I fluently convert between degrees and radians and calculate arc lengths, sector areas, and segment areas?
  • Can I apply the Sine Rule (including the ambiguous case) and Cosine Rule accurately?
  • Can I manipulate trigonometric identities ($\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$, double angles) to solve quadratic trigonometric equations?
  • Can I extract amplitude, period, and phase shifts from sinusoidal modeling functions?
