Learn the ideas

Key ideas and methods

Diagrams, triangles and circle measure

  • A clear diagram helps distinguish a given length or angle from an unknown. Mark right angles, equal lengths and angle labels before selecting a formula.
  • In a right-angled triangle, use sine, cosine and tangent with the correct opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse sides. For a non-right triangle, the sine rule relates side lengths to opposite angles; the cosine rule relates all three sides and an included angle.
  • Radians measure angles by arc length divided by radius. For θ in radians, arc length s = rθ and sector area A = ½r²θ. Use radians in these formulae.

Exact trigonometric values and identities

  • Know the exact sine, cosine and tangent values for standard angles and use the unit circle to determine signs in each quadrant.
  • The identity sin²x + cos²x = 1 connects sine and cosine. Other relationships follow by dividing by sin²x or cos²x when the expressions are defined.
  • The sine rule can have two possible angles in the ambiguous case because sin B = sin(180° − B). Use the triangle angle sum and the given side information to decide which candidates are valid.

Trigonometric graphs and equations

  • The graphs of y = sin x and y = cos x repeat every 2π radians; y = tan x repeats every π and has vertical asymptotes where it is undefined.
  • For y = a sin(b(x − c)) + d, the amplitude is |a|, the period is 2π/|b|, the horizontal shift is c and the vertical shift is d. Read the equation and graph together rather than relying on appearance alone.
  • When solving a trigonometric equation, state the interval, find all solutions in that interval and use the period and symmetry to avoid missing roots. A graphical solution may be approximate; an analytical solution can be exact.

Follow the reasoning

Worked examples

These original examples show common reasoning steps. They are not copied from IB examination papers.

Worked example 1

Find an arc length and sector area

Question. A circle has radius 6 cm and a sector angle of 5π/6 radians. Find the arc length and area of the sector.

  1. s = rθ = 6(5π/6) = 5π cm.
  2. A = ½r²θ = ½(36)(5π/6) = 15π cm².

Answer: Arc length 5π cm; area 15π cm².

Worked example 2

Check the ambiguous case of the sine rule

Question. In a triangle, a = 8 is opposite A = 30° and b = 12 is opposite B. Find the possible values of B.

  1. By the sine rule, sin B / 12 = sin 30° / 8, so sin B = 0.75.
  2. The calculator gives B ≈ 48.6°. The second angle with the same sine is 180° − 48.6° = 131.4°.
  3. Both fit with A = 30° because each leaves a positive angle for the third vertex.

Answer: B ≈ 48.6° or 131.4°.

Worked example 3

Solve a trigonometric equation on an interval

Question. Solve 2 sin x = √3 for 0 ≤ x < 2π.

  1. Divide by 2: sin x = √3/2.
  2. The reference angle is π/3 and sine is positive in quadrants I and II.
  3. The solutions in the requested interval are π/3 and 2π/3.

Answer: x = π/3 or x = 2π/3.

Try it yourself

Practice with worked answers

  1. Question 1. A sector has radius 4 cm and angle 1.2 radians. Find its arc length and area.

    Show worked answer

    s = rθ = 4.8 cm. A = ½r²θ = ½(16)(1.2) = 9.6 cm².

  2. Question 2. Solve cos x = 1/2 for 0 ≤ x < 2π.

    Show worked answer

    Cosine is 1/2 at reference angle π/3 in quadrants I and IV, so x = π/3 or 5π/3.

  3. Question 3. A right triangle has hypotenuse 13 and one leg 5. Find the other leg and the acute angle opposite the leg of length 5.

    Show worked answer

    The other leg is √(13² − 5²) = 12. The angle satisfies sin θ = 5/13, so θ ≈ 22.6°.

Avoid these slips

Common mistakes

  • Using degrees in s = rθ or A = ½r²θ instead of radians.
  • Using the sine rule with a side and a non-opposite angle.
  • Returning only the principal inverse-trigonometric value and missing other solutions in the interval.
  • Leaving off units for a length or area.

Before you move on

Revision checklist

  • Can I choose between right-triangle ratios, the sine rule and the cosine rule?
  • Can I convert between degrees and radians and use circle formulae with radians?
  • Can I use exact values, identities and quadrant signs to solve equations?
  • Can I find every solution within a stated interval?
