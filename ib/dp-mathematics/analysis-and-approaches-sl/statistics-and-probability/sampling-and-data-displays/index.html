Learn the ideas

Key ideas and methods

  • A random sample gives members of a population a known chance of selection; a convenience sample may systematically miss groups.
  • In a histogram with unequal class widths, frequency density = frequency/class width, so bar area represents frequency.
  • A cumulative-frequency graph shows the number of observations at or below a value; use it to estimate medians, quartiles and proportions below a threshold.

Follow the reasoning

Worked example

This original example shows the method step by step; it is not copied from an IB examination paper.

Worked example 1

Compare histogram bar heights

Question. A class interval of width 5 has frequency 20. Find its frequency density.

  1. Use frequency density = frequency ÷ class width.
  2. 20 ÷ 5 = 4.

Answer: Frequency density 4 per unit.

Try it yourself

Practice with worked answers

  1. A histogram class has width 8 and frequency 24. What is its density?

    Show worked answer

    24/8=3.

  2. Name one concern with asking only students who use the school library about reading habits.

    Show worked answer

    It is a convenience sample and may over-represent students who already use the library, creating selection bias.

Avoid these slips

Common mistakes

  • Comparing histogram bar heights when class widths differ.
  • Assuming a large sample is automatically representative.
  • Reading cumulative frequency as the count above a value.
