Normal Distribution and Z-Values

A normal model is centered at its mean and scaled by its standard deviation. Standardization converts a raw value to a common scale.

Learn the ideas

Key ideas and methods

  • For X~N(μ,σ²), the mean is μ and the standard deviation is σ; the second parameter in this notation is the variance.
  • The standardized value is z=(x−μ)/σ. Positive z is above the mean and negative z is below it.
  • For probabilities, specify the tail or interval. A calculator’s normal CDF uses lower and upper bounds, mean and standard deviation; check which tail the question asks for.

Follow the reasoning

Worked example

This original example shows the method step by step; it is not copied from an IB examination paper.

Worked example 1

Standardize a score

Question. A normal variable has mean 100 and standard deviation 15. Find the z-value for x=130.

  1. z=(x−μ)/σ.
  2. z=(130−100)/15=2.

Answer: z=2, so 130 is two standard deviations above the mean.

Try it yourself

Practice with worked answers

  1. X has mean 70 and standard deviation 8. Find the z-value for 58.

    Show worked answer

    z=(58−70)/8=−1.5.

  2. What raw value corresponds to z=1.2 when μ=50 and σ=5?

    Show worked answer

    x=μ+zσ=50+1.2(5)=56.

Avoid these slips

Common mistakes

  • Dividing by variance instead of standard deviation.
  • Reading the second parameter in N(μ,σ²) as σ when it represents σ².
  • Calculating the opposite tail from the one requested.
