Correlation and Regression Lines

Correlation describes association; regression predicts one variable from another. The direction of prediction determines which regression line to use.

Learn the ideas

Key ideas and methods

  • A correlation coefficient summarizes the strength and direction of a linear association, not a causal relationship.
  • The regression of y on x predicts y from x. The regression of x on y predicts x from y; they are not interchangeable.
  • Avoid extrapolating far beyond the observed data. Interpret the prediction in context and consider whether the relationship is plausibly linear.

Follow the reasoning

Worked example

This original example shows the method step by step; it is not copied from an IB examination paper.

Worked example 1

Choose the direction of prediction

Question. A fitted line for predicting test score y from study hours x is y=3.2x+5. Estimate the score for 10 hours.

  1. The question gives a regression of y on x, matching the requested prediction.
  2. Substitute x=10: y=3.2(10)+5=37.

Answer: Estimated score 37, if 10 hours is within a reasonable range of the observed data.

Try it yourself

Practice with worked answers

  1. A line predicts x from y. Which variable is the input to substitute when predicting x?

    Show worked answer

    Substitute the known y-value because the line models x as a function of y.

  2. A strong correlation is observed between ice-cream sales and swimming incidents. Does this show that ice-cream causes incidents?

    Show worked answer

    No. Correlation alone does not establish causation; temperature or season could affect both.

Avoid these slips

Common mistakes

  • Using the regression line in the wrong direction.
  • Claiming that correlation proves causation.
  • Extrapolating without warning beyond the data range.
