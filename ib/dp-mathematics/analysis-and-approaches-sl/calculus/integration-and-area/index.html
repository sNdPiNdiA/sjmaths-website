Integration and Area

An indefinite integral reverses differentiation; a definite integral measures signed accumulation. Geometric area may require changing the sign or subtracting curves.

Learn the ideas

Key ideas and methods

  • Reverse the power rule: ∫xⁿdx=xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1)+C for n≠−1. Always include C for an indefinite integral.
  • For substitution, choose an inner expression u and include du; change limits when evaluating a definite integral in u.
  • A definite integral is signed area. For geometric area, integrate upper minus lower and split at intersections or axis crossings when the order changes.

Follow the reasoning

Worked example

This original example shows the method step by step; it is not copied from an IB examination paper.

Worked example 1

Find area between a line and a curve

Question. Find the area between y=4 and y=x² for 0≤x≤2.

  1. On this interval, 4 is above x².
  2. Area=∫₀²(4−x²)dx.
  3. Evaluate [4x−x³/3]₀²=8−8/3.

Answer: 16/3 square units.

Try it yourself

Practice with worked answers

  1. Evaluate ∫(6x²−4)dx.

    Show worked answer

    2x³−4x+C.

  2. Evaluate ∫₀¹2x(x²+1)³dx.

    Show worked answer

    Let u=x²+1, du=2x dx, limits 1 to 2. ∫₁²u³du=[u⁴/4]₁²=15/4.

Avoid these slips

Common mistakes

  • Omitting +C from an indefinite integral.
  • Forgetting to change or back-substitute limits after a substitution.
  • Treating signed integral as geometric area when the curve is below the axis.
