  1. 5.9Kinematic problems

Learn the ideas

Key ideas and methods

  • If s(t) is position, v(t)=s′(t) is velocity and a(t)=v′(t)=s″(t) is acceleration.
  • Displacement from t=a to b is s(b)−s(a), equal to the signed integral of velocity. Distance is the total length traveled and is never negative.
  • A change in direction can occur when velocity changes sign. Split the time interval at such turning points to calculate total distance.

Follow the reasoning

Worked example

This original example shows the method step by step; it is not copied from an IB examination paper.

Worked example 1

Separate displacement and distance

Question. A particle has s(t)=t³−6t²+9t for 0≤t≤3. Find its displacement and total distance.

  1. v(t)=3t²−12t+9=3(t−1)(t−3), so direction changes at t=1.
  2. s(0)=0, s(1)=4 and s(3)=0.
  3. Displacement is 0−0=0. Distance is |4−0|+|0−4|=8.

Answer: Displacement 0; total distance 8 units.

Try it yourself

Practice with worked answers

  1. If s(t)=4t²+2t, find v(t) and a(t).

    Show worked answer

    v=8t+2 and a=8.

  2. A particle moves from position 2 to position −5. What are its displacement and distance if it moves directly there?

    Show worked answer

    Displacement −5−2=−7; distance 7 units.

Avoid these slips

Common mistakes

  • Calling displacement and distance the same quantity.
  • Forgetting that velocity can be negative while speed is non-negative.
  • Differentiating position twice when only velocity is requested.
