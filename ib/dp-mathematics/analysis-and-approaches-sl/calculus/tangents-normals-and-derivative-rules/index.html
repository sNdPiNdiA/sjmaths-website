Tangents, Normals and Derivative Rules

The derivative gives the tangent gradient at a point. More complex functions need product, chain or quotient rules before the line equation can be written.

Learn the ideas

Key ideas and methods

  • Product rule: (uv)′=u′v+uv′. Chain rule: d/dx f(g(x))=f′(g(x))g′(x). Quotient rule: (u/v)′=(u′v−uv′)/v².
  • A tangent at (a,f(a)) with gradient m has equation y−f(a)=m(x−a).
  • A normal is perpendicular to the tangent; if the tangent gradient is m≠0, the normal gradient is −1/m. Handle a horizontal tangent as a vertical normal separately.

Follow the reasoning

Worked example

This original example shows the method step by step; it is not copied from an IB examination paper.

Worked example 1

Differentiate and find a tangent

Question. For y=(x²+1)eˣ, find the tangent at x=0.

  1. By the product rule, y′=2xeˣ+(x²+1)eˣ=eˣ(x+1)².
  2. At x=0, y=1 and y′=1.
  3. Use y−1=1(x−0).

Answer: The tangent is y=x+1.

Try it yourself

Practice with worked answers

  1. Differentiate sin(x²) with respect to x.

    Show worked answer

    By the chain rule, 2x cos(x²).

  2. Find the normal gradient to a tangent with gradient 3.

    Show worked answer

    The normal gradient is −1/3.

Avoid these slips

Common mistakes

  • Leaving out the derivative of the inner function in the chain rule.
  • Using u′v′ for a product.
  • Using the tangent gradient for the normal.
