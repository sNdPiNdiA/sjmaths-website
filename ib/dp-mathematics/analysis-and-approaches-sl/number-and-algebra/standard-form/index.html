Standard Form (Scientific Notation)

Representing very large and microscopic quantities with scientific accuracy, executing non-calculator powers-of-ten operations, and using the IB 3 significant figures rule.

SL 1.1 Paper 1 & Paper 2 Core AA Syllabus Prerequisites: Exponent Rules, Rounding to 3 s.f.
1.1

Definition & Normalization of Standard Form

  • Official IB Syllabus Definition: A real number expressed in the form: $$a \times 10^k \quad \text{where } 1 \le a < 10 \text{ and } k \in \mathbb{Z}$$ The coefficient $a$ is called the mantissa or significand, and $k$ is the integer exponent (order of magnitude).
  • The Strict Coefficient Bounds ($1 \le a < 10$):
    • $a$ can equal $1$, but $a$ can never equal or exceed $10$.
    • Expressions like $45 \times 10^3$ or $0.35 \times 10^{-4}$ are not in standard form.
  • Decimal Point Shift Rules:
    • Moving decimal point left: increases $k$ by the number of steps ($45000 = 4.5 \times 10^4$).
    • Moving decimal point right: decreases $k$ by the number of steps ($0.00038 = 3.8 \times 10^{-4}$).
  • Normalization Protocol: Whenever a computation yields $a \ge 10$ or $a < 1$, adjust the coefficient and exponent immediately: $$48.6 \times 10^5 = (4.86 \times 10^1) \times 10^5 = 4.86 \times 10^6$$ $$0.027 \times 10^{-3} = (2.7 \times 10^{-2}) \times 10^{-3} = 2.7 \times 10^{-5}$$

Example 1

Converting & Normalizing into Standard Form

Convert the following into standard form $a \times 10^k$ where $1 \le a < 10$ and $k \in \mathbb{Z}$:
(a) $0.0000728$
(b) $842 \times 10^5$
(c) $0.059 \times 10^{-6}$

  1. Part (a): $0.0000728$
    Move decimal point $5$ places to the right to place it between $7$ and $2$: $$0.0000728 = 7.28 \times 10^{-5}$$
  2. Part (b): $842 \times 10^5$
    Convert coefficient $842 = 8.42 \times 10^2$: $$842 \times 10^5 = (8.42 \times 10^2) \times 10^5 = 8.42 \times 10^{2+5} = 8.42 \times 10^7$$
  3. Part (c): $0.059 \times 10^{-6}$
    Convert coefficient $0.059 = 5.9 \times 10^{-2}$: $$0.059 \times 10^{-6} = (5.9 \times 10^{-2}) \times 10^{-6} = 5.9 \times 10^{-2 + (-6)} = 5.9 \times 10^{-8}$$

Answer: (a) $7.28 \times 10^{-5}$; (b) $8.42 \times 10^7$; (c) $5.9 \times 10^{-8}$

1.1

Non-Calculator Operations in Standard Form (Paper 1)

  • Multiplication Law: Multiply coefficients and add powers of $10$: $$(a \times 10^p) \times (b \times 10^q) = (a \times b) \times 10^{p+q}$$ Always normalize the resulting product if $a \times b \ge 10$.
  • Division Law: Divide coefficients and subtract powers of $10$: $$\frac{a \times 10^p}{b \times 10^q} = \left(\frac{a}{b}\r\r\r\right) \times 10^{p-q}$$ Always normalize if $\frac{a}{b} < 1$.
  • Addition and Subtraction (Exponent Alignment): You cannot add or subtract coefficients directly unless both numbers share the same power of $10$!
    1. Identify the higher exponent and convert the smaller number to match it.
    2. Add or subtract the adjusted coefficients: $(a + b') \times 10^{p}$.
    3. Normalize the answer if necessary.

Example 2

Evaluating Non-Calculator Arithmetic in Standard Form

Without using a calculator, evaluate the following, expressing your answers in the form $a \times 10^k$ where $1 \le a < 10$ and $k \in \mathbb{Z}$:
(a) $\frac{(4.5 \times 10^7) \times (8.0 \times 10^{-3})}{1.5 \times 10^2}$
(b) $(6.2 \times 10^6) + (7.9 \times 10^5)$

  1. Part (a): Evaluate numerator product first: $$(4.5 \times 8.0) \times 10^{7 + (-3)} = 36.0 \times 10^4$$ Divide by denominator $1.5 \times 10^2$: $$\frac{36.0 \times 10^4}{1.5 \times 10^2} = \left(\frac{36.0}{1.5}\r\r\r\right) \times 10^{4 - 2} = 24.0 \times 10^2$$ Normalize: $24.0 \times 10^2 = 2.4 \times 10^1 \times 10^2 = 2.4 \times 10^3$.
  2. Part (b): Match exponents to $10^6$: $$7.9 \times 10^5 = 0.79 \times 10^6$$ Add the coefficients: $$(6.2 + 0.79) \times 10^6 = 6.99 \times 10^6$$ Since $1 \le 6.99 < 10$, the expression is already normalized.

Answer: (a) $2.4 \times 10^3$; (b) $6.99 \times 10^6$

1.1

Significant Figures & The IB 3 s.f. Examination Rule

  • Official IB Accuracy Convention: Unless otherwise specified in a question, all non-exact final numerical answers must be given to exactly 3 significant figures (or exact fractions/surds where appropriate).
  • Rules for Identifying Significant Figures in Standard Form: In $a \times 10^k$, all digits written in the coefficient $a$ are significant:
    • $4.5 \times 10^3 \implies 2\text{ s.f.}$
    • $4.50 \times 10^3 \implies 3\text{ s.f.}$ (the trailing zero is significant and required!).
    • $4.500 \times 10^3 \implies 4\text{ s.f.}$
  • Rounding Protocol: Inspect the 4th significant digit:
    • If $\ge 5$, round the 3rd digit up: $6.487 \times 10^4 \to 6.49 \times 10^4$.
    • If $< 5$, keep the 3rd digit: $2.143 \times 10^{-2} \to 2.14 \times 10^{-2}$.
  • Intermediate Calculation Rule: Always retain at least 4 to 5 significant figures (or store values in calculator memory) during multi-step working. Rounding prematurely causes catastrophic accuracy loss that forfeits final accuracy marks ($A0$).

Example 3

Multi-Step Calculation with 3 s.f. Rounding

Calculate $\sqrt{\frac{5.84 \times 10^8}{3.19 \times 10^3}}$, giving your answer in standard form correct to $3$ significant figures.

  1. Evaluate the radicand (inside fraction) with full precision: $$\frac{5.84 \times 10^8}{3.19 \times 10^3} = \left(\frac{5.84}{3.19}\r\r\r\right) \times 10^{8 - 3} = 1.830721 \times 10^5$$
  2. Take the square root: $$\sqrt{1.830721 \times 10^5} = \sqrt{183072.1} \approx 427.869$$
  3. Convert to standard form: $$427.869 = 4.27869 \times 10^2$$
  4. Round to 3 significant figures: The 4th significant digit is $8 \ge 5$, so round $7$ up to $8$: $$4.28 \times 10^2$$

Answer: $4.28 \times 10^2$

1.1

Orders of Magnitude & Scientific Modeling

  • Order of Magnitude Comparison: The order of magnitude is the power of $10$ closest to the number. The ratio of two scales $10^p$ and $10^q$ is $10^{p - q}$. For instance, a planetary distance of $10^{11} \text{ m}$ is $10^{11 - (-10)} = 10^{21}$ times larger than an atomic radius of $10^{-10} \text{ m}$.
  • Calculator $E$ Notation Warning: Calculators often display $3.45\text{E}8$ or $3.45\text{E}-5$. Never write $E$ on an IB examination script! You must write the full mathematical form: $3.45 \times 10^8$ or $3.45 \times 10^{-5}$.
  • Unit Conversions with Standard Form: Convert base units before calculating: $$\text{kilometres to metres: } \times 10^3, \quad \text{millimetres to metres: } \times 10^{-3}, \quad \text{hours to seconds: } \times 3600$$

Example 4

Real-World Astronomical Modeling

The distance from Earth to the Sun is approximately $1.496 \times 10^8\text{ km}$. Light travels at a speed of $3.00 \times 10^8\text{ m s}^{-1}$.
(a) Convert the Earth-Sun distance into metres in standard form.
(b) Calculate the time in seconds it takes for light to travel from the Sun to Earth, giving your answer to $3$ significant figures.

  1. Part (a): Convert km to metres ($1\text{ km} = 10^3\text{ m}$): $$d = (1.496 \times 10^8\text{ km}) \times 10^3\text{ m/km} = 1.496 \times 10^{8+3}\text{ m} = 1.496 \times 10^{11}\text{ m}$$
  2. Part (b): Use time formula $t = \frac{d}{v}$: $$t = \frac{1.496 \times 10^{11}}{3.00 \times 10^8} = \left(\frac{1.496}{3.00}\r\r\r\right) \times 10^{11 - 8} = 0.498667 \times 10^3$$
  3. Normalize and round to 3 significant figures: $$0.498667 \times 10^3 = 4.98667 \times 10^2 \approx 4.99 \times 10^2\text{ seconds} \quad (\text{or } 499\text{ s})$$

Answer: (a) $1.496 \times 10^{11}\text{ m}$; (b) $4.99 \times 10^2\text{ s}$

Example 5 (Archetype 3 & 2)

Measurement Estimation & Percentage Error Analysis

A rectangular plot of land has exact measured length $L = 148.6\text{ m}$ and width $W = 72.4\text{ m}$. A surveyor estimates the length as $150\text{ m}$ and width as $70\text{ m}$.
(a) Calculate the exact area $A_E$ and the surveyor's estimated area $A_A$. [3 marks]
(b) Calculate the percentage error in the surveyor's estimated area, giving your answer to $3$ significant figures. [3 marks]

  1. Part (a): Compute exact and estimated areas: $$A_E = 148.6 \times 72.4 = 10\,758.64\text{ m}^2 \quad [M1][A1]$$ $$A_A = 150 \times 70 = 10\,500\text{ m}^2 \quad [A1]$$
  2. Part (b): Apply the percentage error formula: $$\epsilon = \left| \frac{v_A - v_E}{v_E} \r\r\r\right| \times 100\% = \left| \frac{10\,500 - 10\,758.64}{10\,758.64} \r\r\r\right| \times 100\% \quad [M1]$$ $$\epsilon = \left| \frac{-258.64}{10\,758.64} \r\r\r\right| \times 100\% = \frac{258.64}{10\,758.64} \times 100\% \approx 2.40402\% \quad [A1]$$ Round to $3$ significant figures: $$\epsilon = 2.40\% \quad [A1]$$

Answers: (a) $A_E = 10\,758.64\text{ m}^2$, $A_A = 10\,500\text{ m}^2$; (b) $\epsilon = 2.40\%$

Examiner Commentary: Candidates must always divide by the EXACT value $v_E$ in the denominator. Dividing by the approximate value $v_A = 10\,500$ is penalized with a method mark loss. Also note that trailing zeros in $2.40\%$ are required to demonstrate 3 significant figures.

Exam Archetypes

The 6 Classic IB Exam Question Archetypes

Mastering Standard Form for IB Paper 1 and Paper 2 requires recognizing which archetype is being tested:

Archetype Typical Exam Setup Primary Strategy Common Pitfalls & Traps
1. Standard Form Normalization Express small/large measurements in form $a \times 10^k$, with $1 \le a < 10$ and $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. Shift decimal point until exactly one non-zero digit is to the left of the decimal point. Writing $0.45 \times 10^5$ or $45 \times 10^3$ (invalid coefficient outside $[1, 10)$).
2. Significant Figures & Rounding Round results to 3 significant figures unless specified otherwise. Identify first non-zero digit; count 3 digits; round using 4th digit. Confusing decimal places with significant figures (e.g. $0.00450$ has 3 sig figs, not 5).
3. Absolute & Percentage Error Given exact value $v_E$ and approximated value $v_A$, calculate percentage error. Apply $\epsilon = \left|\frac{v_A - v_E}{v_E}\r\r\r\right| \times 100\%$. Divide by EXACT value $v_E$, never $v_A$. Dividing by approximation $v_A$; forgetting the absolute value signs and reporting negative error.
4. Order of Magnitude Ratio Compare astronomical or atomic scales: how many times larger is the Sun than Earth? Compute ratio $\frac{a_1 \times 10^{k_1}}{a_2 \times 10^{k_2}} = \left(\frac{a_1}{a_2}\r\r\r\right) \times 10^{k_1 - k_2}$. Subtracting exponents incorrectly when dividing powers of 10.
5. Non-Calculator Operations (Paper 1) Calculate $(4 \times 10^5) \times (6 \times 10^{-2})$ or $\frac{1.2 \times 10^4}{3 \times 10^7}$. Group coefficients and powers separately: $(4 \times 6) \times 10^{5-2} = 24 \times 10^3 = 2.4 \times 10^4$. Leaving answer as $24 \times 10^3$ without re-normalizing into standard form.
6. Compound Metric Unit Conversion Convert speeds from $\text{km h}^{-1}$ to $\text{m s}^{-1}$ or density from $\text{g cm}^{-3}$ to $\text{kg m}^{-3}$. Multiply/divide by conversion factors in standard form: $1\text{ km/h} = \frac{10^3\text{ m}}{3.6 \times 10^3\text{ s}} = \frac{1}{3.6}\text{ m/s}$. Squaring or cubing conversion factors incorrectly for area ($\times 10^6$) and volume ($\times 10^9$).

Tier 3: Complex Applications & IB Exam Modeling (Questions 8–10)
Exercise 8 Paper 1 Exponent Algebraic Solving

Given that $(4.0 \times 10^k)^2 = 1.6 \times 10^{13}$, where $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, find the value of $k$.

View Complete Solution
  1. Square the left-hand side expression: $$(4.0 \times 10^k)^2 = (4.0)^2 \times (10^k)^2 = 16.0 \times 10^{2k}$$
  2. Normalize the left-hand side into standard form: $$16.0 \times 10^{2k} = 1.6 \times 10^1 \times 10^{2k} = 1.6 \times 10^{2k + 1}$$
  3. Equate with the right-hand side: $$1.6 \times 10^{2k + 1} = 1.6 \times 10^{13}$$
  4. Equate the exponents of $10$: $$2k + 1 = 13 \implies 2k = 12 \implies k = 6$$

Final Answer: $k = 6$

(b) Express $M_2$ as a percentage of $M_1$, correct to $3$ significant figures.

Exercise 10 Paper 1 Multi-part Velocity & Unit Conversion

A space probe travels at a steady speed of $5.4 \times 10^4\text{ km h}^{-1}$.

(a) Express this speed in metres per second ($\text{m s}^{-1}$) in standard form.

(b) The probe needs to reach a destination located $8.1 \times 10^8\text{ km}$ away. Calculate the time in hours needed for the journey, giving your answer in standard form.

(c) Convert the travel time found in (b) into days, rounded to the nearest integer.

View Complete Solution
  1. Part (a): Convert km/h to m/s ($1\text{ km/h} = \frac{1000\text{ m}}{3600\text{ s}} = \frac{1}{3.6}\text{ m/s}$): $$\text{Speed} = \frac{5.4 \times 10^4 \times 10^3}{3600} = \frac{5.4 \times 10^7}{3.6 \times 10^3} = \left(\frac{5.4}{3.6}\r\r\r\right) \times 10^{7 - 3} = 1.5 \times 10^4\text{ m s}^{-1}$$

Formula Booklet SL 1.1

Standard Form Definition

  • Mathematical Definition: $$a \times 10^k$$
  • Coefficient Constraint: $$1 \le a < 10$$
  • Exponent Constraint: $$k \in \mathbb{Z} \quad (\text{Integer: } \dots, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, \dots)$$
Must Memorize

Arithmetic Laws in Standard Form

  • Multiplication: $$(a \times 10^p) \times (b \times 10^q) = (ab) \times 10^{p+q}$$
  • Division: $$\frac{a \times 10^p}{b \times 10^q} = \left(\frac{a}{b}\r\r\r\right) \times 10^{p-q}$$
  • Powers: $$(a \times 10^p)^n = a^n \times 10^{pn}$$
  • Addition / Subtraction: $$a \times 10^p \pm b \times 10^q = (a \pm b \cdot 10^{q-p}) \times 10^p \quad (p \ge q)$$
IB Exam Rules

Precision & Notation Protocols

  • 3 Significant Figures: Unless told otherwise, all non-exact answers must be rounded to exactly 3 s.f.
  • Exact Numbers: Leave integers and fractions exact (e.g. $\frac{7}{8}$ rather than $0.875$ if not requested as decimal).
  • No Calculator Display Symbols: Never write `3.45E7` or `3.45 * 10^7`. Write $3.45 \times 10^7$.
  • Significant Trailing Zeros: $4.50 \times 10^3$ has 3 s.f.; dropping the zero ($4.5 \times 10^3$) forfeits precision marks.

Critical IB Examination Pitfalls in Standard Form

  • Failing to Normalize the Mantissa: Writing $25.4 \times 10^6$ instead of $2.54 \times 10^7$ or $0.78 \times 10^{-3}$ instead of $7.8 \times 10^{-4}$ loses accuracy marks ($A0$).
  • Writing Calculator $E$ Notation: The symbol $E$ or $EE$ is calculator shorthand. On exam scripts, the symbol $\times 10^k$ is mandatory.
  • Adding Numbers Without Matching Powers: $(3 \times 10^4) + (4 \times 10^3) \neq 7 \times 10^7$. You must convert to $(3 + 0.4) \times 10^4 = 3.4 \times 10^4$.
  • Truncating Without Rounding: Truncating $4.288 \times 10^3$ to $4.28 \times 10^3$ instead of rounding up to $4.29 \times 10^3$ incurs an accuracy penalty.
  • Premature Rounding: Rounding intermediate steps to 3 s.f. can propagate errors into the 3rd figure of the final answer. Store intermediate values in your calculator.

A rectangular silicon chip has length $l = 3.65 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}$ and width $w = 2.40 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}$.

(a) Calculate the area of the chip in square metres ($\text{m}^2$), giving your answer in standard form correct to $3$ significant figures. [2 marks]
(b) The chip contains $1.42 \times 10^7$ transistors evenly distributed over its surface. Calculate the transistor density in transistors per square metre ($\text{transistors m}^{-2}$), giving your answer in standard form to $3$ significant figures. [4 marks]
View Markscheme
(a)
$\text{Area} = l \times w = (3.65 \times 10^{-3}) \times (2.40 \times 10^{-3})$ M1
$= (3.65 \times 2.40) \times 10^{-6} = 8.76 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2$
$\mathbf{8.76 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2}$ A1

(b)
$\text{Density} = \frac{\text{Total Transistors}}{\text{Area}} = \frac{1.42 \times 10^7}{8.76 \times 10^{-6}}$ M1
$= \left(\frac{1.42}{8.76}\r\r\r\right) \times 10^{7 - (-6)} = 0.1621 \times 10^{13}$ A1
Normalize into standard form: $1.621 \times 10^{12}$ M1
Round to 3 s.f.: $\mathbf{1.62 \times 10^{12}\text{ transistors m}^{-2}}$ A1
