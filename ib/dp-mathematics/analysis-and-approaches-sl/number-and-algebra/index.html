Learn the ideas

Core Mathematical Concepts

Standard form and sensible precision

  • Write a non-zero number as $a \times 10^k$, where $1 \le |a| < 10$ and $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. Always normalize the leading coefficient $a$ after multiplication or division.
  • In IB examinations, give non-exact numerical answers to 3 significant figures unless specified otherwise. Keep intermediate values unrounded in memory.

Arithmetic and geometric sequences

  • An arithmetic sequence has a constant common difference $d$: $u_n = u_1 + (n - 1)d$. Its sum is $S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2u_1 + (n - 1)d] = \frac{n}{2}(u_1 + u_n)$.
  • A geometric sequence has a constant common ratio $r$: $u_n = u_1 r^{n-1}$. For $r \ne 1$, $S_n = \frac{u_1(1 - r^n)}{1 - r} = \frac{u_1(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}$.
  • An infinite geometric series converges to a finite sum if and only if $|r| < 1$. Its sum to infinity is $S_\infty = \frac{u_1}{1 - r}$.
  • Compound interest over $k$ periods per year at nominal annual rate $r\%$ is modeled geometrically: $FV = PV \left(1 + \frac{r}{100k}\right)^{kn}$.

Exponents and logarithms

  • Index laws: $a^x a^y = a^{x+y}$, $\frac{a^x}{a^y} = a^{x-y}$, $(a^x)^y = a^{xy}$, $a^{-x} = \frac{1}{a^x}$, $a^{m/n} = \sqrt[n]{a^m}$.
  • Logarithmic definition: $\log_a x = y \iff a^y = x$ with base $a > 0, a \ne 1$ and argument $x > 0$.
  • Log laws: $\log_a(xy) = \log_a x + \log_a y$, $\log_a\left(\frac{x}{y}\right) = \log_a x - \log_a y$, $\log_a(x^k) = k \log_a x$, and change of base $\log_a b = \frac{\ln b}{\ln a}$.

Simple proof and the binomial theorem

  • Direct proof requires rigorous algebraic deductive reasoning starting from one side (LHS) to arrive at the other (RHS). Examples do not constitute proof.
  • A universal conjecture is disproved by providing a single counterexample within the specified domain.
  • The binomial expansion for $n \in \mathbb{N}$ is $(a + b)^n = \sum_{r=0}^n \binom{n}{r} a^{n-r} b^r$, where the general term is $T_{r+1} = \binom{n}{r} a^{n-r} b^r$ with $\binom{n}{r} = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}$.

Follow the reasoning

Worked examples

These original examples show common reasoning steps across Topic 1 concepts.

Worked example 1

Find a term and a partial sum

Question. An arithmetic sequence begins $7, 11, 15, \dots$ Find $u_{12}$ and the sum of the first 12 terms.

  1. The first term is $u_1 = 7$ and the common difference is $d = 11 - 7 = 4$.
  2. $u_{12} = 7 + (12 - 1)(4) = 7 + 44 = 51$.
  3. $S_{12} = \frac{12}{2}[2(7) + 11(4)] = 6(14 + 44) = 6(58) = 348$.

Answer: $u_{12} = 51$ and $S_{12} = 348$.

Worked example 2

Model compound growth

Question. \$5,000 is invested at 4% annual interest compounded quarterly. Find its future value after 3 years, to the nearest dollar.

  1. $PV = 5000$, $r = 4\%$, compounding periods per year $k = 4$, and $n = 3$ years. Total compounding periods $N = 4 \times 3 = 12$.
  2. Quarterly interest rate $= \frac{4\%}{4} = 1\% = 0.01$.
  3. $FV = 5000(1 + 0.01)^{12} = 5000(1.01)^{12} \approx 5000(1.126825) \approx \$5634.13$.

Answer: The value is \$5,634.

Worked example 3

Binomial expansion term

Question. Find the term independent of $x$ in the expansion of $\left(x + \frac{2}{x}\right)^4$.

  1. General term: $T_{r+1} = \binom{4}{r} x^{4-r} \left(\frac{2}{x}\right)^r = \binom{4}{r} 2^r x^{4 - 2r}$.
  2. For term independent of $x$, set exponent $= 0$: $4 - 2r = 0 \implies r = 2$.
  3. $T_3 = \binom{4}{2} 2^2 = 6 \times 4 = 24$.

Answer: 24.

Before you move on

Topic 1 Revision checklist

  • Can I perform operations in standard form and round correctly to 3 significant figures?
  • Can I distinguish between arithmetic ($u_n = u_1 + (n-1)d$) and geometric ($u_n = u_1 r^{n-1}$) sequences and use their sum formulas?
  • Can I apply compound interest and depreciation formulas with various compounding periods?
  • Can I simplify expressions and solve equations using exponent and logarithm laws, while verifying domain restrictions?
  • Can I state the condition for convergence of an infinite geometric series ($|r| < 1$) and calculate $S_\infty$?
  • Can I write a direct deductive algebraic proof using representations for even, odd, and consecutive integers?
  • Can I apply the binomial general term formula $T_{r+1} = \binom{n}{r} a^{n-r} b^r$ to find specific coefficients and terms independent of $x$?
