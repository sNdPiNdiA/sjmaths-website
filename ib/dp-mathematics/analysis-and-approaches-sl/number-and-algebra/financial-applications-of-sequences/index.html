Financial Applications of Sequences

Modeling compound interest across various compounding periods, calculating asset depreciation, solving for investment durations via logarithms, and GDC TVM Solver conventions.

SL 1.4 Paper 1 & Paper 2 Core AA Syllabus Prerequisites: Geometric Sequences, Logarithms
1.4

Compound Interest & Compounding Frequencies

  • The Compound Interest Formula (Formula Booklet): $$FV = PV \left( 1 + \frac{r}{100k} \right)^{kn}$$ where:
    • $FV$: Future Value (final accumulated balance)
    • $PV$: Present Value (initial principal invested)
    • $r$: Nominal annual interest rate in percent (e.g. for $6\%$, $r = 6$, not $0.06$)
    • $k$: Number of compounding periods per year
    • $n$: Number of years of investment
    • $kn$: Total number of compounding periods
  • Standard Compounding Frequencies ($k$):
    • Annually: $k = 1$
    • Semi-annually (Half-yearly): $k = 2$
    • Quarterly: $k = 4$
    • Monthly: $k = 12$
    • Daily: $k = 365$
  • Total Interest Earned: $$\text{Interest Earned} = FV - PV$$

Example 1

Comparing Compounding Frequencies

An investor deposits $\$8000$ into a savings bond paying a nominal annual interest rate of $4.8\%$ for $5$ years. Calculate the future value if interest is compounded:
(a) Annually ($k = 1$)
(b) Monthly ($k = 12$)
Give your answers to the nearest dollar.

  1. Part (a): Annual Compounding ($k = 1$): $$FV = 8000 \left( 1 + \frac{4.8}{100 \times 1} \right)^{1 \times 5} = 8000(1 + 0.048)^5 = 8000(1.048)^5$$ $$FV = 8000(1.26417) \approx \$10\,113.38 \implies \$10\,113$$
  2. Part (b): Monthly Compounding ($k = 12$): $$FV = 8000 \left( 1 + \frac{4.8}{100 \times 12} \right)^{12 \times 5} = 8000 \left( 1 + \frac{4.8}{1200} \right)^{60}$$ $$FV = 8000 (1 + 0.004)^{60} = 8000 (1.004)^{60}$$ $$FV = 8000(1.27064) \approx \$10\,165.13 \implies \$10\,165$$
  3. Comparison: Monthly compounding yields $\$10\,165 - \$10\,113 = \$52$ more than annual compounding due to interest earning interest more frequently.

Answer: (a) $\$10\,113$; (b) $\$10\,165$

1.4

Annual Depreciation (Reducing Balance Method)

  • Depreciation Definition: The reduction in the market value of a physical asset (such as machinery, vehicles, or technology) over time at a constant percentage rate.
  • Reducing Balance Depreciation Formula: $$FV = PV (1 - r)^n$$ where $PV$ is the initial purchase price, $r$ is the annual rate of depreciation expressed as a decimal (e.g. for $15\%$, $r = 0.15$), and $n$ is the number of years.
  • Connection to Geometric Sequences: Depreciation is modeled by a geometric sequence where the common ratio is $R = 1 - r < 1$: $$u_{n+1} = u_1 (1 - r)^n$$ The balance decays exponentially toward zero over time.

Example 2

Vehicle Depreciation Modeling

A company buys a transport truck for $\$45\,000$. The truck depreciates at a rate of $14\%$ per year.
(a) Find the value of the truck after $4$ years.
(b) Calculate the total loss in value of the truck over this $4$-year period.
Give your answers to the nearest dollar.

  1. Part (a): Apply the depreciation formula with $PV = 45\,000, r = 0.14, n = 4$: $$FV = 45\,000(1 - 0.14)^4 = 45\,000(0.86)^4$$ $$(0.86)^4 \approx 0.547008$$ $$FV = 45\,000 \times 0.547008 \approx \$24\,615.36 \implies \$24\,615$$
  2. Part (b): Calculate total loss in value: $$\text{Loss} = PV - FV = \$45\,000 - \$24\,615 = \$20\,385$$

Answer: (a) $\$24\,615$; (b) $\$20\,385$

1.4

Solving for Investment Duration $n$ Using Logarithms

  • Problem Type: Finding how many years (or compounding periods) are required for an investment to reach or double a specified target value.
  • Step-by-Step Algebraic Derivation: Given $FV = PV \left( 1 + \frac{r}{100k} \right)^{kn}$:
    1. Divide by $PV$: $$\frac{FV}{PV} = \left( 1 + \frac{r}{100k} \right)^{kn}$$
    2. Take natural logarithms ($\ln$) of both sides: $$\ln\left(\frac{FV}{PV}\right) = \ln\left[\left( 1 + \frac{r}{100k} \right)^{kn}\right] = kn \ln\left( 1 + \frac{r}{100k} \right)$$
    3. Isolate the number of years $n$: $$n = \frac{\ln(FV / PV)}{k \ln\left( 1 + \frac{r}{100k} \right)}$$
  • Rounding in Financial Contexts: If interest is compounded periodically (e.g. annually or quarterly), round up to the next full compounding period so the target threshold is actually reached.

Example 3

Solving for Doubling Time

An investor deposits $\$6000$ at a nominal annual interest rate of $5.4\%$ compounded quarterly. Find the minimum number of full quarters required for the investment to double in value ($FV \ge \$12\,000$).

  1. Set up the compound interest inequality with $PV = 6000, FV = 12\,000, r = 5.4, k = 4$: $$6000 \left( 1 + \frac{5.4}{400} \right)^{4n} \ge 12\,000$$ $$6000 (1 + 0.0135)^{4n} \ge 12\,000 \implies (1.0135)^{4n} \ge 2$$
  2. Let $N = 4n$ be the total number of compounding quarters: $$(1.0135)^N \ge 2$$
  3. Take natural logarithms on both sides: $$\ln(1.0135^N) \ge \ln 2 \implies N \ln(1.0135) \ge \ln 2$$ $$N \ge \frac{\ln 2}{\ln(1.0135)} \approx \frac{0.693147}{0.0134098} \approx 51.69\text{ quarters}$$
  4. Round up to the next full quarter: $N = 52$ quarters ($52 \div 4 = 13$ years).

Answer: $52$ quarters ($13$ full years)

Example 4 (Archetype 3)

Solving for Investment Horizon via Natural Logarithms

Maria deposits $\$6000$ in a high-yield account offering $4.2\%$ nominal annual interest, compounded monthly.
(a) Calculate the balance in the account after $8$ years. [3 marks]
(b) Find the minimum number of complete years required for the investment to grow to at least $\$15\,000$. [4 marks]

  1. Part (a): Monthly compounding parameters: $$PV = 6000, \quad r = 4.2, \quad k = 12, \quad n = 8 \implies kn = 96$$ $$FV = 6000\left(1 + \frac{4.2}{1200}\right)^{96} = 6000(1 + 0.0035)^{96} = 6000(1.0035)^{96} \quad [M1]$$ $$FV = 6000(1.39868) \approx \$8392.08 \implies \$8392 \quad [A1]$$
  2. Part (b): Set up growth inequality: $$6000(1.0035)^{12n} \ge 15\,000 \implies (1.0035)^{12n} \ge \frac{15\,000}{6000} = 2.5 \quad [M1]$$
  3. Take natural logarithms of both sides: $$\ln\left[(1.0035)^{12n}\right] \ge \ln(2.5) \implies 12n \ln(1.0035) \ge \ln(2.5) \quad [M1]$$ $$n \ge \frac{\ln(2.5)}{12\ln(1.0035)} = \frac{0.91629}{12(0.0034939)} = \frac{0.91629}{0.041926} \approx 21.855 \text{ years} \quad [A1]$$
  4. State minimum complete years: Since interest compounds monthly and complete years are requested: $$n = 22 \text{ complete years} \quad [A1]$$

Answers: (a) $\$8392$; (b) $22$ complete years

Example 5 (Archetype 6 & 2)

Asset Depreciation & Sinking Fund Replacement

A printing press bought for $\$65\,000$ depreciates at an annual reducing rate of $15\%$. Concurrently, a replacement sinking fund is opened with an initial deposit of $\$12\,000$ earning $6\%$ annual interest compounded annually.
(a) Find the value of the press after $5$ years. [2 marks]
(b) Determine the number of years $t$ when the accumulated sinking fund first exceeds the residual value of the press. [5 marks]

  1. Part (a): Evaluate depreciation after 5 years: $$V(5) = 65\,000(1 - 0.15)^5 = 65\,000(0.85)^5 = 65\,000(0.443705) \approx \$28\,840.84 \implies \$28\,841 \quad [M1][A1]$$
  2. Part (b): Equate fund growth and asset depreciation: $$\text{Sinking fund: } S(t) = 12\,000(1 + 0.06)^t = 12\,000(1.06)^t \quad [M1]$$ $$\text{Asset value: } V(t) = 65\,000(0.85)^t$$ We require $S(t) > V(t)$: $$12\,000(1.06)^t > 65\,000(0.85)^t \implies \frac{(1.06)^t}{(0.85)^t} > \frac{65\,000}{12\,000} \quad [M1]$$ $$\left(\frac{1.06}{0.85}\right)^t > \frac{65}{12} \implies (1.247059)^t > 5.41667 \quad [A1]$$
  3. Solve for $t$ using logarithms: $$t \ln(1.247059) > \ln(5.41667) \implies t > \frac{\ln(5.41667)}{\ln(1.247059)} = \frac{1.68944}{0.22079} \approx 7.65 \text{ years} \quad [M1]$$ Thus, after $8$ years, the fund first exceeds the residual value [A1].

Answers: (a) $\$28\,841$; (b) $8$ years

Examiner Commentary: Combining the powers into $\left( rac{1.06}{0.85} ight)^t$ simplifies the algebra dramatically, avoiding the need to solve multiple separate logarithm expressions.

Exam Archetypes

The 6 Classic IB Exam Question Archetypes

Mastering Financial Applications of Sequences for IB Paper 1 and Paper 2 requires recognizing which archetype is being tested:

Archetype Typical Exam Setup Primary Strategy Common Pitfalls & Traps
1. Multi-Frequency Compounding Given nominal rate $r$, find future value when interest is compounded annually, quarterly, or monthly. Use $FV = PV\left(1 + \frac{r}{100k}\right)^{kn}$. Ensure rate is entered as percentage $r$ and $kn$ is total periods. Dividing $r$ by 100 twice; confusing quarterly ($k=4$) with every 4 months ($k=3$).
2. Reducing Balance Depreciation Asset loses $r\%$ of its current value each year. Apply $FV = PV(1 - r)^n$ where $r$ is a decimal (e.g. $0.15$ for $15\%$). Confusing reducing balance depreciation with linear depreciation; using $(1 + r)^{-n}$.
3. Investment Doubling / Growth Horizon Find how many years are needed for money to double or exceed a specific threshold. Set $\frac{FV}{PV} = \left(1 + \frac{r}{100k}\right)^{kn}$, take $\ln$ of both sides, solve for $n$, round UP to next period. Rounding down, which leaves the balance short of the required threshold.
4. Nominal vs. Effective Annual Rate Compare an account paying $6\%$ compounded monthly against $6.1\%$ compounded annually. Compute effective rate $r_{eff} = \left(1 + \frac{r}{100k}\right)^k - 1$ or test $\$1000$ for 1 year. Assuming higher nominal rate is always superior without evaluating compounding frequency.
5. Inflation & Purchasing Power Price levels increase at annual inflation rate $i\%$; find future cost of a basket of goods. Treat as compound interest with $r = i$: $\text{Future Cost} = \text{Current Cost} \times (1 + i)^n$. Subtracting inflation from principal instead of compounding future price.
6. Break-Even & Asset Replacement Asset depreciates while a replacement fund accumulates interest; find break-even year. Set Depreciation Formula = Investment Formula, solve numerically via GDC solver or logarithms. Misidentifying initial deposit vs future balance; neglecting compounding periods.
Tier 1: Core Fluency (Questions 1–4)
Exercise 1 Paper 2 Core Compound Calculation

$\$5000$ is invested at $4.5\%$ compound interest per annum, compounded annually. Calculate the value of the investment after $6$ years, to the nearest dollar.

View Complete Solution
  1. Identify parameters: $PV = 5000, r = 4.5, k = 1, n = 6$.
  2. Apply formula: $$FV = 5000\left(1 + \frac{4.5}{100}\right)^6 = 5000(1.045)^6$$ $$FV = 5000(1.30226) \approx \$6511.30 \implies \$6511$$

Final Answer: $\$6511$

Exercise 2 Paper 2 Quarterly Compounding

$\$10\,000$ is deposited into an account earning $5.2\%$ nominal annual interest, compounded quarterly. Find the balance after $4$ years, to the nearest dollar.

View Complete Solution
  1. Identify parameters: $PV = 10\,000, r = 5.2, k = 4, n = 4 \implies kn = 16$.
  2. Evaluate quarterly rate: $$\frac{r}{100k} = \frac{5.2}{400} = 0.013$$
  3. Calculate future value: $$FV = 10\,000(1 + 0.013)^{16} = 10\,000(1.013)^{16}$$ $$FV = 10\,000(1.22949) \approx \$12\,294.94 \implies \$12\,295$$

Final Answer: $\$12\,295$

Exercise 3 Paper 1 Core Depreciation

A laptop is purchased for $\$2000$. It depreciates at a rate of $20\%$ per year. Without a calculator, find the value of the laptop after $3$ years.

View Complete Solution
  1. Depreciation multiplier: $1 - 0.20 = 0.80 = \frac{4}{5}$.
  2. Evaluate after 3 years: $$FV = 2000 \times (0.80)^3 = 2000 \times \left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^3 = 2000 \times \frac{64}{125}$$ $$\frac{2000}{125} = 16 \implies 16 \times 64 = \$1024$$

Final Answer: $\$1024$

Exercise 4 Paper 2 Finding Interest Rate $r$

An initial sum of $\$7500$ grows to $\$9600$ in $5$ years under annual compound interest. Find the annual interest rate $r$, correct to $3$ significant figures.

View Complete Solution
  1. Set up the equation: $$9600 = 7500(1 + r)^5 \implies (1 + r)^5 = \frac{9600}{7500} = 1.28$$
  2. Take the 5th root: $$1 + r = (1.28)^{1/5} \approx 1.05063$$ $$r \approx 0.05063 \implies 5.06\%$$

Final Answer: $5.06\%$

Tier 2: Exam Modeling (Questions 5–7)
Exercise 5 Paper 2 Investment Comparison

Sophie has $\$15\,000$ to invest for $4$ years. Bank A offers $6.0\%$ compounded annually. Bank B offers $5.85\%$ compounded monthly. Which bank yields the greater return, and by how much?

View Complete Solution
  1. Evaluate Bank A (Annually, $k = 1$): $$FV_A = 15\,000(1 + 0.06)^4 = 15\,000(1.06)^4 \approx 15\,000(1.26248) \approx \$18\,937.15$$
  2. Evaluate Bank B (Monthly, $k = 12, kn = 48$): $$FV_B = 15\,000\left(1 + \frac{5.85}{1200}\right)^{48} = 15\,000(1 + 0.004875)^{48}$$ $$FV_B \approx 15\,000(1.26296) \approx \$18\,944.43$$
  3. Compare: Bank B yields $\$18\,944.43 - \$18\,937.15 = \$7.28$ more than Bank A.

Final Answer: Bank B yields $\$7.28$ more

Exercise 6 Paper 2 Logarithmic Duration Threshold

A company deposits $\$12\,000$ at $4.2\%$ compounded semi-annually. Find the minimum number of complete years needed for the balance to exceed $\$20\,000$.

View Complete Solution
  1. Set up inequality ($k = 2$): $$12\,000\left(1 + \frac{4.2}{200}\right)^{2n} > 20\,000$$ $$(1.021)^{2n} > \frac{20\,000}{12\,000} = \frac{5}{3} \approx 1.66667$$
  2. Take natural logarithms: $$2n \ln(1.021) > \ln\left(\frac{5}{3}\right)$$ $$2n > \frac{\ln(1.66667)}{\ln(1.021)} \approx \frac{0.510826}{0.020783} \approx 24.58\text{ half-years}$$
  3. Convert to years ($n = \frac{24.58}{2} = 12.29$): To complete full years, round up to $13$ years. (After 12.5 years / 25 half-years, it exceeds $\$20\,000$; so $13$ full complete years).

Final Answer: $13$ full years (or $25$ half-year periods)

Exercise 7 Paper 2 Inflation & Real Purchasing Power

Due to inflation of $3.5\%$ per year, the purchasing power of money decreases according to $V = P(1 - 0.035)^t$. Find how many years it takes for the purchasing power of $\$1000$ to be reduced to $\$500$ (i.e. halved).

View Complete Solution
  1. Set up the equation: $$500 = 1000(0.965)^t \implies (0.965)^t = 0.5$$
  2. Solve via logarithms: $$t \ln(0.965) = \ln(0.5) \implies t = \frac{\ln(0.5)}{\ln(0.965)}$$ $$t \approx \frac{-0.693147}{-0.035626} \approx 19.455\text{ years}$$

Final Answer: $19.5$ years

Tier 3: Complex Applications & IB Exam Modeling (Questions 8–10)
Exercise 8 Paper 2 Industrial Equipment Residual Value

A manufacturing press is purchased for $\$120\,000$. It depreciates at a constant annual rate of $16.5\%$. The machine is sold for scrap when its residual value drops below $15\%$ of its original purchase price. After how many full years of service will the machine be scrapped?

View Complete Solution
  1. Set up threshold condition: $$FV < 0.15 \times PV \implies PV(1 - 0.165)^n < 0.15 \times PV$$ $$(0.835)^n < 0.15$$
  2. Solve using natural logarithms ($\ln 0.835 < 0$, flip sign): $$n \ln(0.835) < \ln(0.15) \implies n > \frac{\ln(0.15)}{\ln(0.835)}$$ $$n > \frac{-1.89712}{-0.18032} \approx 10.52\text{ years}$$
  3. Round up to full years: $n = 11$ full years.

Final Answer: $11$ full years

Exercise 9 Paper 1 Algebraic Exponential Modeling

An investment of $\$P$ grows at a nominal annual rate of $r\%$ compounded annually. After $2$ years the balance is $\$7260$, and after $3$ years the balance is $\$7986$. Without using a calculator, find $r$ and $P$.

View Complete Solution
  1. Set up consecutive year equations: $$P(1 + r/100)^2 = 7260 \quad \text{--- (1)}$$ $$P(1 + r/100)^3 = 7986 \quad \text{--- (2)}$$
  2. Divide (2) by (1): $$1 + \frac{r}{100} = \frac{7986}{7260} = \frac{798.6}{726} = 1.10$$ $$\frac{r}{100} = 0.10 \implies r = 10\%$$
  3. Substitute back into (1) to find $P$: $$P(1.10)^2 = 7260 \implies P(1.21) = 7260$$ $$P = \frac{7260}{1.21} = \frac{726\,000}{121} = \$6000$$

Final Answer: $r = 10\%, P = \$6000$

Exercise 10 Paper 2 Multi-part Examination Modeling

On his $18$th birthday, Lucas receives $\$25\,000$. He deposits the entire amount into a high-yield account earning $5.4\%$ nominal annual interest compounded monthly.

(a) Calculate the value of Lucas's investment on his $25$th birthday ($7$ years later), to the nearest dollar.

(b) Find the total interest earned over this $7$-year period.

(c) Determine the age Lucas will be when the investment first reaches $\$60\,000$.

View Complete Solution
  1. Part (a): $PV = 25\,000, r = 5.4, k = 12, n = 7 \implies kn = 84$: $$FV = 25\,000\left(1 + \frac{5.4}{1200}\right)^{84} = 25\,000(1 + 0.0045)^{84} = 25\,000(1.0045)^{84}$$ $$FV \approx 25\,000(1.45789) \approx \$36\,447.33 \implies \$36\,447$$
  2. Part (b): Interest earned: $$\text{Interest} = \$36\,447 - \$25\,000 = \$11\,447$$
  3. Part (c): Solve for $n$ with $FV \ge 60\,000$: $$25\,000(1.0045)^{12n} = 60\,000 \implies (1.0045)^{12n} = 2.4$$ $$12n = \frac{\ln(2.4)}{\ln(1.0045)} \approx \frac{0.875469}{0.0044899} \approx 194.99\text{ months}$$ $$n = \frac{194.99}{12} \approx 16.25\text{ years}$$ $$\text{Lucas's Age} = 18 + 16.25 \approx 34.25 \implies 34\text{ years and } 3\text{ months}$$

Final Answer: (a) $\$36\,447$; (b) $\$11\,447$; (c) Age $34$ (specifically $34$ years and $3$ months)

Formula Booklet SL 1.4

Compound Interest Formula

  • Compound Interest: $$FV = PV \left( 1 + \frac{r}{100k} \right)^{kn}$$
  • Interest Rate: $r$ is nominal annual rate in percent.
  • Compounding Compaction: $k$ is compounding periods/yr; $kn$ is total periods.
Must Memorize

Depreciation & Growth Models

  • Annual Depreciation: $$FV = PV (1 - r)^n \quad (r \text{ as decimal})$$
  • Simple Interest: $$I = \frac{P \times r \times t}{100}$$
  • Logarithmic Duration: $$n = \frac{\ln(FV / PV)}{k \ln\left(1 + \frac{r}{100k}\right)}$$
Exam Reference

Compounding Frequencies Table

  • Annually: $k = 1$
  • Half-Yearly / Semi-Annually: $k = 2$
  • Quarterly: $k = 4$
  • Monthly: $k = 12$
  • Daily: $k = 365$

Critical IB Examination Pitfalls in Financial Math

  • Double Percentage Conversion: In $FV = PV(1 + \frac{r}{100k})^{kn}$, the $100$ is already in the denominator! If $r = 5\%$, write $5$, NOT $0.05$. Writing $0.05 / 100$ divides by $100$ twice.
  • Forgetting the $k$ in the Exponent: Multiplying the denominator by $k$ but forgetting to multiply $n$ by $k$ in the exponent $(kn)$ will severely underestimate compounding growth.
  • Confusing Depreciation with Compound Interest: Depreciation subtracts the rate: $1 - r$. Never add when dealing with decaying asset value!
  • Premature Currency Rounding: Never round intermediate compounding values to the nearest cent/dollar until the very final answer step. Store numbers in calculator memory.
  • GDC TVM Sign Conventions: When using the financial TVM solver on GDCs, cash paid out (investment) must be entered as a negative value ($PV < 0$) so the received balance is positive ($FV > 0$).

Section A: Diagnostic Check

4 core concept check questions. Immediate feedback and scoring.

Score: 0 / 4

Question 1

What is the value of $k$ when an interest rate is compounded quarterly?

Correct! There are 4 quarters in a year, so $k = 4$.

Question 2

A car worth $\$30\,000$ depreciates by $10\%$ per year. What is its value after $2$ years?

Correct! $FV = 30\,000(1 - 0.10)^2 = 30\,000(0.9)^2 = 30\,000(0.81) = \$24\,300$.

Question 3

If $\$1000$ is invested at $6\%$ nominal interest compounded monthly, what is the interest rate applied each month?

Correct! Monthly rate $= \frac{r}{k} = \frac{6\%}{12} = 0.5\%$ (or decimal multiplier $0.005$).

Question 4

In the formula $(1 + r)^n = 3$, how is $n$ expressed in terms of natural logarithms?

Correct! Taking $\ln$ of both sides gives $n \ln(1 + r) = \ln 3 \implies n = \frac{\ln 3}{\ln(1 + r)}$.
