Function Transformations

Transformations connect changes in an equation to changes in its graph. Read inside and outside changes separately to avoid reversing the horizontal direction.

  1. 2.11Transformations

Learn the ideas

Key ideas and methods

  • y=f(x)+k shifts the graph vertically by k; y=f(x−h) shifts it right by h.
  • A factor a outside, y=af(x), scales vertical distances by |a| and reflects across the x-axis if a<0.
  • A factor b inside, y=f(bx), scales horizontal distances by 1/|b| and reflects across the y-axis if b<0. Use a key point to check the direction.

Follow the reasoning

Worked example

This original example shows the method step by step; it is not copied from an IB examination paper.

Worked example 1

Describe a transformed parabola

Question. Describe the transformation from y=x² to y=−2(x−3)²+1.

  1. x−3 shifts the graph 3 units right.
  2. The factor −2 reflects it in the x-axis and stretches it vertically by factor 2.
  3. The +1 shifts it up 1 unit.

Answer: Right 3, reflected in the x-axis, vertical stretch by 2, then up 1; vertex (3,1).

Try it yourself

Practice with worked answers

  1. State the vertex of y=(x+4)²−2.

    Show worked answer

    The vertex is (−4,−2).

  2. How does y=3f(−x) transform y=f(x)?

    Show worked answer

    Reflect in the y-axis, then stretch vertically by factor 3.

Avoid these slips

Common mistakes

  • Reading f(x−h) as a shift left; it shifts right by h.
  • Ignoring the reflection caused by a negative multiplier.
