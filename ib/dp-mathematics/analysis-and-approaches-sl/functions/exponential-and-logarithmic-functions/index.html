Exponential and Logarithmic Functions

Exponential and logarithmic functions are inverse families. Their domains, ranges and growth patterns explain how they model changing quantities.

  1. 2.9Exponential and logarithmic functions

Learn the ideas

Key ideas and methods

  • For a>0 and a≠1, y=aˣ has domain all real x and range y>0; it passes through (0,1).
  • The inverse y=logₐx has domain x>0 and range all real values; its graph reflects y=aˣ across y=x.
  • For a>1 the exponential increases; for 0<a<1 it decreases. A vertical or horizontal shift changes intercepts and asymptotes, so check the transformed equation.

Follow the reasoning

Worked example

This original example shows the method step by step; it is not copied from an IB examination paper.

Worked example 1

Solve an exponential equation

Question. Solve 2ˣ=7 exactly and estimate x to three significant figures.

  1. Take logarithms: x ln 2=ln 7.
  2. Divide by ln 2 to obtain x=ln 7/ln 2.
  3. Evaluate the ratio and round only at the end.

Answer: x=log₂7≈2.81.

Try it yourself

Practice with worked answers

  1. State the domain and range of y=log₃(x−4).

    Show worked answer

    The argument must be positive, so x>4. The range is all real numbers.

  2. Is y=(1/2)ˣ increasing or decreasing?

    Show worked answer

    It is decreasing because its base satisfies 0<1/2<1.

Avoid these slips

Common mistakes

  • Allowing a non-positive input to a real logarithm.
  • Confusing the domain of an exponential function with the domain of its logarithmic inverse.
