Learn the ideas

Key ideas and methods

  • Expanded form ax²+bx+c shows the y-intercept; factorized form shows roots; vertex form a(x−h)²+k shows the vertex (h,k).
  • For ax²+bx+c=0, Δ=b²−4ac: Δ>0 gives two distinct real roots, Δ=0 one repeated real root, and Δ<0 no real roots.
  • For inequalities, locate critical roots and test intervals or use the parabola’s direction. Include equality endpoints for ≤ or ≥ when they satisfy the equation.

Follow the reasoning

Worked example

This original example shows the method step by step; it is not copied from an IB examination paper.

Worked example 1

Solve and interpret a quadratic

Question. Solve 2x²−3x−2=0 and state the discriminant.

  1. Here a=2, b=−3 and c=−2.
  2. Δ=(−3)²−4(2)(−2)=9+16=25, so there are two distinct real roots.
  3. Factor: (2x+1)(x−2)=0.

Answer: x=−1/2 or x=2; Δ=25.

Try it yourself

Practice with worked answers

  1. Find the vertex of y=2(x+1)²−5.

    Show worked answer

    Vertex form gives (−1,−5).

  2. How many distinct real roots does 3x²+2x+4=0 have?

    Show worked answer

    Δ=2²−4(3)(4)=−44<0, so there are no real roots.

Avoid these slips

Common mistakes

  • Using b rather than −b in the quadratic formula.
  • Treating Δ=0 as two distinct roots.
  • Choosing inequality intervals without checking the sign of the quadratic.
