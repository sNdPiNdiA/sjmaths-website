3.1A

3D Solids: Volumes and Surface Areas

  • Right Circular Cone:
    Volume: $$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$$
    Total surface area: $$A = \pi r l + \pi r^2$$
    Curved surface area: $$\pi r l$$
    where slant height $l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}$.
  • Sphere: $$V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3, \quad A = 4\pi r^2$$
  • Right Pyramid: $$V = \frac{1}{3} \times \text{Base Area} \times h$$
  • Angle Between a Line and a Plane: The angle between a line segment and its orthogonal projection onto the plane, computed via right-angled trigonometry $\tan \theta = \frac{\text{vertical height}}{\text{projected base length}}$.

Coordinates, Distances and Midpoints

  • For $A(x_1,y_1,z_1)$ and $B(x_2,y_2,z_2)$, $AB=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2+(z_2-z_1)^2}$. Apply Pythagoras first in a horizontal plane, then vertically.
  • The midpoint is $M(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2},\frac{z_1+z_2}{2})$. Average corresponding coordinates.
  • Worked example: $A(1,2,0)$, $B(4,6,12)$ give $AB=\sqrt{3^2+4^2+12^2}=13$ and $M(2.5,4,6)$.

Space Diagonals and Line–Plane Angles

  • A cuboid with perpendicular dimensions $a,b,h$ has base diagonal $p=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ and space diagonal $D=\sqrt{a^2+b^2+h^2}$.
  • The angle between the space diagonal and the base is found in the right triangle with sides $p,h,D$: $\tan\theta=h/p$ or $\sin\theta=h/D$. A face diagonal is usually not the projection onto the base.
  • Worked example: Dimensions $3,4,12$ give $p=5$, $D=13$ and $\theta=\tan^{-1}(12/5)\approx67.4^\circ$.
  • When the line is parallel to the plane its angle is $0^\circ$; when perpendicular it is $90^\circ$.

  • Volume uses perpendicular height. Cone surface area uses slant height $l$; do not interchange them.
  • For a pyramid, add the areas of the triangular faces separately. Face slant height differs from a sloping edge.
  • Worked example: A square pyramid has base side $6$ and vertical height $4$. Its face slant height is $\sqrt{4^2+3^2}=5$. Thus $V=\frac13(36)(4)=48$ and total surface area $=36+4(\frac12\cdot6\cdot5)=96$.

Composite Solids, Similarity and Units

  • Add volumes of non-overlapping parts; subtract cavities. Shared internal faces do not contribute to external surface area.
  • With linear scale factor $k>0$, areas multiply by $k^2$ and volumes by $k^3$.
  • Worked example: A cone filled to half its perpendicular height contains $1/8$ of its full volume: similar cones have height ratio $1/2$, radius ratio $1/2$, and volume ratio $(1/2)^3$.
  • Use one unit system before substituting: $1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}$, $1\text{ m}^2=10^4\text{ cm}^2$, $1\text{ m}^3=10^6\text{ cm}^3$; $1$ litre $=1000\text{ cm}^3$.
  • Keep full calculator precision until the final answer. Check whether dimensions are radii or diameters, and whether a container is open or closed.

10 Progressive Practice Exercises

Work from single-step calculations to spatial and composite-solid applications.

Exercise 1 (Paper 1 — 3 Marks): A solid metal cone has radius $r = 6.0\text{ cm}$ and vertical height $h = 8.0\text{ cm}$. Calculate the curved surface area of the cone, correct to 3 significant figures.
Slant height $l = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10.0\text{ cm}$ [M1][A1]
Curved surface area = $\pi r l = \pi (6)(10) = 60\pi \approx 188.496 \approx 188\text{ cm}^2$ [A1]

Answer: $188\text{ cm}^2$

Exercise 2: A cuboid measures $6$ cm by $8$ cm by $10$ cm. Find its volume, total surface area and space diagonal.

$V=6(8)(10)=480\text{ cm}^3$. $A=2(48+60+80)=376\text{ cm}^2$. $D=\sqrt{36+64+100}=\sqrt{200}\approx14.1\text{ cm}$.

Exercise 3: Find the distance and midpoint between $A(1,-2,3)$ and $B(5,1,15)$.

Coordinate differences are $(4,3,12)$, so $AB=\sqrt{16+9+144}=13$. The midpoint is $(3,-0.5,9)$.

Exercise 4: A closed cylinder has radius $3$ cm and height $8$ cm. Find its volume and total surface area in terms of $\pi$.

$V=\pi(3)^2(8)=72\pi\text{ cm}^3$. Both circular ends count: $A=2\pi(3)(8)+2\pi(3)^2=66\pi\text{ cm}^2$.

Exercise 5: A sphere has volume $36\pi\text{ cm}^3$. Find its radius and surface area.

$\frac43\pi r^3=36\pi$ gives $r^3=27$, hence $r=3$ cm. Its area is $4\pi(3)^2=36\pi\text{ cm}^2$.

Exercise 6: A right square pyramid has base side $6$ cm and perpendicular height $4$ cm. Find its volume and total surface area.

Base area $=36$. The face slant height is $\sqrt{4^2+3^2}=5$. $V=48\text{ cm}^3$, and $A=36+4(\frac12\cdot6\cdot5)=96\text{ cm}^2$.

Exercise 7: A cuboid has a $6$ m by $8$ m base and height $12$ m. Find the angle between its space diagonal and the base plane.

The base projection has length $\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=10$ m. Thus $\theta=\tan^{-1}(12/10)\approx50.2^\circ$. Using $6$ or $8$ as the horizontal length would give a face angle instead.

Exercise 8: A cone with radius $5$ cm and height $12$ cm stands vertex-down. Water reaches a height of $6$ cm above the vertex. Find the water volume.

Similarity gives water radius $5(6/12)=2.5$ cm. $V=\frac13\pi(2.5)^2(6)=12.5\pi\text{ cm}^3\approx39.3\text{ cm}^3$. This is $1/8$ of the full volume $100\pi$.

Exercise 9: A solid consists of a cylinder of radius $3$ cm and height $10$ cm topped by a hemisphere of the same radius. Find its volume and exposed surface area, including the bottom base.

$V=90\pi+\frac23\pi(3)^3=108\pi\text{ cm}^3$. The joined circular face is internal. Exposed area $=2\pi(3)(10)+2\pi(3)^2+\pi(3)^2=87\pi\text{ cm}^2$.

Exercise 10: A metal sphere of radius $3$ cm is melted into cones, each of radius $1$ cm and height $3$ cm. How many complete cones can be made? State an assumption.

Sphere volume $=36\pi\text{ cm}^3$. Each cone has volume $\frac13\pi(1)^2(3)=\pi\text{ cm}^3$. Hence $36$ complete cones. This assumes no material is lost and density is unchanged.
