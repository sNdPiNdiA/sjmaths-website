Derivatives & Tangent Lines

Understanding the derivative as an instantaneous rate of change, applying the power rule $\frac{d}{dx}(ax^n) = anx^{n-1}$, finding the gradient of curves at given points, and constructing equations of tangent lines and normal lines.

AI SL 5.1 - 5.3 Instantaneous Rates Tangents & Normals ~5 Study Hours
5.1A

The Derivative as an Instantaneous Rate of Change

The derivative function, denoted $f'(x)$ or $\frac{dy}{dx}$, gives the exact slope (gradient) of the tangent line to the curve $y = f(x)$ at any point $(x, y)$:

$$f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$$ $$\text{Gradient of tangent at } x = a: \quad m = f'(a)$$

In physical models, if $s(t)$ represents displacement, $v(t) = s'(t)$ represents instantaneous velocity; if $C(x)$ is cost, $C'(x)$ is marginal cost.

5.2A

The Power Rule for Differentiation

For any term $ax^n$ where $n \in \mathbb{Q}$:

$$\frac{d}{dx}\left(a x^n\right) = a \cdot n x^{n-1}$$

Always convert fractional and negative exponents into power form before differentiating:

  • $\frac{1}{x} = x^{-1} \implies \frac{d}{dx}(x^{-1}) = -1x^{-2} = -\frac{1}{x^2}$
  • $\frac{4}{x^3} = 4x^{-3} \implies \frac{d}{dx}(4x^{-3}) = -12x^{-4} = -\frac{12}{x^4}$
  • $\sqrt{x} = x^{0.5} \implies \frac{d}{dx}(x^{0.5}) = 0.5x^{-0.5} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$
  • The derivative of a constant term is zero: $\frac{d}{dx}(c) = 0$.
5.3A

Equations of Tangents and Normals

At a specific point $(x_1, y_1)$ on the curve:

  • Tangent Line: Shares the exact gradient of the curve: $m_t = f'(x_1)$.
    $$y - y_1 = m_t(x - x_1)$$
  • Normal Line: Perpendicular to the tangent line at $(x_1, y_1)$: $m_n = -\frac{1}{m_t}$.
    $$y - y_1 = -\frac{1}{m_t}(x - x_1)$$

Reading and estimating rates

  • The derivative has units of output per input. Positive derivative means increasing value, not necessarily positive value. A zero derivative need not be a turning point.
  • Estimate a rate at t using the symmetric secant $[f(t+h)-f(t-h)]/(2h)$. It is an estimate from finite data, not an exact instantaneous rate.
  • If f′(a)=0, the tangent is horizontal y=f(a) and the normal is vertical x=a. A cusp or discontinuity can make the derivative undefined.
Exercise 1 [3 marks]

Given $f(x) = 3x^3 - 5x^2 + 7x - 12$, find $f'(x)$.

Solution:

Differentiate term by term using the power rule: [M1]

$\frac{d}{dx}(3x^3) = 9x^2$, $\frac{d}{dx}(-5x^2) = -10x$, $\frac{d}{dx}(7x) = 7$, $\frac{d}{dx}(-12) = 0$. [A1]

$f'(x) = 9x^2 - 10x + 7$. [A1]

Exercise 2 [3 marks]

Find the gradient of the curve $y = 2x^2 - 8x + 3$ at the point where $x = 3$.

Solution:

$\frac{dy}{dx} = 4x - 8$. [A1]

Substitute $x = 3$: $\frac{dy}{dx}\Big|_{x=3} = 4(3) - 8 = 12 - 8$ [M1]

Gradient $m = 4$. [A1]

Exercise 3 [4 marks]

Find the equation of the tangent line to the curve $y = x^2 - 4x + 1$ at the point $P(3, -2)$. Give your answer in the form $y = mx + c$.

Solution:

$\frac{dy}{dx} = 2x - 4$. At $x = 3$: $m_t = 2(3) - 4 = 2$. [M1][A1]

Tangent line through $(3, -2)$ with slope $2$: $y - (-2) = 2(x - 3)$ [M1]

$y + 2 = 2x - 6 \implies y = 2x - 8$. [A1]

Exercise 4 [4 marks]

Find the equation of the normal line to the curve $y = x^2 - 4x + 1$ at the point $P(3, -2)$.

Solution:

Tangent gradient at $x = 3$ is $m_t = 2$.

Normal gradient: $m_n = -\frac{1}{m_t} = -\frac{1}{2} = -0.5$. [M1][A1]

Equation of normal: $y - (-2) = -0.5(x - 3)$ [M1]

$y + 2 = -0.5x + 1.5 \implies y = -0.5x - 0.5$ (or $x + 2y + 1 = 0$). [A1]

Exercise 5 [3 marks]

Differentiate $g(x) = \frac{4}{x^2} + 5\sqrt{x}$ with respect to $x$.

Solution:

Rewrite with index notation: $g(x) = 4x^{-2} + 5x^{0.5}$. [M1]

$g'(x) = 4(-2)x^{-3} + 5(0.5)x^{-0.5}$ [M1]

$g'(x) = -8x^{-3} + 2.5x^{-0.5} = -\frac{8}{x^3} + \frac{2.5}{\sqrt{x}}$. [A1]

Exercise 6 [4 marks]

Find the coordinates of the point on the curve $y = 2x^2 - 10x + 7$ where the tangent is horizontal.

Solution:

A horizontal tangent has gradient $m = 0 \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = 0$. [R1]

$\frac{dy}{dx} = 4x - 10 = 0 \implies 4x = 10 \implies x = 2.5$. [M1][A1]

Find $y$: $y = 2(2.5)^2 - 10(2.5) + 7 = 2(6.25) - 25 + 7 = 12.5 - 25 + 7 = -5.5$. [M1]

Coordinates: $(2.5, -5.5)$. [A1]

Exercise 7 [3 marks]

A particle's displacement is $s(t) = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t + 4$ meters. Find its velocity when $t = 4\text{ seconds}$.

Solution:

Velocity $v(t) = s'(t) = 3t^2 - 12t + 9$. [M1][A1]

When $t = 4$: $v(4) = 3(4^2) - 12(4) + 9 = 3(16) - 48 + 9 = 48 - 48 + 9 = 9\text{ m/s}$. [A1]

Exercise 8 [4 marks]

Show that the curve $y = x^3 + 3x$ has no points where the gradient is negative, and deduce whether the function is strictly increasing.

Solution:

$\frac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 + 3$. [M1][A1]

Since $x^2 \ge 0$ for all real $x$, $3x^2 \ge 0$, which means $3x^2 + 3 \ge 3 > 0$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$. [R1]

Since $\frac{dy}{dx} > 0$ everywhere, the function is strictly increasing. [A1]

Exercise 9 [4 marks]

Find the points on the curve $y = \frac{1}{3}x^3 - 4x + 5$ where the tangent is parallel to the line $y = 5x - 7$.

Solution:

Parallel tangents must have the same slope: $m = 5$. [R1]

$\frac{dy}{dx} = x^2 - 4$. Set $x^2 - 4 = 5 \implies x^2 = 9 \implies x = \pm 3$. [M1][A1]

When $x = 3$: $y = \frac{1}{3}(27) - 4(3) + 5 = 9 - 12 + 5 = 2 \implies (3, 2)$.

When $x = -3$: $y = \frac{1}{3}(-27) - 4(-3) + 5 = -9 + 12 + 5 = 8 \implies (-3, 8)$. [A1]

Exercise 10 [3 marks]

Explain the geometric relationship between the tangent line and the normal line at any point on a smooth curve.

Solution:

The tangent line touches the curve locally and shares the curve's instantaneous direction. [A1]

The normal line passes through the point of contact perpendicular ($90^\circ$) to the tangent line. [A1]

Their gradients satisfy $m_{\text{tangent}} \times m_{\text{normal}} = -1$ (provided neither line is horizontal or vertical). [R1]

Core Formulas (Topic 5.1 - 5.3)

Power Rule $$\frac{d}{dx}(a x^n) = a n x^{n-1}$$
Tangent Line Equation $$y - y_1 = m_t(x - x_1) \quad \text{where } m_t = f'(x_1)$$
Normal Line Equation $$y - y_1 = -\frac{1}{m_t}(x - x_1)$$
Horizontal Tangent Condition $$f'(x) = 0$$

Top 5 IB Exam Traps to Avoid

  • Trap 1: Forgetting that $\frac{d}{dx}(\text{constant}) = 0$: Writing $\frac{d}{dx}(5) = 5$ is a common careless slip.
  • Trap 2: Differentiating denominators directly: In $\frac{1}{x^2}$, you must write $x^{-2}$ and get $-2x^{-3} = -\frac{2}{x^3}$. Do not write $\frac{1}{2x}$!
  • Trap 3: Tangent vs Normal Slope confusion: Tangent gradient is $m$, normal gradient is $-\frac{1}{m}$. Always double-check which line the question asked for.
  • Trap 4: Forgetting the $y$-coordinate: If given only the $x$-value, substitute $x$ into the original function $f(x)$ to get $y_1$ (not into $f'(x)$).
  • Trap 5: Using degrees mode on GDC for rates of change: While AI SL differentiation covers polynomials, ensure angles are always in radians when calculus meets trigonometry.
