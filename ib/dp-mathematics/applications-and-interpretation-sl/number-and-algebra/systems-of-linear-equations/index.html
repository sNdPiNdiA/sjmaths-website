Systems of Linear Equations

Solving simultaneous linear equations in 2 and 3 variables, modeling economic and physical mixture problems, and utilizing GDC matrix polynomial tools.

AI SL 1.5 Paper 1 & Paper 2 Multi-Variable Contexts ~4 Study Hours
1.5A

Systems of 2 Linear Equations

  • A system of two linear equations in variables $x$ and $y$: $$\begin{cases} a_1 x + b_1 y = d_1 \\ a_2 x + b_2 y = d_2 \end{cases}$$
  • Three Possible Outcomes:
    • Unique Solution: The lines intersect at a single point $(x, y)$. The slopes are different ($m_1 \ne m_2$).
    • No Solution (Inconsistent): The lines are parallel with different intercepts ($m_1 = m_2, c_1 \ne c_2$).
    • Infinitely Many Solutions (Dependent): The equations represent the identical line ($m_1 = m_2, c_1 = c_2$).
1.5B

Systems of 3 Linear Equations in 3 Variables

  • In real-world applications, three unknown quantities ($x, y, z$) require three distinct linear relationships: $$\begin{cases} a_1 x + b_1 y + c_1 z = d_1 \\ a_2 x + b_2 y + c_2 z = d_2 \\ a_3 x + b_3 y + c_3 z = d_3 \end{cases}$$
  • Using GDC:
    • TI-84 Plus: Use Apps → PlySmlt2 → Simultaneous Eqn Solver with 3 equations and 3 unknowns.
    • TI-Nspire: Use Menu → Algebra → Solve System of Linear Equations.
    • Casio fx-CG50: Use Equation Mode (EQUA) → Simultaneous (F1) → 3 Unknowns (F2).

10 Progressive Practice Exercises

Carefully calibrated with $[M][A][R]$ markschemes covering contextual word problems.

Exercise 1 (Paper 1 — 3 Marks): A cinema charges $\$12$ for an adult ticket and $\$8$ for a child ticket. A group of 14 people spends a total of $\$136$. Find the number of adult tickets and child tickets purchased.
Let $a$ = adult tickets, $c$ = child tickets.
Equation 1: $a + c = 14$
Equation 2: $12a + 8c = 136$ [M1]
From Eq 1, $c = 14 - a$. Substitute into Eq 2:
$12a + 8(14 - a) = 136 \implies 12a + 112 - 8a = 136 \implies 4a = 24 \implies a = 6$ [A1]
$c = 14 - 6 = 8$ [A1]

Answer: 6 adult tickets, 8 child tickets

Exercise 2 (Paper 2 — 5 Marks): A bakery sells three types of cookies: Chocolate ($x$), Oatmeal ($y$), and Peanut Butter ($z$).
  • The price of 2 Chocolate, 3 Oatmeal, and 1 Peanut Butter cookie is $\$11.00$.
  • The price of 3 Chocolate, 1 Oatmeal, and 2 Peanut Butter cookies is $\$10.50$.
  • The price of 1 Chocolate, 4 Oatmeal, and 3 Peanut Butter cookies is $\$15.50$.
  1. Write down a system of 3 linear equations representing this information.
  2. Use your GDC to find the individual price of each type of cookie.
(a) System of equations: $$\begin{cases} 2x + 3y + z = 11.00 \\ 3x + y + 2z = 10.50 \\ x + 4y + 3z = 15.50 \end{cases}$$ [M1][A1]
(b) Enter coefficients into GDC matrix / polynomial simultaneous solver [M1]:
$x = \$2.00$ [A1]
$y = \$1.50$ [A1]
$z = \$2.50$ [A1]

Answer: Chocolate = $\$2.00$, Oatmeal = $\$1.50$, Peanut Butter = $\$2.50$

Formulae

Systems of Equations Matrix Representation

$$A \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b} \iff \begin{pmatrix} a_1 & b_1 & c_1 \\ a_2 & b_2 & c_2 \\ a_3 & b_3 & c_3 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} d_1 \\ d_2 \\ d_3 \end{pmatrix}$$
Examiner Pitfalls

Common Exam Mistakes in Linear Systems

  • Undefined Variables: Always define variables at the outset: "Let $x$ be the price of..." Omitting variable definitions can lose the method mark $[M1]$.
  • Negative Values in Real Contexts: If your solution gives negative quantities for tickets or weights, check your equation coefficients for sign errors.
