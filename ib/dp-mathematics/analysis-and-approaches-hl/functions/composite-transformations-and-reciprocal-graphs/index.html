Composite Transformations & Reciprocal Graphs

Deduce new function curves from known graphs. Study the reciprocal mapping $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$, the squared mapping $y = [f(x)]^2$, identify invariant points, and classify algebraic and graphical symmetry of odd and even functions.

Syllabus AHL 2.13 Paper 1 & Paper 2 Skill 4 Interactive Tabs 10 Tiered Problems + Mini Test

Figure 2.3: Mapping $y = f(x)$ to Reciprocal $y = 1/f(x)$

1. The Reciprocal Transformation: $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$

Given the graph of $y = f(x)$, sketching $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$ follows strict mathematical rules:

Feature on $y = f(x)$ Corresponding Feature on $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$
$x$-intercepts ($f(x) = 0$) Vertical asymptotes ($x = c$). The signs on each side match the signs of $f(x)$.
Vertical asymptotes ($f(x) \to \pm\infty$) $x$-intercepts ($y \to 0$). The curve passes through or touches $(c, 0)$.
Invariant Points ($f(x) = 1$ or $f(x) = -1$) Points of intersection: $\frac{1}{1} = 1$ and $\frac{1}{-1} = -1$. Curves cross at these heights!
Local maximum at $(a, b)$ with $b > 0$ Local minimum at $\left(a, \frac{1}{b}\right)$.
Horizontal asymptote $y = L$ ($L \neq 0$) Horizontal asymptote $y = \frac{1}{L}$. (If $L = 0$, $y \to \pm\infty$).

2. The Squared Transformation: $y = [f(x)]^2$

  • Range: $y \ge 0$ everywhere (the entire graph lies on or above the $x$-axis).
  • Zeros: Real zeros of $f(x)$ remain zeros, but they become tangent to the $x$-axis (turning points) because the derivative $\frac{d}{dx}[f(x)]^2 = 2f(x)f'(x) = 0$ at all zeros.
  • Invariant Points: Where $f(x) = 0$ ($y = 0$) and $f(x) = 1$ ($y = 1$). Any points where $f(x) = -1$ map to $y = (-1)^2 = 1$.
  • Turning Points: If $f(x)$ has a turning point at $(a, b)$, then $[f(x)]^2$ has a turning point at $(a, b^2)$.

3. Odd and Even Functions (Syllabus 2.13)

  • Even Functions: Satisfy $f(-x) = f(x)$ for all $x$ in the domain.
    • Graphically: Reflective symmetry across the $y$-axis (e.g., $\cos x$, $x^2$, $x^4 + 3$, $|x|$).
    • Derivative property: If $f$ is even, $f'$ is an odd function.
  • Odd Functions: Satisfy $f(-x) = -f(x)$ for all $x$ in the domain.
    • Graphically: Rotational symmetry of order 2 (180° rotation) about the origin $(0, 0)$ (e.g., $\sin x$, $x^3$, $\tan x$, $\frac{1}{x}$).
    • Origin property: If $0$ is in the domain, $f(0) = -f(0) \implies f(0) = 0$.
    • Derivative property: If $f$ is odd, $f'$ is an even function.

4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Sketching Reciprocal of a Quadratic

The graph of $f(x) = (x - 1)(x - 5) = x^2 - 6x + 5$ has vertex at $(3, -4)$. Sketch $g(x) = \frac{1}{f(x)}$, stating all asymptotes and the local maximum.

Solution:

• Zeros of $f(x)$ are $x = 1$ and $x = 5$. Therefore, $g(x)$ has vertical asymptotes at $x = 1$ and $x = 5$.
• As $|x| \to \infty$, $f(x) \to +\infty$, so $g(x) \to 0^+$ (horizontal asymptote $y = 0$).
• The local minimum of $f(x)$ is at $(3, -4)$. The reciprocal transformation maps this to a local maximum at $\left(3, -\frac{1}{4}\right)$.
• Invariant points: $f(x) = 1 \implies x^2 - 6x + 4 = 0 \implies x = 3 \pm \sqrt{5}$. At these points, $g(x) = 1$.

Worked Example 2: Classifying Odd, Even, or Neither

Determine algebraically whether each function is odd, even, or neither:
(a) $f(x) = \frac{x^3}{x^2 + 1}$
(b) $g(x) = x \sin x + x^2$
(c) $h(x) = e^x - e^{-x}$

Solution:

(a) $f(-x) = \frac{(-x)^3}{(-x)^2 + 1} = \frac{-x^3}{x^2 + 1} = -f(x)$.
Since $f(-x) = -f(x)$, $f(x)$ is an odd function.

(b) $g(-x) = (-x)\sin(-x) + (-x)^2 = (-x)(-\sin x) + x^2 = x\sin x + x^2 = g(x)$.
Since $g(-x) = g(x)$, $g(x)$ is an even function.

(c) $h(-x) = e^{-x} - e^{-(-x)} = e^{-x} - e^x = -(e^x - e^{-x}) = -h(x)$.
Since $h(-x) = -h(x)$, $h(x)$ is an odd function.

Worked Example 3: Expressing Any Function as Sum of Odd and Even Parts

Show that any function $f(x)$ can be uniquely expressed as the sum of an even function $f_{\text{even}}(x)$ and an odd function $f_{\text{odd}}(x)$.

Solution:

Define:

$$f_{\text{even}}(x) = \frac{f(x) + f(-x)}{2}, \quad f_{\text{odd}}(x) = \frac{f(x) - f(-x)}{2}$$

Check sum: $f_{\text{even}}(x) + f_{\text{odd}}(x) = \frac{f(x) + f(-x) + f(x) - f(-x)}{2} = \frac{2f(x)}{2} = f(x)$.
Check even property:
$f_{\text{even}}(-x) = \frac{f(-x) + f(-(-x))}{2} = \frac{f(-x) + f(x)}{2} = f_{\text{even}}(x)$ (Even!).
Check odd property:
$f_{\text{odd}}(-x) = \frac{f(-x) - f(-(-x))}{2} = \frac{f(-x) - f(x)}{2} = -\frac{f(x) - f(-x)}{2} = -f_{\text{odd}}(x)$ (Odd!).
Hence any function can be uniquely decomposed into an even and odd part.

Practice Exercises (10 Tiered Questions)

Try these 10 exam-style questions, from core skills to harder problems. Open each question to view its solution.

Exercise 1 (Foundational: Reciprocal of Linear Graph)

The linear function $f(x) = 2x - 6$ has $x$-intercept 3 and $y$-intercept -6. State all asymptotes and the $y$-intercept of $g(x) = \frac{1}{f(x)}$.

Show Full Solution

Zero of $f$ is $x = 3 \implies$ Vertical asymptote is $x = 3$. [A1]
As $|x| \to \infty$, $f(x) \to \pm\infty \implies$ Horizontal asymptote is $y = 0$. [A1]
$y$-intercept: $g(0) = \frac{1}{f(0)} = \frac{1}{-6} = -\frac{1}{6}$. [A1]

Exercise 2 (Foundational: Algebraic Odd/Even Test)

Determine whether $f(x) = x^4 - 3x^2 + 7$ is odd, even, or neither.

Show Full Solution

$f(-x) = (-x)^4 - 3(-x)^2 + 7 = x^4 - 3x^2 + 7 = f(x)$. [M1]
Since $f(-x) = f(x)$, $f$ is an even function. [A1]

Exercise 3 (Foundational: Invariant Points on Squared Graph)

A curve $y = f(x)$ passes through $(-2, 0)$, $(1, 1)$, $(3, -1)$, and $(5, 2)$. State the coordinates of the corresponding points on $y = [f(x)]^2$.

Show Full Solution

• $(-2, 0) \to (-2, 0^2) = (-2, 0)$. [A1]
• $(1, 1) \to (1, 1^2) = (1, 1)$. [A1]
• $(3, -1) \to (3, (-1)^2) = (3, 1)$. [A1]
• $(5, 2) \to (5, 2^2) = (5, 4)$. [A1]

Exercise 4 (Intermediate: Reciprocal Transformation with Stationary Point)

The graph of $f(x)$ has a local minimum at $(4, -2)$. State the coordinates and nature of the corresponding stationary point on $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$.

Show Full Solution

The reciprocal transformation inverts the $y$-coordinate: $y = \frac{1}{-2} = -0.5$. [M1]
The nature flips: a local minimum with negative $y$ becomes a local maximum at $(4, -0.5)$. [A1]

Exercise 5 (Intermediate: Even/Odd Product Properties)

Let $f(x)$ be an odd function and $g(x)$ be an even function. Prove that the product $h(x) = f(x)g(x)$ is an odd function.

Show Full Solution

$h(-x) = f(-x)g(-x)$. [M1]
Since $f$ is odd, $f(-x) = -f(x)$. Since $g$ is even, $g(-x) = g(x)$.
$h(-x) = (-f(x))g(x) = -(f(x)g(x)) = -h(x)$. [M1]
Therefore $h(x)$ is an odd function. [A1]

Exercise 6 (Intermediate: Squared Graph Stationary Behavior)

Let $g(x) = [f(x)]^2$. Use the chain rule to show that $g'(x) = 0$ whenever $f(x) = 0$, and interpret this graphically.

Show Full Solution

By the chain rule: $g'(x) = 2f(x)f'(x)$. [M1]
When $f(x) = 0$, $g'(x) = 2(0)f'(x) = 0$. [A1]
Geometrically, this means that every $x$-intercept of $f(x)$ becomes a stationary turning point on $y = [f(x)]^2$ (the curve touches and is tangent to the $x$-axis without crossing below it). [A1]

Exercise 7 (Exam Standard: Reciprocal of Trigonometric Graph)

Given $f(x) = 2\sin x + 1$ for $0 \le x \le 2\pi$, find the equations of all vertical asymptotes of $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$.

Show Full Solution

Vertical asymptotes occur where $f(x) = 0$: [M1]
$2\sin x + 1 = 0 \implies \sin x = -\frac{1}{2}$. [M1]
In $[0, 2\pi]$, reference angle is $\frac{\pi}{6}$. Quadrants 3 and 4:
$x = \pi + \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{7\pi}{6}$ and $x = 2\pi - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{11\pi}{6}$. [A1]
Vertical asymptotes are $x = \frac{7\pi}{6}$ and $x = \frac{11\pi}{6}$. [A1]

Exercise 8 (Advanced: Composite Odd/Even Functions)

Let $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ be functions. Prove that:
(a) If $g(x)$ is even, then $(f \circ g)(x)$ is always even, regardless of $f$.
(b) If both $f$ and $g$ are odd, then $(f \circ g)(x)$ is odd.

Show Full Solution

(a) Even inner function: [M1]
$(f \circ g)(-x) = f(g(-x))$. Since $g$ is even, $g(-x) = g(x)$.
$= f(g(x)) = (f \circ g)(x)$. Thus $(f \circ g)$ is even. [A1]

(b) Both odd: [M1]
$(f \circ g)(-x) = f(g(-x))$. Since $g$ is odd, $g(-x) = -g(x)$.
$= f(-g(x))$. Since $f$ is odd, $f(-u) = -f(u)$.
$= -f(g(x)) = -(f \circ g)(x)$. Thus $(f \circ g)$ is odd. [A1]

Exercise 9 (Advanced: Reciprocal Graph with Oblique Asymptote)

The function $f(x) = \frac{x^2 - 1}{x + 2}$ has an oblique asymptote. Describe the behavior of $g(x) = \frac{1}{f(x)}$ as $x \to \pm\infty$, and state its horizontal asymptote.

Show Full Solution

$g(x) = \frac{1}{f(x)} = \frac{x + 2}{x^2 - 1}$. [M1]
In $g(x)$, degree of numerator is 1, degree of denominator is 2. [M1]
As $x \to \pm\infty$, $\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{x + 2}{x^2 - 1} = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{1/x + 2/x^2}{1 - 1/x^2} = \frac{0}{1} = 0$. [A1]
Therefore, the oblique asymptote of $f(x)$ maps to the horizontal asymptote $y = 0$ on the reciprocal curve $g(x)$. [A1]

Exercise 10 (Advanced: Integral of Even and Odd Functions)

Use symmetry properties to evaluate:
(a) $\int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4} x^5 \cos(x^2)\,dx$
(b) $\int_{-2}^2 (3x^2 + 5x^3)\,dx$

Show Full Solution

(a) Odd integrand: [M1]
Let $k(x) = x^5 \cos(x^2)$.
$k(-x) = (-x)^5 \cos((-x)^2) = -x^5 \cos(x^2) = -k(x)$ (Odd!).
The integral of any continuous odd function over symmetric bounds $[-a, a]$ is strictly zero.
$\int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4} x^5 \cos(x^2)\,dx = 0$. [A1]

(b) Splitting into even and odd parts: [M1]
$\int_{-2}^2 (3x^2 + 5x^3)\,dx = 3\int_{-2}^2 x^2\,dx + 5\int_{-2}^2 x^3\,dx$.
$5x^3$ is odd, so $\int_{-2}^2 5x^3\,dx = 0$. [A1]
$3x^2$ is even, so $3\int_{-2}^2 x^2\,dx = 2 \times 3\int_0^2 x^2\,dx = 6\left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^2 = 2(8) = 16$. [A1]

Must-Memorize Principles (Not in Booklet)

1. Reciprocal Mapping $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$

  • $f(x) = 0 \implies$ Vertical asymptote.
  • Vertical asymptote of $f(x) \implies x$-intercept of $\frac{1}{f(x)}$.
  • $f(x) = \pm 1 \implies$ Invariant points.
  • Local maximum $(a, b) \implies$ Local minimum $\left(a, \frac{1}{b}\right)$.

2. Odd and Even Algebraic Tests

  • Even: $f(-x) = f(x) \implies$ Symmetric about the $y$-axis.
  • Odd: $f(-x) = -f(x) \implies$ Rotational symmetry 180° about the origin.

Examination Pitfalls & Examiner Tips

Sign Consistency in Reciprocals

The reciprocal of a positive number is ALWAYS positive, and the reciprocal of a negative number is ALWAYS negative. The graph of $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$ must have identical signs to $f(x)$ in every interval!

Odd Function Value at Origin

If an odd function is defined at $x = 0$, its value MUST be 0 ($f(0) = 0$). If $f(0) \neq 0$, the function cannot be odd!

Question 5 (Paper 1 Style · 6 marks)

The function $f$ is defined by $f(x) = x\sqrt{4 - x^2}$ for $-2 \le x \le 2$.
(a) Show that $f$ is an odd function. [2 marks]
(b) Find the stationary points of $f(x)$. [3 marks]
(c) Hence sketch the graph of $f(x)$ showing all key features. [1 mark]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Odd function proof: [2 marks]
$f(-x) = (-x)\sqrt{4 - (-x)^2} = -x\sqrt{4 - x^2} = -f(x)$ [M1].
Since $f(-x) = -f(x)$ for all $x \in [-2, 2]$, $f$ is an odd function [A1].

(b) Stationary points: [3 marks]
$f'(x) = 1\sqrt{4 - x^2} + x\left(\frac{-2x}{2\sqrt{4 - x^2}}\right) = \sqrt{4 - x^2} - \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{4 - x^2}} = \frac{4 - 2x^2}{\sqrt{4 - x^2}}$ [M1].
$f'(x) = 0 \implies 4 - 2x^2 = 0 \implies x^2 = 2 \implies x = \pm\sqrt{2}$ [A1].
• At $x = \sqrt{2}$: $y = \sqrt{2}\sqrt{4 - 2} = \sqrt{2}\sqrt{2} = 2$. Local maximum at $(\sqrt{2}, 2)$.
• At $x = -\sqrt{2}$: by odd symmetry, local minimum at $(-\sqrt{2}, -2)$ [A1].

(c) Sketch: [1 mark]
Passes through $(-2, 0), (0, 0), (2, 0)$ with rotational symmetry order 2 about origin [A1].

Question 6 (Paper 2 Style · 8 marks)

Let $f(x) = \frac{1}{x^2 - 4}$.
(a) State the equations of all asymptotes of $y = f(x)$. [2 marks]
(b) Sketch $y = f(x)$ and on the same axes sketch $y = [f(x)]^2$. [3 marks]
(c) Find the coordinates of the invariant points where $f(x) = [f(x)]^2$. [3 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Asymptotes: [2 marks]
Vertical asymptotes: $x^2 - 4 = 0 \implies x = 2$ and $x = -2$ [A1].
Horizontal asymptote: $\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{1}{x^2 - 4} = 0 \implies y = 0$ [A1].

(b) Sketch comparison: [3 marks]
• $f(x)$ is negative for $-2 < x < 2$ with local max at $(0, -0.25)$ [A1].
• $[f(x)]^2 = \frac{1}{(x^2 - 4)^2}$ is positive everywhere, with local min at $(0, 0.0625)$ and asymptotes at $x = \pm 2, y = 0$ [A1][A1].

(c) Invariant points: [3 marks]
$f(x) = [f(x)]^2 \iff f(x)[1 - f(x)] = 0$ [M1].
Since $f(x) = \frac{1}{x^2 - 4} \neq 0$, we must have $f(x) = 1$ [M1].
$\frac{1}{x^2 - 4} = 1 \implies x^2 - 4 = 1 \implies x^2 = 5 \implies x = \pm\sqrt{5}$ [A1].
Invariant points: $(\sqrt{5}, 1)$ and $(-\sqrt{5}, 1)$.
