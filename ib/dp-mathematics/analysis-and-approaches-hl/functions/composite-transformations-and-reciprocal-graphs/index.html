Composite Transformations & Reciprocal Graphs

Deduce new function curves from known graphs. Master the reciprocal mapping $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$, the squared mapping $y = [f(x)]^2$, strict order of operations for composite shifts and stretches, invariant points, and algebraic/graphical symmetries of odd and even functions.

1. The Reciprocal Transformation: $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$

In IB DP Mathematics AA HL, sketching $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$ given the visual curve or equation of $y = f(x)$ is tested heavily on Paper 1 (non-calculator). The geometric mapping rules must be memorized and applied systematically:

Feature on Original $y = f(x)$ Transformed Feature on $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$ Underlying Mathematical Justification
Zeros / $x$-intercepts: $(c, 0)$ Vertical Asymptotes: $x = c$ $\lim_{x \to c} \frac{1}{f(x)} = \pm\infty$. Signs on either side match the sign of $f(x)$!
Vertical Asymptotes: $x = k$ $x$-intercepts: $(k, 0)$ (or open holes) As $x \to k$, $|f(x)| \to \infty \implies \frac{1}{f(x)} \to 0$.
Invariant Points: $f(x) = 1$ or $f(x) = -1$ Points of Intersection: $y = 1$ or $y = -1$ $\frac{1}{1} = 1$ and $\frac{1}{-1} = -1$. The curves intersect exactly at these two horizontal lines.
Local Maximum: $(p, q)$ with $q > 0$ Local Minimum: $\left(p, \frac{1}{q}\right)$ $\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{1}{f(x)}\right] = -\frac{f'(x)}{[f(x)]^2} = 0 \iff f'(x) = 0$. Sign of slope inverts!
Local Minimum: $(u, v)$ with $v < 0$ Local Maximum: $\left(u, \frac{1}{v}\right)$ Reciprocal flips turning point nature while preserving the $x$-coordinate.
Horizontal Asymptote: $y = L$ ($L \neq 0$) Horizontal Asymptote: $y = \frac{1}{L}$ If $f(x) \to \pm\infty$ as $x \to \pm\infty$, then $\frac{1}{f(x)} \to 0$, giving horizontal asymptote $y = 0$.

2. The Squared Transformation: $y = [f(x)]^2$

The squared mapping $y = [f(x)]^2$ creates distinct geometric and calculus features:

  • Strict Non-Negativity: The entire curve satisfies $y \ge 0$ for all $x \in \text{dom}(f)$. No portion of the graph exists below the $x$-axis.
  • Zeros Become Stationary Tangencies: By the chain rule, $\frac{d}{dx}[f(x)]^2 = 2f(x)f'(x)$. Whenever $f(x) = 0$, the derivative is strictly zero! Thus, every simple root of $f(x)$ becomes a local minimum and tangent to the $x$-axis.
  • Invariant Points: Where $f(x) = 0 \implies y = 0$; where $f(x) = 1 \implies y = 1$. Any points where $f(x) = -1$ reflect to $y = (-1)^2 = 1$.
  • Turning Points: If $(a, b)$ is a turning point on $f(x)$, then $(a, b^2)$ is a turning point on $[f(x)]^2$. Notice that if $b < 0$, a local minimum of $f(x)$ with negative height reflects and squares into a local maximum on $[f(x)]^2$!

3. Composite Transformations & Strict Order of Operations

A general composite transformation is represented as:

$$y = A \cdot f(B(x - C)) + D$$

Horizontal vs. Vertical Execution

• Horizontal Transformations (Inner operations on $x$): Affect only $x$-coordinates. Always factor the coefficient of $x$: $f(Bx - E) = f\left(B\left(x - \frac{E}{B}\right)\right)$.
  1. Horizontal stretch by scale factor $\frac{1}{|B|}$ (and reflection across $y$-axis if $B < 0$).
  2. Horizontal translation by vector $\begin{pmatrix} C \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} E/B \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

• Vertical Transformations (Outer operations on $y$): Follow standard arithmetic order of operations (BODMAS):
  1. Vertical stretch by scale factor $|A|$ (and reflection across $x$-axis if $A < 0$).
  2. Vertical translation by vector $\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ D \end{pmatrix}$.

4. Odd and Even Functions (Syllabus AHL 2.13)

Algebraic and graphical symmetry is tested both independently and within calculus integration questions:

  • Even Functions: Satisfy $f(-x) = f(x)$ for all $x \in \text{dom}(f)$.
    • Graphical Symmetry: Reflectional symmetry about the vertical $y$-axis ($x = 0$).
    • Calculus Properties: The derivative of an even function is an odd function: $f'(-x) = -f'(x)$.
    • Definite Integral: $\int_{-a}^a f(x)\,dx = 2\int_0^a f(x)\,dx$.
  • Odd Functions: Satisfy $f(-x) = -f(x)$ for all $x \in \text{dom}(f)$.
    • Graphical Symmetry: Rotational symmetry of order 2 (180° rotation) about the origin $(0, 0)$.
    • Origin Property: If $0 \in \text{dom}(f)$, then $f(0) = -f(0) \implies 2f(0) = 0 \implies \mathbf{f(0) = 0}$. (An odd function defined at $x = 0$ must pass through the origin).
    • Calculus Properties: The derivative of an odd function is an even function: $f'(-x) = f'(x)$.
    • Definite Integral: $\int_{-a}^a f(x)\,dx = 0$ (net signed area cancels).
  • Decomposition Theorem: Any function $f(x)$ with symmetric domain can be uniquely decomposed into an even part $f_E(x)$ and an odd part $f_O(x)$: $$f(x) = \underbrace{\frac{f(x) + f(-x)}{2}}_{f_E(x) \text{ (even)}} + \underbrace{\frac{f(x) - f(-x)}{2}}_{f_O(x) \text{ (odd)}}$$

The 6 Classic IB Exam Question Archetypes

Every transformation and symmetry question on IB DP Mathematics AA HL Paper 1 and Paper 2 conforms to one of these six primary archetypes:

Archetype Typical Exam Setup Primary Strategy / Steps Common Traps & Mark Losses
1. Reciprocal Curve Sketch Given visual graph or polynomial $f(x)$, sketch $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$ indicating all asymptotes and turning points. 1. Set vertical asymptotes at roots $f(x) = 0$.
2. Flip local max $(p, q) \to$ local min $(p, 1/q)$.
3. Determine end behavior $f \to \pm\infty \implies y \to 0$.
4. Mark invariant points at $y = \pm 1$.		 Flipping sign: reciprocal of negative numbers must remain strictly negative! Missing dashed asymptote equations ($x = c, y = 0$).
2. Squared Curve Deduction Given $y = f(x)$, sketch $y = [f(x)]^2$ and find all invariant points where $f(x) = [f(x)]^2$. 1. Reflect all negative parts above $x$-axis.
2. Make all $x$-intercepts tangent turning points ($y'=0$).
3. Square $y$-values: $(x, y) \to (x, y^2)$.
4. Invariant points only occur at $f(x) = 0$ and $f(x) = 1$.		 Drawing sharp V-corners at the $x$-axis like an absolute value graph instead of smooth rounded parabolic tangencies ($g'=0$).
3. Order of Composite Transformations Describe a full sequence of geometric transformations mapping $y = f(x)$ to $y = A f(B x - C) + D$. 1. Factor $B$: $f(B(x - C/B))$.
2. Horizontal: stretch by factor $1/B$, translate $+C/B$.
3. Vertical: stretch by factor $A$, translate $+D$.		 Stating horizontal shift as $+C$ instead of $+C/B$. Confusing the order of stretch and translation.
4. Odd / Even Algebraic Proof Show whether a rational, trigonometric, or exponential composite function is odd, even, or neither. 1. Explicitly evaluate $f(-x)$ step by step.
2. Simplify and factor $-1$ if testing oddness.
3. State conclusion: $f(-x) = f(x) \implies$ even, or $f(-x) = -f(x) \implies$ odd.		 Testing with single numbers (e.g. $f(-2) = f(2)$) instead of general algebraic variable $-x$. Forgetting domain symmetry check.
5. Symmetric Integral Evaluation Evaluate $\int_{-a}^a g(x)\,dx$ without finding antiderivative, where $g(x)$ contains odd/even components. 1. Split integrand into even and odd terms.
2. Odd term integral vanishes: $\int_{-a}^a g_{\text{odd}}(x)\,dx = 0$.
3. Compute $2\int_0^a g_{\text{even}}(x)\,dx$.		 Attempting tedious integration by parts or substitution on non-integrable odd terms instead of using parity properties.
6. Invariant Points & Inequalities Solve inequalities of the form $f(x) \ge \frac{1}{f(x)}$ or find intersection coordinates. 1. Set $f(x) - \frac{1}{f(x)} = \frac{[f(x)]^2 - 1}{f(x)} \ge 0$.
2. Sign analysis on $[f(x) - 1][f(x) + 1] / f(x)$.
3. Read intervals directly from graphic sketches.		 Multiplying across by $f(x)$ without considering its sign, which inverts inequality signs when $f(x) < 0$.

Authentic IB DP Exam Worked Exemplars

Worked Exemplar 1: Reciprocal of a Cubic Polynomial (Paper 1 Style · 7 marks)

The function $f$ is a cubic polynomial with real coefficients. The graph of $y = f(x)$ has local maximum at $(-2, 4)$, local minimum at $(2, -1)$, and zeros at $x = -3$, $x = 0$, and $x = 3$.
(a) State the equations of all asymptotes of the graph of $g(x) = \frac{1}{f(x)}$. [3 marks]
(b) State the coordinates and nature of all local extrema of $g(x)$. [2 marks]
(c) Sketch the graph of $y = g(x)$, clearly showing all asymptotes and stationary points. [2 marks]

Official Markscheme Solution:

(a) Asymptotes: [3 marks]
• Vertical asymptotes occur where $f(x) = 0$: $x = -3$, $x = 0$, $x = 3$ [M1][A1].
• Horizontal asymptote: As $|x| \to \infty$, $|f(x)| \to \infty \implies \lim_{|x| \to \infty} \frac{1}{f(x)} = 0 \implies y = 0$ [A1].
Examiner Tip: Always write full equations with variables ($x = -3, x = 0, x = 3, y = 0$). Stating just numbers loses accuracy marks.

(b) Extrema: [2 marks]
• Local maximum on $f$ at $(-2, 4)$ maps to a local minimum at $\left(-2, \frac{1}{4}\right)$ [A1].
• Local minimum on $f$ at $(2, -1)$ maps to a local maximum at $\left(2, \frac{1}{-1}\right) = (2, -1)$ [A1].

(c) Sketch: [2 marks]
• 4 branches separated by the 3 vertical asymptotes $x = -3, 0, 3$ with correct asymptotic signs matching $f(x)$ [A1].
• Correct local minimum at $(-2, 0.25)$ and local maximum at $(2, -1)$, curve approaching $y = 0$ as $x \to \pm\infty$ [A1].

Worked Exemplar 2: Squared Curve Analysis & Tangencies (Paper 1 Style · 6 marks)

Let $f(x) = x(x - 4)^2$. Consider the function $h(x) = [f(x)]^2$.
(a) Find the roots of $h(x) = 0$ and state their multiplicity. [2 marks]
(b) Using the derivative $h'(x)$, prove that the graph of $y = h(x)$ has horizontal tangents at all its $x$-intercepts. [2 marks]
(c) Determine the coordinates of all invariant points where $f(x) = h(x)$. [2 marks]

Official Markscheme Solution:

(a) Roots: [2 marks]
$h(x) = [x(x - 4)^2]^2 = x^2 (x - 4)^4$ [M1].
Roots are $x = 0$ (multiplicity 2) and $x = 4$ (multiplicity 4) [A1].

(b) Derivative Proof: [2 marks]
By chain rule: $h'(x) = 2 f(x) f'(x)$ [M1].
At any $x$-intercept, $f(x) = 0$.
Therefore, $h'(x) = 2(0) f'(x) = 0$ [R1].
Since the derivative is zero at $x = 0$ and $x = 4$, the tangent to $y = h(x)$ is horizontal ($y' = 0$) at every $x$-intercept [AG].

(c) Invariant Points: [2 marks]
$f(x) = [f(x)]^2 \iff f(x)[1 - f(x)] = 0 \iff f(x) = 0$ or $f(x) = 1$ [M1].
• $f(x) = 0 \implies x = 0$ and $x = 4$, yielding invariant points $(0, 0)$ and $(4, 0)$ [A1].
• $f(x) = 1 \implies x(x - 4)^2 = 1$. These points have coordinates $(x_i, 1)$ where $x_i$ are the real solutions to $x(x - 4)^2 = 1$.

Worked Exemplar 3: Composite Transformation Sequence (Paper 1 Style · 5 marks)

The graph of $y = f(x)$ is transformed to the graph of $y = -3 f(2x + 8) - 5$.
(a) Describe in full the precise sequence of geometric transformations required. [4 marks]
(b) If $f(x)$ has a local maximum at $(1, 3)$, find the coordinates of the corresponding point on the transformed curve. [1 mark]

Official Markscheme Solution:

(a) Sequence of Transformations: [4 marks]
First factor the argument: $-3 f(2(x + 4)) - 5$ [M1].
1. Horizontal stretch by a scale factor of $\frac{1}{2}$ parallel to the $x$-axis [A1].
2. Horizontal translation by the vector $\begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$ (or 4 units in the negative $x$-direction) [A1].
3. Vertical stretch by a scale factor of 3 parallel to the $y$-axis and reflection in the $x$-axis [A1].
4. Vertical translation by the vector $\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix}$ (or 5 units downwards) [A1].

(b) Transformed Point: [1 mark]
• New $x$-coordinate: $x_{\text{new}} = \frac{1}{2}(1) - 4 = 0.5 - 4 = -3.5$ [M1].
• New $y$-coordinate: $y_{\text{new}} = -3(3) - 5 = -9 - 5 = -14$.
Transformed point is $(-3.5, -14)$ [A1].

Worked Exemplar 4: Parity Decomposition & Definite Integral (Paper 2 Style · 7 marks)

Let $f(x) = \frac{\sin x + x^2}{1 + x^2}$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}$.
(a) Express $f(x)$ as the sum of an even function $f_E(x)$ and an odd function $f_O(x)$. [3 marks]
(b) Hence evaluate the exact value of $\int_{-2}^2 f(x)\,dx$. [4 marks]

Official Markscheme Solution:

(a) Parity Decomposition: [3 marks]
$f(-x) = \frac{\sin(-x) + (-x)^2}{1 + (-x)^2} = \frac{-\sin x + x^2}{1 + x^2}$ [M1].
• $f_E(x) = \frac{f(x) + f(-x)}{2} = \frac{\frac{\sin x + x^2}{1 + x^2} + \frac{-\sin x + x^2}{1 + x^2}}{2} = \frac{2x^2}{2(1 + x^2)} = \frac{x^2}{1 + x^2}$ [A1].
• $f_O(x) = \frac{f(x) - f(-x)}{2} = \frac{\frac{\sin x + x^2}{1 + x^2} - \frac{-\sin x + x^2}{1 + x^2}}{2} = \frac{2\sin x}{2(1 + x^2)} = \frac{\sin x}{1 + x^2}$ [A1].

(b) Integral Evaluation: [4 marks]
$\int_{-2}^2 f(x)\,dx = \int_{-2}^2 f_E(x)\,dx + \int_{-2}^2 f_O(x)\,dx$ [M1].
Since $f_O(x)$ is continuous and odd on $[-2, 2]$, $\int_{-2}^2 f_O(x)\,dx = 0$ [R1].
Since $f_E(x)$ is even, $\int_{-2}^2 f_E(x)\,dx = 2 \int_0^2 \frac{x^2}{1 + x^2}\,dx$ [M1].
Use algebraic division: $\frac{x^2}{1 + x^2} = \frac{x^2 + 1 - 1}{x^2 + 1} = 1 - \frac{1}{1 + x^2}$ [M1].
$= 2 \left[ x - \arctan x \right]_0^2 = 2 (2 - \arctan 2 - 0) = 4 - 2\arctan 2$ [A1].

Worked Exemplar 5: Rational Function Symmetries & Reciprocal Analysis (Paper 2 Style · 8 marks)

Let $f(x) = \frac{x}{x^2 - 1}$ for $x \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{-1, 1\}$.
(a) Show algebraically that $f$ is an odd function and state its geometric symmetry. [2 marks]
(b) Determine the equations of all asymptotes of $y = f(x)$. [2 marks]
(c) Deduce the function $g(x) = \frac{1}{f(x)}$ and find all real solutions to $g(x) = x$. [4 marks]

Official Markscheme Solution:

(a) Odd Symmetry: [2 marks]
$f(-x) = \frac{-x}{(-x)^2 - 1} = \frac{-x}{x^2 - 1} = -f(x)$ [M1].
Since $f(-x) = -f(x)$ for all $x$ in its symmetric domain, $f$ is an odd function.
Geometric symmetry: Rotational symmetry of order 2 (180° rotational symmetry) about the origin $(0, 0)$ [A1].

(b) Asymptotes: [2 marks]
• Vertical asymptotes: $x^2 - 1 = 0 \implies x = 1$ and $x = -1$ [A1].
• Horizontal asymptote: $\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{x}{x^2 - 1} = 0 \implies y = 0$ [A1].

(c) Reciprocal & Equation Solving: [4 marks]
$g(x) = \frac{1}{f(x)} = \frac{x^2 - 1}{x} = x - \frac{1}{x}$ for $x \neq 0, \pm 1$ [A1].
Set $g(x) = x \implies x - \frac{1}{x} = x$ [M1].
$-\frac{1}{x} = 0$, which has no real solution [A1].
Alternatively: $x^2 - 1 = x^2 \implies -1 = 0$, a contradiction [R1].
Hence, there are no real solutions to $g(x) = x$.

Practice Exercises (10 Tiered Questions)

Work through these 10 examination-calibrated exercises ranging from foundational skills to challenging HL Paper 1 and Paper 2 problems. Expand each exercise to view the full IB markscheme and examiner advice.

Exercise 1 (Foundational: Reciprocal of a Linear Graph)

The linear function $f(x) = 2x - 6$ has $x$-intercept 3 and $y$-intercept -6. State all asymptotes and the $y$-intercept of $g(x) = \frac{1}{f(x)}$.

Show Full Markscheme

• Zero of $f(x)$ occurs at $x = 3 \implies$ Vertical asymptote: $x = 3$ [A1].
• As $|x| \to \infty$, $f(x) \to \pm\infty \implies \frac{1}{f(x)} \to 0 \implies$ Horizontal asymptote: $y = 0$ [A1].
• $y$-intercept: $g(0) = \frac{1}{f(0)} = \frac{1}{-6} = -\frac{1}{6}$ [A1].
Examiner Advice: Asymptotes must always be stated as complete equations ($x = 3, y = 0$), not just values.

Exercise 2 (Foundational: Algebraic Odd/Even Classification)

Determine algebraically whether $f(x) = x^4 - 3x^2 + 7$ is odd, even, or neither.

Show Full Markscheme

Substitute $-x$ into the function: [M1]
$f(-x) = (-x)^4 - 3(-x)^2 + 7 = x^4 - 3x^2 + 7$ [A1].
Since $f(-x) = f(x)$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$, $f$ is an even function [A1].
Examiner Advice: State the concluding identity $f(-x) = f(x)$ clearly to earn the final reasoning mark.

Exercise 3 (Foundational: Invariant Points on a Squared Graph)

A curve $y = f(x)$ passes through $(-2, 0)$, $(1, 1)$, $(3, -1)$, and $(5, 2)$. State the coordinates of the corresponding points on $y = [f(x)]^2$.

Show Full Markscheme

Apply the transformation $(x, y) \to (x, y^2)$ to each coordinate: [M1]
• $(-2, 0) \to (-2, 0^2) = (-2, 0)$ [A1].
• $(1, 1) \to (1, 1^2) = (1, 1)$ [A1].
• $(3, -1) \to (3, (-1)^2) = (3, 1)$ [A1].
• $(5, 2) \to (5, 2^2) = (5, 4)$ [A1].
Examiner Advice: Note that $(3, -1)$ reflects across the $x$-axis because squaring converts negative ordinates to positive.

Exercise 4 (Intermediate: Reciprocal Transformation of a Stationary Point)

The graph of $f(x)$ has a local minimum at $(4, -2)$. State the coordinates and nature of the corresponding stationary point on $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$.

Show Full Markscheme

• The reciprocal transformation inverts the $y$-coordinate while preserving the $x$-coordinate: $y = \frac{1}{-2} = -0.5$ [M1].
• Nature of stationary point: Since $\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{1}{f(x)}\right] = -\frac{f'(x)}{[f(x)]^2}$, the sign of the slope flips on either side of the point. A local minimum with negative height becomes a local maximum at $(4, -0.5)$ [A1].
Examiner Advice: A common trap is assuming that because $y$ is negative, the nature doesn't flip. All local extrema flip nature under the reciprocal mapping!

Exercise 5 (Intermediate: Product of Odd and Even Functions)

Let $f(x)$ be an odd function and $g(x)$ be an even function. Prove algebraically that the product $h(x) = f(x)g(x)$ is an odd function.

Show Full Markscheme

Evaluate $h(-x)$: [M1]
$h(-x) = f(-x) \cdot g(-x)$ [M1].
Since $f$ is odd, $f(-x) = -f(x)$. Since $g$ is even, $g(-x) = g(x)$ [R1].
$h(-x) = (-f(x)) \cdot g(x) = -(f(x)g(x)) = -h(x)$ [A1].
Since $h(-x) = -h(x)$, $h(x)$ is an odd function [AG].

Exercise 6 (Intermediate: Squared Graph Stationary Tangencies)

Let $g(x) = [f(x)]^2$. Use the chain rule to show that $g'(x) = 0$ whenever $f(x) = 0$, and explain the graphical significance.

Show Full Markscheme

By the chain rule: $g'(x) = 2f(x)f'(x)$ [M1].
When $f(x) = 0$: $g'(x) = 2(0)f'(x) = 0$ (assuming $f'(x)$ exists) [A1].
Graphical significance: Every real root of $f(x)$ becomes a stationary turning point on $y = [f(x)]^2$. Because $[f(x)]^2 \ge 0$, the curve touches and is tangent to the $x$-axis, forming a local minimum at each zero without crossing below the axis [R1].

Exercise 7 (Exam Standard: Reciprocal of a Trigonometric Function)

Given $f(x) = 2\sin x + 1$ for $0 \le x \le 2\pi$, find the equations of all vertical asymptotes of $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$.

Show Full Markscheme

Vertical asymptotes occur where $f(x) = 0$: [M1]
$2\sin x + 1 = 0 \implies \sin x = -\frac{1}{2}$ [M1].
In the domain $[0, 2\pi]$, the reference angle is $\arcsin(1/2) = \frac{\pi}{6}$.
Sine is negative in Quadrants 3 and 4:
• Quadrant 3: $x = \pi + \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{7\pi}{6}$ [A1].
• Quadrant 4: $x = 2\pi - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{11\pi}{6}$ [A1].
Vertical asymptotes: $x = \frac{7\pi}{6}$ and $x = \frac{11\pi}{6}$.

Exercise 8 (Advanced: Parity of Composite Functions)

Let $f$ and $g$ be two functions. Prove that:
(a) If $g$ is an even function, then $(f \circ g)$ is an even function for any function $f$.
(b) If both $f$ and $g$ are odd functions, then $(f \circ g)$ is an odd function.

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Even inner function: [2 marks]
Consider $(f \circ g)(-x) = f(g(-x))$ [M1].
Since $g$ is even, $g(-x) = g(x)$.
Therefore, $f(g(-x)) = f(g(x)) = (f \circ g)(x)$ [A1].
Hence $(f \circ g)$ is an even function [AG].

(b) Both functions odd: [2 marks]
Consider $(f \circ g)(-x) = f(g(-x))$ [M1].
Since $g$ is odd, $g(-x) = -g(x)$.
$= f(-g(x))$. Since $f$ is odd, $f(-u) = -f(u)$.
$= -f(g(x)) = -(f \circ g)(x)$ [A1].
Hence $(f \circ g)$ is an odd function [AG].

Exercise 9 (Advanced: Reciprocal of a Rational Function with Oblique Asymptote)

The function $f(x) = \frac{x^2 - 1}{x + 2}$ has an oblique asymptote. Describe the asymptotic behavior of $g(x) = \frac{1}{f(x)}$ as $x \to \pm\infty$, and find its horizontal asymptote.

Show Full Markscheme

Construct the reciprocal function: [M1]
$g(x) = \frac{1}{f(x)} = \frac{x + 2}{x^2 - 1}$ for $x \neq \pm 1$ [A1].
In $g(x)$, the degree of the numerator (1) is strictly less than the degree of the denominator (2) [R1].
Take the limits as $x \to \pm\infty$:
$\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{x + 2}{x^2 - 1} = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{\frac{1}{x} + \frac{2}{x^2}}{1 - \frac{1}{x^2}} = \frac{0}{1} = 0$ [M1].
Horizontal asymptote: $y = 0$ [A1].
Key Takeaway: Whenever $f(x)$ has an oblique asymptote ($|f(x)| \to \infty$ as $|x| \to \infty$), its reciprocal $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$ always possesses the horizontal asymptote $y = 0$.

Exercise 10 (Advanced: Definite Integrals Using Parity Properties)

Use odd and even parity properties to evaluate the exact value of:
(a) $\int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4} x^5 \cos(x^2)\,dx$
(b) $\int_{-2}^2 (3x^2 + 5x^3)\,dx$

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Odd integrand: [2 marks]
Let $k(x) = x^5 \cos(x^2)$. Test parity: [M1]
$k(-x) = (-x)^5 \cos((-x)^2) = -x^5 \cos(x^2) = -k(x)$.
Since $k(x)$ is continuous and odd, its integral over any symmetric interval $[-a, a]$ is strictly zero:
$\int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4} x^5 \cos(x^2)\,dx = 0$ [A1].

(b) Splitting into even and odd components: [3 marks]
$\int_{-2}^2 (3x^2 + 5x^3)\,dx = \int_{-2}^2 3x^2\,dx + \int_{-2}^2 5x^3\,dx$ [M1].
• $5x^3$ is an odd function, so $\int_{-2}^2 5x^3\,dx = 0$ [R1].
• $3x^2$ is an even function, so $\int_{-2}^2 3x^2\,dx = 2 \int_0^2 3x^2\,dx = 6 \left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^2 = 2(2^3) = 16$ [A1].
Total value: $16 + 0 = 16$ [A1].

The official IB DP Mathematics AA Formula Booklet provides basic translation and stretch notation:

  • $y = f(x) + d$: Vertical translation by $\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ d \end{pmatrix}$.
  • $y = f(x - c)$: Horizontal translation by $\begin{pmatrix} c \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
  • $y = a f(x)$: Vertical stretch by scale factor $a$.
  • $y = f(b x)$: Horizontal stretch by scale factor $\frac{1}{b}$.

Crucial Note: The Formula Booklet does NOT include rules for reciprocal curves $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$, squared curves $y = [f(x)]^2$, or algebraic tests for odd/even functions. These must be committed to memory.

Must-Memorize Principles (Not in Formula Booklet)

1. Reciprocal Transformation Checklist: $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$

  • $f(x) = 0 \implies$ Vertical asymptote $x = c$.
  • Vertical asymptote on $f(x) \implies x$-intercept on $\frac{1}{f(x)}$.
  • Invariant points: $f(x) = 1 \implies y = 1$; $f(x) = -1 \implies y = -1$.
  • Local maximum $(p, q)$ with $q > 0 \implies$ Local minimum $\left(p, \frac{1}{q}\right)$.
  • Local minimum $(u, v)$ with $v < 0 \implies$ Local maximum $\left(u, \frac{1}{v}\right)$.
  • Sign rule: Sign of $\frac{1}{f(x)}$ is identical to the sign of $f(x)$ across all intervals.

2. Squared Transformation Checklist: $y = [f(x)]^2$

  • Range is non-negative: $y \ge 0$ everywhere.
  • Zeros become stationary tangencies: $\frac{d}{dx}[f(x)]^2 = 2f(x)f'(x) = 0$ at all roots.
  • Invariant points: $(x, 0) \to (x, 0)$ and $(x, 1) \to (x, 1)$. Also $(x, -1) \to (x, 1)$.
  • Turning point $(a, b) \to (a, b^2)$. If $b < 0$, minimum flips to maximum!

3. Odd and Even Function Symmetry Checklist

  • Even Function: $f(-x) = f(x)$ $\implies$ Reflectional symmetry across the $y$-axis ($x = 0$). Integral: $\int_{-a}^a f(x)\,dx = 2\int_0^a f(x)\,dx$.
  • Odd Function: $f(-x) = -f(x)$ $\implies$ 180° rotational symmetry about origin $(0, 0)$. If $f(0)$ exists, $f(0) = 0$. Integral: $\int_{-a}^a f(x)\,dx = 0$.
  • Calculus Parity Inversion: $\frac{d}{dx}[\text{even}] = \text{odd}$; $\frac{d}{dx}[\text{odd}] = \text{even}$.
  • Decomposition: $f_E(x) = \frac{f(x) + f(-x)}{2}$, $f_O(x) = \frac{f(x) - f(-x)}{2}$.

High-Frequency Examination Pitfalls

Pitfall 1: Sign Discrepancies on Reciprocal Curves

Remember that $\frac{1}{\text{negative}} = \text{negative}$. When $f(x)$ is negative, $\frac{1}{f(x)}$ must also be negative. Students frequently reflect the reciprocal curve into the upper half-plane by mistake!

Pitfall 2: Value of Odd Function at the Origin

If an odd function is defined at $x = 0$, its value must be 0: $f(0) = -f(0) \implies 2f(0) = 0 \implies f(0) = 0$. If a given function has $f(0) \neq 0$ (for instance, $f(0) = 3$), it can never be an odd function!

Pitfall 3: Tangency vs. Sharp Corner on $[f(x)]^2$

Do not sketch squared graphs with sharp V-shaped corners at the $x$-axis like an absolute value graph $|f(x)|$. Because $\frac{d}{dx}[f(x)]^2 = 2f(x)f'(x) = 0$ at roots, the curve must be smoothly parabolic and horizontal at every zero!

Question 5 (Paper 1 Style · 6 marks)

The function $f$ is defined by $f(x) = x\sqrt{4 - x^2}$ for $-2 \le x \le 2$.
(a) Show algebraically that $f$ is an odd function. [2 marks]
(b) Find the stationary points of $f(x)$ and determine their nature. [3 marks]
(c) Hence sketch the graph of $f(x)$ showing all key features. [1 mark]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Odd function proof: [2 marks]
$f(-x) = (-x)\sqrt{4 - (-x)^2} = -x\sqrt{4 - x^2} = -f(x)$ [M1].
Since $f(-x) = -f(x)$ for all $x \in [-2, 2]$, $f$ is an odd function [A1].

(b) Stationary points: [3 marks]
$f'(x) = 1\sqrt{4 - x^2} + x\left(\frac{-2x}{2\sqrt{4 - x^2}}\right) = \frac{4 - x^2 - x^2}{\sqrt{4 - x^2}} = \frac{4 - 2x^2}{\sqrt{4 - x^2}}$ [M1].
$f'(x) = 0 \implies 4 - 2x^2 = 0 \implies x^2 = 2 \implies x = \pm\sqrt{2}$ [A1].
• At $x = \sqrt{2}$: $y = \sqrt{2}\sqrt{4 - 2} = 2$. Local maximum at $(\sqrt{2}, 2)$.
• At $x = -\sqrt{2}$: by odd symmetry, local minimum at $(-\sqrt{2}, -2)$ [A1].

(c) Sketch: [1 mark]
Curve passing through endpoints $(-2, 0)$ and $(2, 0)$, passing through $(0, 0)$ with 180° rotational symmetry [A1].

Question 6 (Paper 2 Style · 8 marks)

Let $f(x) = \frac{1}{x^2 - 4}$.
(a) State the equations of all asymptotes of $y = f(x)$. [2 marks]
(b) Sketch $y = f(x)$ and on the same axes sketch $y = [f(x)]^2$. [3 marks]
(c) Find the coordinates of all invariant points where $f(x) = [f(x)]^2$. [3 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Asymptotes: [2 marks]
Vertical asymptotes: $x^2 - 4 = 0 \implies x = 2$ and $x = -2$ [A1].
Horizontal asymptote: $\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{1}{x^2 - 4} = 0 \implies y = 0$ [A1].

(b) Sketch comparison: [3 marks]
• $f(x)$ has vertical asymptotes at $x = \pm 2$, local max at $(0, -0.25)$, branches above $y = 0$ for $|x| > 2$ [A1].
• $[f(x)]^2 = \frac{1}{(x^2 - 4)^2}$ is strictly positive everywhere, asymptotes at $x = \pm 2, y = 0$, local min at $(0, 0.0625)$ [A1][A1].

(c) Invariant points: [3 marks]
$f(x) = [f(x)]^2 \iff f(x)[1 - f(x)] = 0$ [M1].
Since $f(x) = \frac{1}{x^2 - 4} \neq 0$ for all real $x$, we require $f(x) = 1$ [M1].
$\frac{1}{x^2 - 4} = 1 \implies x^2 - 4 = 1 \implies x^2 = 5 \implies x = \pm\sqrt{5}$ [A1].
Invariant points: $(\sqrt{5}, 1)$ and $(-\sqrt{5}, 1)$.
