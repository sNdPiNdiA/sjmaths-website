3D Vectors & The Scalar Product

Extend vector algebra to three-dimensional Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^3$. Calculate magnitudes, unit vectors, directional cosines, scalar dot products $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = |\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|\cos\theta$, orthogonality tests, and vector projections.

1. Vectors in Three Dimensions ($\mathbb{R}^3$)

A vector $\mathbf{v}$ in 3D can be written in component form with standard unit vectors $\mathbf{i}, \mathbf{j}, \mathbf{k}$ or in column vector form:

$$\mathbf{v} = v_1 \mathbf{i} + v_2 \mathbf{j} + v_3 \mathbf{k} = \begin{pmatrix} v_1 \\ v_2 \\ v_3 \end{pmatrix}$$
  • Magnitude (Length): $|\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{v_1^2 + v_2^2 + v_3^2}$.
  • Unit Vector: A vector with magnitude 1 in the direction of $\mathbf{v}$: $\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{v}|}$.
  • Distance between points $A(x_1, y_1, z_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2, z_2)$: $$AB = |\vec{AB}| = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2 + (z_2 - z_1)^2}$$

2. The Scalar (Dot) Product

The scalar product of two vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ is a scalar defined algebraically and geometrically as:

$$\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = u_1 v_1 + u_2 v_2 + u_3 v_3 = |\mathbf{u}| |\mathbf{v}| \cos\theta$$

where $\theta$ is the angle between the two vectors ($0 \le \theta \le \pi$).

3. Key Applications of the Scalar Product

  • Angle between two vectors: $$\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{u}| |\mathbf{v}|}$$
  • Orthogonality (Perpendicularity) Test: Non-zero vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ are perpendicular if and only if: $$\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0 \iff \theta = \frac{\pi}{2} = 90^\circ$$
  • Vector Projection of $\mathbf{u}$ onto $\mathbf{v}$ (Syllabus 3.11):
    • Scalar projection (signed length): $|\mathbf{u}|\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{v}|}$.
    • Vector projection (the component vector along $\mathbf{v}$): $$\text{proj}_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{u}) = \left(\frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{v}|}\right) \hat{\mathbf{v}} = \left(\frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{v}|^2}\right) \mathbf{v}$$

4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Angle Between Two 3D Vectors

Find the angle between $\mathbf{u} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$ in degrees.

Solution:

• Scalar product: $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = (2)(1) + (-1)(2) + (2)(2) = 2 - 2 + 4 = 4$.
• Magnitudes: $|\mathbf{u}| = \sqrt{2^2 + (-1)^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3$.
$|\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{1^2 + 2^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 4} = 3$.
• Cosine of angle:

$$\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|} = \frac{4}{3 \times 3} = \frac{4}{9}$$ $$\theta = \arccos\left(\frac{4}{9}\right) \approx 63.6^\circ$$

Worked Example 2: Finding Parameter for Orthogonality

The vectors $\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ k \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} k+1 \\ 4 \\ k \end{pmatrix}$ are perpendicular. Find all possible values of $k$.

Solution:

For perpendicular vectors, $\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = 0$:

$$3(k + 1) + k(4) + (-2)(k) = 0$$ $$3k + 3 + 4k - 2k = 0 \implies 5k + 3 = 0 \implies 5k = -3 \implies k = -\frac{3}{5}$$

Worked Example 3: Vector Projection

Find the vector projection of $\mathbf{u} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$ onto $\mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

Solution:

• $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 4(2) + 1(-2) + 3(1) = 8 - 2 + 3 = 9$.
• $|\mathbf{v}|^2 = 2^2 + (-2)^2 + 1^2 = 4 + 4 + 1 = 9$.
• Vector projection:

$$\text{proj}_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{u}) = \left(\frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{v}|^2}\right)\mathbf{v} = \left(\frac{9}{9}\right)\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

Exercise 1 (Foundational: Magnitude and Unit Vector)

Given $\mathbf{a} = 2\mathbf{i} - 3\mathbf{j} + 6\mathbf{k}$, find $|\mathbf{a}|$ and the unit vector in the direction of $\mathbf{a}$.

Show Full Solution

$|\mathbf{a}| = \sqrt{2^2 + (-3)^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{4 + 9 + 36} = \sqrt{49} = 7$. [A1]
Unit vector: $\hat{\mathbf{a}} = \frac{1}{7}(2\mathbf{i} - 3\mathbf{j} + 6\mathbf{k}) = \begin{pmatrix} 2/7 \\ -3/7 \\ 6/7 \end{pmatrix}$. [A1]

Exercise 2 (Foundational: Scalar Product)

Calculate $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}$ where $\mathbf{u} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ -1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

Show Full Solution

$\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 5(-2) + (-1)(3) + 4(1) = -10 - 3 + 4 = -9$. [A1]

Exercise 3 (Foundational: Orthogonality)

Show that $\mathbf{p} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 4 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{q} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ are perpendicular.

Show Full Solution

$\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{q} = 1(6) + 4(-1) + (-2)(1) = 6 - 4 - 2 = 0$. [M1]
Since the scalar product is 0, the vectors are perpendicular. [A1]

Exercise 4 (Intermediate: Angle Between Vectors)

Find the angle between $\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j}$ and $\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k}$ in radians.

Show Full Solution

$\mathbf{a} = (1, 1, 0)$, $\mathbf{b} = (0, 1, 1)$. [M1]
$\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = 1(0) + 1(1) + 0(1) = 1$.
$|\mathbf{a}| = \sqrt{1+1+0} = \sqrt{2}$, $|\mathbf{b}| = \sqrt{0+1+1} = \sqrt{2}$. [M1]
$\cos\theta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1}{2} \implies \theta = \frac{\pi}{3}$. [A1]

Exercise 5 (Intermediate: Vector Projection Length)

Find the scalar projection of $\mathbf{x} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$ onto $\mathbf{y} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

Show Full Solution

$\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y} = 1(2) + 2(2) + 3(1) = 2 + 4 + 3 = 9$. [M1]
$|\mathbf{y}| = \sqrt{4 + 4 + 1} = 3$. [M1]
Scalar projection $= \frac{\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}}{|\mathbf{y}|} = \frac{9}{3} = 3$. [A1]

Exercise 6 (Intermediate: Quadratic Parameter for Angle)

Vectors $\mathbf{u} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ p \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix} p \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ have an angle of $60^\circ$. Find $p$.

Show Full Solution

$\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = p + p + 2 = 2p + 2$. [M1]
$|\mathbf{u}| = \sqrt{1 + p^2 + 4} = \sqrt{p^2 + 5}$, $|\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{p^2 + 1 + 1} = \sqrt{p^2 + 2}$.
$\cos(60^\circ) = \frac{1}{2} = \frac{2p + 2}{\sqrt{(p^2 + 5)(p^2 + 2)}}$. [M1]
Square both sides: $\frac{1}{4} = \frac{4(p+1)^2}{p^4 + 7p^2 + 10} \implies p^4 + 7p^2 + 10 = 16(p^2 + 2p + 1) = 16p^2 + 32p + 16$.
$p^4 - 9p^2 - 32p - 6 = 0$. Using GDC/analytical inspection, $p \approx 3.73$. [A1]

Exercise 7 (Exam Standard: Triangle Geometry via Vectors)

Points in 3D are $A(1, 0, 2)$, $B(2, 3, -1)$, and $C(4, 1, 1)$. Find $\angle BAC$.

Show Full Solution

$\vec{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}$, $\vec{AC} = \mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$. [M1]
$\vec{AB}\cdot\vec{AC} = 1(3) + 3(1) + (-3)(-1) = 3 + 3 + 3 = 9$. [M1]
$|\vec{AB}| = \sqrt{1 + 9 + 9} = \sqrt{19}$, $|\vec{AC}| = \sqrt{9 + 1 + 1} = \sqrt{11}$. [M1]
$\cos(\angle BAC) = \frac{9}{\sqrt{19}\sqrt{11}} = \frac{9}{\sqrt{209}} \approx 0.6225$.
$\angle BAC = \arccos(0.6225) \approx 51.5^\circ$ (or $0.899$ rad). [A1]

Exercise 8 (Advanced: Decomposing a Vector into Parallel & Perpendicular Components)

Given $\mathbf{u} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$, decompose $\mathbf{u}$ into $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{u}_\parallel + \mathbf{u}_\perp$ where $\mathbf{u}_\parallel$ is parallel to $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{u}_\perp$ is perpendicular to $\mathbf{v}$.

Show Full Solution

$\mathbf{u}_\parallel = \text{proj}_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{u}) = \left(\frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{v}|^2}\right)\mathbf{v}$. [M1]
$\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 3(1) - 1(2) + 2(-2) = 3 - 2 - 4 = -3$.
$|\mathbf{v}|^2 = 1^2 + 2^2 + (-2)^2 = 9$.
$\mathbf{u}_\parallel = -\frac{3}{9}\mathbf{v} = -\frac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1/3 \\ -2/3 \\ 2/3 \end{pmatrix}$. [A1]
$\mathbf{u}_\perp = \mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u}_\parallel = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} -1/3 \\ -2/3 \\ 2/3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 10/3 \\ -1/3 \\ 4/3 \end{pmatrix}$. [A1]
Verify $\mathbf{u}_\perp \cdot \mathbf{v} = \frac{10}{3}(1) - \frac{1}{3}(2) + \frac{4}{3}(-2) = \frac{10 - 2 - 8}{3} = 0$. [A1]

Exercise 9 (Advanced: Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality for Vectors)

Prove the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality $|\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}| \le |\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|$ for any vectors $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and state when equality holds.

Show Full Solution

By definition, $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = |\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|\cos\theta$. [M1]
Take the absolute value: $|\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}| = |\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}||\cos\theta|$. [M1]
Since $-1 \le \cos\theta \le 1$ for all real $\theta$, $|\cos\theta| \le 1$.
Therefore $|\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}| = |\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}||\cos\theta| \le |\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|(1) = |\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|$. [A1]
Equality holds if and only if $|\cos\theta| = 1 \iff \theta = 0$ or $\pi$, meaning $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ are collinear (scalar multiples). [A1]

Exercise 10 (Advanced: Work Done and Vector Force)

A constant force $\mathbf{F} = 4\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j} + 5\mathbf{k}$ N moves a particle from point $A(1, 2, -1)$ to $B(3, 0, 4)$ (coordinates in metres). Calculate the work done by the force in Joules.

Show Full Solution

Displacement vector $\mathbf{d} = \vec{AB} = \begin{pmatrix} 3-1 \\ 0-2 \\ 4-(-1) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -2 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}$ m. [M1]
Work done $W = \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{d}$. [M1]
$W = 4(2) + (-2)(-2) + 5(5) = 8 + 4 + 25 = 37$ J. [A1]

Examination Pitfalls & Examiner Tips

Scalar vs Vector Output

The scalar (dot) product outputs a SINGLE NUMBER (scalar). Writing a vector as the result of a dot product is an immediate error.

Degrees vs Radians in Vector Angles

Read the question carefully! Unless degrees are specified, IB exam standard defaults to 3 significant figures in radians.

Question 5 (Paper 1 Style · 6 marks)

The vertices of a triangle in 3D are $A(2, 1, 3)$, $B(4, 3, 2)$, and $C(1, 5, 6)$.
(a) Find the position vectors $\vec{BA}$ and $\vec{BC}$. [2 marks]
(b) Calculate $\vec{BA}\cdot\vec{BC}$. [2 marks]
(c) Hence deduce whether triangle $ABC$ is acute, right-angled, or obtuse at vertex $B$. [2 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Position vectors: [2 marks]
$\vec{BA} = \mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 2-4 \\ 1-3 \\ 3-2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ [A1].
$\vec{BC} = \mathbf{c} - \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 1-4 \\ 5-3 \\ 6-2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 2 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$ [A1].

(b) Dot product: [2 marks]
$\vec{BA}\cdot\vec{BC} = (-2)(-3) + (-2)(2) + 1(4) = 6 - 4 + 4 = 6$ [A1].

(c) Nature of angle $B$: [2 marks]
Since $\vec{BA}\cdot\vec{BC} = 6 > 0$, $\cos B > 0$ [M1].
Therefore $\angle B$ is an acute angle [A1].

Question 6 (Paper 2 Style · 7 marks)

Consider vectors $\mathbf{a} = 2\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j} - 2\mathbf{k}$ and $\mathbf{b} = 3\mathbf{i} - 4\mathbf{k}$.
(a) Find the vector projection of $\mathbf{a}$ onto $\mathbf{b}$. [3 marks]
(b) Find the component of $\mathbf{a}$ perpendicular to $\mathbf{b}$. [2 marks]
(c) Verify that your perpendicular component is indeed orthogonal to $\mathbf{b}$. [2 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Vector projection: [3 marks]
$\mathbf{a} = (2, 1, -2)$, $\mathbf{b} = (3, 0, -4)$.
$\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = 2(3) + 1(0) + (-2)(-4) = 6 + 0 + 8 = 14$ [M1].
$|\mathbf{b}|^2 = 3^2 + 0^2 + (-4)^2 = 9 + 16 = 25$ [A1].
$\text{proj}_{\mathbf{b}}(\mathbf{a}) = \frac{14}{25}\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 42/25 \\ 0 \\ -56/25 \end{pmatrix}$ [A1].

(b) Perpendicular component: [2 marks]
$\mathbf{a}_\perp = \mathbf{a} - \text{proj}_{\mathbf{b}}(\mathbf{a}) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 42/25 \\ 0 \\ -56/25 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 8/25 \\ 1 \\ 6/25 \end{pmatrix}$ [M1][A1].

(c) Verification: [2 marks]
$\mathbf{a}_\perp \cdot \mathbf{b} = \frac{8}{25}(3) + 1(0) + \frac{6}{25}(-4) = \frac{24}{25} - \frac{24}{25} = 0$ [M1].
Since dot product is 0, orthogonality is verified [A1].
