Compound Angle Identities & Double Angles

Derive and apply addition and subtraction formulas for sine, cosine, and tangent. Master double angle identities, power-reduction substitutions, and the harmonic synthesis $a\sin x + b\cos x = R\sin(x \pm \alpha) = R\cos(x \mp \beta)$.

Syllabus AHL 3.9 Paper 1 & Paper 2 Skill 4 Interactive Tabs 10 Tiered Problems + Mini Test

Figure 3.2: Harmonic Combination: $a\sin x + b\cos x = R\sin(x + \alpha)$

1. Compound Angle Addition and Subtraction Formulas

The addition and subtraction formulas express trigonometric ratios of compound arguments $(A \pm B)$ in terms of individual ratios of $A$ and $B$:

$$\sin(A \pm B) = \sin A\cos B \pm \cos A\sin B$$ $$\cos(A \pm B) = \cos A\cos B \mp \sin A\sin B$$ $$\tan(A \pm B) = \frac{\tan A \pm \tan B}{1 \mp \tan A\tan B}$$

Critical Sign Reversal: Note carefully that the cosine expansion reverses signs: $\cos(A + B)$ has a minus between the terms, whereas $\cos(A - B)$ has a plus.

2. Double Angle Formulas

Setting $A = B = \theta$ produces the standard double angle formulas:

$$\sin(2\theta) = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta$$ $$\cos(2\theta) = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta = 2\cos^2\theta - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta$$ $$\tan(2\theta) = \frac{2\tan\theta}{1 - \tan^2\theta}$$

Rearranging the three forms of $\cos(2\theta)$ yields the power-reduction identities, indispensable for Paper 1 calculus integration:

$$\cos^2\theta = \frac{1 + \cos(2\theta)}{2}, \quad \sin^2\theta = \frac{1 - \cos(2\theta)}{2}$$

3. Harmonic Linear Combination: $a\sin x + b\cos x$

Any linear sum of sine and cosine waves sharing identical period can be condensed into a single phase-shifted sinusoid. By expanding $R\sin(x + \alpha) = R\cos\alpha\sin x + R\sin\alpha\cos x$ and equating coefficients:

$$a\sin x + b\cos x = R\sin(x + \alpha) \quad \text{where} \quad R = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}, \quad \tan\alpha = \frac{b}{a}$$ $$a\cos x + b\sin x = R\cos(x - \beta) \quad \text{where} \quad R = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}, \quad \tan\beta = \frac{b}{a}$$

Analytical Power of Harmonic Form

• Extrema & Range: Since $-1 \le \sin(x + \alpha) \le 1$, the expression $a\sin x + b\cos x$ has an absolute maximum of $+R$ and absolute minimum of $-R$. The range is $[-\sqrt{a^2+b^2}, \sqrt{a^2+b^2}]$.

• Solving Linear Trig Equations: Equations of the form $a\sin x + b\cos x = c$ simplify directly to $\sin(x + \alpha) = \frac{c}{R}$. Real solutions exist if and only if $|c| \le R = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$.

The 6 Classic IB Exam Question Archetypes

Every compound angle and double angle question on IB DP Mathematics AA HL Paper 1 and Paper 2 conforms to one of these six primary archetypes:

Archetype Typical Exam Setup Primary Strategy / Steps Common Traps & Mark Losses
1. Exact Values of Non-Standard Angles Find exact surd values of $\cos(75^\circ), \sin(15^\circ), \tan(\pi/12)$ without a calculator. 1. Decompose angle into standard angles: $75^\circ = 45^\circ + 30^\circ$, $\pi/12 = \pi/4 - \pi/6$.
2. Apply addition/subtraction formula.
3. Rationalize denominators if needed.		 Sign slip in cosine addition formula ($\cos(A+B) = \cos A\cos B - \sin A\sin B$). Arithmetic errors in fraction addition.
2. Double-Angle Factoring Equations Solve equations like $\sin(2x) = \cos x$ or $\cos(2x) + 3\sin x = 2$ on $x \in [0, 2\pi]$. 1. Substitute $\sin(2x) = 2\sin x\cos x$ or $\cos(2x) = 1 - 2\sin^2 x$.
2. Factor out common terms or solve the resulting quadratic.
3. Collect solutions in all valid quadrants.		 Dividing through by $\cos x$ and losing the roots where $\cos x = 0$! Always factor instead of dividing by variables.
3. Harmonic Linear Synthesis $R\cos(x \pm \alpha)$ Express $\sqrt{3}\sin x + \cos x$ in the form $R\sin(x + \alpha)$, find range, and solve for roots. 1. Calculate $R = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$.
2. Determine $\alpha = \arctan(|b/a|)$ and verify quadrant from signs.
3. Solve $\sin(x + \alpha) = c/R$ by adjusting domain interval.		 Forgetting to shift the domain bounds when solving for the compound argument $(x + \alpha)$, leading to missing solutions.
4. Multiple-Angle Identity Proofs Prove triple angle identities such as $\cos(3\theta) = 4\cos^3\theta - 3\cos\theta$ or $\tan(3\theta)$. 1. Split $3\theta = 2\theta + \theta$.
2. Expand using compound angle formulas.
3. Substitute double-angle formulas and express everything in the target ratio.		 Manipulating both LHS and RHS simultaneously. In an IB "Show that" question, start from one side and logically deduce the other.
5. Power Reduction in Calculus Evaluate $\int \cos^2 x\,dx$, $\int \sin^4 x\,dx$, or find volume of revolution involving $\sin^2 x$. 1. Substitute $\cos^2 x = \frac{1 + \cos 2x}{2}$ or $\sin^2 x = \frac{1 - \cos 2x}{2}$.
2. Integrate linear and cosine terms directly.
3. Apply fundamental theorem of calculus.		 Attempting integration by parts repeatedly instead of power reduction. Forgetting the chain rule coefficient $1/2$ when integrating $\cos(2x)$.
6. Geometry Applications with $\tan(A - B)$ Find angle subtended by a flagpole or billboard on a wall using right-triangle tangent differences. 1. Set $\theta = \alpha - \beta$ where $\alpha, \beta$ are elevations to top and bottom.
2. Express $\tan\alpha = h_1/x$ and $\tan\beta = h_2/x$.
3. Apply $\tan\theta = \frac{\tan\alpha - \tan\beta}{1 + \tan\alpha\tan\beta}$.		 Applying subtraction formula to angles directly instead of taking tangents first. Algebraic errors in clearing complex fractions.

Authentic IB DP Exam Worked Exemplars

Worked Exemplar 1: Exact Value via Addition Formula (Paper 1 Style · 5 marks)

Find the exact value of $\tan\left(\frac{5\pi}{12}\right)$ in the form $a + \sqrt{b}$ where $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.

Official Markscheme Solution:

Decompose the fraction: $\frac{5\pi}{12} = \frac{2\pi}{12} + \frac{3\pi}{12} = \frac{\pi}{6} + \frac{\pi}{4}$ [M1].
Apply the tangent addition formula: [M1]
$\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{6} + \frac{\pi}{4}\right) = \frac{\tan(\pi/6) + \tan(\pi/4)}{1 - \tan(\pi/6)\tan(\pi/4)}$.

Substitute exact standard values $\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ and $\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = 1$: [A1]
$= \frac{\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} + 1}{1 - \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)(1)} = \frac{1 + \sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3} - 1}$.

Rationalize the denominator by multiplying by $(\sqrt{3} + 1)$: [M1]
$= \frac{(\sqrt{3} + 1)^2}{(\sqrt{3} - 1)(\sqrt{3} + 1)} = \frac{3 + 2\sqrt{3} + 1}{3 - 1} = \frac{4 + 2\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2 + \sqrt{3}$ [A1].
Here $a = 2, b = 3 \implies 2 + \sqrt{3}$ [AG].

Worked Exemplar 2: Solving Equations by Factoring Double Angles (Paper 1 Style · 6 marks)

Solve the equation $\sin(2x) = \sqrt{2}\cos x$ for $0 \le x \le 2\pi$.

Official Markscheme Solution:

Substitute double angle formula $\sin(2x) = 2\sin x\cos x$: [M1]
$2\sin x\cos x = \sqrt{2}\cos x \implies 2\sin x\cos x - \sqrt{2}\cos x = 0$ [A1].
Factor out $\cos x$: [M1]
$\cos x (2\sin x - \sqrt{2}) = 0$.

• Branch 1: $\cos x = 0$
In $[0, 2\pi]$: $x = \frac{\pi}{2}$ and $x = \frac{3\pi}{2}$ [A1].

• Branch 2: $2\sin x - \sqrt{2} = 0 \implies \sin x = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
In $[0, 2\pi]$, sine is positive in Quadrants 1 and 2: [M1]
$x = \frac{\pi}{4}$ and $x = \pi - \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{3\pi}{4}$ [A1].
All solutions: $x \in \left\{\frac{\pi}{4}, \frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3\pi}{4}, \frac{3\pi}{2}\right\}$.
Examiner Warning: Dividing across by $\cos x$ immediately loses the two solutions $\frac{\pi}{2}$ and $\frac{3\pi}{2}$, costing 3 out of 6 marks!

Worked Exemplar 3: Harmonic Linear Combination Equation (Paper 2 Style · 7 marks)

Consider the function $f(x) = 3\sin(2x) - 4\cos(2x)$ for $0 \le x \le \pi$.
(a) Express $f(x)$ in the form $R\sin(2x - \alpha)$ where $R > 0$ and $0 < \alpha < \frac{\pi}{2}$. [3 marks]
(b) Hence find the coordinates of the maximum point on the graph of $f(x)$ on $[0, \pi]$. [2 marks]
(c) Solve the equation $f(x) = 2.5$ for $0 \le x \le \pi$. [2 marks]

Official Markscheme Solution:

(a) Harmonic Form: [3 marks]
Expand $R\sin(2x - \alpha) = R\cos\alpha\sin(2x) - R\sin\alpha\cos(2x)$ [M1].
Equating coefficients: $R\cos\alpha = 3$ and $R\sin\alpha = 4$.
$R = \sqrt{3^2 + (-4)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$ [A1].
$\tan\alpha = \frac{4}{3} \implies \alpha = \arctan(4/3) \approx 0.9273$ rad [A1].
$f(x) = 5\sin(2x - 0.927)$ rad.

(b) Maximum Point: [2 marks]
Maximum occurs when $\sin(2x - 0.9273) = 1$:
$2x - 0.9273 = \frac{\pi}{2} \approx 1.5708 \implies 2x = 2.4981 \implies x \approx 1.25$ rad [A1].
Maximum height is $R = 5$. Max point: $(1.25, 5)$ [A1].

(c) Equation Solving: [2 marks]
$5\sin(2x - 0.9273) = 2.5 \implies \sin(2x - 0.9273) = 0.5$ [M1].
For $x \in [0, \pi]$, the argument $u = 2x - 0.9273 \in [-0.9273, 2\pi - 0.9273] \approx [-0.927, 5.356]$:
$u_1 = \frac{\pi}{6} \approx 0.5236 \implies 2x = 0.5236 + 0.9273 = 1.4509 \implies x_1 \approx 0.725$ rad [A1].
$u_2 = \frac{5\pi}{6} \approx 2.6180 \implies 2x = 2.6180 + 0.9273 = 3.5453 \implies x_2 \approx 1.77$ rad [A1].
Solutions: $x \approx 0.725, 1.77$ rad.

Worked Exemplar 4: Triple Angle Proof (Paper 1 Style · 6 marks)

Prove that $\cos(3\theta) = 4\cos^3\theta - 3\cos\theta$ for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$.

Official Markscheme Solution:

Start with LHS: write $3\theta = 2\theta + \theta$ [M1].
$\cos(3\theta) = \cos(2\theta + \theta) = \cos(2\theta)\cos\theta - \sin(2\theta)\sin\theta$ [M1].

Substitute the double angle identities: [M1]
$\cos(2\theta) = 2\cos^2\theta - 1$
$\sin(2\theta) = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta$
$= (2\cos^2\theta - 1)\cos\theta - (2\sin\theta\cos\theta)\sin\theta$ [A1]
$= 2\cos^3\theta - \cos\theta - 2\sin^2\theta\cos\theta$.

Substitute $\sin^2\theta = 1 - \cos^2\theta$ to express in powers of $\cos\theta$ only: [M1]
$= 2\cos^3\theta - \cos\theta - 2(1 - \cos^2\theta)\cos\theta$
$= 2\cos^3\theta - \cos\theta - 2\cos\theta + 2\cos^3\theta$ [A1]
$= 4\cos^3\theta - 3\cos\theta = \text{RHS}$ [AG].

Worked Exemplar 5: Definite Integration Using Power Reduction (Paper 1 Style · 7 marks)

Evaluate the exact value of $\int_0^{\pi/4} \cos^4 x\,dx$.

Official Markscheme Solution:

Write $\cos^4 x = (\cos^2 x)^2$ and apply power reduction: [M1]
$\cos^2 x = \frac{1 + \cos(2x)}{2} \implies \cos^4 x = \left(\frac{1 + \cos(2x)}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1 + 2\cos(2x) + \cos^2(2x)}{4}$ [M1].

Apply power reduction to $\cos^2(2x)$: [M1]
$\cos^2(2x) = \frac{1 + \cos(4x)}{2}$ [A1].
Substitute back into the expression:
$\cos^4 x = \frac{1}{4} \left[ 1 + 2\cos(2x) + \frac{1 + \cos(4x)}{2} \right] = \frac{1}{4} \left[ \frac{3}{2} + 2\cos(2x) + \frac{1}{2}\cos(4x) \right]$
$= \frac{3}{8} + \frac{1}{2}\cos(2x) + \frac{1}{8}\cos(4x)$ [A1].

Integrate term-by-term: [M1]
$\int_0^{\pi/4} \left( \frac{3}{8} + \frac{1}{2}\cos(2x) + \frac{1}{8}\cos(4x) \right)\,dx = \left[ \frac{3}{8}x + \frac{1}{4}\sin(2x) + \frac{1}{32}\sin(4x) \right]_0^{\pi/4}$ [M1].
Evaluate at upper limit $x = \frac{\pi}{4}$:
$= \frac{3}{8}\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) + \frac{1}{4}\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) + \frac{1}{32}\sin(\pi) = \frac{3\pi}{32} + \frac{1}{4}(1) + 0 = \frac{3\pi}{32} + \frac{1}{4}$ [A1].
At lower limit $x = 0$, all terms vanish.
Exact value: $\frac{3\pi + 8}{32}$ [A1].

Practice Exercises (10 Tiered Questions)

Work through these 10 examination-calibrated exercises ranging from core foundational skills to advanced Paper 1 and Paper 2 problems. Expand each exercise to view the full IB markscheme and examiner advice.

Exercise 1 (Foundational: Double Angle Sine and Cosine)

Given that $\sin\theta = \frac{3}{5}$ and $\theta$ is an acute angle, find the exact value of $\sin(2\theta)$ and $\cos(2\theta)$.

Show Full Markscheme

Since $\theta$ is acute, $\cos\theta = \sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^2} = \frac{4}{5}$ [M1].
• $\sin(2\theta) = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta = 2\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)\left(\frac{4}{5}\right) = \frac{24}{25}$ [A1].
• $\cos(2\theta) = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta = \left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^2 = \frac{16 - 9}{25} = \frac{7}{25}$ [A1].

Exercise 2 (Foundational: Exact Value of Tan 15°)

Use the compound angle formula for $\tan(A - B)$ to find the exact value of $\tan(15^\circ)$ in simplest surd form.

Show Full Markscheme

$\tan(15^\circ) = \tan(45^\circ - 30^\circ) = \frac{\tan 45^\circ - \tan 30^\circ}{1 + \tan 45^\circ\tan 30^\circ}$ [M1].
Substitute $\tan 45^\circ = 1$ and $\tan 30^\circ = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$:
$= \frac{1 - 1/\sqrt{3}}{1 + 1/\sqrt{3}} = \frac{\sqrt{3} - 1}{\sqrt{3} + 1}$ [A1].
Rationalize: $\frac{(\sqrt{3} - 1)^2}{3 - 1} = \frac{4 - 2\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2 - \sqrt{3}$ [A1].

Exercise 3 (Foundational: Power Reduction in Cosine)

Express $\cos^2(3x)$ in terms of the first power of a cosine function.

Show Full Markscheme

Apply identity $\cos^2\theta = \frac{1 + \cos(2\theta)}{2}$ with $\theta = 3x$ [M1]:
$\cos^2(3x) = \frac{1 + \cos(6x)}{2} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\cos(6x)$ [A1].

Exercise 4 (Intermediate: Solving Cosine Double Angle Quadratic)

Solve $\cos(2x) + \cos x = 0$ for $0 \le x \le 2\pi$.

Show Full Markscheme

Substitute $\cos(2x) = 2\cos^2 x - 1$: [M1]
$2\cos^2 x + \cos x - 1 = 0$ [A1].
Factor the quadratic: $(2\cos x - 1)(\cos x + 1) = 0$ [M1].
• $\cos x = \frac{1}{2} \implies x = \frac{\pi}{3}, \frac{5\pi}{3}$ [A1].
• $\cos x = -1 \implies x = \pi$ [A1].
Solutions: $x \in \left\{\frac{\pi}{3}, \pi, \frac{5\pi}{3}\right\}$.

Exercise 5 (Intermediate: Compound Angle Geometry)

Let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be acute angles such that $\tan\alpha = \frac{1}{2}$ and $\tan\beta = \frac{1}{3}$. Show that $\alpha + \beta = \frac{\pi}{4}$.

Show Full Markscheme

Apply tangent addition formula: [M1]
$\tan(\alpha + \beta) = \frac{\tan\alpha + \tan\beta}{1 - \tan\alpha\tan\beta} = \frac{1/2 + 1/3}{1 - (1/2)(1/3)}$ [M1]
$= \frac{5/6}{1 - 1/6} = \frac{5/6}{5/6} = 1$ [A1].
Since $\alpha, \beta$ are acute ($0 < \alpha, \beta < \frac{\pi}{2}$) and $\tan\alpha, \tan\beta < 1$, we have $0 < \alpha, \beta < \frac{\pi}{4}$, so $0 < \alpha + \beta < \frac{\pi}{2}$ [R1].
Therefore, $\alpha + \beta = \arctan(1) = \frac{\pi}{4}$ [AG].

Exercise 6 (Intermediate: Harmonic Conversion)

Express $\sin x - \sqrt{3}\cos x$ in the form $R\sin(x - \alpha)$ where $R > 0$ and $0 < \alpha < \frac{\pi}{2}$.

Show Full Markscheme

Expand $R\sin(x - \alpha) = R\cos\alpha\sin x - R\sin\alpha\cos x$ [M1].
Equating coefficients: $R\cos\alpha = 1$ and $R\sin\alpha = \sqrt{3}$.
$R = \sqrt{1^2 + (-\sqrt{3})^2} = \sqrt{1 + 3} = 2$ [A1].
$\tan\alpha = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{1} = \sqrt{3} \implies \alpha = \frac{\pi}{3}$ [A1].
Result: $2\sin\left(x - \frac{\pi}{3}\right)$.

Exercise 7 (Exam Standard: Double Angle Tangent Equation)

Solve $\tan(2x) = 4\tan x$ for $0 < x < \pi$.

Show Full Markscheme

Substitute $\tan(2x) = \frac{2\tan x}{1 - \tan^2 x}$: [M1]
$\frac{2\tan x}{1 - \tan^2 x} = 4\tan x \implies 2\tan x = 4\tan x(1 - \tan^2 x)$
$2\tan x [ 1 - 2(1 - \tan^2 x) ] = 0 \implies 2\tan x (2\tan^2 x - 1) = 0$ [M1].
• $\tan x = 0$: In $(0, \pi)$, no solutions (endpoints excluded).
• $2\tan^2 x - 1 = 0 \implies \tan^2 x = \frac{1}{2} \implies \tan x = \pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ [A1].
$\tan x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \implies x = \arctan(1/\sqrt{2}) \approx 0.615$ rad [A1].
$\tan x = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \implies x = \pi - 0.615 \approx 2.53$ rad [A1].
Solutions: $x \approx 0.615, 2.53$ rad.

Exercise 8 (Advanced: Product-to-Sum and Triple Angles)

Show that $\sin(3\theta) = 3\sin\theta - 4\sin^3\theta$.

Show Full Markscheme

$\sin(3\theta) = \sin(2\theta + \theta) = \sin(2\theta)\cos\theta + \cos(2\theta)\sin\theta$ [M1].
Substitute $\sin(2\theta) = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta$ and $\cos(2\theta) = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta$: [M1]
$= (2\sin\theta\cos\theta)\cos\theta + (1 - 2\sin^2\theta)\sin\theta$
$= 2\sin\theta\cos^2\theta + \sin\theta - 2\sin^3\theta$ [A1].
Replace $\cos^2\theta = 1 - \sin^2\theta$: [M1]
$= 2\sin\theta(1 - \sin^2\theta) + \sin\theta - 2\sin^3\theta = 2\sin\theta - 2\sin^3\theta + \sin\theta - 2\sin^3\theta$
$= 3\sin\theta - 4\sin^3\theta$ [A1][AG].

Exercise 9 (Advanced: Maximum of Linear Combination with Shift)

Find the maximum value of $g(x) = \frac{12}{5\sin x + 12\cos x + 15}$ and state the value of $x \in [0, 2\pi]$ at which it occurs.

Show Full Markscheme

Convert denominator into harmonic form: $5\sin x + 12\cos x = R\sin(x + \alpha)$ [M1].
$R = \sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{25 + 144} = 13$ [A1].
$\tan\alpha = \frac{12}{5} \implies \alpha = \arctan(2.4) \approx 1.176$ rad.
Denominator is $13\sin(x + 1.176) + 15$.
The fraction $g(x)$ is maximized when its denominator is minimized [R1].
Minimum of denominator occurs when $\sin(x + 1.176) = -1$ [M1]:
$\text{Denominator}_{\min} = 13(-1) + 15 = 2$.
Maximum value of $g(x) = \frac{12}{2} = 6$ [A1].
Occurs at $x + 1.176 = \frac{3\pi}{2} \approx 4.7124 \implies x \approx 4.7124 - 1.176 = 3.54$ rad [A1].

Exercise 10 (Advanced: Tangent Half-Angle Substitution)

Let $t = \tan\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)$. Prove that $\sin\theta = \frac{2t}{1 + t^2}$ and $\cos\theta = \frac{1 - t^2}{1 + t^2}$.

Show Full Markscheme

Consider $\sin\theta = 2\sin(\theta/2)\cos(\theta/2)$ [M1].
Divide and multiply by $\cos^2(\theta/2) + \sin^2(\theta/2) = 1$:
$\sin\theta = \frac{2\sin(\theta/2)\cos(\theta/2)}{\cos^2(\theta/2) + \sin^2(\theta/2)}$ [M1].
Divide numerator and denominator by $\cos^2(\theta/2)$: [M1]
$= \frac{2\tan(\theta/2)}{1 + \tan^2(\theta/2)} = \frac{2t}{1 + t^2}$ [A1][AG].
Similarly, $\cos\theta = \cos^2(\theta/2) - \sin^2(\theta/2) = \frac{\cos^2(\theta/2) - \sin^2(\theta/2)}{\cos^2(\theta/2) + \sin^2(\theta/2)}$ [M1].
Divide by $\cos^2(\theta/2)$:
$= \frac{1 - \tan^2(\theta/2)}{1 + \tan^2(\theta/2)} = \frac{1 - t^2}{1 + t^2}$ [A1][AG].

Syllabus 3.9: Compound & Double Angle Formulas

The official IB DP Mathematics AA Formula Booklet provides:

  • $\sin(A \pm B) = \sin A\cos B \pm \cos A\sin B$
  • $\cos(A \pm B) = \cos A\cos B \mp \sin A\sin B$
  • $\tan(A \pm B) = \frac{\tan A \pm \tan B}{1 \mp \tan A\tan B}$
  • $\sin(2\theta) = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta$
  • $\cos(2\theta) = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta = 2\cos^2\theta - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta$
  • $\tan(2\theta) = \frac{2\tan\theta}{1 - \tan^2\theta}$

Must-Memorize Principles (Not in Formula Booklet)

1. Power-Reduction Identities (Crucial for Integration)

  • $\cos^2\theta = \frac{1 + \cos(2\theta)}{2}$
  • $\sin^2\theta = \frac{1 - \cos(2\theta)}{2}$
  • $\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta = \frac{1}{4}\sin^2(2\theta) = \frac{1 - \cos(4\theta)}{8}$

2. Harmonic Synthesis Form

  • $a\sin x + b\cos x = R\sin(x + \alpha)$ where $R = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}, \quad \cos\alpha = \frac{a}{R}, \quad \sin\alpha = \frac{b}{R}$
  • $a\cos x + b\sin x = R\cos(x - \beta)$ where $R = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}, \quad \cos\beta = \frac{a}{R}, \quad \sin\beta = \frac{b}{R}$
  • Range: $[-\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}, \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}]$; Maximum $= +R$, Minimum $= -R$.

3. Triple Angle and Half-Angle $t$-Formulas

  • $\cos(3\theta) = 4\cos^3\theta - 3\cos\theta$
  • $\sin(3\theta) = 3\sin\theta - 4\sin^3\theta$
  • $t = \tan(\theta/2) \implies \sin\theta = \frac{2t}{1 + t^2}, \quad \cos\theta = \frac{1 - t^2}{1 + t^2}, \quad \tan\theta = \frac{2t}{1 - t^2}$

High-Frequency Examination Pitfalls

Pitfall 1: Sign Reversal in Cosine Addition

Remember that $\cos(A + B) = \cos A\cos B - \sin A\sin B$. The plus sign in the argument becomes a MINUS in the expansion!

Pitfall 2: Dividing by Variables and Losing Solutions

Never divide an equation like $\sin(2x) = \cos x$ by $\cos x$! This discards all solutions where $\cos x = 0$ (such as $x = \frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3\pi}{2}$). Always factor: $\cos x (2\sin x - 1) = 0$.

Pitfall 3: Not Shifting the Domain for Compound Arguments

When solving $\sin(2x - \alpha) = c$ for $x \in [0, 2\pi]$, remember that the argument $u = 2x - \alpha$ spans $[-\alpha, 4\pi - \alpha]$. If you search only within $[0, 2\pi]$, you will miss valid solutions!

Question 5 (Paper 1 Style · 6 marks)

Let $f(x) = \cos(2x) - 3\cos x + 2$ for $0 \le x \le 2\pi$.
(a) Show that $f(x) = 2\cos^2 x - 3\cos x + 1$. [2 marks]
(b) Hence solve $f(x) = 0$ for $x \in [0, 2\pi]$. [4 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Quadratic Form: [2 marks]
Substitute $\cos(2x) = 2\cos^2 x - 1$: [M1]
$f(x) = (2\cos^2 x - 1) - 3\cos x + 2 = 2\cos^2 x - 3\cos x + 1$ [A1][AG].

(b) Solving Equation: [4 marks]
Factorize the quadratic in $\cos x$: [M1]
$(2\cos x - 1)(\cos x - 1) = 0$.
• $\cos x = \frac{1}{2}$: In $[0, 2\pi]$, reference angle is $\frac{\pi}{3}$.
Quadrants 1 and 4: $x = \frac{\pi}{3}$ and $x = 2\pi - \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{5\pi}{3}$ [A1][A1].
• $\cos x = 1$: In $[0, 2\pi]$, $x = 0$ and $x = 2\pi$ [A1].
Solutions: $x \in \left\{0, \frac{\pi}{3}, \frac{5\pi}{3}, 2\pi\right\}$ [A1].

Question 6 (Paper 2 Style · 8 marks)

The depth of water, $h$ meters, at an estuary entrance is modeled by $h(t) = 4\sin(0.5t) + 3\cos(0.5t) + 8$ where $t$ is the time in hours after midnight.
(a) Express $4\sin(0.5t) + 3\cos(0.5t)$ in the form $R\sin(0.5t + \alpha)$ where $R > 0$ and $0 < \alpha < \frac{\pi}{2}$. [3 marks]
(b) Find the maximum water depth and the earliest time after midnight at which it occurs. [3 marks]
(c) A ferry requires a minimum depth of 10.5 meters to enter. Determine the length of time in the first 12-hour period that the ferry can enter. [2 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Harmonic Form: [3 marks]
$R = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5$ [A1].
$R\sin(0.5t + \alpha) = R\cos\alpha\sin(0.5t) + R\sin\alpha\cos(0.5t)$
$R\cos\alpha = 4, R\sin\alpha = 3 \implies \tan\alpha = \frac{3}{4} \implies \alpha = \arctan(0.75) \approx 0.6435$ rad [A1].
$h(t) = 5\sin(0.5t + 0.6435) + 8$ [A1].

(b) Maximum Depth and Time: [3 marks]
Maximum depth: $h_{\max} = 5(1) + 8 = 13$ meters [A1].
Occurs when $\sin(0.5t + 0.6435) = 1 \implies 0.5t + 0.6435 = \frac{\pi}{2} \approx 1.5708$ [M1].
$0.5t = 1.5708 - 0.6435 = 0.9273 \implies t \approx 1.85$ hours after midnight (approx 01:51 AM) [A1].

(c) Duration Ferry Can Enter: [2 marks]
Set $h(t) \ge 10.5 \implies 5\sin(0.5t + 0.6435) + 8 \ge 10.5$
$\sin(0.5t + 0.6435) \ge \frac{2.5}{5} = 0.5$ [M1].
The sine function is $\ge 0.5$ over the interval $\left[\frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{5\pi}{6}\right]$, which has angular width $\frac{5\pi}{6} - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{2\pi}{3}$ rad.
Set $0.5 \Delta t = \frac{2\pi}{3} \implies \Delta t = \frac{4\pi}{3} \approx 4.19$ hours [A1].
The ferry can enter for approximately 4.19 hours (4 hours 11 minutes).
