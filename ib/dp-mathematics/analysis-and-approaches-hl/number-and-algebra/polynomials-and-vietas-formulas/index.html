Polynomials & Vieta’s Formulas

Connect polynomial coefficients and roots. Master the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, the Complex Conjugate Root Theorem, polynomial division, Vieta’s relations for cubic and quartic polynomials, symmetric power sums, and equation transformations.

1. Fundamental Theorem of Algebra & Conjugate Root Theorem

A general polynomial $P(z)$ of degree $n \ge 1$ with complex coefficients is defined as:

$$P(z) = a_n z^n + a_{n-1} z^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 z + a_0, \quad a_n \neq 0$$
  • Fundamental Theorem of Algebra (FTA): Every non-constant polynomial of degree $n$ has at least one root in $\mathbb{C}$. Consequently, $P(z)$ factors completely into exactly $n$ linear complex factors: $$P(z) = a_n (z - \alpha_1)(z - \alpha_2)\dots(z - \alpha_n)$$ where roots $\alpha_i$ are counted according to their algebraic multiplicities.
  • Complex Conjugate Root Theorem: If all coefficients $a_0, a_1, \dots, a_n \in \mathbb{R}$, and $z = u + iv$ ($v \neq 0$) is a root of $P(z) = 0$, then its complex conjugate $z^* = u - iv$ is also a root.
    Proof Sketch: Taking the conjugate of the equation: $$\overline{P(z)} = \overline{\sum_{k=0}^n a_k z^k} = \sum_{k=0}^n \overline{a_k} (\bar{z})^k = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k (\bar{z})^k = P(\bar{z})$$ since $a_k \in \mathbb{R} \implies \overline{a_k} = a_k$. If $P(z) = 0$, then $P(\bar{z}) = \overline{0} = 0$. $\blacksquare$
    Irreducible Real Factors: The product of the conjugate pair gives a strictly real quadratic factor: $$(z - (u+iv))(z - (u-iv)) = (z - u)^2 - (iv)^2 = z^2 - 2uz + (u^2 + v^2) \in \mathbb{R}[z]$$ Hence, every polynomial with real coefficients factors completely over $\mathbb{R}$ into linear factors and irreducible quadratic factors with $\Delta < 0$.
  • Odd Degree Implication: Because non-real roots of real polynomials occur in pairs of two, any polynomial with real coefficients of odd degree must have at least one real root.

2. Polynomial Division, Remainder Theorem & Factor Theorem

For any polynomial $P(x)$ and non-zero divisor $D(x)$, there exist unique polynomials $Q(x)$ (quotient) and $R(x)$ (remainder) such that:

$$P(x) = D(x) Q(x) + R(x), \quad \text{where } \deg(R) < \deg(D) \text{ or } R(x) = 0$$
  • Remainder Theorem: When divided by a linear factor $(x - c)$, the remainder is a constant $R = P(c)$. If divided by $(ax - b)$, the remainder is $P\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)$.
  • Factor Theorem: $(x - c)$ is a factor of $P(x) \iff P(c) = 0 \iff x = c$ is a root of $P(x) = 0$.
  • Repeated Roots and Derivatives: A root $\alpha$ has multiplicity $m \ge 2$ if and only if $P(\alpha) = 0$ and $P'(\alpha) = 0$. For multiplicity $m$, $P(\alpha) = P'(\alpha) = \dots = P^{(m-1)}(\alpha) = 0$ while $P^{(m)}(\alpha) \neq 0$.

3. Vieta’s Formulas (Roots and Coefficients)

By expanding $P(x) = a_n \prod_{i=1}^n (x - \alpha_i) = a_n \left( x^n - e_1 x^{n-1} + e_2 x^{n-2} - \dots + (-1)^n e_n \right)$ and matching coefficients with $a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_0$, we obtain Vieta’s relations for elementary symmetric sums $e_k$:

Polynomial Sum of Roots: $\sum \alpha$ Sum of Products in Pairs: $\sum \alpha\beta$ Sum of Products in Triples: $\sum \alpha\beta\gamma$ Product of All Roots
Quadratic
$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$		 $\alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a}$ — — $\alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a}$
Cubic
$ax^3 + bx^2 + cx + d = 0$		 $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = -\frac{b}{a}$ $\alpha\beta + \beta\gamma + \gamma\alpha = \frac{c}{a}$ — $\alpha\beta\gamma = -\frac{d}{a}$
Quartic
$ax^4 + bx^3 + cx^2 + dx + e = 0$		 $\sum \alpha = -\frac{b}{a}$ $\sum \alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a}$ $\sum \alpha\beta\gamma = -\frac{d}{a}$ $\alpha\beta\gamma\delta = \frac{e}{a}$

4. Key Symmetric Sums & Newton's Sums Recurrence

In IB exam problems, you will evaluate power sums $S_k = \sum \alpha^k$ without solving for the roots explicitly:

  • Sum of Squares: $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 + \gamma^2 = (\sum \alpha)^2 - 2 \sum \alpha\beta$
  • Sum of Reciprocals: $\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta} + \frac{1}{\gamma} = \frac{\alpha\beta + \beta\gamma + \gamma\alpha}{\alpha\beta\gamma} = \frac{c/a}{-d/a} = -\frac{c}{d}$
  • Sum of Reciprocal Squares: $\frac{1}{\alpha^2} + \frac{1}{\beta^2} + \frac{1}{\gamma^2} = \left(\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta} + \frac{1}{\gamma}\right)^2 - 2\left(\frac{1}{\alpha\beta} + \frac{1}{\beta\gamma} + \frac{1}{\gamma\alpha}\right) = \left(\frac{\sum \alpha\beta}{\alpha\beta\gamma}\right)^2 - 2\left(\frac{\sum \alpha}{\alpha\beta\gamma}\right)$
  • Newton’s Sums (Power Sum Recurrence): For $P(x) = x^n + a_{n-1}x^{n-1} + \dots + a_0 = 0$, every root $\alpha_i$ satisfies $\alpha_i^k + a_{n-1}\alpha_i^{k-1} + \dots = 0$. Summing across all roots gives: $$S_k + a_{n-1} S_{k-1} + a_{n-2} S_{k-2} + \dots + a_{n-k} \cdot k = 0 \quad (\text{for } 1 \le k \le n)$$ and for $k \ge n$: $$S_k + a_{n-1} S_{k-1} + a_{n-2} S_{k-2} + \dots + a_0 S_{k-n} = 0$$

5. Transforming Polynomial Equations

Instead of calculating symmetric sums, you can find a polynomial equation whose roots are simple transformations of the original roots by substitution:

  • Reciprocal roots $\frac{1}{\alpha}$: Let $y = \frac{1}{x} \implies x = \frac{1}{y}$. Substitute into $P(x) = 0 \implies P\left(\frac{1}{y}\right) = 0$.
  • Shifted roots $\alpha + k$: Let $y = x + k \implies x = y - k$. Substitute $P(y - k) = 0$.
  • Scaled roots $k\alpha$: Let $y = kx \implies x = \frac{y}{k}$. Substitute $P\left(\frac{y}{k}\right) = 0$.
  • Squared roots $\alpha^2$: Let $y = x^2 \implies x = \sqrt{y}$. Rearrange $P(x)$ to isolate odd powers on one side, square both sides, and substitute $x^2 = y$.

The 6 Classic IB Exam Question Archetypes

Every polynomial question in Paper 1 and Paper 2 falls into one of these six primary archetypes:

Set up linear system $P(a) = R_1$ and $P(b) = R_2$; solve simultaneously for the parameters. Sign errors when substituting negative values (e.g., $(-2)^3 = -8$ vs $8$).

Express the target expression in terms of $e_1, e_2, e_3$; or use $P(-1) = -(\alpha+1)(\beta+1)(\gamma+1)$. Sign confusion in Vieta formulas: $e_1 = -b/a$, $e_2 = +c/a$, $e_3 = -d/a$, $e_4 = +e/a$.

6. Five Comprehensive Worked Exemplars

Exemplar 1 (Archetype 1 & 4): Factorization of Real Quartic with Complex Roots

Question: A quartic polynomial with real coefficients is given by $P(z) = z^4 - 6z^3 + 23z^2 - 34z + 26$.
Given that $z_1 = 1 + i$ is a zero of $P(z)$:
(a) Write down another zero $z_2$ of $P(z)$. [1 mark]
(b) Find the quadratic factor corresponding to these two zeros. [2 marks]
(c) Hence find all four zeros of $P(z)$ in the form $a \pm bi$. [4 marks]

Solution:

(a) Complex Conjugate Root: Because all coefficients of $P(z)$ are real, non-real roots occur in complex conjugate pairs. Thus, $z_2 = z_1^* = 1 - i$. [A1]

(b) Real Quadratic Factor: [M1]

$$(z - (1 + i))(z - (1 - i)) = ((z - 1) - i)((z - 1) + i) = (z - 1)^2 - i^2 = z^2 - 2z + 1 - (-1) = z^2 - 2z + 2 \quad [\text{A1}]$$

(c) Finding Remaining Zeros: Perform polynomial long division of $P(z)$ by $(z^2 - 2z + 2)$: [M1]

$$z^4 - 6z^3 + 23z^2 - 34z + 26 = (z^2 - 2z + 2)(z^2 - 4z + 13) \quad [\text{A1}]$$

Set the quadratic quotient to zero: $z^2 - 4z + 13 = 0$. [M1]

$$z = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{(-4)^2 - 4(1)(13)}}{2} = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 52}}{2} = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{-36}}{2} = \frac{4 \pm 6i}{2} = 2 \pm 3i \quad [\text{A1}]$$

The four zeros of $P(z)$ are: $1 + i, \; 1 - i, \; 2 + 3i, \; 2 - 3i$.

Exemplar 2 (Archetype 2): Symmetric Sums & Factored Products

Question: The roots of the cubic equation $2x^3 - 8x^2 + 6x - 5 = 0$ are $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$. Without solving the equation, determine the exact value of:
(a) $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 + \gamma^2$ [3 marks]
(b) $\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta} + \frac{1}{\gamma}$ [2 marks]
(c) $(2 - \alpha)(2 - \beta)(2 - \gamma)$ [3 marks]

Solution:

Identify Vieta's relations with $a = 2, b = -8, c = 6, d = -5$: [M1]

$$\sum \alpha = -\frac{-8}{2} = 4, \quad \sum \alpha\beta = \frac{6}{2} = 3, \quad \alpha\beta\gamma = -\frac{-5}{2} = \frac{5}{2} \quad [\text{A1}]$$

(a) Sum of Squares: [M1]

$$\alpha^2 + \beta^2 + \gamma^2 = (\alpha + \beta + \gamma)^2 - 2(\alpha\beta + \beta\gamma + \gamma\alpha) = 4^2 - 2(3) = 16 - 6 = 10 \quad [\text{A1}]$$

(b) Sum of Reciprocals: [M1]

$$\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta} + \frac{1}{\gamma} = \frac{\alpha\beta + \beta\gamma + \gamma\alpha}{\alpha\beta\gamma} = \frac{3}{5/2} = \frac{6}{5} \quad [\text{A1}]$$

(c) Factored Product:
Method 1 (Polynomial evaluation): Notice that $P(x) = 2(x - \alpha)(x - \beta)(x - \gamma)$. [M1]
Therefore, $(2 - \alpha)(2 - \beta)(2 - \gamma) = \frac{P(2)}{2}$. [A1]
Evaluate $P(2) = 2(2)^3 - 8(2)^2 + 6(2) - 5 = 16 - 32 + 12 - 5 = -9$.
Hence $(2 - \alpha)(2 - \beta)(2 - \gamma) = \frac{-9}{2} = -4.5$. [A1]
Method 2 (Expansion): $8 - 4(\alpha+\beta+\gamma) + 2(\sum \alpha\beta) - \alpha\beta\gamma = 8 - 4(4) + 2(3) - \frac{5}{2} = 8 - 16 + 6 - 2.5 = -4.5$.

Exemplar 3 (Archetype 3): Roots in Geometric Progression

Question: The cubic polynomial $x^3 - 14x^2 + 56x - 64 = 0$ has three positive roots $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ which form a geometric progression.
(a) Find all three roots. [5 marks]
(b) Verify that the sum of pairwise products equals $56$. [2 marks]

Solution:

(a) Finding the Roots: Let the roots in GP be $\frac{k}{r}, k, kr$ with $r > 0$. [M1]
By Vieta's third formula for the product of roots: [M1]

$$\left(\frac{k}{r}\right)(k)(kr) = k^3 = -\frac{-64}{1} = 64 \implies k = \sqrt[3]{64} = 4 \quad [\text{A1}]$$

By Vieta's first formula for the sum of roots: [M1]

$$\frac{4}{r} + 4 + 4r = 14 \implies \frac{4}{r} + 4r = 10 \implies 4 + 4r^2 = 10r \implies 2r^2 - 5r + 2 = 0$$ $$(2r - 1)(r - 2) = 0 \implies r = 2 \text{ or } r = \frac{1}{2} \quad [\text{A1}]$$

For $r = 2$, roots are $\frac{4}{2} = 2, 4, 4(2) = 8$. (For $r = 1/2$, roots are $8, 4, 2$, which is identical).
The three roots are $2, 4, 8$. [A1]

(b) Verification: [M1]

$$\sum \alpha\beta = (2)(4) + (4)(8) + (2)(8) = 8 + 32 + 16 = 56 \quad [\text{AG}] [\text{A1}]$$

Exemplar 4 (Archetype 5): Polynomial Transformation

Question: The roots of the cubic equation $x^3 - 3x^2 + 4x - 2 = 0$ are $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$.
Find a cubic equation with integer coefficients whose roots are $\frac{1}{\alpha}, \frac{1}{\beta}, \frac{1}{\gamma}$. [4 marks]

Solution:

Let $y = \frac{1}{x} \implies x = \frac{1}{y}$. [M1]
Substitute $x = \frac{1}{y}$ into the original polynomial equation: [M1]

$$\left(\frac{1}{y}\right)^3 - 3\left(\frac{1}{y}\right)^2 + 4\left(\frac{1}{y}\right) - 2 = 0$$ $$\frac{1}{y^3} - \frac{3}{y^2} + \frac{4}{y} - 2 = 0$$

Multiply through by $y^3$ (since $y \neq 0$): [M1]

$$1 - 3y + 4y^2 - 2y^3 = 0$$

Multiply by $-1$ to make the leading coefficient positive: [A1]

$$2y^3 - 4y^2 + 3y - 1 = 0$$

Check using Vieta: For new equation, sum of roots $= \frac{4}{2} = 2$.
Original $\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta} + \frac{1}{\gamma} = \frac{\sum \alpha\beta}{\alpha\beta\gamma} = \frac{4}{2} = 2$. Verified!

Exemplar 5 (Archetype 6): Trigonometric Roots & Chebyshev Product

Question:
(a) Use the identity $\cos(3\theta) = 4\cos^3\theta - 3\cos\theta$ to show that $x_1 = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{9}\right)$, $x_2 = \cos\left(\frac{7\pi}{9}\right)$, and $x_3 = \cos\left(\frac{13\pi}{9}\right)$ are roots of $8x^3 - 6x - 1 = 0$. [4 marks]
(b) Hence evaluate the exact value of $\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{9}\right)\cos\left(\frac{7\pi}{9}\right)\cos\left(\frac{13\pi}{9}\right)$. [2 marks]

Solution:

(a) Linking to Triple Angle Identity: Let $x = \cos\theta$. [M1]
Substitute into $8x^3 - 6x - 1 = 0$: [M1]

$$2(4x^3 - 3x) - 1 = 0 \implies 2\cos(3\theta) - 1 = 0 \implies \cos(3\theta) = \frac{1}{2}$$

Solve $\cos(3\theta) = \frac{1}{2}$ in the general interval: [M1]

$$3\theta = \frac{\pi}{3}, \; 2\pi - \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{5\pi}{3}, \; 2\pi + \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{7\pi}{3}, \; 4\pi - \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{11\pi}{3}, \; 4\pi + \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{13\pi}{3}$$

Dividing by 3 gives distinct angles: $\theta = \frac{\pi}{9}, \; \frac{7\pi}{9}, \; \frac{13\pi}{9}$. [A1]
Since a cubic polynomial has at most three distinct roots, these three distinct cosine values are precisely the three roots of $8x^3 - 6x - 1 = 0$. [AG]

(b) Product of Roots via Vieta: By Vieta's formula for the cubic $8x^3 + 0x^2 - 6x - 1 = 0$ with $a = 8, d = -1$: [M1]

$$\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{9}\right)\cos\left(\frac{7\pi}{9}\right)\cos\left(\frac{13\pi}{9}\right) = -\frac{d}{a} = -\frac{-1}{8} = \frac{1}{8} \quad [\text{A1}]$$

Practice Exercises (10 Tiered Questions)

Exercise 1 (Foundational: Remainder & Factor Theorem)

When $P(x) = 2x^3 + ax^2 - 5x + 6$ is divided by $(x - 2)$, the remainder is 16. Find the value of $a$, and hence determine whether $(x + 3)$ is a factor of $P(x)$.

Show Full Solution

By Remainder Theorem: $P(2) = 16$. [M1]
$2(2)^3 + a(2)^2 - 5(2) + 6 = 16 \implies 16 + 4a - 10 + 6 = 16 \implies 4a + 12 = 16 \implies 4a = 4 \implies a = 1$. [A1]
Now $P(x) = 2x^3 + x^2 - 5x + 6$.
Test $(x + 3)$: $P(-3) = 2(-27) + (9) - 5(-3) + 6 = -54 + 9 + 15 + 6 = -24 \neq 0$. [M1]
Since $P(-3) \neq 0$, $(x + 3)$ is not a factor of $P(x)$. [A1]

Exercise 2 (Foundational: Vieta for Quadratic)

The quadratic $3x^2 - 12x + 7 = 0$ has roots $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Find the value of $\alpha^2 + \beta^2$.

Show Full Solution

From Vieta: $\alpha + \beta = -\frac{-12}{3} = 4$, and $\alpha\beta = \frac{7}{3}$. [M1]
$\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 2\alpha\beta = 4^2 - 2\left(\frac{7}{3}\right) = 16 - \frac{14}{3} = \frac{34}{3}$. [A1]

Exercise 3 (Foundational: Complex Conjugate Roots)

A polynomial with real coefficients has degree 4. Two of its roots are $2 + 3i$ and $-4i$. State the remaining two roots.

Show Full Solution

By the Complex Conjugate Root Theorem, because all coefficients are real, non-real roots must occur in conjugate pairs. [R1]
• The conjugate of $2 + 3i$ is $2 - 3i$. [A1]
• The conjugate of $-4i = 0 - 4i$ is $4i = 0 + 4i$. [A1]
Remaining two roots: $2 - 3i$ and $4i$.

Exercise 4 (Intermediate: Cubic Vieta Calculation)

The roots of $x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6 = 0$ are $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$. Calculate $\frac{1}{\alpha\beta} + \frac{1}{\beta\gamma} + \frac{1}{\gamma\alpha}$.

Show Full Solution

Combine over a common denominator: [M1]
$\frac{1}{\alpha\beta} + \frac{1}{\beta\gamma} + \frac{1}{\gamma\alpha} = \frac{\gamma + \alpha + \beta}{\alpha\beta\gamma} = \frac{\alpha + \beta + \gamma}{\alpha\beta\gamma}$. [A1]
From Vieta: $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 6$, and $\alpha\beta\gamma = -\frac{-6}{1} = 6$. [M1]
Result: $\frac{6}{6} = 1$. [A1]

Exercise 5 (Intermediate: Arithmetic Progression of Roots)

The roots of $x^3 - 9x^2 + 23x - 15 = 0$ form an arithmetic progression. Find all three roots.

Show Full Solution

Let the roots in AP be $k - d, k, k + d$. [M1]
By Vieta for sum: $(k - d) + k + (k + d) = 3k = 9 \implies k = 3$. [A1]
By Vieta for product: $(3 - d)(3)(3 + d) = 3(9 - d^2) = 15 \implies 9 - d^2 = 5 \implies d^2 = 4 \implies d = \pm 2$. [M1]
For $d = 2$, roots are $3 - 2 = 1, 3, 3 + 2 = 5$. [A1]
Roots are $1, 3, 5$.

Exercise 6 (Intermediate: Constructing Polynomial with Shifted Roots)

The roots of $x^2 - 5x + 3 = 0$ are $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Find a quadratic equation with integer coefficients whose roots are $2\alpha + 1$ and $2\beta + 1$.

Show Full Solution

$\alpha + \beta = 5$, $\alpha\beta = 3$. [M1]
Sum of new roots: $(2\alpha + 1) + (2\beta + 1) = 2(\alpha + \beta) + 2 = 2(5) + 2 = 12$. [A1]
Product of new roots: $(2\alpha + 1)(2\beta + 1) = 4\alpha\beta + 2(\alpha + \beta) + 1 = 4(3) + 2(5) + 1 = 12 + 10 + 1 = 23$. [A1]
Equation: $x^2 - 12x + 23 = 0$. [A1]

Exercise 7 (Exam Standard: Quartic Factorization over $\mathbb{C}$)

The quartic polynomial $P(z) = z^4 - 2z^3 + 6z^2 - 8z + 8$ has real coefficients. Given that $z = 2i$ is a root, factorize $P(z)$ completely into four linear factors over $\mathbb{C}$.

Show Full Solution

Since coefficients are real, $z = -2i$ is also a root. [R1]
Quadratic factor: $(z - 2i)(z + 2i) = z^2 + 4$. [M1]
Polynomial division of $P(z)$ by $(z^2 + 4)$: [M1]
$z^4 - 2z^3 + 6z^2 - 8z + 8 = (z^2 + 4)(z^2 - 2z + 2)$. [A1]
Solve $z^2 - 2z + 2 = 0 \implies z = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{4 - 8}}{2} = 1 \pm i$. [A1]
Linear factorization: $P(z) = (z - 2i)(z + 2i)(z - (1+i))(z - (1-i))$. [A1]

Exercise 8 (Advanced: Vieta for Quartic with Root Relation)

The roots of the quartic $x^4 - 8x^3 + 14x^2 + 8x - 15 = 0$ satisfy the relation $\alpha + \beta = \gamma + \delta$. Find all four roots.

Show Full Solution

By Vieta for sum: $(\alpha + \beta) + (\gamma + \delta) = 8$.
Since $\alpha + \beta = \gamma + \delta$, both must equal $4$: $\alpha + \beta = 4$ and $\gamma + \delta = 4$. [M1]
Let $P(x) = (x^2 - 4x + p)(x^2 - 4x + q) = 0$. [M1]
Expand: $x^4 - 8x^3 + (p + q + 16)x^2 - 4(p + q)x + pq$. [M1]
Match coefficients: $p + q + 16 = 14 \implies p + q = -2$, and $pq = -15$.
Roots of $t^2 + 2t - 15 = 0$ are $3$ and $-5$.
Factors: $(x^2 - 4x + 3)(x^2 - 4x - 5) = 0$. [A1]
• $x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0 \implies (x - 1)(x - 3) = 0 \implies x = 1, 3$.
• $x^2 - 4x - 5 = 0 \implies (x - 5)(x + 1) = 0 \implies x = 5, -1$.
The four roots are $-1, 1, 3, 5$. [A1]

Exercise 9 (Advanced: Newton Sums for Cubic)

Let $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ be the roots of $x^3 - 3x + 1 = 0$. Using the identity $S_n = \alpha^n + \beta^n + \gamma^n$ where $S_n - 3S_{n-2} + S_{n-3} = 0$, calculate $S_4 = \alpha^4 + \beta^4 + \gamma^4$.

Show Full Solution

From the equation $x^3 = 3x - 1$, multiplying by $x^{n-3}$ gives $x^n = 3x^{n-2} - x^{n-3}$, so $S_n = 3S_{n-2} - S_{n-3}$. [M1]
• $S_0 = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3$.
• $S_1 = \alpha + \beta + \gamma = 0$ (no $x^2$ term). [A1]
• $S_2 = (\alpha+\beta+\gamma)^2 - 2(\alpha\beta+\beta\gamma+\gamma\alpha) = 0^2 - 2(-3) = 6$. [A1]
• $S_3 = 3S_1 - S_0 = 3(0) - 3 = -3$. [A1]
• $S_4 = 3S_2 - S_1 = 3(6) - 0 = 18$. [A1]

Exercise 10 (Advanced: Vieta with Trigonometric Roots)

Show that the roots of $8x^3 - 6x - 1 = 0$ are $\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{9}\right)$, $\cos\left(\frac{7\pi}{9}\right)$, and $\cos\left(\frac{13\pi}{9}\right)$, and deduce the exact value of $\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{9}\right)\cos\left(\frac{7\pi}{9}\right)\cos\left(\frac{13\pi}{9}\right)$.

Show Full Solution

Recall the triple angle identity: $\cos(3\theta) = 4\cos^3\theta - 3\cos\theta$. [M1]
Let $x = \cos\theta$. Then $8x^3 - 6x = 2(4x^3 - 3x) = 2\cos(3\theta)$.
The equation becomes $2\cos(3\theta) - 1 = 0 \implies \cos(3\theta) = \frac{1}{2}$. [M1]
Solutions for $3\theta$: $3\theta = \frac{\pi}{3}, \frac{7\pi}{3}, \frac{13\pi}{3} \implies \theta = \frac{\pi}{9}, \frac{7\pi}{9}, \frac{13\pi}{9}$. [A1]
Since the cubic has degree 3, these 3 distinct cosine values are all three roots of $8x^3 - 6x - 1 = 0$.
By Vieta's formula for the product of roots: [M1]
$\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{9}\right)\cos\left(\frac{7\pi}{9}\right)\cos\left(\frac{13\pi}{9}\right) = -\frac{-1}{8} = \frac{1}{8}$. [A1]

IB Formula Booklet References (Topic 1)

Syllabus AHL 1.16: Polynomials & Vieta’s Relations

For polynomial equation $a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0 = 0$ with roots $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_n$:

$$\sum \alpha_i = -\frac{a_{n-1}}{a_n}, \quad \sum_{i < j} \alpha_i \alpha_j = \frac{a_{n-2}}{a_n}, \quad \dots, \quad \prod_{i=1}^n \alpha_i = (-1)^n \frac{a_0}{a_n}$$

Must-Memorize Principles (Not in Booklet)

1. Complex Conjugate Root Theorem

If coefficients are real, any non-real root $z = a + bi$ must be paired with $z^* = a - bi$. Any real polynomial of odd degree must have at least one real root.

2. Useful Symmetric Root Expansions

  • $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 + \gamma^2 = (\sum \alpha)^2 - 2\sum\alpha\beta$
  • $\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta} + \frac{1}{\gamma} = \frac{\sum\alpha\beta}{\alpha\beta\gamma} = -\frac{c}{d}$
  • $(\alpha + 1)(\beta + 1)(\gamma + 1) = 1 + \sum\alpha + \sum\alpha\beta + \alpha\beta\gamma = -P(-1) \text{ (monic)}$

3. Newton's Sums Recurrence Formula

For monic cubic $x^3 + px^2 + qx + r = 0$ with power sums $S_k = \alpha^k + \beta^k + \gamma^k$:

$$S_1 + p = 0 \implies S_1 = -p$$ $$S_2 + p S_1 + 2q = 0 \implies S_2 = -p S_1 - 2q$$ $$S_3 + p S_2 + q S_1 + 3r = 0 \implies S_3 = -p S_2 - q S_1 - 3r$$ $$S_k + p S_{k-1} + q S_{k-2} + r S_{k-3} = 0 \quad (\text{for } k \ge 4)$$

Examination Pitfalls & Examiner Tips

Sign Flips in Vieta’s Product

Remember that the signs alternate: $\sum \alpha_i$ has a negative sign, $\sum \alpha_i \alpha_j$ is positive, and the product is $(-1)^n \frac{a_0}{a_n}$. For a cubic ($n=3$), product is $-\frac{d}{a}$, while for a quartic ($n=4$), product is $+\frac{e}{a}$.

Conjugate Theorem Requires REAL Coefficients

The conjugate root theorem does NOT apply if any coefficient is complex! For instance, $z^2 - iz = 0$ has roots $0$ and $i$, which are not conjugates because the coefficient $-i$ is not real.

Division Algorithm with Quadratic Remainder

When dividing $P(x)$ by a quadratic $(x-a)(x-b)$, the remainder must be written as a linear expression $R(x) = Ax + B$. Do NOT assume the remainder is a numerical constant.

Question 5 (Paper 1 Style · 6 marks)

The cubic polynomial $P(x) = x^3 - 7x^2 + kx - 8$ has three positive real roots $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ that form a geometric progression.
(a) Show that the middle term of the geometric progression is 2. [2 marks]
(b) Hence determine the common ratio of the progression and find all three roots. [3 marks]
(c) Find the value of $k$. [1 mark]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Middle root value: [2 marks]
Let the roots be $\frac{b}{r}, b, br$. [M1]
By Vieta's formula for the product of roots:
$\left(\frac{b}{r}\right)(b)(br) = b^3 = -\frac{-8}{1} = 8 \implies b = 2$ [A1].

(b) Finding the roots: [3 marks]
By Vieta's formula for the sum of roots:
$\frac{2}{r} + 2 + 2r = 7 \implies \frac{2}{r} + 2r = 5$ [M1]
Multiply by $r$: $2r^2 - 5r + 2 = 0 \implies (2r - 1)(r - 2) = 0 \implies r = 2$ or $r = \frac{1}{2}$ [A1].
Both ratios yield the same set of roots: $1, 2, 4$ [A1].

(c) Finding $k$: [1 mark]
$k = \sum \alpha\beta = (1)(2) + (2)(4) + (1)(4) = 2 + 8 + 4 = 14$ [A1].

Question 6 (Paper 2 Style · 7 marks)

Consider the polynomial $P(z) = z^4 - 2z^3 + 6z^2 - 8z + 8$.
(a) Given that $z = 2i$ is a root of $P(z) = 0$, write down another imaginary root. [1 mark]
(b) Find the quadratic factor of $P(z)$ corresponding to these imaginary roots. [2 marks]
(c) Factorize $P(z)$ completely over the complex field $\mathbb{C}$, and sketch the four roots on an Argand diagram. [4 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Conjugate root: [1 mark]
Since all coefficients of $P(z)$ are real, $z = -2i$ is another root [A1].

(b) Quadratic factor: [2 marks]
$(z - 2i)(z + 2i) = z^2 - (2i)^2 = z^2 + 4$ [M1][A1].

(c) Complete factorization & Argand sketch: [4 marks]
Perform polynomial division of $P(z)$ by $(z^2 + 4)$: [M1]
$z^4 - 2z^3 + 6z^2 - 8z + 8 = (z^2 + 4)(z^2 - 2z + 2)$ [A1].
Solve $z^2 - 2z + 2 = 0$:
$z = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{4 - 8}}{2} = \frac{2 \pm 2i}{2} = 1 \pm i$ [A1].
The four roots are $2i, -2i, 1 + i, 1 - i$.
Argand diagram: Points plotted at $(0, 2), (0, -2), (1, 1), (1, -1)$ with symmetric reflection across the real axis [A1].
