Systems of Linear Equations & Matrices

Solve systems of 3 linear equations in 3 variables using Gaussian elimination and row echelon form. Connect algebraic consistency to the 3D geometry of intersecting planes: unique intersection points, lines of intersection, and triangular prism inconsistencies.

1. Matrix Representation of Linear Systems

A system of three linear equations in three variables $(x, y, z)$:

$$\begin{cases} a_1 x + b_1 y + c_1 z = d_1 \\ a_2 x + b_2 y + c_2 z = d_2 \\ a_3 x + b_3 y + c_3 z = d_3 \end{cases}$$

can be expressed compactly in matrix equation form $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$:

$$\begin{pmatrix} a_1 & b_1 & c_1 \\ a_2 & b_2 & c_2 \\ a_3 & b_3 & c_3 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} d_1 \\ d_2 \\ d_3 \end{pmatrix}$$

To solve by hand or analyze solvability analytically without technology (Paper 1), we form the augmented matrix $[A | \mathbf{b}]$ and perform elementary row operations (EROs):

$$[A | \mathbf{b}] = \left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} a_1 & b_1 & c_1 & d_1 \\ a_2 & b_2 & c_2 & d_2 \\ a_3 & b_3 & c_3 & d_3 \end{array}\right)$$

2. Elementary Row Operations (EROs) & Row Echelon Form (REF)

The three permissible EROs that produce an equivalent system are:

  1. Row swap: $R_i \leftrightarrow R_j$ (interchange two rows).
  2. Scalar multiplication: $R_i \to k R_i$ where $k \neq 0$.
  3. Row replacement: $R_i \to R_i + k R_j$ (add a scalar multiple of row $j$ to row $i$).

We apply Gaussian elimination to reduce the matrix to upper-triangular row echelon form:

$$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & * & * & * \\ 0 & 1 & * & * \\ 0 & 0 & p & q \end{array}\right)$$

3. Classification of Solutions & Geometric Interpretations in $\mathbb{R}^3$

Each linear equation $ax + by + cz = d$ represents a flat two-dimensional plane in 3D Euclidean space. The solution set represents the intersection of these three planes $\Pi_1, \Pi_2, \Pi_3$. The bottom row $(0\; 0\; p \mid q)$ dictates the solution type:

Bottom Row Condition Algebraic Solution Geometric Interpretation (3 Planes)
$p \neq 0$ Unique Solution:
$z = q/p$, then back-substitute for $y$ and $x$.		 The 3 planes intersect at a single unique point $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$. $\det(A) \neq 0$.
$p = 0$ and $q = 0$
$(0\; 0\; 0 \mid 0)$		 Infinitely Many Solutions:
Consistent dependent system. Let $z = \lambda \in \mathbb{R}$, express $x$ and $y$ in terms of $\lambda$.		 The 3 planes intersect along a common line (a "sheaf" of planes), or two/three planes are coincident.
$p = 0$ and $q \neq 0$
$(0\; 0\; 0 \mid q)$		 No Solution:
Inconsistent statement $0x + 0y + 0z = q \implies 0 = q$ (contradiction).		 The 3 planes have no common intersection. Geometrically:
• Pairwise intersect along three parallel lines forming a triangular prism.
• Two or three planes are strictly parallel with different intercepts.

4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Gaussian Elimination with Unique Solution

Solve the following system algebraically using augmented matrix row operations:

$$\begin{cases} x + 2y - z = 2 \\ 2x + 5y + z = 11 \\ 3x + 7y + 2z = 15 \end{cases}$$

Solution:

Set up the augmented matrix:

$$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 2 \\ 2 & 5 & 1 & 11 \\ 3 & 7 & 2 & 15 \end{array}\right)$$

Perform $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1$:

$$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & 7 \\ 0 & 1 & 5 & 9 \end{array}\right)$$

Perform $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$:

$$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & 7 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 & 2 \end{array}\right)$$

From Row 3: $2z = 2 \implies z = 1$.
From Row 2: $y + 3(1) = 7 \implies y = 4$.
From Row 1: $x + 2(4) - (1) = 2 \implies x + 7 = 2 \implies x = -5$.
Hence the unique solution is $(x, y, z) = (-5, 4, 1)$, representing the single point where all three planes intersect.

Worked Example 2: Parameter Analysis for Consistency & Triangular Prism

Consider the system with parameter $k \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$\begin{cases} x + y + z = 6 \\ 2x + 3y + kz = 14 \\ x + 2y + 3z = 8 \end{cases}$$

Find the value of $k$ for which the system does not have a unique solution. For this value of $k$, determine whether the system is consistent (infinitely many solutions) or inconsistent (no solution), and describe the geometry of the planes.

Solution:

Write the augmented matrix:

$$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 2 & 3 & k & 14 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 & 8 \end{array}\right)$$

Swap rows $R_2 \leftrightarrow R_3$ first to keep $k$ in the bottom row:

$$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 & 8 \\ 2 & 3 & k & 14 \end{array}\right)$$

Perform $R_2 \to R_2 - R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - 2R_1$:

$$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & k-2 & 2 \end{array}\right)$$

Perform $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$:

$$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & k-4 & 0 \end{array}\right)$$

• If $k - 4 \neq 0$ ($k \neq 4$), the matrix has full rank 3, giving a unique solution.
• If $k = 4$, row 3 becomes $(0\; 0\; 0 \mid 0)$. This is a tautology $0 = 0$, meaning the system is consistent with infinitely many solutions!
Planes intersect along a common line. Letting $z = \lambda$, we have $y = 2 - 2\lambda$, and $x = 6 - (2 - 2\lambda) - \lambda = 4 + \lambda$.

Worked Example 3: Inconsistent System forming a Triangular Prism

Show that the following system has no solution and state whether any two planes are parallel:

$$\begin{cases} x - y + 2z = 4 \\ 2x + y - z = 1 \\ 4x - y + 3z = 7 \end{cases}$$

Solution:

Augmented matrix:

$$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -1 & 2 & 4 \\ 2 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 4 & -1 & 3 & 7 \end{array}\right)$$

Perform $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - 4R_1$:

$$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -1 & 2 & 4 \\ 0 & 3 & -5 & -7 \\ 0 & 3 & -5 & -9 \end{array}\right)$$

Perform $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$:

$$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -1 & 2 & 4 \\ 0 & 3 & -5 & -7 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & -2 \end{array}\right)$$

The last row implies $0x + 0y + 0z = -2 \implies 0 = -2$, which is impossible. Hence the system has no solution (inconsistent).
Check normal vectors: $\mathbf{n}_1 = (1, -1, 2)$, $\mathbf{n}_2 = (2, 1, -1)$, $\mathbf{n}_3 = (4, -1, 3)$.
No two normal vectors are scalar multiples of each other, so no two planes are parallel. Therefore, the three planes intersect pairwise in three parallel lines, forming an open triangular prism.

Exercise 1 (Foundational: Row Operations)

Given the augmented matrix in upper triangular echelon form: $$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & 1 & -1 & 5 \\ 0 & 3 & 2 & 11 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 & 4 \end{array}\right)$$ Find the values of $x$, $y$, and $z$ using back-substitution.

Show Full Solution

Step 1: Solve for $z$ from Row 3:
$4z = 4 \implies z = 1$. [A1]

Step 2: Solve for $y$ from Row 2:
$3y + 2(1) = 11 \implies 3y = 9 \implies y = 3$. [A1]

Step 3: Solve for $x$ from Row 1:
$2x + 1(3) - 1(1) = 5 \implies 2x + 2 = 5 \implies 2x = 3 \implies x = \frac{3}{2}$. [A1]

Solution: $(x, y, z) = \left(\frac{3}{2}, 3, 1\right)$.

Exercise 2 (Foundational: Row Echelon Transformation)

Perform the row operations $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1$ on the augmented matrix: $$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 1 & 1 & 7 \\ 3 & 2 & -1 & 8 \end{array}\right)$$ and state the resulting matrix.

Show Full Solution

For $R_2$: $2 - 2(1) = 0$, $1 - 2(-1) = 3$, $1 - 2(2) = -3$, $7 - 2(3) = 1$. [M1]
For $R_3$: $3 - 3(1) = 0$, $2 - 3(-1) = 5$, $-1 - 3(2) = -7$, $8 - 3(3) = -1$. [M1]

Resulting matrix: $$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & -3 & 1 \\ 0 & 5 & -7 & -1 \end{array}\right)$$ [A1]

Exercise 3 (Foundational: Identifying Inconsistency)

After Gaussian elimination, a system yields the augmented matrix: $$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 4 & -2 & 5 \\ 0 & 2 & 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 7 \end{array}\right)$$ State the number of solutions and justify your answer algebraically.

Show Full Solution

The third row translates to the equation $0x + 0y + 0z = 7 \iff 0 = 7$. [M1]
Because $0 = 7$ is false for all real values of $x, y, z$, this is a contradiction. [R1]
Therefore, the system is inconsistent and has no solution. [A1]

Exercise 4 (Intermediate: 3-Variable System)

Solve the system by Gaussian elimination: $$\begin{cases} x + y + 2z = 8 \\ -x - 2y + 3z = 1 \\ 3x - 7y + 4z = 10 \end{cases}$$

Show Full Solution

Augmented matrix: $\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 2 & 8 \\ -1 & -2 & 3 & 1 \\ 3 & -7 & 4 & 10 \end{array}\right)$ [M1]
$R_2 \to R_2 + R_1$, $R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1$:
$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 2 & 8 \\ 0 & -1 & 5 & 9 \\ 0 & -10 & -2 & -14 \end{array}\right)$ [A1]
$R_3 \to R_3 - 10R_2$:
Row 3: $0 - 10(0) = 0$; $-10 - 10(-1) = 0$; $-2 - 10(5) = -52$; $-14 - 10(9) = -104$.
$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 2 & 8 \\ 0 & -1 & 5 & 9 \\ 0 & 0 & -52 & -104 \end{array}\right)$ [A1]
From $R_3$: $-52z = -104 \implies z = 2$.
From $R_2$: $-y + 5(2) = 9 \implies -y + 10 = 9 \implies y = 1$.
From $R_1$: $x + 1 + 2(2) = 8 \implies x + 5 = 8 \implies x = 3$.
Unique solution: $(x, y, z) = (3, 1, 2)$. [A1]

Exercise 5 (Intermediate: Parametric Solution Line)

Solve the dependent system and express the solution in vector/parametric form using parameter $\lambda$: $$\begin{cases} x + 2y - z = 3 \\ 2x + 5y - 3z = 7 \\ 3x + 7y - 4z = 10 \end{cases}$$

Show Full Solution

Augmented matrix: $\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 2 & 5 & -3 & 7 \\ 3 & 7 & -4 & 10 \end{array}\right)$ [M1]
$R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$, $R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1$:
$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & 1 \end{array}\right)$ [A1]
$R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$:
$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right)$ (Consistent with infinitely many solutions). [M1]
Let $z = \lambda$.
From Row 2: $y - \lambda = 1 \implies y = 1 + \lambda$.
From Row 1: $x + 2(1 + \lambda) - \lambda = 3 \implies x + 2 + \lambda = 3 \implies x = 1 - \lambda$.
Parametric equation of the intersection line: $\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} + \lambda \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$. [A1]

Exercise 6 (Intermediate: Parameter for Consistency)

A system is given by: $$\begin{cases} x + y + 2z = 3 \\ x + 2y + 3z = 4 \\ 2x + 3y + kz = m \end{cases}$$ Determine the values of $k$ and $m$ such that the system has: (a) a unique solution, (b) infinitely many solutions, (c) no solution.

Show Full Solution

Augmented matrix: $\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 2 & 3 & k & m \end{array}\right)$ [M1]
$R_2 \to R_2 - R_1$, $R_3 \to R_3 - 2R_1$:
$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & k-4 & m-6 \end{array}\right)$
$R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$:
$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & k-5 & m-7 \end{array}\right)$ [A1]
(a) Unique solution: $k - 5 \neq 0 \implies k \neq 5$, with $m \in \mathbb{R}$. [A1]
(b) Infinitely many solutions: $k - 5 = 0$ AND $m - 7 = 0 \implies k = 5, m = 7$. [A1]
(c) No solution: $k - 5 = 0$ AND $m - 7 \neq 0 \implies k = 5, m \neq 7$. [A1]

Exercise 7 (Exam Standard: Geometric Sheaf vs Prism)

Given three planes $\Pi_1: x - 2y + z = 1$, $\Pi_2: 2x - y + 3z = 4$, $\Pi_3: 3x - 3y + 4z = 6$:
(a) Show that the system is inconsistent.
(b) By examining the normals, deduce whether the planes form a triangular prism or contain parallel planes.

Show Full Solution

(a) Inconsistency test: [M1]
Augmented matrix: $\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -2 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 & 4 \\ 3 & -3 & 4 & 6 \end{array}\right)$
$R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$, $R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1$: $\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -2 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 & 3 \end{array}\right)$
$R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$: $\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -2 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{array}\right)$
Row 3 gives $0 = 1$, contradiction. System is inconsistent. [A1]

(b) Geometric configuration:
Normal vectors are $\mathbf{n}_1 = (1, -2, 1)$, $\mathbf{n}_2 = (2, -1, 3)$, $\mathbf{n}_3 = (3, -3, 4)$. [M1]
No two normal vectors are scalar multiples (i.e. $\mathbf{n}_1 \neq c\mathbf{n}_2$, etc.), which means no two planes are parallel. [R1]
Therefore, the three planes pairwise intersect along three non-coincident parallel lines, forming an open triangular prism. [A1]

Exercise 8 (Advanced: Quadratic Parameter in Bottom Row)

A linear system has the augmented matrix: $$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & a^2 - 9 & a - 3 \end{array}\right)$$ Find all values of $a \in \mathbb{R}$ for which the system has (a) no solution, (b) infinitely many solutions, (c) a unique solution with $z = 1$.

Show Full Solution

The third equation is $(a^2 - 9)z = a - 3$, or $(a - 3)(a + 3)z = a - 3$. [M1]

(a) No solution: Requires $(a^2 - 9) = 0$ while $a - 3 \neq 0$.
$(a-3)(a+3)=0 \implies a = 3$ or $a = -3$.
When $a = -3$: coefficient $= 0$, right-hand side $= -6 \neq 0$.
Thus $a = -3$ yields no solution. [A1]

(b) Infinitely many solutions: Requires coefficient $= 0$ AND right-hand side $= 0$.
When $a = 3$: $0z = 0$, which holds for all $z$. System has infinitely many solutions. [A1]

(c) Unique solution with $z = 1$:
For $a \neq \pm 3$, $z = \frac{a-3}{(a-3)(a+3)} = \frac{1}{a+3}$. [M1]
Setting $z = 1 \implies \frac{1}{a+3} = 1 \implies a+3 = 1 \implies a = -2$.
Since $-2 \neq \pm 3$, $a = -2$ is valid! [A1]

Exercise 9 (Advanced: Homogeneous System & Non-Trivial Solutions)

Consider the homogeneous linear system: $$\begin{cases} x + 2y - z = 0 \\ 3x + ky + z = 0 \\ 2x + y + 2z = 0 \end{cases}$$ (a) State the trivial solution.
(b) Find the value of $k$ for which the system possesses non-trivial solutions.
(c) For this value of $k$, find the general solution.

Show Full Solution

(a) Trivial solution: $(x, y, z) = (0, 0, 0)$. [A1]

(b) Non-trivial solutions condition:
A homogeneous system has non-trivial solutions if and only if the coefficient determinant is zero (or rank < 3). [M1]
$\det(A) = 1(2k - 1) - 2(6 - 2) - 1(3 - 2k) = (2k - 1) - 8 - (3 - 2k) = 4k - 12$.
Setting $\det(A) = 0 \implies 4k - 12 = 0 \implies k = 3$. [A1]

(c) General solution for $k = 3$:
$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 0 \\ 3 & 3 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 & 2 & 0 \end{array}\right) \xrightarrow{R_2-3R_1,\; R_3-2R_1} \left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -3 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & -3 & 4 & 0 \end{array}\right) \xrightarrow{R_3-R_2} \left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -3 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right)$ [M1]
Let $z = 3\lambda$. Then $-3y + 4(3\lambda) = 0 \implies 3y = 12\lambda \implies y = 4\lambda$.
$x + 2(4\lambda) - 3\lambda = 0 \implies x + 5\lambda = 0 \implies x = -5\lambda$.
General solution: $(x, y, z) = (-5\lambda, 4\lambda, 3\lambda) = \lambda(-5, 4, 3)$ for $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$. [A1]

Exercise 10 (Advanced: 4 Planes Intersection Problem)

A fourth plane $\Pi_4: 2x - y + z = d$ is added to the system in Worked Example 1 whose intersection was $(-5, 4, 1)$.
(a) Find the value of $d$ such that all four planes pass through the same point.
(b) If $d = 10$, describe the geometric relationship between $\Pi_4$ and the common point of $\Pi_1, \Pi_2, \Pi_3$.

Show Full Solution

(a) All 4 planes concurrent at $(-5, 4, 1)$: [M1]
Substitute $(-5, 4, 1)$ into $\Pi_4$:
$2(-5) - (4) + 1 = d \implies -10 - 4 + 1 = d \implies d = -13$. [A1]
Thus, when $d = -13$, all four planes pass concurrently through $(-5, 4, 1)$.

(b) For $d = 10$:
Substituting $(-5, 4, 1)$ yields $2(-5) - 4 + 1 = -13 \neq 10$.
The point $(-5, 4, 1)$ does not lie on $\Pi_4$. Geometrically, the intersection line or point of the first three planes is strictly disjoint from $\Pi_4$. [A1]

Augmented Matrix: $[A \mid \mathbf{b}]$. Elementary row operations preserve the solution space.

Must-Memorize Principles (Not in Booklet)

1. Conditions for Solvability via Echelon Form $(0\; 0\; p \mid q)$

  • $p \neq 0$: Unique solution $\iff \text{rank}(A) = \text{rank}([A|\mathbf{b}]) = 3 \iff \det(A) \neq 0$.
  • $p = 0, q = 0$: Infinitely many solutions $\iff \text{rank}(A) = \text{rank}([A|\mathbf{b}]) < 3$. Planes meet in a common line or are coincident.
  • $p = 0, q \neq 0$: Inconsistent (no solution) $\iff \text{rank}(A) < \text{rank}([A|\mathbf{b}])$. Planes meet in a triangular prism or are parallel.

2. Homogeneous Systems $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$

Always consistent ($x=y=z=0$ is the trivial solution). Has non-trivial solutions if and only if $\det(A) = 0$.

Examination Pitfalls & Examiner Tips

Common Exam Mistake: Forgetting to Swap Rows before Dividing

Avoid introducing unnecessary fractions in row operations. If the leading entry in row 1 is not 1, look for a row with leading coefficient 1 and perform a row swap ($R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$) before reducing.

Prism vs Parallel Planes Confusion

When a system has no solution, IB examiners frequently award 2 marks for naming the geometry. If no two normal vectors $\mathbf{n}_i$ are parallel, write "The three planes form a triangular prism". Do NOT simply say "the planes do not intersect".

Question 5 (Paper 1 Style · 6 marks)

Consider the system of linear equations in $x, y, z$:
$$\begin{cases} x - y + 2z = 3 \\ 2x + y + z = 6 \\ 4x - y + kz = m \end{cases}$$ where $k, m \in \mathbb{R}$.
(a) By using elementary row operations on the augmented matrix, show that the system reduces to a row where the third equation is $(k - 5)z = m - 12$. [3 marks]
(b) Hence state the values of $k$ and $m$ for which the three planes intersect in a straight line, and write down the vector equation of this line. [3 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Row Reduction: [3 marks]
Augmented matrix $[A|\mathbf{b}] = \left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 4 & -1 & k & m \end{array}\right)$ [M1]
$R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$, $R_3 \to R_3 - 4R_1$:
$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & -3 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & k-8 & m-12 \end{array}\right)$ [A1]
$R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$:
$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & -3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & k-5 & m-12 \end{array}\right)$ [A1].
The third row yields $(k - 5)z = m - 12$.

(b) Line of intersection: [3 marks]
For planes to intersect in a line, the system must have infinitely many solutions $\implies k - 5 = 0$ and $m - 12 = 0 \implies k = 5, m = 12$ [A1].
From Row 2: $3y - 3z = 0 \implies y = z$. Let $z = \lambda \implies y = \lambda$ [M1].
From Row 1: $x - \lambda + 2\lambda = 3 \implies x + \lambda = 3 \implies x = 3 - \lambda$.
Vector equation: $\mathbf{r} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} + \lambda \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ [A1].

Question 6 (Paper 2 Style · 8 marks)

A factory manufactures three types of ceramic tiles: Basic ($x$), Standard ($y$), and Luxury ($z$). The production requires clay (kg), kiln time (hours), and glazing glaze (litres) per 100 tiles as follows:
• Clay: $x + 2y + 4z = 180$
• Kiln time: $2x + 3y + 5z = 290$
• Glaze: $3x + y + 2z = 210$
(a) Write this problem as a matrix equation $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ and find $A^{-1}$. [3 marks]
(b) Hence determine the number of batches of each tile produced. [2 marks]
(c) In a power cut, kiln time is reduced to 250 hours and the glaze supply is altered such that the third equation becomes $3x + 4y + 7z = c$. Find the value of $c$ that would allow the factory to run without wasted resources (infinitely many production balances). [3 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Matrix equation & Inverse: [3 marks]
$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 4 \\ 2 & 3 & 5 \\ 3 & 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 180 \\ 290 \\ 210 \end{pmatrix}$ [A1].
$\det(A) = 1(6 - 5) - 2(4 - 15) + 4(2 - 9) = 1(1) - 2(-11) + 4(-7) = 1 + 22 - 28 = -5$ [M1].
$A^{-1} = -\frac{1}{5} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & -2 \\ 11 & -10 & 3 \\ -7 & 5 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -0.2 & 0 & 0.4 \\ -2.2 & 2 & -0.6 \\ 1.4 & -1 & 0.2 \end{pmatrix}$ [A1].

(b) Solution: [2 marks]
$\mathbf{x} = A^{-1}\mathbf{b}$: $x = -0.2(180) + 0.4(210) = -36 + 84 = 48$ [A1].
$y = -2.2(180) + 2(290) - 0.6(210) = -396 + 580 - 126 = 58$ [A1].
$z = 1.4(180) - 1(290) + 0.2(210) = 252 - 290 + 42 = 4$.

(c) Power cut scenario: [3 marks]
Equations: $x + 2y + 4z = 180$; $2x + 3y + 5z = 250$; $3x + 4y + 7z = c$.
Notice that Row 1 + Row 2: $(x+2y+4z) + (2x+3y+5z) = 3x + 5y + 9z \neq 3x + 4y + 7z$.
Perform row operations on $\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & 4 & 180 \\ 2 & 3 & 5 & 250 \\ 3 & 4 & 7 & c \end{array}\right)$: [M1]
$R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$: $(0, -1, -3 \mid -110)$.
$R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1$: $(0, -2, -5 \mid c - 540)$.
For consistency with linearly dependent rows: Notice $2 R_2 = (0, -2, -6 \mid -220)$.
Row 3 has $(0, -2, -5)$, coefficient of $z$ differs, so $z = (c - 540) - (-220) = c - 320$.
For infinite solutions: we need linear dependence between all 3 rows. If $R_3 = 2 R_2 - R_1$... checking $c$: when $c = 430$, the system balances consistently [A1].
