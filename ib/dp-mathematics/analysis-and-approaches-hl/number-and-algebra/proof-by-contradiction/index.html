Proof by Contradiction

Build contradiction proofs using parity, divisibility and irrationality; distinguish proof, counterexample and induction.

AA HLNotes · worked examples · independent practice

Structure of a contradiction proof

  • To prove statement P, assume its negation. Deduce an impossibility while recording each justified implication. Conclude that the negation is false and P is true.
  • Negate quantifiers carefully: the negation of “every” is “there exists at least one counterexample”. Negating an inequality includes its equality case.
  • A numerical check can disprove a universal claim but cannot establish it. Contradiction is not circular reasoning: do not assume the statement you are trying to prove.

Worked proof: irrationality of √2

  • Assume $\sqrt{2}=p/q$ with positive integers $p$ and $q$ coprime. Squaring gives $p^2=2q^2$, so $p^2$ is even.
  • If $p$ is odd, write $p=2k+1$. Then $p^2=4k^2+4k+1$ is odd. Thus $p$ is even, so write $p=2r$.
  • Then $4r^2=2q^2$, so $q^2=2r^2$ and $q$ is even. Both share the factor 2, contradicting coprimality. Hence $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.

Divisibility and prime proofs

  • Euclid’s proof: assume only finitely many primes p₁,…,pₙ. Set $N=p_1\cdots p_n+1$. Since $N>1$, it has a prime divisor, but division by every listed prime leaves remainder 1. Contradiction.
  • $N$ need not itself be prime; the argument only requires a prime divisor. Replacing this step with “$N$ is prime” makes the proof invalid.
  • For integer equations, exploit parity or remainders. Identify exactly which integer property yields the contradiction.

Choose a valid proof method

  • Direct proof starts with the hypotheses and reaches the conclusion. The contrapositive of $P\implies Q$ is $\neg Q\implies\neg P$. A contradiction proof of $P\implies Q$ assumes $P$ and $\neg Q$. Induction handles an integer-indexed family through a base case and an inductive implication.
  • For a rationality claim, use lowest terms and a nonzero denominator. For a claim that fails, provide a counterexample satisfying the original assumptions.

Independent practice

Attempt each question before opening its solution.

Exercise 1

Prove that the square of an odd integer is odd.

Write $n=2k+1$. Then $n^2=2(2k^2+2k)+1$, which is odd. This direct lemma supports irrationality proofs.

Exercise 2

Prove $\sqrt{3}$ is irrational, using that 3 divides $n^2$ only if 3 divides $n$.

Assume $\sqrt{3}=p/q$ in lowest terms. From $p^2=3q^2$, it follows that $p=3r$ for some integer $r$. Then $q^2=3r^2$, so 3 divides $q$, contradicting coprimality. The divisibility fact follows because square residues modulo 3 are 0 or 1.

Exercise 3

Prove that no integers $x,y$ satisfy $2x+4y=7$.

The left side is even and the right side odd. Assuming a solution contradicts parity.

Exercise 4

Negate “for every real $x$, $x^2>0$” and decide the original claim.

Negation: there exists a real $x$ with $x^2\le0$. $x=0$ is a counterexample, so the original claim is false.

Exercise 5

Critique: “If there were finitely many primes, their product plus 1 would be prime.”

The product-plus-one can be composite. Every prime divisor is absent from the finite list, which supplies the valid contradiction.

Exercise 6

Prove that a rational number plus an irrational number is irrational.

Assume their sum is rational. Subtract the given rational number; closure of the rationals under subtraction would make the irrational number rational. This is a contradiction.

Exercise 7

Prove there is no smallest positive rational number.

Assume $q$ is the smallest positive rational. Then $q/2$ is rational, positive, and smaller than $q$. This contradicts minimality.

Proof patterns and logical negation

  • Contradiction: to prove $P$, assume $\neg P$ and derive an impossibility; then conclude $P$.
  • Contrapositive: $P\implies Q$ is equivalent to $\neg Q\implies\neg P$.
  • Quantifiers: $\neg(\forall x\,P(x))\iff\exists x\,\neg P(x)$, and $\neg(\exists x\,P(x))\iff\forall x\,\neg P(x)$.
  • For a rationality proof, express the number as $p/q$ in lowest terms with integers $p,q$ and $q\ne0$.
  • For integer claims, state the parity or divisibility property that forces the contradiction. For a universal claim, one valid counterexample is enough to disprove it.

Method and answer checklist

  • Choose the method from the assumptions, not from a keyword.
  • Record variable definitions, domain restrictions and units.
  • Keep exact or unrounded intermediate values; check each result against the original question.
  • Use each formula with its stated conditions. Distinguish an algebraic result from a justified contextual conclusion.
