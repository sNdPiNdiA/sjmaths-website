Volumes of Revolution (x and y Axes)

Calculate volumes of solids generated by revolving regions $2\pi$ radians about coordinate axes: $V = \pi \int [f(x)]^2 dx$ around the $x$-axis, $V = \pi \int [g(y)]^2 dy$ around the $y$-axis, and the washer method $V = \pi \int (f^2 - g^2) dx$.

1. The Geometric Mechanics of Volume of Revolution

A solid of revolution is generated when a two-dimensional planar region is revolved through $360^\circ$ ($2\pi$ radians) about a fixed coplanar straight line called the axis of revolution.

In the IB DP AA HL curriculum, volumes are evaluated strictly by the Method of Slicing (Circular Disks and Annular Washers) perpendicular to the axis of rotation:

  • Differential Element: Slicing a solid perpendicular to its axis produces a circular cross-section of radius $r$ and infinitesimal thickness ($dx$ or $dy$).
  • Differential Volume: $dV = \text{Area of Circle} \times \text{Thickness} = \pi [r]^2 \, dx$ (or $\pi [r]^2 \, dy$).
  • Riemann Sum Limit: The total volume is the limit of the sum of $n$ circular slices as thickness $\Delta x \to 0$: $$V = \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^n \pi [r(x_i^*)]^2 \Delta x = \pi \int_a^b [r(x)]^2 \, dx$$

2. Revolution About Coordinate Axes

A. Rotation About the x-axis (Topic 5.11 SL & HL)

When the plane region bounded by $y = f(x)$, the $x$-axis, and vertical lines $x = a$ and $x = b$ is revolved about the $x$-axis:

$$V_x = \pi \int_a^b [f(x)]^2 \, dx = \pi \int_a^b y^2 \, dx$$

Crucial Integrand Identities: Slicing produces squared functions that often require trigonometric or algebraic identities:

  • $\sin^2(kx) = \frac{1 - \cos(2kx)}{2}$ and $\cos^2(kx) = \frac{1 + \cos(2kx)}{2}$
  • $(e^{kx})^2 = e^{2kx}$ (remember that powers multiply: $(e^x)^2 = e^{2x}$, NOT $e^{x^2}$!)
  • $\left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}} \right)^2 = \frac{1}{x^2 + a^2} \implies \int \to \frac{1}{a}\arctan\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)$

B. Rotation About the y-axis (Topic 5.14 AHL Exclusive)

When the plane region bounded by the curve, the $y$-axis, and horizontal lines $y = c$ and $y = d$ is revolved $360^\circ$ about the $y$-axis:

$$V_y = \pi \int_c^d [g(y)]^2 \, dy = \pi \int_c^d x^2 \, dy$$

Mandatory 3-Step Protocol for y-axis Rotations:

  1. Invert the Function: Solve the equation $y = f(x)$ explicitly for $x$ in terms of $y$: $x = g(y)$.
  2. Convert Limits: Ensure limits are $y$-values ($c = f(a), d = f(b)$). Never evaluate $dy$ across $x$-limits!
  3. Square and Integrate: Evaluate $\pi \int_c^d [g(y)]^2 dy$.

3. The Washer Method: Region Between Two Intersecting Curves

When the rotated region is bounded between an outer boundary $y_1 = f(x)$ and an inner boundary $y_2 = g(x)$ with $|f(x)| \ge |g(x)|$, cross-sections perpendicular to the axis form annular rings (washers):

$$\text{Area of Washer} = \pi R^2 - \pi r^2 = \pi \left( R^2 - r^2 \right)$$ $$V_x = \pi \int_a^b \left( [f(x)]^2 - [g(x)]^2 \right) dx$$

Fatal Examination Trap: Difference of Squares vs Square of Difference

Never write $\pi \int (f(x) - g(x))^2 dx$! The area of a washer with outer radius $R$ and inner hole $r$ is $\pi(R^2 - r^2)$, NOT $\pi(R - r)^2$. Writing $(f - g)^2$ loses all method and accuracy marks instantly in IB markschemes!

4. Rotations About Shifted (Non-Coordinate) Lines

IB AA HL frequently examines solids revolved around horizontal lines $y = k$ or vertical lines $x = h$:

A. Rotation About a Horizontal Line $y = k$

The radius of each circular slice is the perpendicular distance from the curve to the line $y = k$:

$$r(x) = |f(x) - k| \implies V = \pi \int_a^b (f(x) - k)^2 \, dx$$

If bounded between two curves $f(x)$ (outer) and $g(x)$ (inner) relative to $y = k$:

$$V = \pi \int_a^b \left( (f(x) - k)^2 - (g(x) - k)^2 \right) dx$$

B. Rotation About a Vertical Line $x = h$

The radius is the horizontal distance from the curve $x = g(y)$ to the vertical axis $x = h$:

$$r(y) = |g(y) - h| \implies V = \pi \int_c^d (g(y) - h)^2 \, dy$$

5. Dynamic Modeling & Fluid Rates of Change

In Paper 2 / Section B applied questions, containers (vases, goblets, bowls) are modeled by revolving curves about an axis. If fluid fills the container at rate $\frac{dV}{dt}$:

By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, the volume at liquid level $h$ is $V(h) = \pi \int_0^h [r(y)]^2 dy$. Differentiating with respect to $h$ yields:

$$\frac{dV}{dh} = \pi [r(h)]^2 = \text{Surface Area of Liquid}$$

Connecting volume rate of change to water rising speed using the chain rule:

$$\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{dV}{dh} \cdot \frac{dh}{dt} \implies \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{1}{\pi [r(h)]^2} \frac{dV}{dt}$$

6. The 6 Classic IB Exam Question Archetypes

Every volume of revolution problem in IB DP Mathematics AA HL fits into one of these six primary archetypes:

Archetype Mathematical Setup Primary Exam Strategy Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls
1. Single Curve x-axis Rotation $y = f(x)$ on $[a, b]$ about $x$-axis Square $y$ algebraically; apply double angle identities for $\sin^2 x$ or $\cos^2 x$. Prepend $\pi$. Omitting $\pi$ in the final answer; squaring $e^x$ as $e^{x^2}$ instead of $e^{2x}$.
2. Single Curve y-axis Rotation $y = f(x)$ on $x \in [a, b]$ about $y$-axis Invert to find $x = g(y)$, calculate $y$-limits $c = f(a), d = f(b)$, evaluate $\pi \int_c^d [g(y)]^2 dy$. Integrating $[g(y)]^2$ across $x$-limits ($a$ and $b$) instead of $y$-limits ($c$ and $d$).
3. Washer Method Between Curves Region between $y_1$ and $y_2$ rotated $360^\circ$ Find intersection points; establish $y_1 \ge y_2$; evaluate $\pi \int (y_1^2 - y_2^2) dx$. Integrating $(y_1 - y_2)^2$ (fatal error); subtracting in reverse order producing negative volume.
4. Shifted Axis of Rotation Revolving about $y = k$ or $x = h$ Identify radius as distance $|f(x) - k|$; expand $(f(x) - k)^2$ before integrating. Adding $k$ instead of subtracting ($f(x) + k$); confusing horizontal ($y=k$) and vertical ($x=h$) axes.
5. Container Modeling & Fluid Rates Revolving cross-section with fluid pumped at rate $\frac{dV}{dt}$ Express $V(h) = \pi \int_0^h x^2 dy$, compute $\frac{dV}{dh} = \pi [x(h)]^2$, apply $\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{dV/dt}{\pi [x(h)]^2}$. Attempting complicated integral differentiation instead of applying FTC $\frac{d}{dh}\int_0^h = \text{integrand}$.
6. Theoretical Solid Derivations "Show that volume of sphere is $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$" or cone $\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$ Write equation of generating 2D curve (line $y = \frac{r}{h}x$ or circle $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$), set bounds, evaluate. Failing to clearly state the 2D bounding equations or line slope before integrating.

7. Fully Worked Exemplar Solutions (IB Exam Standard)

Worked Example 1: Trigonometric x-axis Rotation (Paper 1 Standard)

The curve $y = 2\sin(x)$ from $x = 0$ to $x = \frac{\pi}{2}$ is rotated $360^\circ$ about the $x$-axis.
Find the exact volume of the solid generated, expressing your answer in the form $k\pi^2$ where $k \in \mathbb{Q}$.

Solution:

1. State the formula for volume of revolution about the $x$-axis: [M1]

$$V = \pi \int_0^{\pi/2} y^2 \, dx = \pi \int_0^{\pi/2} [2\sin(x)]^2 \, dx = 4\pi \int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^2(x) \, dx \quad \text{[A1]}$$

2. Apply the double angle identity $\sin^2(x) = \frac{1 - \cos(2x)}{2}$: [M1]

$$V = 4\pi \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{1 - \cos(2x)}{2} \, dx = 2\pi \int_0^{\pi/2} (1 - \cos(2x)) \, dx$$

3. Integrate term-by-term: [A1]

$$V = 2\pi \left[ x - \frac{1}{2}\sin(2x) \right]_0^{\pi/2}$$

4. Evaluate at the boundaries: [M1]

$$\text{Upper limit } \left(x = \frac{\pi}{2}\right): \frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{1}{2}\sin(\pi) = \frac{\pi}{2} - 0 = \frac{\pi}{2}$$ $$\text{Lower limit } (x = 0): 0 - \frac{1}{2}\sin(0) = 0$$ $$V = 2\pi \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - 0 \right) = \pi^2 \quad \text{[A1]}$$

Conclusion: The exact volume is $\pi^2$ (with $k = 1$).

Worked Example 2: Inverting Functions for y-axis Rotation (Paper 1 Standard)

Consider the curve $y = \ln(x - 1)$ for $x > 1$. The finite region bounded by the curve, the $y$-axis, the line $y = 0$, and the line $y = 1$ is rotated $360^\circ$ about the $y$-axis.
Determine the exact volume of the solid formed.

Solution:

1. Invert the equation to express $x$ in terms of $y$: [M1]

$$y = \ln(x - 1) \implies e^y = x - 1 \implies x = e^y + 1 \quad \text{[A1]}$$

2. Set up the volume integral with respect to $y$ between $y = 0$ and $y = 1$: [M1]

$$V = \pi \int_0^1 x^2 \, dy = \pi \int_0^1 (e^y + 1)^2 \, dy$$

3. Expand the binomial integrand: [M1]

$$(e^y + 1)^2 = (e^y)^2 + 2e^y + 1 = e^{2y} + 2e^y + 1$$

4. Integrate term-by-term: [A1]

$$V = \pi \left[ \frac{1}{2}e^{2y} + 2e^y + y \right]_0^1$$

5. Substitute limits carefully: [M1]

$$\text{Upper: } \frac{1}{2}e^2 + 2e + 1$$ $$\text{Lower: } \frac{1}{2}e^0 + 2e^0 + 0 = \frac{1}{2}(1) + 2(1) + 0 = \frac{5}{2}$$ $$V = \pi \left[ \left(\frac{1}{2}e^2 + 2e + 1\right) - \frac{5}{2} \right] = \pi \left( \frac{1}{2}e^2 + 2e - \frac{3}{2} \right) = \frac{\pi}{2}(e^2 + 4e - 3) \quad \text{[A1]}$$

Worked Example 3: The Washer Method (Paper 2 Standard)

The finite region $R$ is bounded by the parabola $y = 4 - x^2$ and the line $y = x + 2$.
(a) Find the $x$-coordinates of the points of intersection of the two curves. [2 marks]
(b) The region $R$ is rotated through $2\pi$ radians about the $x$-axis. Find the exact volume of the resulting solid. [5 marks]

Solution:

(a) Points of intersection:

$$4 - x^2 = x + 2 \implies x^2 + x - 2 = 0 \quad \text{[M1]}$$ $$(x + 2)(x - 1) = 0 \implies x = -2 \quad \text{and} \quad x = 1 \quad \text{[A1]}$$

(b) Washer Method Volume:

On the interval $[-2, 1]$, $4 - x^2 \ge x + 2$, so $y_{\text{outer}} = 4 - x^2$ and $y_{\text{inner}} = x + 2$. [R1]

$$V = \pi \int_{-2}^1 \left( [y_{\text{outer}}]^2 - [y_{\text{inner}}]^2 \right) dx = \pi \int_{-2}^1 \left( (4 - x^2)^2 - (x + 2)^2 \right) dx \quad \text{[M1]}$$

Expand both terms: [M1]

$$(4 - x^2)^2 = 16 - 8x^2 + x^4$$ $$(x + 2)^2 = x^2 + 4x + 4$$ $$\text{Integrand } = (16 - 8x^2 + x^4) - (x^2 + 4x + 4) = x^4 - 9x^2 - 4x + 12 \quad \text{[A1]}$$

Integrate: [M1]

$$V = \pi \left[ \frac{x^5}{5} - 3x^3 - 2x^2 + 12x \right]_{-2}^1$$

Evaluate limits:

$$\text{At } x = 1: \frac{1}{5} - 3 - 2 + 12 = \frac{1}{5} + 7 = \frac{36}{5}$$ $$\text{At } x = -2: \frac{-32}{5} - 3(-8) - 2(4) + 12(-2) = -\frac{32}{5} + 24 - 8 - 24 = -\frac{32}{5} - 8 = -\frac{72}{5}$$ $$V = \pi \left( \frac{36}{5} - \left(-\frac{72}{5}\right) \right) = \pi \left(\frac{108}{5}\right) = \frac{108\pi}{5} \approx 67.86 \quad \text{[A1]}$$

Worked Example 4: Shifted Axis Rotation & Dynamic Fluid Rate (Paper 2 Section B)

A wine glass bowl is formed by revolving the curve $y = x^2$ from $y = 0$ to $y = 4$ about the line $y = 4$, where $x \ge 0$ and dimensions are in centimetres.
(a) Find the volume of the glass bowl. [4 marks]
(b) If wine is poured into the bowl at a steady rate of $12\text{ cm}^3/\text{s}$, calculate the rate of change of the wine depth when the depth is $2\text{ cm}$. [4 marks]

Solution:

(a) Shifted Axis Volume:

The axis of rotation is the horizontal line $y = 4$. For $x \in [0, 2]$, the curve is $y = x^2$.
The radius from the line $y = 4$ down to the curve is $r(x) = 4 - x^2$. [M1]

$$V = \pi \int_0^2 (4 - x^2)^2 \, dx = \pi \int_0^2 (16 - 8x^2 + x^4) \, dx \quad \text{[M1]}$$ $$V = \pi \left[ 16x - \frac{8}{3}x^3 + \frac{x^5}{5} \right]_0^2 = \pi \left( 32 - \frac{64}{3} + \frac{32}{5} \right) \quad \text{[A1]}$$ $$\text{Common denominator } 15: \frac{480 - 320 + 96}{15} = \frac{256}{15}$$ $$V = \frac{256\pi}{15}\text{ cm}^3 \approx 53.6\text{ cm}^3 \quad \text{[A1]}$$

(b) Related Rates of Change:

Let $h$ be the depth of wine measured from the bottom of the bowl ($y = 0$).
The liquid surface at height $h$ is a circular disk with radius $R(h) = x = \sqrt{h}$. [M1]
Cross-sectional surface area at depth $h$: $A(h) = \pi [R(h)]^2 = \pi (\sqrt{h})^2 = \pi h$. [A1]
By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, $\frac{dV}{dh} = A(h) = \pi h$.
Apply the Chain Rule: [M1]

$$\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{dV}{dh} \cdot \frac{dh}{dt} \implies 12 = (\pi h) \cdot \frac{dh}{dt}$$

At depth $h = 2\text{ cm}$:

$$12 = 2\pi \cdot \frac{dh}{dt} \implies \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{12}{2\pi} = \frac{6}{\pi}\text{ cm/s} \approx 1.91\text{ cm/s} \quad \text{[A1]}$$

Worked Example 5: Volume of Revolution About the y-axis with Inverted Function (Paper 2 Standard)

The curve $y = \ln x$ from $y = 0$ to $y = 2$ is rotated through $2\pi$ radians about the $y$-axis.
Find the exact volume of the solid generated. [5 marks]

Solution:

1. Express $x$ in terms of $y$:

$$y = \ln x \implies x = e^y \quad [M1]$$

2. State volume of revolution formula about the $y$-axis:

$$V = \pi \int_c^d [x(y)]^2\,dy = \pi \int_0^2 (e^y)^2\,dy \quad [M1]$$ $$= \pi \int_0^2 e^{2y}\,dy \quad [A1]$$

3. Integrate:

$$V = \pi \left[ \frac{e^{2y}}{2} \right]_0^2 = \frac{\pi}{2}\left( e^{2(2)} - e^{2(0)} \right) \quad [M1]$$ $$= \frac{\pi}{2}(e^4 - 1) \approx 84.18 \quad [A1]$$

Examiner Commentary: When rotating about the $y$-axis, always integrate with respect to $y$ between $y$-limits. Integrating $\pi \int (\ln x)^2 dx$ would calculate rotation about the $x$-axis!

Practice Exercises (10 Tiered Questions)

Complete these 10 exam-style questions spanning core skills, standard exam problems, and advanced HL challenges. Open each question to inspect the full step-by-step IB markscheme.

Exercise 1 (Foundational: Standard x-axis Rotation)

Find the volume of the solid generated when the curve $y = \sqrt{x}$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 4$ is revolved $360^\circ$ about the $x$-axis.

Show Full Solution

Formula for rotation about the $x$-axis: $V = \pi \int_a^b y^2 \, dx$. [M1]

$$V = \pi \int_0^4 (\sqrt{x})^2 \, dx = \pi \int_0^4 x \, dx \quad \text{[A1]}$$ $$V = \pi \left[ \frac{1}{2}x^2 \right]_0^4 = \pi \left( \frac{16}{2} - 0 \right) = 8\pi \quad \text{[A1]}$$
Exercise 2 (Foundational: Exponential Rotation)

Find the volume of the solid generated by revolving the region bounded by $y = e^x$, the lines $x = 0$, $x = 2$, and the $x$-axis about the $x$-axis.

Show Full Solution

Set up the integral: $V = \pi \int_0^2 (e^x)^2 \, dx$. [M1]

Using index laws $(e^x)^2 = e^{2x}$: [A1]

$$V = \pi \int_0^2 e^{2x} \, dx = \pi \left[ \frac{1}{2}e^{2x} \right]_0^2 = \frac{\pi}{2}(e^4 - e^0) = \frac{\pi}{2}(e^4 - 1) \quad \text{[A1]}$$

Examiner Pitfall: Do not write $e^{x^2}$. Powers multiply: $(e^x)^2 = e^{2x}$.

Exercise 3 (Foundational: y-axis Parabolic Rotation)

The region enclosed by $y = x^2$, the $y$-axis, and the line $y = 4$ is revolved $360^\circ$ about the $y$-axis. Calculate the volume.

Show Full Solution

Express $x^2$ in terms of $y$: $y = x^2 \implies x^2 = y$. [M1]

Set up the integral with respect to $y$ from $y = 0$ to $y = 4$: [M1]

$$V = \pi \int_0^4 x^2 \, dy = \pi \int_0^4 y \, dy$$ $$V = \pi \left[ \frac{1}{2}y^2 \right]_0^4 = \pi \left( \frac{16}{2} \right) = 8\pi \quad \text{[A1]}$$
Exercise 4 (Intermediate: Trigonometric Double Angle Rotation)

Find the volume formed when the region bounded by $y = \sin x$ from $x = 0$ to $x = \pi$ and the $x$-axis is rotated $360^\circ$ about the $x$-axis.

Show Full Solution

Set up the volume integral: $V = \pi \int_0^\pi \sin^2 x \, dx$. [M1]

Substitute double angle identity $\sin^2 x = \frac{1 - \cos 2x}{2}$: [M1]

$$V = \pi \int_0^\pi \frac{1 - \cos 2x}{2} \, dx = \frac{\pi}{2} \left[ x - \frac{1}{2}\sin 2x \right]_0^\pi \quad \text{[A1]}$$

Substitute limits:

$$V = \frac{\pi}{2} \left( \pi - \frac{1}{2}\sin(2\pi) - (0 - 0) \right) = \frac{\pi}{2}(\pi - 0) = \frac{\pi^2}{2} \quad \text{[A1]}$$
Exercise 5 (Intermediate: Washer Method Between Line and Parabola)

The region between $y = x$ and $y = x^2$ is revolved $360^\circ$ about the $x$-axis. Find the volume of the resulting solid.

Show Full Solution

Points of intersection: $x = x^2 \implies x(x - 1) = 0 \implies x = 0, 1$. [A1]

On $[0, 1]$, $x \ge x^2$, so $y_{\text{outer}} = x$ and $y_{\text{inner}} = x^2$. [R1]

$$V = \pi \int_0^1 \left( [x]^2 - [x^2]^2 \right) dx = \pi \int_0^1 (x^2 - x^4) \, dx \quad \text{[M1]}$$ $$V = \pi \left[ \frac{1}{3}x^3 - \frac{1}{5}x^5 \right]_0^1 = \pi \left( \frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{5} \right) = \frac{2\pi}{15} \quad \text{[A1]}$$
Exercise 6 (Intermediate: Logarithmic Curve Revolved About y-axis)

Find the volume generated when the curve $y = \ln x$, from $y = 0$ to $y = 2$, is rotated $360^\circ$ about the $y$-axis.

Show Full Solution

Invert to express $x$ in terms of $y$: $y = \ln x \implies x = e^y$. [M1]

Set up integral with respect to $y$: [M1]

$$V = \pi \int_0^2 x^2 \, dy = \pi \int_0^2 (e^y)^2 \, dy = \pi \int_0^2 e^{2y} \, dy$$ $$V = \pi \left[ \frac{1}{2}e^{2y} \right]_0^2 = \frac{\pi}{2}(e^4 - e^0) = \frac{\pi}{2}(e^4 - 1) \quad \text{[A1]}$$
Exercise 7 (Exam Standard: Rational Curve Yielding Arctangent)

The region enclosed by $y = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}$, the $x$-axis, $x = 0$, and $x = 1$ is rotated $360^\circ$ about the $x$-axis. Find the volume.

Show Full Solution

Square the function: $y^2 = \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}} \right)^2 = \frac{1}{x^2 + 1}$. [M1]

$$V = \pi \int_0^1 y^2 \, dx = \pi \int_0^1 \frac{1}{x^2 + 1} \, dx \quad \text{[A1]}$$

Recognize standard arctan antiderivative: [M1]

$$V = \pi [\arctan x]_0^1 = \pi (\arctan(1) - \arctan(0)) = \pi \left( \frac{\pi}{4} - 0 \right) = \frac{\pi^2}{4} \quad \text{[A1]}$$
Exercise 8 (Advanced: Shifted Horizontal Line of Revolution)

Find the volume generated by rotating the region enclosed by $y = x^2$ and $y = 4$ about the line $y = 4$.

Show Full Solution

The line of rotation is $y = 4$. Radius from axis to curve: $r(x) = 4 - x^2$. [M1]

Limits: $x^2 = 4 \implies x = -2$ to $x = 2$. By symmetry: $2\pi \int_0^2$. [R1]

$$V = \pi \int_{-2}^2 (4 - x^2)^2 \, dx = 2\pi \int_0^2 (16 - 8x^2 + x^4) \, dx \quad \text{[M1]}$$ $$V = 2\pi \left[ 16x - \frac{8}{3}x^3 + \frac{1}{5}x^5 \right]_0^2 = 2\pi \left( 32 - \frac{64}{3} + \frac{32}{5} \right) \quad \text{[A1]}$$ $$32 - \frac{64}{3} + \frac{32}{5} = \frac{480 - 320 + 96}{15} = \frac{256}{15} \implies V = \frac{512\pi}{15} \quad \text{[A1]}$$
Exercise 9 (Advanced: Goblet Modeling & Water Level Rate)

A glass goblet is modeled by revolving $y = 2 + \sqrt{x}$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 9$ about the $x$-axis (all dimensions in cm).
(a) Find the total volume of the solid in $\text{cm}^3$.
(b) Water fills the goblet at $10\text{ cm}^3/\text{s}$. Find the rate of change of depth $h$ when $h = 4\text{ cm}$.

Show Full Solution

(a) Volume of solid:

$$V = \pi \int_0^9 (2 + \sqrt{x})^2 \, dx = \pi \int_0^9 (4 + 4x^{1/2} + x) \, dx \quad \text{[M1]}$$ $$V = \pi \left[ 4x + \frac{8}{3}x^{3/2} + \frac{x^2}{2} \right]_0^9 = \pi \left( 36 + \frac{8}{3}(27) + \frac{81}{2} \right) \quad \text{[A1]}$$ $$V = \pi (36 + 72 + 40.5) = 148.5\pi = \frac{297\pi}{2}\text{ cm}^3 \quad \text{[A1]}$$

(b) Related rates for depth $h$:

By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus: $\frac{dV}{dh} = \pi [2 + \sqrt{h}]^2$. [M1]

At $h = 4$: $\frac{dV}{dh} = \pi (2 + \sqrt{4})^2 = \pi (4)^2 = 16\pi$. [A1]

Using chain rule $\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{dV}{dh} \cdot \frac{dh}{dt}$: [M1]

$$10 = 16\pi \cdot \frac{dh}{dt} \implies \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{10}{16\pi} = \frac{5}{8\pi}\text{ cm/s} \approx 0.199\text{ cm/s} \quad \text{[A1]}$$
Exercise 10 (Advanced: Derivation of the Torus Volume)

A circle of radius $r$ centered at $(R, 0)$ where $R > r$ has equation $(x - R)^2 + y^2 = r^2$. Rotating this circle $360^\circ$ about the $y$-axis generates a torus. Prove using calculus that the volume of the torus is $V = 2\pi^2 R r^2$.

Show Full Solution

Solve circle equation for $x$: $(x - R)^2 = r^2 - y^2 \implies x = R \pm \sqrt{r^2 - y^2}$. [M1]

Outer radius: $x_2 = R + \sqrt{r^2 - y^2}$; Inner radius: $x_1 = R - \sqrt{r^2 - y^2}$.

Washer difference of squares: [M1]

$$x_2^2 - x_1^2 = (x_2 - x_1)(x_2 + x_1) = \left(2\sqrt{r^2 - y^2}\right)(2R) = 4R\sqrt{r^2 - y^2} \quad \text{[A1]}$$

Volume integral across $y \in [-r, r]$: [M1]

$$V = \pi \int_{-r}^r (x_2^2 - x_1^2) \, dy = 4\pi R \int_{-r}^r \sqrt{r^2 - y^2} \, dy$$

Geometric evaluation: $\int_{-r}^r \sqrt{r^2 - y^2} dy$ is the area of a semicircle of radius $r$, which is $\frac{1}{2}\pi r^2$: [R1]

$$V = 4\pi R \left( \frac{1}{2}\pi r^2 \right) = 2\pi^2 R r^2 \quad \text{[AG]}$$

Official IB Formula Booklet References (Topics 5.11 & 5.14)

Syllabus 5.11: Revolution about the x-axis

$$V = \pi \int_a^b y^2 \, dx = \pi \int_a^b [f(x)]^2 \, dx$$

Syllabus 5.14 AHL: Revolution about the y-axis

$$V = \pi \int_c^d x^2 \, dy = \pi \int_c^d [g(y)]^2 \, dy$$

Condition: The equation must be rearranged to express $x$ as a function of $y$ ($x = g(y)$), and limits $c$ and $d$ must be $y$-coordinates.

Essential Non-Booklet Formulas & Templates to Memorize

1. The Washer Method (Hollow Solids)

• About $x$-axis: $V = \pi \int_a^b \left( [y_{\text{outer}}]^2 - [y_{\text{inner}}]^2 \right) dx$

• About $y$-axis: $V = \pi \int_c^d \left( [x_{\text{outer}}]^2 - [x_{\text{inner}}]^2 \right) dy$

2. Shifted Axes of Revolution

• About horizontal line $y = k$: $V = \pi \int_a^b (f(x) - k)^2 \, dx$

• About vertical line $x = h$: $V = \pi \int_c^d (g(y) - h)^2 \, dy$

3. Dynamic Fluid Rates

$$\frac{dV}{dh} = \pi [r(h)]^2 \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{1}{\pi [r(h)]^2} \frac{dV}{dt}$$

Examination Pitfalls & Examiner Traps

Forgetting the Multiplier $\pi$

The most pervasive error across all IB papers is omitting the constant $\pi$. Double-check that your volume expression begins with $\pi$.

The Washer Square Error: $(f - g)^2$ vs $(f^2 - g^2)$

Never square the difference of functions! The area of a washer ring is $\pi R^2 - \pi r^2 = \pi (R^2 - r^2)$. Calculating $[f(x) - g(x)]^2$ results in an immediate loss of all method marks.

Mixing $x$ and $y$ in y-axis Rotations

When rotating about the $y$-axis, the entire integral must be in terms of $y$. Integrating $x^2$ with respect to $y$ requires replacing $x^2$ with $[g(y)]^2$, and the integration limits must be $y$-coordinates.

Examination Paper 1/2 Question 5 (FRQ) · Dual Axis Comparison

Let $R$ be the region enclosed by $y = e^x$, the line $x = 1$, and both coordinate axes.
(a) Find the volume $V_x$ when $R$ is revolved $360^\circ$ about the $x$-axis. [3 marks]
(b) Find the volume $V_y$ when $R$ is revolved $360^\circ$ about the $y$-axis. [4 marks]

View Official IB Markscheme

(a) Volume around $x$-axis:

$$V_x = \pi \int_0^1 (e^x)^2 dx = \pi \int_0^1 e^{2x} dx \quad \text{[M1]}$$ $$V_x = \pi \left[ \frac{1}{2}e^{2x} \right]_0^1 = \frac{\pi}{2}(e^2 - 1) \quad \text{[A1][A1]}$$

(b) Volume around $y$-axis:

The region spans $y$ from $0$ to $e$. From $y=0$ to $y=1$, it is a cylinder of radius $x=1$. From $y=1$ to $y=e$, the curve bounds it with outer radius $1$ and inner radius $x = \ln y$ [M1]:

$$V_y = \pi \int_0^1 (1)^2 dy + \pi \int_1^e \left( 1^2 - (\ln y)^2 \right) dy \quad \text{[M1]}$$ $$V_1 = \pi(1) = \pi$$

Using $\int (\ln y)^2 dy = y(\ln y)^2 - 2y\ln y + 2y$ [M1]:

$$\int_1^e (\ln y)^2 dy = [e(1) - 2e(1) + 2e] - [0 - 0 + 2] = e - 2$$ $$\pi \int_1^e (1 - (\ln y)^2) dy = \pi [ (e - 1) - (e - 2) ] = \pi(1) = \pi$$ $$V_y = \pi + \pi = 2\pi \quad \text{[A1]}$$
Examination Paper 1/2 Question 6 (FRQ) · Architectural Dome Model

An architectural dome has the shape of a solid formed by rotating $y = 6 - \frac{x^2}{6}$ about the $y$-axis, where $x, y$ are measured in metres and $y \ge 0$.
(a) Find the maximum height and the ground radius of the dome. [2 marks]
(b) Calculate the enclosed volume of the dome in $\text{m}^3$. [4 marks]

View Official IB Markscheme

(a) Dimensions:

At $x=0$, maximum height is $y = 6\text{ m}$ [A1].

At ground level $y=0$: $6 - \frac{x^2}{6} = 0 \implies x^2 = 36 \implies x = 6\text{ m}$ ground radius [A1].

(b) Volume calculation:

Express $x^2$ in terms of $y$:

$$\frac{x^2}{6} = 6 - y \implies x^2 = 6(6 - y) = 36 - 6y \quad \text{[M1]}$$

Integrate with respect to $y$ from $y = 0$ to $y = 6$ [M1]:

$$V = \pi \int_0^6 x^2 dy = \pi \int_0^6 (36 - 6y) dy \quad \text{[A1]}$$ $$V = \pi \left[ 36y - 3y^2 \right]_0^6 = \pi [ 36(6) - 3(36) ] = \pi [ 216 - 108 ] = 108\pi\text{ m}^3 \quad \text{[A1]}$$ $$\approx 339.3\text{ m}^3$$
