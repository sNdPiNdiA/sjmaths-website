Implicit Differentiation & Related Rates

Differentiate relations where $y$ is defined implicitly $F(x, y) = 0$. Find tangents, normals, and second derivatives $d^2y/dx^2$, and model dynamic physical systems using multivariable related rates of change.

Syllabus AHL 5.10, 5.11 Paper 1 & Paper 2 Skill 4 Interactive Tabs 10 Tiered Problems + Mini Test

Figure 5.2: Implicit Curve Tangent & Conical Related Rates

1. Implicit Differentiation Fundamentals

When an equation connects $x$ and $y$ implicitly in the form $F(x, y) = 0$ (e.g. $x^3 + y^3 = 3xy$), we differentiate both sides with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as an implicit function of $x$ via the chain rule:

$$\frac{d}{dx}[y^n] = n y^{n-1} \frac{dy}{dx}$$ $$\frac{d}{dx}[x y] = (1)y + x\frac{dy}{dx} = y + x\frac{dy}{dx} \quad \text{(Product Rule)}$$ $$\frac{d}{dx}[\sin y] = \cos y \frac{dy}{dx}, \quad \frac{d}{dx}[e^y] = e^y \frac{dy}{dx}$$

After differentiating, collect all terms containing $\frac{dy}{dx}$ on one side and factor out $\frac{dy}{dx}$ to isolate it.

2. Tangents and Normals to Implicit Curves

At a given point $(x_1, y_1)$ on the curve:

  • Gradient of Tangent: $m = \left.\frac{dy}{dx}\right|_{(x_1, y_1)}$.
  • Tangent Equation: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.
  • Normal Equation: $y - y_1 = -\frac{1}{m}(x - x_1)$ (where $m \neq 0$).
  • Horizontal Tangent: Occurs where $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$.
  • Vertical Tangent: Occurs where $\frac{dy}{dx} \to \pm\infty$ (denominator of derivative equals 0).

3. Related Rates of Change (Syllabus 5.11)

Related rates problems connect the rates of change of multiple related physical quantities with respect to time $t$ using the chain rule:

$$\frac{dy}{dt} = \frac{dy}{dx} \cdot \frac{dx}{dt}$$

Systematic 4-Step Method:

  1. Identify given rates and required rate: e.g., given $\frac{dV}{dt}$, find $\frac{dh}{dt}$.
  2. Write a geometric equation relating variables: e.g. volume of cone $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$.
  3. Eliminate unwanted variables: Use geometric similarity (e.g., $\frac{r}{h} = \frac{R}{H} \implies r = \frac{R}{H}h$).
  4. Differentiate with respect to $t$: Apply the chain rule $\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{dV}{dh} \frac{dh}{dt}$, substitute instantaneous values, and solve.

4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Folium of Descartes Tangent

Find the equation of the tangent to the curve $x^3 + y^3 = 6xy$ at the point $(3, 3)$.

Solution:

Differentiate implicitly with respect to $x$:

$$3x^2 + 3y^2 \frac{dy}{dx} = 6\left(1 \cdot y + x\frac{dy}{dx}\right) = 6y + 6x\frac{dy}{dx}$$

Divide by 3:

$$x^2 + y^2 \frac{dy}{dx} = 2y + 2x\frac{dy}{dx}$$ $$(y^2 - 2x)\frac{dy}{dx} = 2y - x^2 \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{2y - x^2}{y^2 - 2x}$$

Evaluate at $(3, 3)$:

$$m = \left.\frac{dy}{dx}\right|_{(3, 3)} = \frac{2(3) - 3^2}{3^2 - 2(3)} = \frac{6 - 9}{9 - 6} = \frac{-3}{3} = -1$$

Equation of tangent: $y - 3 = -1(x - 3) \implies y - 3 = -x + 3 \implies x + y = 6$.

Worked Example 2: Inverted Conical Tank Related Rates

Water pours into an inverted conical tank of height 6 m and base radius 2 m at a constant rate of $0.5\text{ m}^3/\text{min}$. At what rate is the water level rising when the water is 3 m deep?

Solution:

Given: $\frac{dV}{dt} = 0.5\text{ m}^3/\text{min}$. Required: $\left.\frac{dh}{dt}\right|_{h=3}$.
Volume of cone: $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$.
By similar triangles: $\frac{r}{h} = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3} \implies r = \frac{h}{3}$.
Substitute $r$ into volume formula:

$$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi \left(\frac{h}{3}\right)^2 h = \frac{\pi h^3}{27}$$

Differentiate with respect to $t$:

$$\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{3\pi h^2}{27} \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{\pi h^2}{9} \frac{dh}{dt}$$

Substitute $h = 3$ and $\frac{dV}{dt} = 0.5$:

$$0.5 = \frac{\pi (3^2)}{9} \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{9\pi}{9} \frac{dh}{dt} = \pi \frac{dh}{dt}$$ $$\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{0.5}{\pi} = \frac{1}{2\pi} \approx 0.159\text{ m/min}$$

Worked Example 3: Sliding Ladder Problem

A ladder of length 5 m leans against a vertical wall. The base of the ladder slides away from the wall at $0.8\text{ m/s}$. How fast is the top of the ladder sliding down the wall when the base is 3 m from the wall?

Solution:

Let $x$ be the distance from wall to base, and $y$ be the height of the ladder on the wall.
By Pythagoras: $x^2 + y^2 = 5^2 = 25$.
Given: $\frac{dx}{dt} = 0.8\text{ m/s}$. When $x = 3$, $y = \sqrt{25 - 9} = 4\text{ m}$.
Differentiate with respect to $t$:

$$2x\frac{dx}{dt} + 2y\frac{dy}{dt} = 0 \implies x\frac{dx}{dt} + y\frac{dy}{dt} = 0$$ $$3(0.8) + 4\frac{dy}{dt} = 0 \implies 2.4 + 4\frac{dy}{dt} = 0 \implies \frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{2.4}{4} = -0.6\text{ m/s}$$

The top of the ladder is sliding down at a speed of $0.6\text{ m/s}$.

Practice Exercises (10 Tiered Questions)

Try these 10 exam-style questions, from core skills to harder problems. Open each question to view its solution.

Exercise 1 (Foundational: Basic Implicit Derivative)

Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ for the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 100$ at the point $(6, 8)$.

Show Full Solution

$2x + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{x}{y}$. [M1]
At $(6, 8)$: $\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{6}{8} = -\frac{3}{4}$. [A1]

Exercise 2 (Foundational: Product Term Differentiation)

Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ in terms of $x$ and $y$ for $x^2 y + y^3 = 5$.

Show Full Solution

$\frac{d}{dx}(x^2 y) = 2xy + x^2\frac{dy}{dx}$. [M1]
$2xy + x^2\frac{dy}{dx} + 3y^2\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$
$(x^2 + 3y^2)\frac{dy}{dx} = -2xy \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{2xy}{x^2 + 3y^2}$. [A1]

Exercise 3 (Foundational: Expanding Circle Rate of Change)

The radius of a circle increases at $3\text{ cm/s}$. Find the rate of increase of its area when $r = 10\text{ cm}$.

Show Full Solution

$A = \pi r^2 \implies \frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r \frac{dr}{dt}$. [M1]
$\frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi(10)(3) = 60\pi \approx 188.5\text{ cm}^2/\text{s}$. [A1]

Exercise 4 (Intermediate: Tangent and Normal to Ellipse)

Find the equations of the tangent and normal to the ellipse $x^2 + 4y^2 = 8$ at the point $(2, 1)$.

Show Full Solution

$2x + 8y\frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{2x}{8y} = -\frac{x}{4y}$. [M1]
At $(2, 1)$: $m = -\frac{2}{4(1)} = -\frac{1}{2}$. [A1]
Tangent: $y - 1 = -\frac{1}{2}(x - 2) \implies x + 2y = 4$. [A1]
Normal: gradient $= 2 \implies y - 1 = 2(x - 2) \implies y = 2x - 3$. [A1]

Exercise 5 (Intermediate: Expanding Spherical Balloon)

A spherical balloon is inflated such that its volume increases at $100\text{ cm}^3/\text{s}$. Find the rate at which its surface area is increasing when the radius is $5\text{ cm}$.

Show Full Solution

$V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \implies \frac{dV}{dt} = 4\pi r^2 \frac{dr}{dt}$. [M1]
$100 = 4\pi (25) \frac{dr}{dt} = 100\pi \frac{dr}{dt} \implies \frac{dr}{dt} = \frac{1}{\pi}\text{ cm/s}$. [A1]
Surface area $S = 4\pi r^2 \implies \frac{dS}{dt} = 8\pi r \frac{dr}{dt}$. [M1]
$\frac{dS}{dt} = 8\pi(5)\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right) = 40\text{ cm}^2/\text{s}$. [A1]

Exercise 6 (Intermediate: Second Derivative Implicitly)

Find $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ in terms of $y$ for the curve $x^2 - y^2 = 4$.

Show Full Solution

$2x - 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{x}{y}$. [M1]
Differentiate again using quotient rule: [M1]
$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{1(y) - x\frac{dy}{dx}}{y^2} = \frac{y - x(x/y)}{y^2} = \frac{y^2 - x^2}{y^3}$. [M1]
Since $x^2 - y^2 = 4 \implies y^2 - x^2 = -4$:
$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = -\frac{4}{y^3}$. [A1]

Exercise 7 (Exam Standard: Horizontal and Vertical Tangents)

Find all points on the curve $x^2 + xy + y^2 = 27$ where the tangent is horizontal.

Show Full Solution

$2x + \left(y + x\frac{dy}{dx}\right) + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \implies (x + 2y)\frac{dy}{dx} = -(2x + y)$. [M1]
$\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{2x + y}{x + 2y}$.
Horizontal tangent requires $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \implies 2x + y = 0 \implies y = -2x$. [A1]
Substitute into curve equation: [M1]
$x^2 + x(-2x) + (-2x)^2 = 27 \implies x^2 - 2x^2 + 4x^2 = 27 \implies 3x^2 = 27 \implies x^2 = 9 \implies x = \pm 3$.
• $x = 3 \implies y = -6$. Point: $(3, -6)$. [A1]
• $x = -3 \implies y = 6$. Point: $(-3, 6)$. [A1]

Exercise 8 (Advanced: Walking Away from Streetlight Shadow Rate)

A person 1.8 m tall walks away from a 6 m tall streetlight at $1.5\text{ m/s}$. Find: (a) the rate at which the length of the person's shadow is growing, (b) the speed of the tip of the shadow.

Show Full Solution

Let $x$ be the distance from streetlight to person, $s$ be shadow length. [M1]
By similar triangles: $\frac{s}{1.8} = \frac{x + s}{6} \implies 6s = 1.8x + 1.8s \implies 4.2s = 1.8x \implies s = \frac{1.8}{4.2}x = \frac{3}{7}x$. [A1]
(a) Shadow length growth: $\frac{ds}{dt} = \frac{3}{7}\frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{3}{7}(1.5) = \frac{4.5}{7} = \frac{9}{14} \approx 0.643\text{ m/s}$. [A1]
(b) Speed of tip of shadow: Tip position $= x + s = x + \frac{3}{7}x = \frac{10}{7}x$.
Speed $= \frac{10}{7}\frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{10}{7}(1.5) = \frac{15}{7} \approx 2.14\text{ m/s}$. [A1]

Exercise 9 (Advanced: Exponential-Trig Implicit Curve)

Find the gradient of the tangent to $e^y \sin x = x + y$ at the origin $(0, 0)$.

Show Full Solution

Differentiate implicitly: [M1]
$\frac{d}{dx}[e^y \sin x] = e^y \frac{dy}{dx} \sin x + e^y \cos x$.
$\frac{d}{dx}[x + y] = 1 + \frac{dy}{dx}$.
Equation: $e^y \sin x \frac{dy}{dx} + e^y \cos x = 1 + \frac{dy}{dx}$. [A1]
Substitute $(x, y) = (0, 0)$: [M1]
$e^0 \sin(0) \frac{dy}{dx} + e^0 \cos(0) = 1 + \frac{dy}{dx}$
$0 + 1(1) = 1 + \frac{dy}{dx} \implies 1 = 1 + \frac{dy}{dx} \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = 0$. [A1]

Exercise 10 (Advanced: Trough Water Rate Problem)

A water trough of length 4 m has an equilateral triangular cross-section with sides 2 m pointing downwards. Water is pumped in at $0.2\text{ m}^3/\text{min}$. Find the rate of rise of the water level when the depth is 1 m.

Show Full Solution

Cross section is an equilateral triangle of side 2. Height $H = 2\sin(60^\circ) = \sqrt{3}$. Base $B = 2$. [M1]
At depth $h$, water surface width $b$: $\frac{b}{h} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \implies b = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}h$. [M1]
Cross-sectional area: $A = \frac{1}{2} b h = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}h\right)h = \frac{h^2}{\sqrt{3}}$.
Total volume $V = \text{Area} \times \text{length} = 4\left(\frac{h^2}{\sqrt{3}}\right) = \frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}h^2$. [A1]
$\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{8}{\sqrt{3}}h \frac{dh}{dt}$. [M1]
When $h = 1$ and $\frac{dV}{dt} = 0.2$:
$0.2 = \frac{8}{\sqrt{3}}(1) \frac{dh}{dt} \implies \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{0.2\sqrt{3}}{8} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{40} \approx 0.0433\text{ m/min}$. [A1]

Examination Pitfalls & Examiner Tips

Forgetting $\frac{dy}{dx}$ on $y$-terms

Every time you differentiate a term with $y$ with respect to $x$, you MUST multiply by $\frac{dy}{dx}$. Writing $\frac{d}{dx}(y^2) = 2y$ instead of $2y\frac{dy}{dx}$ is the most common error in HL calculus.

Substituting Numbers TOO EARLY in Related Rates

Never substitute the numerical snapshot (e.g. $h = 3$) BEFORE differentiating! If you substitute early, the quantity becomes a constant and its derivative erroneously vanishes to zero.

Question 5 (Paper 1 Style · 6 marks)

A curve is defined implicitly by $x^2 + 2xy + 3y^2 = 11$.
(a) Show that $\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{x + y}{x + 3y}$. [3 marks]
(b) Find the coordinates of the two points on the curve where the tangent is parallel to the $x$-axis. [3 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Implicit differentiation: [3 marks]
$2x + 2\left(y + x\frac{dy}{dx}\right) + 6y\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$ [M1]
$2x + 2y + (2x + 6y)\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$
Divide by 2: $(x + 3y)\frac{dy}{dx} = -(x + y) \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{x + y}{x + 3y}$ [A1].

(b) Horizontal tangents: [3 marks]
Parallel to $x$-axis $\implies \frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \implies x + y = 0 \implies y = -x$ [M1].
Substitute into curve equation:
$x^2 + 2x(-x) + 3(-x)^2 = 11 \implies x^2 - 2x^2 + 3x^2 = 11 \implies 2x^2 = 11 \implies x = \pm\sqrt{\frac{11}{2}}$ [A1].
Points: $\left(\sqrt{5.5}, -\sqrt{5.5}\right)$ and $\left(-\sqrt{5.5}, \sqrt{5.5}\right)$ [A1].

Question 6 (Paper 2 Style · 8 marks)

A sand timer consists of sand falling from an upper container onto a conical pile. The height of the cone is always equal to its base radius ($h = r$). Sand falls at a rate of $12\text{ cm}^3/\text{s}$.
(a) Express the volume $V$ in terms of $h$ only. [1 mark]
(b) Find the rate at which the height of the sand pile is increasing when $h = 6\text{ cm}$. [3 marks]
(c) Find the rate at which the base area of the cone is increasing at this instant. [4 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Volume formula: [1 mark]
$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3}\pi h^3$ [A1].

(b) Rate of height increase: [3 marks]
$\frac{dV}{dt} = \pi h^2 \frac{dh}{dt}$ [M1].
Substitute $h = 6$ and $\frac{dV}{dt} = 12$:
$12 = \pi (36) \frac{dh}{dt} \implies \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{12}{36\pi} = \frac{1}{3\pi} \approx 0.106\text{ cm/s}$ [A1].

(c) Rate of base area increase: [4 marks]
Base area $A = \pi r^2 = \pi h^2$ [M1].
$\frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi h \frac{dh}{dt}$ [M1].
Substitute $h = 6$ and $\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{1}{3\pi}$:
$\frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi(6)\left(\frac{1}{3\pi}\right) = 12\pi \left(\frac{1}{3\pi}\right) = 4\text{ cm}^2/\text{s}$ [A1].
