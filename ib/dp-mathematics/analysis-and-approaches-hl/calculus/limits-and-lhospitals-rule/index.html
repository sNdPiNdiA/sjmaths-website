Advanced Limits & L’Hôpital’s Rule

Evaluate indeterminate limits using L’Hôpital’s rule $\lim \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$ for $\frac{0}{0}$ and $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$. Transform products $0 \times \infty$ and differences $\infty - \infty$, and study function growth hierarchies.

Figure 5.1: Geometric Intuition of L'Hôpital's Rule at Indeterminate Form $\frac{0}{0}$

1. Indeterminate Forms

Direct substitution into a limit $\lim_{x \to c} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)}$ may lead to an undefined indeterminate expression:

  • Primary Indeterminate Quotients: $\frac{0}{0}$ and $\frac{\pm\infty}{\pm\infty}$.
  • Indeterminate Products: $0 \times \infty$.
  • Indeterminate Differences: $\infty - \infty$.
  • Warning: $\frac{0}{\infty} = 0$, $\frac{\infty}{0} = \pm\infty$, and $\frac{k}{0} = \text{undefined/}\pm\infty$ are determinate forms. Do NOT use L'Hôpital's rule on determinate forms!

2. L'Hôpital's Rule Statement (Syllabus 5.12)

Suppose $f$ and $g$ are differentiable near $c$ and $g'(x) \neq 0$. If:

$$\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \lim_{x \to c} g(x) = 0 \quad \left(\frac{0}{0}\right)$$

OR

$$\lim_{x \to c} |f(x)| = \infty \quad \text{and} \quad \lim_{x \to c} |g(x)| = \infty \quad \left(\frac{\infty}{\infty}\right)$$

Then:

$$\lim_{x \to c} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x \to c} \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$$

provided the limit on the right exists (or is $\pm\infty$).

Examiner Requirement: In IB exams, you must explicitly state that the form is $\frac{0}{0}$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$ before differentiating!

3. Transforming Other Indeterminate Forms

  • Product $0 \times \infty$: Rewrite $f(x) g(x)$ as a quotient: $$f(x) g(x) = \frac{f(x)}{1/g(x)} \quad \left(\frac{0}{0}\right) \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{g(x)}{1/f(x)} \quad \left(\frac{\infty}{\infty}\right)$$
  • Difference $\infty - \infty$: Combine into a single algebraic fraction via a common denominator before evaluating.

4. Growth Rates Hierarchy at Infinity

As $x \to \infty$, functions grow at fundamentally different orders of magnitude:

$$\ln x \ll x^p \ll a^x \ll x! \ll x^x \quad (p > 0, a > 1)$$

Consequently: $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\ln x}{x^p} = 0$ and $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{x^p}{e^x} = 0$.

5. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Basic 0/0 Limit

Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^{3x} - 1 - 3x}{x^2}$.

Solution:

Direct substitution at $x = 0$:

$$\text{Numerator: } e^0 - 1 - 0 = 0, \quad \text{Denominator: } 0^2 = 0 \quad \implies \text{Indeterminate form } \frac{0}{0}$$

Apply L'Hôpital's Rule by differentiating numerator and denominator:

$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\frac{d}{dx}(e^{3x} - 1 - 3x)}{\frac{d}{dx}(x^2)} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{3e^{3x} - 3}{2x}$$

Direct substitution at $x = 0$: $\frac{3(1) - 3}{0} = \frac{0}{0}$. Indeterminate again!
Apply L'Hôpital's Rule a second time:

$$= \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\frac{d}{dx}(3e^{3x} - 3)}{\frac{d}{dx}(2x)} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{9e^{3x}}{2} = \frac{9(1)}{2} = \frac{9}{2}$$

Worked Example 2: Product Form $0 \times \infty$

Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0^+} x^2 \ln x$.

Solution:

As $x \to 0^+$, $x^2 \to 0$ and $\ln x \to -\infty$, which is an indeterminate product $0 \times (-\infty)$.

Rewrite as a quotient $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$ by moving $x^2$ to the denominator:

$$\lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{\ln x}{x^{-2}}$$

Form is $\frac{-\infty}{\infty}$. Apply L'Hôpital's Rule:

$$= \lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{1/x}{-2x^{-3}} = \lim_{x \to 0^+} \left(-\frac{1}{2} \frac{x^3}{x}\right) = \lim_{x \to 0^+} \left(-\frac{1}{2} x^2\right) = -\frac{1}{2}(0)^2 = 0$$

Worked Example 3: Difference Form $\infty - \infty$

Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \left( \frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{\sin x} \right)$.

Solution:

Direct substitution gives $\infty - \infty$. Combine over a common denominator:

$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x - x}{x \sin x}$$

At $x = 0$, this is form $\frac{0}{0}$. Apply L'Hôpital's Rule:

$$= \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\cos x - 1}{\sin x + x\cos x}$$

At $x = 0$, $\frac{1 - 1}{0 + 0} = \frac{0}{0}$. Apply L'Hôpital's Rule a second time:

$$= \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{-\sin x}{\cos x + (\cos x - x\sin x)} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{-\sin x}{2\cos x - x\sin x} = \frac{0}{2(1) - 0} = \frac{0}{2} = 0$$

Exercise 1 (Foundational: Standard Trig Limit)

Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(5x)}{2x}$ using L'Hôpital's rule.

Show Full Solution

Direct substitution: $\frac{\sin 0}{0} = \frac{0}{0}$ (indeterminate form). [M1]
Apply L'Hôpital: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{5\cos(5x)}{2} = \frac{5\cos(0)}{2} = \frac{5(1)}{2} = \frac{5}{2}$. [A1]

Exercise 2 (Foundational: Infinity over Infinity)

Evaluate $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{3x^2 + 1}{5x^2 - 4x}$.

Show Full Solution

Form $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$. [M1]
Apply L'Hôpital: $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{6x}{10x - 4} = \frac{\infty}{\infty}$.
Apply L'Hôpital again: $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{6}{10} = \frac{3}{5}$. [A1]

Exercise 3 (Foundational: Exponential over Linear)

Evaluate $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{e^{2x}}{x}$.

Show Full Solution

Form $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$. [M1]
Apply L'Hôpital: $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{2e^{2x}}{1} = +\infty$. [A1]

Exercise 4 (Intermediate: Repeated L'Hôpital with Trig Functions)

Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2}$.

Show Full Solution

Form $\frac{1 - 1}{0} = \frac{0}{0}$. [M1]
First application: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{2x}$ (still $\frac{0}{0}$).
Second application: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\cos x}{2} = \frac{1}{2}$. [A1]

Exercise 5 (Intermediate: Natural Log Limit)

Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{\ln x}{x^2 - 1}$.

Show Full Solution

Form $\frac{\ln 1}{1 - 1} = \frac{0}{0}$. [M1]
Apply L'Hôpital: $\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{1/x}{2x} = \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{1}{2x^2} = \frac{1}{2}$. [A1]

Exercise 6 (Intermediate: Product Form at Zero)

Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0^+} x \ln(x)$.

Show Full Solution

Form $0 \times (-\infty)$. Rewrite: $\lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{\ln x}{1/x}$ (form $\frac{-\infty}{\infty}$). [M1]
Apply L'Hôpital: $\lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{1/x}{-1/x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0^+} (-x) = 0$. [A1]

Exercise 7 (Exam Standard: Limit with Inverse Trig)

Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\arctan x - x}{x^3}$.

Show Full Solution

Form $\frac{0 - 0}{0} = \frac{0}{0}$. [M1]
First application: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\frac{1}{1+x^2} - 1}{3x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\frac{1 - (1+x^2)}{1+x^2}}{3x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{-x^2}{3x^2(1+x^2)}$. [M1]
Cancel $x^2$: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{-1}{3(1+x^2)} = -\frac{1}{3}$. [A1]

Exercise 8 (Advanced: Unknown Parameter in Limit)

Given that $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(2x) + a x + b x^3}{x^3} = L$ where $L$ is a finite non-zero constant, determine all possible values of $a$, $b$, and $L$.

Show Full Solution

At $x = 0$, denominator is 0. For limit to be finite, numerator must be 0: $\sin 0 + 0 = 0$ (holds).
Apply L'Hôpital: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2\cos(2x) + a + 3bx^2}{3x^2}$. [M1]
At $x = 0$, denominator is 0. So numerator must be 0:
$2\cos(0) + a + 0 = 2 + a = 0 \implies a = -2$. [A1]
Now limit is: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2\cos(2x) - 2 + 3bx^2}{3x^2}$ (form $\frac{0}{0}$).
Apply L'Hôpital again: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{-4\sin(2x) + 6bx}{6x} = \lim_{x \to 0} \left(-\frac{4\sin(2x)}{6x} + b\right)$. [M1]
Since $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(2x)}{x} = 2$: $-\frac{4(2)}{6} + b = -\frac{4}{3} + b$.
For any finite non-zero $L$: $L = b - \frac{4}{3}$. (If evaluated through third L'Hôpital: $\frac{-8\cos(2x) + 6b}{6} = \frac{-8 + 6b}{6} = b - \frac{4}{3}$). [A1]
All solutions: $a=-2$, $b\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\frac43\}$ and $L=b-\frac43$. The non-zero condition excludes $b=\frac43$. There is no unique value of $b$ unless the value of $L$ is supplied.

Exercise 9 (Advanced: Indeterminate Power $1^\infty$)

Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} (1 + 3x)^{1/x}$ using natural logarithms.

Show Full Solution

Let $y = (1 + 3x)^{1/x}$. Then $\ln y = \frac{\ln(1 + 3x)}{x}$. [M1]
Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\ln(1 + 3x)}{x}$ (form $\frac{0}{0}$): [M1]
Apply L'Hôpital: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\frac{3}{1+3x}}{1} = 3$. [A1]
Since $\lim_{x \to 0} \ln y = 3$, $\lim_{x \to 0} y = e^3$. [A1]

Exercise 10 (Advanced: Difference of Radicals Limit)

Evaluate $\lim_{x \to \infty} \left( \sqrt{x^2 + 4x} - x \right)$.

Show Full Solution

Form $\infty - \infty$. Multiply by conjugate: [M1]
$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{(\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} - x)(\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} + x)}{\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} + x} = \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{(x^2 + 4x) - x^2}{\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} + x} = \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{4x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} + x}$. [M1]
Divide numerator and denominator by $x$:
$= \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{4}{\sqrt{1 + 4/x} + 1} = \frac{4}{\sqrt{1 + 0} + 1} = \frac{4}{2} = 2$. [A1]

Syllabus 5.12: L’Hôpital’s Rule

$$\lim_{x \to c} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x \to c} \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$$

Condition: $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = 0$ and $\lim_{x \to c} g(x) = 0$, OR $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = \pm\infty$ and $\lim_{x \to c} g(x) = \pm\infty$.

Must-Memorize Principles (Not in Booklet)

1. Converting Product Form $0 \times \infty$

$$f(x)g(x) = \frac{f(x)}{1/g(x)} \quad \left(\frac{0}{0}\right)$$

2. Growth Hierarchy at Infinity

$$\ln x \ll x^k \ll e^x \quad (k > 0)$$

Examination Pitfalls & Examiner Tips

Never Apply Quotient Rule to L'Hôpital

L'Hôpital's rule requires differentiating the numerator and denominator separately: $\frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$. Do NOT use the quotient rule $\frac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}$!

Check the Condition FIRST

Always state "Form $\frac{0}{0}$" or "Form $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$". Applying L'Hôpital to a determinate limit (e.g. $\frac{1}{0}$ or $\frac{2}{3}$) will yield completely incorrect answers.

Question 5 (Paper 1 Style · 6 marks)

Consider the limit $L = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\cos(2x) - 1 + 2x^2}{x^4}$.
(a) State the indeterminate form of the limit at $x = 0$. [1 mark]
(b) By repeated application of L’Hôpital’s rule, find the value of $L$. [5 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Indeterminate form: [1 mark]
At $x = 0$: $\cos(0) - 1 + 0 = 0$, denominator $0^4 = 0$. Form is $\frac{0}{0}$ [A1].

(b) Repeated L'Hôpital: [5 marks]
1st: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{-2\sin(2x) + 4x}{4x^3}$ (form $\frac{0}{0}$) [M1].
2nd: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{-4\cos(2x) + 4}{12x^2}$ (form $\frac{-4+4}{0} = \frac{0}{0}$) [M1].
3rd: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{8\sin(2x)}{24x}$ (form $\frac{0}{0}$) [M1].
4th: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{16\cos(2x)}{24} = \frac{16(1)}{24} = \frac{2}{3}$ [A1].
$L = \frac{2}{3}$ [A1].

Question 6 (Paper 2 Style · 7 marks)

Let $f(x) = \frac{x - \sin x}{x^3}$ for $x \neq 0$.
(a) Find $\lim_{x \to 0} f(x)$. [3 marks]
(b) Hence define $f(0)$ so that $f$ is continuous at $x = 0$. [1 mark]
(c) Find $\lim_{x \to \infty} f(x)$. [3 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Limit at 0: [3 marks]
Form $\frac{0 - 0}{0} = \frac{0}{0}$ [M1].
1st: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{3x^2}$ (form $\frac{0}{0}$) [M1].
2nd: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{6x} = \frac{1}{6}$ [A1].

(b) Value for continuity: [1 mark]
$f(0) = \lim_{x \to 0} f(x) = \frac{1}{6}$ [A1].

(c) Limit at infinity: [3 marks]
$f(x) = \frac{x}{x^3} - \frac{\sin x}{x^3} = \frac{1}{x^2} - \frac{\sin x}{x^3}$ [M1].
As $x \to \infty$, $\frac{1}{x^2} \to 0$.
Since $-1 \le \sin x \le 1$, $-\frac{1}{x^3} \le \frac{\sin x}{x^3} \le \frac{1}{x^3}$.
By the squeeze theorem, $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\sin x}{x^3} = 0$ [M1].
Therefore $\lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = 0 - 0 = 0$ [A1].
