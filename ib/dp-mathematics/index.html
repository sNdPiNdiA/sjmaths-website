AA vs AI

Math AA vs Math AI: Which Should You Choose?

The two DP Mathematics courses have different emphases, assessment styles, and intended student profiles. A clear comparison helps you make the right choice for your DP programme.

Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (AA)

Emphasises mathematical rigour, proof, and abstraction. Students develop strong algebraic manipulation skills and engage with mathematics as a discipline in its own right.

Best suited for:

  • Mathematics, physics, engineering
  • Computer science, actuarial science
  • Students who enjoy abstract reasoning

Mathematics: Applications and Interpretation (AI)

Emphasises real-world applications, mathematical modelling, and the use of technology. Students develop statistical thinking and apply mathematics in authentic contexts.

Best suited for:

  • Social sciences, economics, biology
  • Business, design, psychology
  • Students who prefer applied contexts

Curriculum

Curriculum Information

IB Diploma Programme Mathematics is studied over two years as part of the full DP. Students choose one of the two courses (AA or AI) at one of the two levels (SL or HL) based on their interests, intended university subject, and school offering.

Each course is assessed through two external examination papers and one internal assessment — the Mathematical Exploration (IA). The IA is an individual piece of written work in which students investigate an area of mathematics they find interesting, assessed by their teacher against IB criteria and moderated externally.

HL courses require approximately 240 teaching hours, and SL courses require approximately 150 hours. Both AA HL and AI HL include an additional paper (Paper 3) involving extended problem-solving. AA HL Paper 3 focuses on abstract proof and analysis; AI HL Paper 3 focuses on mathematical modelling in context.

Curriculum content is defined in the IB Mathematics Guide. Topic pages and practice resources on SJMaths are designed to cover the syllabus content applicable to the current examination sessions, with curriculum information maintained in data files rather than hard-coded into page URLs, so that syllabus updates can be reflected without changing permanent links.
