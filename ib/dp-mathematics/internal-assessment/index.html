Orientation

What is the IB Mathematical Exploration?

The Mathematical Exploration is a 12–20 page investigation required for AA SL/HL and AI SL/HL. Students choose a question and use mathematics to investigate it.

Assessment note: University-level mathematics is not required. The IB rewards mathematics appropriate to the course, explained clearly, with personal engagement and critical reflection.

Rubric & Marks

Assessment Criteria (20 marks)

Every IB Math exploration is assessed against five criteria. Use the rubric to see what each criterion requires.

Criterion A: Presentation 4 Marks

Evaluates structure, coherence, organization, and conciseness.

  • Well-Structured: Clear Introduction, Rationale, Aim, Analysis, and Conclusion.
  • Coherent & Focused: Reader can follow the flow without confusion or unnecessary tangents.
  • Concise: Aim for 12–20 pages. Avoid repetitive repetitive calculations; summarize large tables in appendices.
Criterion B: Mathematical Communication 4 Marks

Evaluates mathematical language, notation, symbols, graphs, and terminology.

  • Standard Notation: Use proper math notation (e.g. \( \int_{a}^{b} f(x)dx \), \( \chi^2 \)) instead of calculator syntax like y=2x^2.
  • Graph Quality: Fully labeled axes, clear scales, titles, and units on every diagram.
  • Defined Variables: Define every symbol upon first introduction (e.g. "Let \( r \) represent the rate in \(\text{m/s}\)").
Criterion C: Personal Engagement 3 Marks

Evaluates authentic interest, curiosity, independent thought, and creative thinking.

  • Genuine Rationale: Avoid fake stories ("I love coffee so I modeled cooling"). Show real curiosity or collected primary data.
  • Original Direction: Take a textbook model and adapt or customize it to your unique context.
  • Independent Thought: Evidence of problem-solving when obstacles arise during modeling.
Criterion D: Reflection 3 Marks

Evaluates critical evaluation, interpretation, and consideration of limitations.

  • Ongoing Reflection: Do not save reflection only for the conclusion; reflect at each key step.
  • Meaning in Context: What does an \( R^2 = 0.94 \) actually signify for your real-world system?
  • Model Limitations: Analyze assumptions, percentage errors, boundary conditions, and valid domains.
Criterion E: Use of Mathematics 6 Marks

Evaluates knowledge, understanding, sophistication (HL), and mathematical rigor.

Standard Level (SL)

  • Demonstrates full, thorough understanding of SL syllabus topics.
  • Completely correct mathematical execution without routine calculation errors.
  • Rigorous explanations of why formulas work, not just plug-and-chug.

Higher Level (HL)

  • Requires sophistication or mathematical depth commensurate with HL.
  • Uses advanced concepts: differential equations, complex numbers, matrices, multivariable extensions.
  • Thorough, complete mathematical justification with rigorous proof or error quantification.

Framework

Five-Stage Writing Roadmap

1. Introduction & Rationale

State your explicit mathematical aim. Explain why this question matters to you personally. Establish your scope and assumptions right away.

2. Methodology & Data

Explain how you collected data or set up your equations. Justify why chosen models fit the phenomena. Define all variables with correct units.

3. Mathematical Analysis

Show complete, rigorous working. Include diagrams, function graphs, and clear step-by-step logic. Don't hide steps; explain the 'why' behind each equation.

4. Ongoing Critical Reflection

Evaluate errors as they occur. Discuss goodness-of-fit, limitations of assumptions, and unexpected anomalies in your models.

5. Conclusion & Future Scope

Summarize answers to your original aim. Propose concrete ways to extend the mathematics. Include a full bibliography with formatted citations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use the same topic as a classmate?

You can investigate a similar broad theme (e.g. projectile motion or epidemic models), but your specific research question, collected data, and mathematical angles must be completely independent. Collaboration is strictly prohibited under IB academic integrity rules.

Is an IA in Analysis & Approaches harder than Applications & Interpretation?

No, both are marked on the same 20-point rubric. AA IAs generally feature algebraic proof, calculus, or function analysis, while AI IAs feature data collection, mathematical modeling, technology, and statistical hypothesis testing.

What font, spacing, and formatting should I use?

Use a clean professional font (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman, or Computer Modern if using LaTeX) at 11 or 12pt with 1.5 line spacing. Always write equations using Word Equation Editor, MathType, or LaTeX.
