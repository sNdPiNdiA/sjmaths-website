2.1

Scientific Notation (Standard Form $a \times 10^k$)

  • Formal Definition: A number is written in scientific notation (standard form) when expressed as: $$a \times 10^k \quad \text{where } 1 \le |a| < 10 \text{ and } k \in \mathbb{Z}$$ • If the original number is $\ge 10$, then $k > 0$ (e.g., $450\,000 = 4.5 \times 10^5$).
    • If the original number is between $0$ and $1$, then $k < 0$ (e.g., $0.00072 = 7.2 \times 10^{-4}$).
  • Computations in Standard Form:
    • Multiplication: $(a \times 10^p) \times (b \times 10^q) = (ab) \times 10^{p+q}$. Adjust $ab$ if $ab \ge 10$.
    • Division: $\frac{a \times 10^p}{b \times 10^q} = \left(\frac{a}{b}\right) \times 10^{p-q}$. Adjust $\frac{a}{b}$ if $\frac{a}{b} < 1$.
    • Addition/Subtraction: Convert both numbers to share the same power of $10$ before combining coefficients: $$(3.2 \times 10^4) + (4.5 \times 10^3) = (3.2 \times 10^4) + (0.45 \times 10^4) = (3.2 + 0.45) \times 10^4 = 3.65 \times 10^4$$

Example 1 · Criterion A & D

Scientific Notation Multiplication and Division

The speed of light in vacuum is approximately $c = 3.0 \times 10^8\text{ m s}^{-1}$. The mean distance from the Sun to Mars is $2.28 \times 10^{11}\text{ m}$.
(a) Calculate the time (in seconds) for light from the Sun to reach Mars. Give your answer in scientific notation.
(b) Convert this time into minutes to 3 significant figures.

  1. (a) Time calculation: $$t = \frac{\text{Distance}}{c} = \frac{2.28 \times 10^{11}}{3.0 \times 10^8} = \left(\frac{2.28}{3.0}\right) \times 10^{11 - 8} = 0.76 \times 10^3\text{ s}$$ Normalizing into standard form ($1 \le a < 10$): $$t = (7.6 \times 10^{-1}) \times 10^3 = 7.6 \times 10^2\text{ seconds}$$
  2. (b) Conversion to minutes: $$t = \frac{760\text{ s}}{60\text{ s min}^{-1}} = 12.666\dots\text{ minutes} \approx 12.7\text{ minutes (to 3 s.f.)}$$

Answers: (a) $7.6 \times 10^2\text{ s}$; (b) $12.7\text{ minutes}$.

2.2

Significant Figures, Rounding & Upper/Lower Error Bounds

  • Rules for Counting Significant Figures (s.f.):
    • All non-zero digits are significant ($4.27 \to 3$ s.f.).
    • Captive zeros between non-zero digits are significant ($5004 \to 4$ s.f., $3.08 \to 3$ s.f.).
    • Leading zeros before the first non-zero digit are never significant ($0.0075 \to 2$ s.f.).
    • Trailing zeros in a decimal portion are significant ($4.500 \to 4$ s.f.).
  • Degree of Accuracy (IB Standard): Unless stated otherwise, answers must be given to 3 significant figures (or exact values).
  • Upper and Lower Error Bounds: When a value $x$ is rounded to the nearest unit of measurement $U$: $$\text{Maximum Absolute Error} = \frac{U}{2}$$ $$\text{Lower Bound (LB)} = x - \frac{U}{2}, \quad \text{Upper Bound (UB)} = x + \frac{U}{2}$$ $$\text{Error Interval: } \text{LB} \le x < \text{UB}$$
  • Bounds for Operations:
    • Sum: $\text{UB}(A + B) = \text{UB}_A + \text{UB}_B$, $\text{LB}(A + B) = \text{LB}_A + \text{LB}_B$
    • Difference: $\text{UB}(A - B) = \text{UB}_A - \text{LB}_B$, $\text{LB}(A - B) = \text{LB}_A - \text{UB}_B$
    • Product: $\text{UB}(A \times B) = \text{UB}_A \times \text{UB}_B$, $\text{LB}(A \times B) = \text{LB}_A \times \text{LB}_B$ (for positive quantities)
    • Quotient: $\text{UB}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right) = \frac{\text{UB}_A}{\text{LB}_B}$, $\text{LB}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right) = \frac{\text{LB}_A}{\text{UB}_B}$

Example 2 · Criterion A & D

Calculating Error Bounds for Area and Speed

A rectangle has length $L = 8.4\text{ cm}$ and width $W = 5.6\text{ cm}$, each rounded to $1\text{ decimal place}$.
(a) State the lower and upper bounds of $L$ and $W$.
(b) Calculate the lower bound and upper bound of the area $A$.
(c) State the area value to an appropriate degree of accuracy.

  1. (a) Individual bounds (unit $U = 0.1$, error margin $= 0.05$): $$8.35 \le L < 8.45\text{ cm}$$ $$5.55 \le W < 5.65\text{ cm}$$
  2. (b) Area bounds: $$\text{LB}(A) = \text{LB}(L) \times \text{LB}(W) = 8.35 \times 5.55 = 46.3425\text{ cm}^2$$ $$\text{UB}(A) = \text{UB}(L) \times \text{UB}(W) = 8.45 \times 5.65 = 47.7425\text{ cm}^2$$
  3. (c) Degree of accuracy: Both bounds round to $47\text{ cm}^2$ (to 2 s.f.) or $50\text{ cm}^2$ (to 1 s.f.). Because the tens digit is $4$ and units digit differs ($46$ vs $47$), $A = 47\text{ cm}^2$ is the most reliable representation.

Answers: (a) $L \in [8.35, 8.45)$, $W \in [5.55, 5.65)$; (b) $\text{LB} = 46.3425\text{ cm}^2$, $\text{UB} = 47.7425\text{ cm}^2$; (c) $47\text{ cm}^2$.

2.3

Surds: Simplification, Arithmetic & Conjugates

  • Fundamental Radical Laws: $$\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a} \cdot \sqrt{b} \quad (a, b \ge 0)$$ $$\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}} \quad (a \ge 0, b > 0)$$
  • Simplifying to $k\sqrt{m}$: Factor out the largest square number ($4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, \dots$): $$\sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36 \times 2} = \sqrt{36} \times \sqrt{2} = 6\sqrt{2}$$
  • Adding/Subtracting Like Surds: Surds with identical radicands combine algebraically: $$4\sqrt{18} - 2\sqrt{50} = 4(3\sqrt{2}) - 2(5\sqrt{2}) = 12\sqrt{2} - 10\sqrt{2} = 2\sqrt{2}$$
  • Expanding Brackets: $$(3 + 2\sqrt{5})(4 - \sqrt{5}) = 12 - 3\sqrt{5} + 8\sqrt{5} - 2(5) = 2 + 5\sqrt{5}$$
  • Conjugate Difference of Squares: $$(a + \sqrt{b})(a - \sqrt{b}) = a^2 - (\sqrt{b})^2 = a^2 - b \in \mathbb{Q}$$

Example 3 · Criterion A & C

Rationalising Monomial and Binomial Denominators

Rationalise the denominator and simplify each expression:
(a) $\frac{12}{\sqrt{6}}$   (b) $\frac{4}{3 - \sqrt{5}}$   (c) $\frac{\sqrt{3} + 1}{\sqrt{3} - 1}$

  1. (a) Monomial denominator: Multiply numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{6}$: $$\frac{12}{\sqrt{6}} \times \frac{\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{6}} = \frac{12\sqrt{6}}{6} = 2\sqrt{6}$$
  2. (b) Binomial denominator: Multiply by the conjugate $3 + \sqrt{5}$: $$\frac{4}{3 - \sqrt{5}} \times \frac{3 + \sqrt{5}}{3 + \sqrt{5}} = \frac{4(3 + \sqrt{5})}{3^2 - (\sqrt{5})^2} = \frac{4(3 + \sqrt{5})}{9 - 5} = \frac{4(3 + \sqrt{5})}{4} = 3 + \sqrt{5}$$
  3. (c) Conjugate with square: $$\frac{\sqrt{3} + 1}{\sqrt{3} - 1} \times \frac{\sqrt{3} + 1}{\sqrt{3} + 1} = \frac{(\sqrt{3} + 1)^2}{(\sqrt{3})^2 - 1^2} = \frac{3 + 2\sqrt{3} + 1}{3 - 1} = \frac{4 + 2\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2 + \sqrt{3}$$

Answers: (a) $2\sqrt{6}$; (b) $3 + \sqrt{5}$; (c) $2 + \sqrt{3}$.

Criterion C Question 5 (Standard · 4 marks)

Rationalise the denominator of each fraction, expressing your answer in simplest form:
(a) $\frac{15}{2\sqrt{5}}$ [2 marks]
(b) $\frac{6}{4 - \sqrt{10}}$ [2 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Monomial: [2 marks]
$\frac{15}{2\sqrt{5}} \times \frac{\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{5}} = \frac{15\sqrt{5}}{2 \times 5} = \frac{15\sqrt{5}}{10} = \frac{3\sqrt{5}}{2}$ [A2]

(b) Binomial: [2 marks]
Multiply by conjugate $4 + \sqrt{10}$:
$\frac{6(4 + \sqrt{10})}{4^2 - (\sqrt{10})^2} = \frac{6(4 + \sqrt{10})}{16 - 10} = \frac{6(4 + \sqrt{10})}{6} = 4 + \sqrt{10}$ [A2]

Criterion C Question 7 (Extended · 4 marks)

Given that $\frac{\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3}} = a + b\sqrt{21}$, where $a, b \in \mathbb{Q}$:
Find the exact values of $a$ and $b$. Show all steps clearly.

Show Full Markscheme

Step 1: Multiply by conjugate of denominator [1 mark]
Multiply numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}$ [M1]: $$\frac{(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3})(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3})}{(\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3})(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3})}$$

Step 2: Expand numerator and denominator [2 marks]
Denominator: $(\sqrt{7})^2 - (\sqrt{3})^2 = 7 - 3 = 4$ [A1]
Numerator: $(\sqrt{7})^2 + 2\sqrt{7}\sqrt{3} + (\sqrt{3})^2 = 7 + 2\sqrt{21} + 3 = 10 + 2\sqrt{21}$ [A1]

Step 3: Simplify and extract coefficients [1 mark]
$$\frac{10 + 2\sqrt{21}}{4} = \frac{10}{4} + \frac{2}{4}\sqrt{21} = \frac{5}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{21}$$ $$a = \frac{5}{2}, \quad b = \frac{1}{2}$$ [A1]

Criterion D Question 8 (Real-Life Bounds · 5 marks)

A sprinter completes a $100\text{ m}$ dash.
• The distance $d = 100\text{ m}$ is measured correct to the nearest metre.
• The time $t = 9.8\text{ seconds}$ is recorded correct to the nearest $0.1\text{ second}$.
(a) Write down the lower and upper bounds of $d$ and $t$. [2 marks]
(b) Calculate the maximum possible average speed of the sprinter in $\text{m s}^{-1}$. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2 marks]
(c) Calculate the minimum possible average speed to 3 significant figures. [1 mark]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Bounds of $d$ and $t$: [2 marks]
$d$: unit $1\text{ m} \implies 99.5 \le d < 100.5\text{ m}$ [A1]
$t$: unit $0.1\text{ s} \implies 9.75 \le t < 9.85\text{ s}$ [A1]

(b) Maximum average speed: [2 marks]
To maximize $\text{Speed} = \frac{d}{t}$, use $\text{UB}(d)$ and $\text{LB}(t)$ [M1]: $$\text{Speed}_{\max} = \frac{100.5}{9.75} \approx 10.3077\text{ m s}^{-1} \approx \mathbf{10.3\text{ m s}^{-1} \text{ (3 s.f.)}}$$ [A1]

(c) Minimum average speed: [1 mark]
$$\text{Speed}_{\min} = \frac{\text{LB}(d)}{\text{UB}(t)} = \frac{99.5}{9.85} \approx 10.1015\text{ m s}^{-1} \approx \mathbf{10.1\text{ m s}^{-1} \text{ (3 s.f.)}}$$ [A1]

Criterion D Question 9 (Astrophysics Application · 5 marks)

The mass of planet Earth is approximately $M = 5.97 \times 10^{24}\text{ kg}$ and the mass of the Moon is $m = 7.35 \times 10^{22}\text{ kg}$.
(a) Calculate how many times more massive Earth is than the Moon. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2 marks]
(b) Calculate the combined mass of the Earth-Moon system in scientific notation ($a \times 10^k$). [2 marks]
(c) Express the difference between their masses in scientific notation. [1 mark]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Ratio: [2 marks]
$\text{Ratio} = \frac{5.97 \times 10^{24}}{7.35 \times 10^{22}} = \left(\frac{5.97}{7.35}\right) \times 10^2 \approx 0.81224 \times 10^2 \approx \mathbf{81.2\text{ times}}$ [A2]

(b) Combined mass: [2 marks]
Convert to same power: $7.35 \times 10^{22} = 0.0735 \times 10^{24}$ [M1]
$M + m = (5.97 + 0.0735) \times 10^{24} = \mathbf{6.04 \times 10^{24}\text{ kg}}$ (to 3 s.f.) [A1]

(c) Difference: [1 mark]
$M - m = (5.97 - 0.0735) \times 10^{24} = \mathbf{5.90 \times 10^{24}\text{ kg}}$ (to 3 s.f.) [A1]

Criterion B Question 10 (Investigating Radicals · 5 marks)

Consider the expression $P_n = (\sqrt{n+1} - \sqrt{n})(\sqrt{n+1} + \sqrt{n})$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.
(a) Simplify $P_n$ algebraically for any positive integer $n$. [1 mark]
(b) Hence, show that $\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+1} + \sqrt{n}} = \sqrt{n+1} - \sqrt{n}$. [1 mark]
(c) Use this identity to evaluate the telescoping sum without a calculator: $$S = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} + 1} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2}} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{4} + \sqrt{3}} + \dots + \frac{1}{\sqrt{100} + \sqrt{99}}$$ Show all intermediate terms and state your final exact value. [3 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Difference of squares: [1 mark]
$P_n = (\sqrt{n+1})^2 - (\sqrt{n})^2 = (n + 1) - n = 1$ [A1]

(b) Conjugate reciprocal: [1 mark]
Divide both sides by $(\sqrt{n+1} + \sqrt{n})$:
$\sqrt{n+1} - \sqrt{n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n+1} + \sqrt{n}}$ $\blacksquare$ [R1]

(c) Telescoping sum evaluation: [3 marks]
Rewrite each term using the identity [M1]:
$T_1 = \sqrt{2} - 1$
$T_2 = \sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2}$
$T_3 = \sqrt{4} - \sqrt{3}$
$\dots$
$T_{99} = \sqrt{100} - \sqrt{99}$
Notice that all intermediate terms cancel in pairs [M1]:
$S = -1 + (\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{2}) + (\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{3}) + \dots + (\sqrt{99} - \sqrt{99}) + \sqrt{100}$
$S = -1 + \sqrt{100} = -1 + 10 = \mathbf{9}$ [A1]

Forms of Number Quick Reference

Scientific Notation / Standard Form

Definition: $$a \times 10^k, \quad 1 \le |a| < 10, \; k \in \mathbb{Z}$$
Multiplication: $$(a \times 10^p)(b \times 10^q) = (ab) \times 10^{p+q}$$
Division: $$\frac{a \times 10^p}{b \times 10^q} = \left(\frac{a}{b}\right) \times 10^{p-q}$$

Upper & Lower Error Bounds

Margin for unit $U$: $$\pm \frac{U}{2}$$
Error Interval: $$x - \frac{U}{2} \le x < x + \frac{U}{2}$$
Division Bound Rule: $$\text{UB}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right) = \frac{\text{UB}_A}{\text{LB}_B}, \quad \text{LB}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right) = \frac{\text{LB}_A}{\text{UB}_B}$$

Surd Laws & Arithmetic

Multiplication / Division: $$\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}, \quad \sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}$$
Simplification: $$\sqrt{k^2 m} = k\sqrt{m}$$

Conjugate Rationalisation

Monomial: $$\frac{c}{\sqrt{a}} = \frac{c\sqrt{a}}{a}$$
Binomial Conjugate: $$\frac{c}{a + \sqrt{b}} \times \frac{a - \sqrt{b}}{a - \sqrt{b}} = \frac{c(a - \sqrt{b})}{a^2 - b}$$

Top 5 Common MYP Pitfalls

1. Violating the Range of $a$ in $a \times 10^k$

Writing $12.5 \times 10^4$ or $0.75 \times 10^{-3}$ loses Criterion C communication marks. The leading coefficient must satisfy $1 \le |a| < 10$: write $1.25 \times 10^5$ and $7.5 \times 10^{-4}$.

2. Adding Radicands Under Square Roots

$\sqrt{a + b} \ne \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$! For example, $\sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5$, whereas $\sqrt{9} + \sqrt{16} = 3 + 4 = 7$. Square roots do not distribute across addition.

3. Mismatching Bounds in Division & Subtraction

To find the maximum of $\frac{A}{B}$, students often compute $\frac{\text{UB}_A}{\text{UB}_B}$. This is wrong! Dividing by a larger number reduces the result. The true maximum is always $\frac{\text{UB}_A}{\text{LB}_B}$.

4. Dropping Signs in Conjugate Products

In $(2 - \sqrt{7})(2 + \sqrt{7}) = 2^2 - (\sqrt{7})^2 = 4 - 7 = -3$. Do not accidentally write $4 + 7 = 11$. Remember that $(A - B)(A + B) = A^2 - B^2$.

Forms of Number Knowledge Check

Answer these 5 questions to test standard form, bounds, and surd simplification skills.

Question 1 of 5 Criterion A 1 Mark

Which of the following represents $(5.0 \times 10^7) \times (6.0 \times 10^{-3})$ correctly in standard form?

Explanation: $(5.0 \times 6.0) \times 10^{7 + (-3)} = 30 \times 10^4$. In standard form, $1 \le a < 10$, so $30 \times 10^4 = (3.0 \times 10^1) \times 10^4 = 3.0 \times 10^5$.
Question 2 of 5 Criterion A 1 Mark

What is the simplest radical form of $\sqrt{75} + \sqrt{48}$?

Explanation: $\sqrt{75} = \sqrt{25 \times 3} = 5\sqrt{3}$. $\sqrt{48} = \sqrt{16 \times 3} = 4\sqrt{3}$. $5\sqrt{3} + 4\sqrt{3} = 9\sqrt{3}$.
Question 3 of 5 Criterion A 1 Mark

A plank has a length of $3.6\text{ m}$ rounded to the nearest $0.1\text{ m}$. What is the lower bound of its length?

Explanation: For a rounding unit of $0.1\text{ m}$, the error margin is $\frac{0.1}{2} = 0.05\text{ m}$. $\text{Lower Bound} = 3.6 - 0.05 = 3.55\text{ m}$.
Question 4 of 5 Criterion A 1 Mark

What is the result of rationalising $\frac{10}{\sqrt{5} + 1}$?

Explanation: Multiply numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{5} - 1$: $$\frac{10(\sqrt{5} - 1)}{(\sqrt{5})^2 - 1^2} = \frac{10(\sqrt{5} - 1)}{5 - 1} = \frac{10(\sqrt{5} - 1)}{4} = \frac{5(\sqrt{5} - 1)}{2}$$
Question 5 of 5 Criterion D 1 Mark

A car travels distance $D = 120\text{ km}$ (to nearest $10\text{ km}$) in time $T = 2\text{ hours}$ (to nearest hour). What is the upper bound of the average speed?

Explanation: $D$ bounds: unit $10 \implies [115, 125)\text{ km}$. $\text{UB}(D) = 125\text{ km}$. $T$ bounds: unit $1 \implies [1.5, 2.5)\text{ h}$. $\text{LB}(T) = 1.5\text{ h}$. $\text{UB}(\text{Speed}) = \frac{\text{UB}(D)}{\text{LB}(T)} = \frac{125}{1.5} \approx 83.33\dots\text{ km/h}$.
