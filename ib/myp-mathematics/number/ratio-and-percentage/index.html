7.1

Direct and Inverse Proportionality Models

  • Direct Proportion ($y \propto x$): As variable $x$ scales by factor $c$, variable $y$ scales by the exact same factor: $$y = kx \iff \frac{y}{x} = k \quad (\text{constant } k > 0)$$ • The graph of $y$ against $x$ is a straight line passing through the origin $(0, 0)$ with gradient $k$.
    • Powers: $y \propto x^2 \iff y = kx^2$; $y \propto \sqrt{x} \iff y = k\sqrt{x}$.
  • Inverse Proportion ($y \propto \frac{1}{x}$): As variable $x$ multiplies, variable $y$ divides proportionally: $$y = \frac{k}{x} \iff xy = k \quad (\text{constant } k > 0)$$ • The graph of $y$ against $x$ is a rectangular hyperbola with asymptotes at $x = 0$ and $y = 0$.
    • Powers: $y \propto \frac{1}{x^2} \iff y = \frac{k}{x^2}$ (inverse-square laws e.g. gravitation, light intensity).

Example 1 · Criterion A

Solving Direct and Inverse Proportion Problems

(a) The electrical resistance $R$ of a wire is directly proportional to its length $L$. A wire of length $12\text{ m}$ has resistance $4.8\text{ }\Omega$. Find the resistance of a $35\text{ m}$ wire of the same material.
(b) The illumination intensity $I$ of a light source is inversely proportional to the square of distance $d$. At $d = 2\text{ m}$, $I = 150\text{ lux}$. Find $I$ when $d = 5\text{ m}$.

  1. (a) Direct proportion: $$R = kL \implies 4.8 = k(12) \implies k = \frac{4.8}{12} = 0.4\text{ }\Omega\text{ m}^{-1}$$ For $L = 35\text{ m}$: $$R = 0.4 \times 35 = 14\text{ }\Omega$$
  2. (b) Inverse-square proportion: $$I = \frac{k}{d^2} \implies 150 = \frac{k}{2^2} \implies k = 150 \times 4 = 600\text{ lux m}^2$$ For $d = 5\text{ m}$: $$I = \frac{600}{5^2} = \frac{600}{25} = 24\text{ lux}$$

Answers: (a) $14\text{ }\Omega$; (b) $24\text{ lux}$.

7.2

Percentage Change & Reverse Percentages

  • Decimal Multipliers:
    • An increase of $r\%$ corresponds to multiplier $M = 1 + \frac{r}{100}$ (e.g., $+15\% \to \times 1.15$).
    • A decrease of $r\%$ corresponds to multiplier $M = 1 - \frac{r}{100}$ (e.g., $-20\% \to \times 0.80$).
  • Percentage Change Formula: $$\% \text{ Change} = \frac{\text{New Value} - \text{Original Value}}{\text{Original Value}} \times 100\%$$
  • Reverse percentages: When given the final value after a percentage change and asked for the original value: $$\text{Original Value} = \frac{\text{Final Value}}{\text{Multiplier}} = \frac{\text{Final Value}}{1 \pm \frac{r}{100}}$$ Never calculate $r\%$ of the final value and add/subtract it!

Example 2 · Criterion A

Forward Multipliers and Reverse Percentages

(a) A retailer purchases a coat for $\$80$ and marks it up by $35\%$. Find the retail price.
(b) In a end-of-season sale, a laptop is discounted by $20\%$ to a sale price of $\$640$. Calculate the original price before the discount.

  1. (a) Markup: $$\text{Multiplier} = 1 + 0.35 = 1.35$$ $$\text{Retail Price} = \$80 \times 1.35 = \$108$$
  2. (b) Reverse percentage: $$\text{Multiplier for } 20\% \text{ reduction} = 1 - 0.20 = 0.80$$ $$\text{Original Price} = \frac{\$640}{0.80} = \$800$$ (Check: $20\%$ of $\$800 = \$160$; $\$800 - \$160 = \$640$ ✓).

Answers: (a) $\$108$; (b) $\$800$.

7.3

Financial Mathematics: Compound Interest & Depreciation

  • Compound Interest (with compounding frequency $k$ times/year): $$FV = PV \left(1 + \frac{r}{100k}\right)^{kn}$$ where:
    • $PV$ = Present Value (initial principal deposited/borrowed)
    • $FV$ = Future Value (total balance after interest)
    • $r$ = annual nominal interest rate (%)
    • $k$ = compounding periods per year (annual $k=1$, semi-annual $k=2$, quarterly $k=4$, monthly $k=12$, daily $k=365$)
    • $n$ = time in years
    • $\text{Total Interest Earned} = FV - PV$.
  • Depreciation (Reducing Balance Decay): For an asset losing $r\%$ of its value each year: $$V(n) = V_0 \left(1 - \frac{r}{100}\right)^n$$

Example 3 · Criterion A & D

Comparing Compounding Frequencies and Asset Depreciation

(a) $\$6000$ is invested for $4\text{ years}$ at $5.4\%$ p.a. compounded monthly ($k = 12$). Calculate the total interest earned to the nearest cent.
(b) A delivery van purchased for $\$32\,000$ depreciates at $15\%$ per annum. Calculate its book value after $3\text{ years}$.

  1. (a) Monthly compound interest: $$PV = 6000, \quad r = 5.4, \quad k = 12, \quad n = 4 \implies kn = 12 \times 4 = 48$$ $$\text{Periodic rate} = \frac{5.4}{12} = 0.45\% = 0.0045$$ $$FV = 6000(1 + 0.0045)^{48} = 6000(1.0045)^{48} \approx 6000 \times 1.240509 = \$7443.05$$ $$\text{Interest} = FV - PV = 7443.05 - 6000 = \mathbf{\$1443.05}$$
  2. (b) Vehicle depreciation: $$V_3 = 32000(1 - 0.15)^3 = 32000(0.85)^3 = 32000 \times 0.614125 = \mathbf{\$19\,652}$$

Answers: (a) $\$1443.05$; (b) $\$19\,652$.

A house is purchased for $\$450\,000$ and sold 5 years later for $\$558\,000$.
Calculate the percentage profit made on the sale.

Criterion A Question 4 (Standard · 4 marks)

Variable $y$ is inversely proportional to $x$. When $x = 8$, $y = 15$.
(a) Find the equation linking $y$ and $x$. [2 marks]
(b) Find $y$ when $x = 24$. [1 mark]
(c) Find $x$ when $y = 5$. [1 mark]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Formula: [2 marks]
$y = \frac{k}{x} \implies 15 = \frac{k}{8} \implies k = 15 \times 8 = 120$
$\mathbf{y = \frac{120}{x}}$ [A2]

(b) $y$ when $x = 24$: [1 mark]
$y = \frac{120}{24} = \mathbf{5}$ [A1]

(c) $x$ when $y = 5$: [1 mark]
$5 = \frac{120}{x} \implies x = \frac{120}{5} = \mathbf{24}$ [A1]

Criterion A Question 5 (Standard · 4 marks)

The braking distance $d$ (metres) of a vehicle is directly proportional to the square of its speed $v$ ($\text{km/h}$).
At a speed of $v = 40\text{ km/h}$, the braking distance is $d = 12\text{ m}$.
(a) Express $d$ in terms of $v$. [2 marks]
(b) Calculate the braking distance at $v = 100\text{ km/h}$. [2 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Constant of proportionality: [2 marks]
$d = k v^2 \implies 12 = k(40)^2 = 1600k$
$k = \frac{12}{1600} = \frac{3}{400} = 0.0075$
$\mathbf{d = 0.0075 v^2}$ [A2]

(b) Distance at 100 km/h: [2 marks]
$d = 0.0075 \times (100)^2 = 0.0075 \times 10\,000 = \mathbf{75\text{ metres}}$ [A2]

Criterion D Question 6 (Financial Comparison · 4 marks)

An investor has $\$10\,000$ to invest for $3\text{ years}$. Two banks offer different terms:
• Bank Alpha: $5.0\%$ simple interest per annum.
• Bank Beta: $4.8\%$ compound interest per annum compounded annually.
Calculate the interest earned from each bank and state which bank yields the higher return.

Show Full Markscheme

Bank Alpha (Simple Interest): [2 marks]
$I = \frac{P \cdot r \cdot t}{100} = \frac{10000 \times 5 \times 3}{100} = \mathbf{\$1500}$ [A2]

Bank Beta (Compound Interest): [2 marks]
$FV = 10000(1 + 0.048)^3 = 10000(1.048)^3 = 10000(1.15101) = \$11\,510.10$
$I = 11510.10 - 10000 = \mathbf{\$1510.10}$ [A1]
Bank Beta yields a higher return by $\$10.10$. [A1]

Criterion D Question 7 (Compound Periods · 5 marks)

A sum of $\$8000$ is deposited into a fixed deposit account paying $6\%$ annual nominal interest for $5\text{ years}$.
(a) Calculate the future value if interest is compounded quarterly ($k = 4$). [2 marks]
(b) Calculate the future value if interest is compounded monthly ($k = 12$). [2 marks]
(c) Find the additional interest earned by compounding monthly rather than quarterly. [1 mark]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Quarterly compounding: [2 marks]
$kn = 4 \times 5 = 20$; periodic rate $= \frac{6}{4} = 1.5\% = 0.015$
$FV = 8000(1.015)^{20} \approx 8000 \times 1.346855 = \mathbf{\$10\,774.84}$ [A2]

(b) Monthly compounding: [2 marks]
$kn = 12 \times 5 = 60$; periodic rate $= \frac{6}{12} = 0.5\% = 0.005$
$FV = 8000(1.005)^{60} \approx 8000 \times 1.348850 = \mathbf{\$10\,790.80}$ [A2]

(c) Difference: [1 mark]
$\text{Difference} = 10\,790.80 - 10\,774.84 = \mathbf{\$15.96}$ [A1]

Criterion D Question 8 (Industrial Equipment Depreciation · 4 marks)

A laser CNC cutting machine is purchased for $\$75\,000$. Its salvage value depreciates by $18\%$ per annum.
(a) Write a formula for its book value $V(t)$ after $t$ years. [1 mark]
(b) Calculate the value of the machine after $4\text{ years}$. [1 mark]
(c) Determine the minimum number of years after which the machine’s value will drop below $\$20\,000$. [2 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Formula: [1 mark]
$V(t) = 75000(1 - 0.18)^t = \mathbf{75000(0.82)^t}$ [A1]

(b) Value after 4 years: [1 mark]
$V(4) = 75000(0.82)^4 = 75000(0.45212) \approx \mathbf{\$33\,909.13}$ [A1]

(c) Time below $20\,000: [2 marks]
$75000(0.82)^t < 20000 \implies (0.82)^t < \frac{20000}{75000} = \frac{4}{15} \approx 0.2667$
$t \ln(0.82) < \ln(4/15) \implies t > \frac{\ln(4/15)}{\ln(0.82)} = \frac{-1.32176}{-0.19845} \approx 6.66$
It drops below $\$20\,000$ after $\mathbf{7\text{ years}}$. [A2]

Criterion D Question 9 (Currency Exchange & Fees · 4 marks)

A traveler exchanges $\$1200\text{ USD}$ to Euros ($\text{EUR}$).
• Bank rate: $1\text{ USD} = 0.92\text{ EUR}$.
• The bank charges a flat commission fee of $2.5\%$ deducted from the USD amount before exchange.
(a) Calculate the amount of USD remaining after the commission fee is deducted. [2 marks]
(b) Calculate the total Euros received by the traveler. [2 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Fee deduction: [2 marks]
$\text{Commission fee} = 2.5\% \text{ of } 1200 = 0.025 \times 1200 = \$30$
$\text{Remaining USD} = 1200 - 30 = \mathbf{\$1170\text{ USD}}$ [A2]

(b) Currency conversion: [2 marks]
$\text{EUR} = 1170 \times 0.92 = \mathbf{1076.40\text{ EUR}}$ [A2]

Criterion B Question 10 (Successive Discounts vs Single Discount · 4 marks)

A department store advertises a "Double Discount Sale": an item is discounted by $20\%$, and customers with a loyalty card receive an additional $10\%$ off the sale price.
(a) Show that the combined overall discount is $28\%$ and NOT $30\%$. [2 marks]
(b) A loyalty customer pays $\$144$ for a jacket under this promotion. Find the original price of the jacket. [2 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Combined multiplier: [2 marks]
Multiplier $1 = 1 - 0.20 = 0.80$
Multiplier $2 = 1 - 0.10 = 0.90$
$\text{Overall Multiplier} = 0.80 \times 0.90 = 0.72$
$\text{Overall discount} = (1 - 0.72) \times 100\% = \mathbf{28\%} \ne 30\%$ $\blacksquare$ [R2]

(b) Original price: [2 marks]
$\text{Original} = \frac{\$144}{0.72} = \mathbf{\$200}$ [A2]

Ratio & Percentage Quick Reference

Proportionality Models

Direct Proportion: $$y = kx \iff \frac{y}{x} = k$$
Inverse Proportion: $$y = \frac{k}{x} \iff xy = k$$

Percentage Rules

Multiplier: $$M = 1 \pm \frac{r}{100}$$
% Change: $$\frac{\text{New} - \text{Original}}{\text{Original}} \times 100\%$$
Reverse %: $$\text{Original} = \frac{\text{Final}}{M}$$

Compound Interest

Periodic Compounding: $$FV = PV \left(1 + \frac{r}{100k}\right)^{kn}$$
Frequencies $k$: $$\text{Quarterly } k=4, \text{ Monthly } k=12$$

Depreciation (Decay)

Book Value: $$V = V_0 \left(1 - \frac{r}{100}\right)^n$$

Top 4 Percentage Pitfalls

1. The Reverse Percentage Subtraction Error

If a price increases by $20\%$ to $\$120$, the original is NOT $\$120 - 20\%(\$120) = \$96$. The original is $\frac{120}{1.20} = \$100$. Percentages apply to the original base, not the final result.

2. Adding Successive Percentages

A $10\%$ rise followed by another $10\%$ rise is NOT a $20\%$ rise! Multipliers multiply: $1.10 \times 1.10 = 1.21$, which is a $21\%$ overall rise.

3. Forgetting Compounding Multiplier in Exponent

For monthly compounding over $3$ years, the exponent is $12 \times 3 = 36$ periods, and rate is divided by $12$. Writing $(1 + 0.06)^3$ calculates annual compounding, missing monthly earnings.

4. Confusing Direct vs Inverse Graphs

Direct proportion graphs are straight lines through $(0,0)$. Inverse proportion graphs are curves that never touch the axes.

Ratio & Percentage Check

Answer these 5 questions to test ratio division, proportion, percentage change, and financial interest.

Question 1 of 5 Criterion A 1 Mark

A jacket on sale for $\$180$ has been discounted by $25\%$. What was the original price of the jacket?

Explanation: $\text{Multiplier} = 1 - 0.25 = 0.75$. $\text{Original} = \frac{\$180}{0.75} = \$240$.
Question 2 of 5 Criterion A 1 Mark

If $y$ is inversely proportional to $x$ and $y = 6$ when $x = 10$, what is the value of $y$ when $x = 15$?

Explanation: $xy = k \implies k = 10 \times 6 = 60$. When $x = 15$: $y = \frac{60}{15} = 4$.
Question 3 of 5 Criterion A 1 Mark

Divide $\$450$ in the ratio $2 : 3 : 4$. What is the largest share?

Explanation: Total parts $= 2 + 3 + 4 = 9$. Value per part $= \frac{450}{9} = \$50$. Largest share $= 4 \times \$50 = \$200$.
Question 4 of 5 Criterion D 1 Mark

An investment of $\$4000$ earns $5\%$ compound interest compounded annually. What is its total value after $2\text{ years}$?

Explanation: $FV = 4000(1.05)^2 = 4000(1.1025) = \$4410$.
Question 5 of 5 Criterion D 1 Mark

A car bought for $\$25\,000$ depreciates by $10\%$ in year 1 and by $10\%$ in year 2. What is its value at the end of year 2?

Explanation: $V = 25000(0.90)^2 = 25000(0.81) = \$20\,250$.
