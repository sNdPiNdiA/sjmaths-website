Integer Exponents & Index Laws

Laws of indices for positive, negative, and zero exponent powers, simplifying multi-variable expressions, and solving exponential equations with common bases.

Statement of Inquiry Establishing generalized operational rules for exponents enables the logical simplification of complex relationships.
3.1

The Fundamental Laws of Indices

  • The Power Concept: For base $a$ and exponent $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$: $$a^n = \underbrace{a \times a \times \dots \times a}_{n \text{ factors}}$$ Critical Sign Distinction: $(-3)^2 = (-3) \times (-3) = +9$, whereas $-3^2 = -(3 \times 3) = -9$. Order of operations applies exponentiation before unary negation.
  • Law 1 (Product Law): When multiplying terms with identical bases, add their exponents: $$a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$$
  • Law 2 (Quotient Law): When dividing terms with identical bases, subtract the denominator exponent: $$\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n} \quad (a \ne 0)$$
  • Law 3 (Power of a Power): Multiply exponents when raising a power to another power: $$(a^m)^n = a^{m \cdot n}$$
  • Law 4 (Power of a Product & Quotient): Exponents distribute across multiplication and division: $$(ab)^n = a^n b^n, \quad \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n = \frac{a^n}{b^n} \quad (b \ne 0)$$

Example 1 · Criterion A

Simplifying Multi-Variable Index Expressions

Simplify the algebraic expression, expressing your final answer with positive indices only: $$\frac{(2x^3 y^{-2})^4 \times (3x^{-4} y^5)^2}{6x^{-5} y^3}$$

  1. Step 1: Distribute powers across brackets: $$(2x^3 y^{-2})^4 = 2^4 \cdot x^{3 \times 4} \cdot y^{(-2) \times 4} = 16 x^{12} y^{-8}$$ $$(3x^{-4} y^5)^2 = 3^2 \cdot x^{(-4) \times 2} \cdot y^{5 \times 2} = 9 x^{-8} y^{10}$$
  2. Step 2: Multiply the numerator terms: $$\text{Numerator} = (16 \times 9) \cdot x^{12 + (-8)} \cdot y^{-8 + 10} = 144 x^4 y^2$$
  3. Step 3: Divide by denominator: $$\frac{144 x^4 y^2}{6 x^{-5} y^3} = \left(\frac{144}{6}\right) \cdot x^{4 - (-5)} \cdot y^{2 - 3} = 24 x^9 y^{-1}$$
  4. Step 4: Express with positive indices: $$24 x^9 y^{-1} = \frac{24x^9}{y}$$

Answer: $\frac{24x^9}{y}$.

3.2

Zero and Negative Integer Exponents (Logical Proofs)

  • Proof of the Zero Exponent Law ($a^0 = 1$): Using the quotient law on equal powers: $$\frac{a^n}{a^n} = a^{n - n} = a^0 \quad \text{for any } a \ne 0$$ Any non-zero quantity divided by itself equals $1$: $$\frac{a^n}{a^n} = 1 \implies a^0 = 1 \quad (a \ne 0)$$ Note: $0^0$ is mathematically indeterminate.
  • Proof of the Negative Exponent Law ($a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$): Applying the quotient law with $m = 0$: $$\frac{1}{a^n} = \frac{a^0}{a^n} = a^{0 - n} = a^{-n}$$ Therefore, a negative exponent represents the multiplicative inverse (reciprocal): $$a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}, \quad \frac{1}{a^{-n}} = a^n$$
  • Fractional Bases with Negative Exponents: $$\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{-n} = \frac{1}{\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n} = \frac{1}{\frac{a^n}{b^n}} = \frac{b^n}{a^n} = \left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^n$$ Inverting the base fraction flips the sign of the exponent.

Example 2 · Criterion A & C

Evaluating Negative Powers and Fractions

Evaluate each expression as an exact integer or fraction:
(a) $5^{-3}$   (b) $\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{-2}$   (c) $\frac{2^{-1} + 3^{-1}}{2^{-1} - 3^{-1}}$

  1. (a) Simple reciprocal: $$5^{-3} = \frac{1}{5^3} = \frac{1}{125}$$
  2. (b) Inverted fraction: $$\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{-2} = \left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^2 = \frac{16}{9} = 1\frac{7}{9}$$
  3. (c) Compound negative powers: $$2^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}, \quad 3^{-1} = \frac{1}{3}$$ $$\text{Numerator} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{3 + 2}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$$ $$\text{Denominator} = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{3 - 2}{6} = \frac{1}{6}$$ $$\frac{\frac{5}{6}}{\frac{1}{6}} = \frac{5}{6} \times \frac{6}{1} = 5$$

Answers: (a) $\frac{1}{125}$; (b) $\frac{16}{9}$; (c) $5$.

3.3

Solving Exponential Equations with Common Bases

  • Base Equivalence Principle: $$\text{If } a^{f(x)} = a^{g(x)} \quad \text{and } a \notin \{-1, 0, 1\}, \quad \text{then } f(x) = g(x)$$
  • General Solving Strategy:
    • Step 1: Identify a common prime base (e.g., $4, 8, 16, 32 \to 2$; $9, 27, 81 \to 3$; $25, 125 \to 5$).
    • Step 2: Express both sides as single powers of that base using $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$.
    • Step 3: Equate exponents and solve the resulting linear or quadratic equation.
  • Factoring Exponential Equations: When powers with sums appear (e.g., $2^{x+2} + 2^x = 40$), factor out the common power $2^x$: $$2^x(2^2 + 1) = 40 \implies 2^x(5) = 40 \implies 2^x = 8 = 2^3 \implies x = 3$$

Example 3 · Criterion A

Solving Base Equivalence Equations

Solve for $x \in \mathbb{R}$:
(a) $9^{2x - 1} = 27^{x + 2}$
(b) $4^{x+1} - 4^{x-1} = 60$

  1. (a) Common base $3$: $$9 = 3^2, \quad 27 = 3^3$$ $$(3^2)^{2x - 1} = (3^3)^{x + 2} \implies 3^{2(2x - 1)} = 3^{3(x + 2)}$$ $$3^{4x - 2} = 3^{3x + 6}$$ Equate exponents: $$4x - 2 = 3x + 6 \implies 4x - 3x = 6 + 2 \implies x = 8$$
  2. (b) Factoring common factor $4^{x-1}$ or $4^x$: $$4 \cdot 4^x - \frac{1}{4} \cdot 4^x = 60$$ $$4^x\left(4 - \frac{1}{4}\right) = 60 \implies 4^x\left(\frac{15}{4}\right) = 60$$ $$4^x = 60 \times \frac{4}{15} = 4 \times 4 = 16$$ $$4^x = 4^2 \implies x = 2$$

Answers: (a) $x = 8$; (b) $x = 2$.

Criterion B Question 6 (Pattern Investigation · 5 marks)

Investigate the last digit (units digit) of powers of $7$:
• $7^1 = 7$, $7^2 = 49$, $7^3 = 343$, $7^4 = 2401$, $7^5 = 16807$.
(a) State the repeating pattern and period length of the units digit of $7^n$. [2 marks]
(b) Predict the units digit of $7^{82}$. Justify your reasoning using modular division. [2 marks]
(c) State the units digit of $7^{2025} + 3$. [1 mark]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Pattern and cycle length: [2 marks]
The units digits cycle as $\{7, 9, 3, 1\}$ with period length $T = 4$. [A2]

(b) Units digit of $7^{82}$: [2 marks]
Divide exponent by period: $82 \div 4 = 20\text{ remainder } 2$ [M1].
The remainder of $2$ corresponds to the 2nd digit in the cycle $\implies \mathbf{9}$. [A1]

(c) Units digit of $7^{2025} + 3$: [1 mark]
$2025 \div 4 = 506\text{ remainder } 1$. Last digit of $7^{2025}$ is $7$.
Units digit of $7 + 3 = 10 \implies \mathbf{0}$. [A1]

Criterion C Question 7 (Factoring Powers · 4 marks)

Show that for all positive integers $n$, the expression $\frac{3^{n+3} - 3^{n+1}}{2 \cdot 3^n}$ is an integer constant independent of $n$. Find its value.

Show Full Markscheme

Step 1: Factor out $3^n$ from numerator [2 marks]
$3^{n+3} = 3^n \cdot 3^3 = 27 \cdot 3^n$
$3^{n+1} = 3^n \cdot 3^1 = 3 \cdot 3^n$
$\text{Numerator} = 3^n(27 - 3) = 24 \cdot 3^n$ [A2]

Step 2: Simplify with denominator [2 marks]
$$\frac{24 \cdot 3^n}{2 \cdot 3^n} = \frac{24}{2} = 12$$ Since $12$ contains no variable $n$, the expression is constant for all $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$. [A2]

Criterion A Question 8 (Extended · 4 marks)

Solve the equation for $x$: $$3^{2x} - 10 \cdot 3^x + 9 = 0$$

Let $u = 3^x$. Then $3^{2x} = (3^x)^2 = u^2$ [M1]: $$u^2 - 10u + 9 = 0$$

Criterion D Question 9 (Computer Science Application · 4 marks)

In computer architecture, data storage units are defined in base 2:
• $1\text{ Kilobyte (KB)} = 2^{10}\text{ bytes}$
• $1\text{ Megabyte (MB)} = 2^{20}\text{ bytes}$
• $1\text{ Gigabyte (GB)} = 2^{30}\text{ bytes}$
(a) How many Kilobytes are in $1\text{ Gigabyte}$? Express your answer as a power of 2, then as an ordinary integer. [2 marks]
(b) A solid-state drive has capacity $64\text{ GB}$. Express its total byte capacity in standard form $a \times 10^k$ to 3 significant figures. [2 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Kilobytes in 1 GB: [2 marks]
$\text{Ratio} = \frac{2^{30}\text{ bytes}}{2^{10}\text{ bytes}} = 2^{30 - 10} = \mathbf{2^{20}\text{ KB}}$ [A1]
$2^{20} = (2^{10})^2 = 1024^2 = \mathbf{1\,048\,576\text{ KB}}$ [A1]

(b) Total bytes of 64 GB SSD: [2 marks]
$64\text{ GB} = 2^6 \times 2^{30} = 2^{36}\text{ bytes}$ [M1]
$2^{36} = 68\,719\,476\,736\text{ bytes} \approx \mathbf{6.87 \times 10^{10}\text{ bytes}}$ [A1]

Criterion D Question 10 (Cell Biology Modeling · 5 marks)

A strain of bacteria undergoes binary fission, doubling in population every $30\text{ minutes}$. An initial culture contains $N_0 = 500$ bacteria.
(a) Write a formula for the population $N(t)$ after $t$ hours. [2 marks]
(b) Calculate the population after $4\text{ hours}$. [1 mark]
(c) Determine after how many hours the culture population will reach $128\,000$. [2 marks]

Show Full Markscheme

(a) Model formula: [2 marks]
In $t$ hours, the number of 30-minute intervals is $2t$.
$N(t) = 500 \times 2^{2t}$ [A2]

(b) Population at $t = 4$: [1 mark]
$N(4) = 500 \times 2^{2(4)} = 500 \times 2^8 = 500 \times 256 = \mathbf{128\,000\text{ bacteria}}$ [A1]

(c) Time to reach $128\,000$: [2 marks]
$500 \times 2^{2t} = 128\,000 \implies 2^{2t} = \frac{128\,000}{500} = 256 = 2^8$ [M1]
$2t = 8 \implies t = \mathbf{4\text{ hours}}$ [A1]

Index Laws Quick Reference

Operational Index Laws

Product Rule: $$a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$$
Quotient Rule: $$\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n} \quad (a \ne 0)$$
Power to a Power: $$(a^m)^n = a^{m \cdot n}$$

Zero & Negative Powers

Zero Power: $$a^0 = 1 \quad (a \ne 0)$$
Reciprocal / Negative: $$a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}, \quad \frac{1}{a^{-n}} = a^n$$
Fraction Inversion: $$\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{-n} = \left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^n$$

Distributive Laws

Product Power: $$(ab)^n = a^n b^n$$
Quotient Power: $$\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n = \frac{a^n}{b^n} \quad (b \ne 0)$$

Base Equivalence Rule

Equality of Exponents: $$a^x = a^y \implies x = y \quad (a \ne 0, \pm 1)$$
Common Base Conversions: $$4 = 2^2, \; 8 = 2^3, \; 9 = 3^2, \; 27 = 3^3$$

Top 5 Common Student Traps

1. Confusing Powers of Negatives: $(-2)^4$ vs $-2^4$

$(-2)^4 = (-2)(-2)(-2)(-2) = +16$, but $-2^4 = -(2^4) = -16$. Brackets dictate whether the negative sign is part of the base!

2. Adding Bases in Product Rule

$2^3 \times 2^4 = 2^{3+4} = 2^7$. Students often write $4^7$ by multiplying the bases together. The base remains unchanged.

3. Misunderstanding $(a + b)^n$

$(a + b)^2 \ne a^2 + b^2$! Exponents distribute across multiplication and division, NOT across addition or subtraction.

4. Applying Negative Exponents to Coefficients

In $5x^{-2}$, only $x$ has the negative exponent: $5x^{-2} = \frac{5}{x^2}$. It is NOT $\frac{1}{5x^2}$ unless grouped as $(5x)^{-2}$.

Integer Exponents Check

Solve these 5 questions to test index laws, negative powers, and exponential equations.

Question 1 of 5 Criterion A 1 Mark

Which expression is equivalent to $\frac{x^4 \times (x^2)^{-3}}{x^{-5}}$?

Explanation: $\text{Numerator} = x^4 \times x^{2 \times (-3)} = x^4 \times x^{-6} = x^{4 - 6} = x^{-2}$. $\text{Division} = \frac{x^{-2}}{x^{-5}} = x^{-2 - (-5)} = x^3$.
Question 2 of 5 Criterion A 1 Mark

What is the exact value of $\left(\frac{2}{5}\right)^{-3}$?

Explanation: Invert the fraction to flip exponent sign: $\left(\frac{2}{5}\right)^{-3} = \left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^3 = \frac{5^3}{2^3} = \frac{125}{8}$.
Question 3 of 5 Criterion A 1 Mark

If $3^{x+2} = \frac{1}{27}$, what is the value of $x$?

Explanation: $\frac{1}{27} = \frac{1}{3^3} = 3^{-3}$. $3^{x+2} = 3^{-3} \implies x + 2 = -3 \implies x = -5$.
Question 4 of 5 Criterion A 1 Mark

Solve for $x$: $4^{x-1} = 8^{2x+1}$.

Explanation: Base 2: $(2^2)^{x-1} = (2^3)^{2x+1} \implies 2^{2x-2} = 2^{6x+3}$. $2x - 2 = 6x + 3 \implies 4x = -5 \implies x = -\frac{5}{4}$.
Question 5 of 5 Criterion C 1 Mark

Which expression simplifies $2^{n+1} + 2^n$ to a single term?

Explanation: Factor out $2^n$: $2^{n+1} + 2^n = 2^n(2^1 + 1) = 3 \cdot 2^n$.
