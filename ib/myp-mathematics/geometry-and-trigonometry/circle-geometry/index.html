Circle Geometry: Theorems, Tangents & Chords

Angle at the Center & The Semicircle Theorem

Every arc of a circle subtends angles both at the center of the circle and at any point along the remaining circumference.

The Central Angle Theorem:
The angle subtended by an arc at the center of a circle is twice the angle subtended by it at any point on the circumference: $$\angle AOB = 2 \times \angle APB$$

Proof Sketch (Deductive Reasoning): Draw the radius $OP$. Triangle $OAP$ is isosceles ($OA = OP = r$), so $\angle OAP = \angle OPA = x$. Triangle $OBP$ is also isosceles ($OB = OP = r$), so $\angle OBP = \angle OPB = y$. The exterior angle of $\triangle OAP$ at $O$ is $2x$, and for $\triangle OBP$ it is $2y$. Hence: $$\angle AOB = 2x + 2y = 2(x + y) = 2\angle APB$$

Angle in a Semicircle (Thales's Theorem):
If $AB$ is a diameter passing through center $O$, the angle at the center is a straight angle ($180^\circ$). Therefore, the angle subtended at the circumference is always a right angle: $$\angle APB = 90^\circ$$

The angle at the center ($2\theta$) is double the angle on the circumference ($\theta$) subtended by arc $AB$.

2

Angles in the Same Segment & Cyclic Quadrilaterals

When multiple angles are subtended by the same chord or arc into the same segment, they share equal angular measures.

Angles in the Same Segment:
Angles subtended by the same arc (or chord) at the circumference in the same segment are equal: $$\angle APB = \angle AQB$$
Cyclic Quadrilateral Theorems:
A quadrilateral whose four vertices all lie on a circle is called a cyclic quadrilateral.
  • Opposite angles are supplementary: $\angle A + \angle C = 180^\circ$ and $\angle B + \angle D = 180^\circ$.
  • Exterior angle property: An exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral is equal to the interior opposite angle.

In any cyclic quadrilateral, opposite pairs of vertices sum to $180^\circ$.

3

Tangents, Radii & The Alternate Segment Theorem

A tangent is a straight line that touches the circle at exactly one point (point of tangency).

Key Tangent Properties:
  1. Perpendicular Radius: The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact ($OT \perp PT \implies \angle OTP = 90^\circ$).
  2. Tangents from an External Point: Tangents drawn to a circle from an external point $P$ to points of contact $T_1$ and $T_2$ are equal in length: $PT_1 = PT_2$.
  3. The line connecting $P$ to center $O$ bisects $\angle T_1 P T_2$ and $\angle T_1 O T_2$.
The Alternate Segment Theorem (Extended):
The angle between a tangent and a chord drawn through the point of contact is equal to the angle subtended by the chord in the alternate segment: $$\angle TAB = \angle ACB$$

Alternate Segment Theorem: The angle between tangent and chord $AB$ equals the inscribed angle $\angle ACB$ in the alternate segment.

4

Chords & Intersecting Chords Theorem (Power of a Point)

Chords possess important symmetry properties and proportional relationships when they intersect inside or outside the circle.

Perpendicular Bisector of a Chord:
A line drawn from the center of a circle perpendicular to a chord bisects the chord: $$OM \perp AB \implies AM = MB = \frac{1}{2}AB$$ Conversely, the perpendicular bisector of any chord passes through the center $O$.
Intersecting Chords & Secants (Power of a Point):
  • Internal Intersection: If chords $AB$ and $CD$ intersect at point $P$ inside the circle: $$PA \times PB = PC \times PD$$
  • External Secant Intersection: If secants $PAB$ and $PCD$ meet at external point $P$: $$PA \times PB = PC \times PD$$
  • Tangent-Secant Theorem: If a secant $PAB$ and tangent $PT$ meet at external point $P$: $$PT^2 = PA \times PB$$

Worked Example 1: Multi-Theorem Angle Deduction

In a circle with center $O$, points $A$, $B$, and $C$ lie on the circumference. Line $TA$ is a tangent to the circle at point $A$. Given that $\angle TAB = 52^\circ$ and $\angle OBC = 28^\circ$, calculate:
a) $\angle ACB$
b) $\angle AOB$
c) $\angle BAC$

Step-by-step Solution:

Part a: By the Alternate Segment Theorem, the angle between the tangent $TA$ and the chord $AB$ equals the angle subtended by $AB$ in the alternate segment:
$\angle ACB = \angle TAB = 52^\circ$ [Criterion A: Knowledge & Understanding]

Part b: The angle subtended by arc $AB$ at the center $O$ is double the angle subtended at the circumference by the same arc:
$\angle AOB = 2 \times \angle ACB = 2 \times 52^\circ = 104^\circ$

Part c: In $\triangle OBC$, $OB = OC$ (both are radii of the circle), so $\triangle OBC$ is isosceles.
Therefore, $\angle OCB = \angle OBC = 28^\circ$.
Now, $\angle ACO = \angle ACB - \angle OCB = 52^\circ - 28^\circ = 24^\circ$.
In $\triangle OAC$, $OA = OC$ (both radii), so $\triangle OAC$ is isosceles $\implies \angle OAC = \angle ACO = 24^\circ$.
In $\triangle OAB$, $OA = OB$ (both radii), so $\triangle OAB$ is isosceles with vertex angle $\angle AOB = 104^\circ$.
Base angles: $\angle OAB = \frac{180^\circ - 104^\circ}{2} = \frac{76^\circ}{2} = 38^\circ$.
Therefore, $\angle BAC = \angle OAB + \angle OAC = 38^\circ + 24^\circ = 62^\circ$. [Criterion C: Clear Mathematical Communication]

Worked Example 2: Extended Tangent-Secant Theorem

A tangent $PT$ of length $12\text{ cm}$ touches a circle at $T$. A secant line from $P$ passes through the circle, intersecting it at points $A$ and $B$, where $A$ lies between $P$ and $B$. If $PA = 8\text{ cm}$, determine the length of chord $AB$.

Step-by-step Solution:

By the Tangent-Secant Theorem: $$PT^2 = PA \times PB$$ Substitute known lengths: $$12^2 = 8 \times PB$$ $$144 = 8 \times PB \implies PB = \frac{144}{8} = 18\text{ cm}$$ Since $P$, $A$, and $B$ are collinear and $A$ lies between $P$ and $B$: $$PB = PA + AB \implies 18 = 8 + AB \implies AB = 10\text{ cm}$$ [Criterion A: Accurate Computation]

Tiered Practice Exercises

10 carefully sequenced problems advancing from foundational angle theorem applications to complex proofs and algebraic geometric modelling.

Tier 1: Standard Exercise 1

Points $A$, $B$, and $C$ lie on a circle with center $O$. If $\angle AOB = 136^\circ$, find the size of $\angle ACB$ subtended at the circumference.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

The angle subtended at the circumference is half the angle subtended at the center: [M1]
$\angle ACB = \frac{1}{2} \times \angle AOB = \frac{1}{2} \times 136^\circ = 68^\circ$. [A1]

Tier 1: Standard Exercise 2

In a circle, $AB$ is a diameter and $C$ is a point on the circumference. If $\angle ABC = 38^\circ$, calculate $\angle BAC$.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

By the angle in a semicircle theorem, $\angle ACB = 90^\circ$. [M1]
The sum of angles in $\triangle ABC$ is $180^\circ$:
$\angle BAC = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 38^\circ = 52^\circ$. [A1]

Tier 1: Standard Exercise 3

Quadrilateral $PQRS$ is cyclic. If $\angle P = (2x + 15)^\circ$ and $\angle R = (3x - 10)^\circ$, find the value of $x$ and the measure of angle $P$.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral sum to $180^\circ$: [M1]
$(2x + 15) + (3x - 10) = 180$
$5x + 5 = 180 \implies 5x = 175 \implies x = 35$. [A1]
$\angle P = 2(35) + 15 = 70 + 15 = 85^\circ$. [A1]

Tier 2: Intermediate Exercise 4

Tangents $PA$ and $PB$ are drawn to a circle with center $O$ from an external point $P$. If $\angle APB = 46^\circ$, find the measure of the reflex angle $\angle AOB$.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

Tangents are perpendicular to radii: $\angle OAP = 90^\circ$ and $\angle OBP = 90^\circ$. [M1]
In quadrilateral $OAPB$, angles sum to $360^\circ$:
$\angle AOB = 360^\circ - 90^\circ - 90^\circ - 46^\circ = 180^\circ - 46^\circ = 134^\circ$. [M1]
Reflex $\angle AOB = 360^\circ - 134^\circ = 226^\circ$. [A1]

Tier 2: Intermediate Exercise 5

A line $XY$ is tangent to a circle at $B$. Chord $AB$ creates an angle $\angle ABX = 64^\circ$. Point $C$ is on the major arc $AB$. Find $\angle ACB$ and state the theorem used.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

By the Alternate Segment Theorem, the angle between the tangent $XY$ and chord $AB$ equals the angle subtended by chord $AB$ in the alternate segment: [M1][R1]
$\angle ACB = \angle ABX = 64^\circ$. [A1]

Tier 2: Intermediate Exercise 6

A circle has center $O$ and radius $13\text{ cm}$. A chord $CD$ is located $5\text{ cm}$ away from the center (perpendicular distance). Calculate the length of the chord $CD$.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

Let $M$ be the midpoint of $CD$. $OM \perp CD$ and $OM = 5\text{ cm}$, $OC = 13\text{ cm}$. [M1]
In right-angled triangle $\triangle OMC$, by Pythagoras:
$MC^2 = OC^2 - OM^2 = 13^2 - 5^2 = 169 - 25 = 144 \implies MC = 12\text{ cm}$. [A1]
Since the perpendicular from the center bisects the chord, $CD = 2 \times MC = 2 \times 12 = 24\text{ cm}$. [A1]

Tier 2: Intermediate Exercise 7

Two chords $AB$ and $CD$ intersect inside a circle at point $X$. If $AX = 4\text{ cm}$, $XB = 9\text{ cm}$, and $CX = 3\text{ cm}$, find the length of segment $XD$ and the total length of chord $CD$.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

By the Intersecting Chords Theorem: [M1]
$AX \times XB = CX \times XD$
$4 \times 9 = 3 \times XD \implies 36 = 3 \times XD \implies XD = 12\text{ cm}$. [A1]
Total chord length $CD = CX + XD = 3 + 12 = 15\text{ cm}$. [A1]

Tier 3: Extended Exercise 8

A secant line from an external point $P$ intersects a circle at $A$ and $B$ (with $PA < PB$). A second secant from $P$ passes through the center $O$ of the circle, intersecting it at $C$ and $D$. If $PA = 4\text{ cm}$, $AB = 8\text{ cm}$, and the circle has radius $r = 5\text{ cm}$, determine the distance $PO$ from point $P$ to the center of the circle.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

Let $d = PO$. The secant through the center intersects the circle at $C$ (near point) and $D$ (far point):
$PC = d - r = d - 5$ and $PD = d + r = d + 5$. [M1]
Total length $PB = PA + AB = 4 + 8 = 12\text{ cm}$.
By the Intersecting Secants Theorem (Power of a Point): [M1]
$PA \times PB = PC \times PD$
$4 \times 12 = (d - 5)(d + 5)$
$48 = d^2 - 25 \implies d^2 = 73 \implies d = \sqrt{73} \approx 8.54\text{ cm}$. [A1][R1]

Tier 3: Extended Exercise 9

Points $A, B, C, D$ lie consecutively on a circle. Chords $AC$ and $BD$ intersect at right angles at $M$. Prove that $MA^2 + MB^2 + MC^2 + MD^2 = 4R^2$, where $R$ is the radius of the circle.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

Let diameter $AA'$ be drawn through $A$. Then $\angle A'CA = 90^\circ$ (angle in semicircle), so $AC \perp A'C$. [M1]
Since $BD \perp AC$, $A'C$ is parallel to $BD$.
In a circle, parallel chords trap congruent arcs, so chord $A'D = BC$. [M1]
In right-angled triangle $\triangle ACA'$, by Pythagoras: $AC^2 + A'C^2 = (2R)^2 = 4R^2$. [M1]
Substitute $A'C^2$ via right-angled triangle $\triangle A'CD$: $A'D^2 = BC^2$.
Also, in right triangles at $M$ with perpendicular chords:
$AB^2 = MA^2 + MB^2$, $BC^2 = MB^2 + MC^2$, $CD^2 = MC^2 + MD^2$, $DA^2 = MD^2 + MA^2$.
Summing these pairs reveals: $MA^2 + MB^2 + MC^2 + MD^2 = 4R^2$. [A1][R1]

Tier 3: Extended Exercise 10

Criterion B & D Investigation: An architect is designing a circular observation rotunda. Two sightlines from an observation tower $P$ outside the perimeter intersect the rotunda: one is tangent to the eastern edge at $T$, and the other passes through the center doors at $A$ and exits at $B$. If $PA = x$, $AB = 2x + 1$, and $PT = x + 4$, find $x$ and determine the rotunda's internal diameter $AB$.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

Total secant length $PB = PA + AB = x + (2x + 1) = 3x + 1$. [M1]
By the Tangent-Secant Theorem: $PT^2 = PA \times PB$ [M1]
$(x + 4)^2 = x(3x + 1)$
$x^2 + 8x + 16 = 3x^2 + x$
Rearrange into a quadratic equation: $2x^2 - 7x - 16 = 0$. [M1]
Using the quadratic formula: $x = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{(-7)^2 - 4(2)(-16)}}{2(2)} = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{49 + 128}}{4} = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{177}}{4}$.
Since distance must be positive: $x = \frac{7 + 13.304}{4} = \frac{20.304}{4} \approx 5.076\text{ m}$. [A1]
The diameter $AB = 2x + 1 = 2(5.076) + 1 \approx 11.15\text{ m}$. [A1][R1: Contextual reflection]

Top 5 Common MYP Circle Pitfalls

  • Confusing Central Angle with Circumference Angle: Students often set $\angle AOB = \frac{1}{2}\angle APB$ instead of $\angle AOB = 2\angle APB$. Remember: the central angle is always wider and opens more broadly than the angle at the circumference.
  • Assuming Any 4-Sided Shape Inside a Circle is Cyclic: All four vertices must lie directly on the circumference. If one vertex is at the center $O$ (e.g., $OABC$), it is NOT a cyclic quadrilateral and opposite angles do NOT sum to $180^\circ$.
  • Wrong Segments in Intersecting Chords / Secants: When applying $PA \cdot PB = PC \cdot PD$ externally, students often use $AB$ instead of the total secant length $PB = PA + AB$. Always measure all segments from the point of intersection $P$ to the circle!
  • Misidentifying the Alternate Segment: The angle between the tangent and chord $AB$ equals the angle inside the triangle subtended by $AB$ on the opposite (alternate) side, not the adjacent angle in the same segment.
  • Missing Hidden Isosceles Triangles: Any triangle formed by two radii from center $O$ to circumference points (e.g., $\triangle OAB$) is automatically an isosceles triangle ($OA = OB = r$). Always mark these equal sides immediately.

Self-Grading Mini Test

5 quiz questions on circle theorems. Choose an answer to see feedback and a worked solution.

Question 1 of 5 Angle at Center vs Circumference

In a circle with center $O$, points $A$ and $B$ lie on the circumference. If $\angle APB = 44^\circ$, what is the size of the central angle $\angle AOB$?

Explanation: The central angle is twice the inscribed angle on the circumference subtended by the same arc: $\angle AOB = 2 \times 44^\circ = 88^\circ$.

Question 2 of 5 Cyclic Quadrilateral

In a cyclic quadrilateral $ABCD$, $\angle A = 73^\circ$ and $\angle B = 105^\circ$. What is the measure of angle $C$?

Explanation: Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to $180^\circ$. Therefore, $\angle C = 180^\circ - \angle A = 180^\circ - 73^\circ = 107^\circ$.

Question 3 of 5 Angle in a Semicircle

Segment $AB$ is the diameter of a circle. Point $C$ lies on the circumference. If $\angle CAB = 32^\circ$, what is the measure of $\angle ABC$?

Explanation: Because $AB$ is the diameter, $\angle ACB = 90^\circ$. In right triangle $\triangle ABC$, $\angle ABC = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 32^\circ = 58^\circ$.

Question 4 of 5 Intersecting Chords Theorem

Chords $AB$ and $CD$ intersect inside a circle at $P$. If $PA = 3\text{ cm}$, $PB = 8\text{ cm}$, and $PC = 4\text{ cm}$, what is the length of $PD$?

Explanation: By the intersecting chords theorem: $PA \times PB = PC \times PD \implies 3 \times 8 = 4 \times PD \implies 24 = 4 \times PD \implies PD = 6\text{ cm}$.

Question 5 of 5 Tangent-Secant Theorem

From an external point $P$, tangent $PT = 6\text{ cm}$ touches a circle at $T$. A secant line from $P$ cuts the circle at $A$ and $B$. If $PA = 4\text{ cm}$, what is the length of chord $AB$?

Explanation: By the Tangent-Secant Theorem: $PT^2 = PA \times PB \implies 6^2 = 4 \times PB \implies 36 = 4 \times PB \implies PB = 9\text{ cm}$. Then $AB = PB - PA = 9 - 4 = 5\text{ cm}$.
