Polynomials: Division, Factor & Remainder Theorems

Perform algebraic long division, execute synthetic division, evaluate remainders without division via the Remainder Theorem, and factorise cubic polynomials with the Factor Theorem.

Statement of Inquiry Decomposing complex polynomial expressions into linear and quadratic factors through deductive logic provides exact solutions to higher-degree systems.
7.1

Polynomial Anatomy & The Division Algorithm

  • Polynomial Definition: An expression of the form: $$P(x) = a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0 \quad (a_n \ne 0)$$ Where $n \in \mathbb{N}$ is the degree, $a_n$ is the leading coefficient, and $a_0$ is the constant term.
  • The Division Algorithm for Polynomials: When a dividend polynomial $P(x)$ is divided by a divisor polynomial $D(x)$ ($D(x) \ne 0$), there exist unique polynomials $Q(x)$ (the quotient) and $R(x)$ (the remainder) such that: $$\mathbf{P(x) = D(x) \cdot Q(x) + R(x)}$$ Where either $R(x) = 0$ or $\deg(R(x)) < \deg(D(x))$. In fractional form: $$\frac{P(x)}{D(x)} = Q(x) + \frac{R(x)}{D(x)}$$
  • Degree Rule: If $\deg(P) = n$ and $\deg(D) = m$ ($n \ge m$), then $\deg(Q) = n - m$. If dividing by a linear divisor $D(x) = x - c$, the remainder $R$ is always a constant of degree $0$.
7.2

The Remainder Theorem

  • Statement of the Theorem: When a polynomial $P(x)$ is divided by a linear divisor of the form $(x - c)$, the constant remainder is: $$\mathbf{R = P(c)}$$
  • Proof: By the division algorithm, $P(x) = (x - c)Q(x) + R$. Substituting $x = c$: $$P(c) = (c - c)Q(c) + R = 0 \cdot Q(c) + R = R \quad \blacksquare$$
  • Generalisation for $(ax - b)$: When $P(x)$ is divided by $(ax - b)$, the remainder is: $$R = P\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)$$
7.3

The Factor Theorem

  • Statement of the Theorem: A linear binomial $(x - c)$ is a factor of the polynomial $P(x)$ if and only if the remainder is zero: $$\mathbf{(x - c) \text{ is a factor of } P(x) \iff P(c) = 0}$$
  • Root and Factor Duality: If $x = c$ is a root (zero) of the equation $P(x) = 0$, then $(x - c)$ is a factor of $P(x)$, and the graph of $y = P(x)$ passes through $(c, 0)$.
  • Rational Root Theorem (Heuristic for Guessing Roots): For a polynomial $a_n x^n + \dots + a_0$ with integer coefficients, any rational root $x = \frac{p}{q}$ (in simplest form) must satisfy: $$p \text{ is a factor of the constant term } a_0, \quad \text{and } q \text{ is a factor of the leading coefficient } a_n$$ For monic polynomials ($a_n = 1$), test integer factors of the constant term $a_0$: $\pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3, \dots$
7.4

Completely Factoring Cubics & Curve Sketching

  • Full 3-Step Cubic Factoring Algorithm:
    1. Find First Linear Factor: Use Factor Theorem to test small integer divisors of the constant term until $P(c) = 0$. This identifies factor $(x - c)$.
    2. Perform Polynomial Division: Divide $P(x)$ by $(x - c)$ using long division or synthetic division to obtain quadratic quotient $Q(x) = ax^2 + bx + d$.
    3. Decompose the Quadratic Factor: Factorise $Q(x)$ via $ac$ method or find remaining roots via quadratic formula: $$P(x) = (x - c)(x - r_2)(x - r_3)$$
  • Sketching Cubics: Identify real $x$-intercepts, $y$-intercept $(0, P(0))$, and end behaviour:
    • If $a > 0$: As $x \to \infty, y \to \infty$; as $x \to -\infty, y \to -\infty$ (starts bottom-left, finishes top-right).
    • If $a < 0$: Starts top-left, finishes bottom-right.
    • Repeated factor $(x - c)^2$: graph touches $x$-axis and turns around (tangent).

Worked Examples (Criterion A & C)

Example 1 Unknown Coefficients via Remainder Theorem

Problem: When $P(x) = 2x^3 + kx^2 - 5x + 6$ is divided by $(x - 2)$, the remainder is $16$. Find the value of constant $k$.

Step 1: Apply the Remainder Theorem

The remainder upon division by $(x - 2)$ is $P(2)$. Set $P(2) = 16$.

Step 2: Evaluate $P(2)$ $$P(2) = 2(2)^3 + k(2)^2 - 5(2) + 6 = 2(8) + 4k - 10 + 6 = 16 + 4k - 4 = 12 + 4k$$
Step 3: Solve for $k$ $$12 + 4k = 16 \implies 4k = 4 \implies \mathbf{k = 1}$$ Criterion A: Direct application of remainder theorem without costly long division
Example 2 Full Cubic Factorisation & Solving

Problem: Factorise completely and solve: $P(x) = x^3 - 4x^2 - 7x + 10 = 0$.

Step 1: Test factors of $10$ ($\pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 5, \pm 10$)

Test $x = 1$: $P(1) = (1)^3 - 4(1)^2 - 7(1) + 10 = 1 - 4 - 7 + 10 = 0$.

By the Factor Theorem, $\mathbf{(x - 1)}$ is a factor.

Step 2: Divide $P(x)$ by $(x - 1)$

Using polynomial long division:

$$(x^3 - 4x^2 - 7x + 10) \div (x - 1) = x^2 - 3x - 10$$
Step 3: Factorise the quadratic quotient $x^2 - 3x - 10$ $$x^2 - 3x - 10 = (x - 5)(x + 2)$$
Step 4: State complete factorisation and solutions $$P(x) = \mathbf{(x - 1)(x - 5)(x + 2)}$$

Roots to $P(x) = 0$: $\mathbf{x = 1, \quad x = 5, \quad x = -2}$.

Criterion C: Logical systematic workflow from root testing to complete factorisation

Question 1 Tier 1: Standard 3 Marks

Find the remainder when $P(x) = 3x^3 - 5x^2 + 2x - 7$ is divided by $(x - 2)$ without performing long division.

[M1] Apply Remainder Theorem: $R = P(2)$.
[M1] Substitute $x = 2$: $P(2) = 3(2)^3 - 5(2)^2 + 2(2) - 7 = 3(8) - 5(4) + 4 - 7$.
[A1] Calculate: $24 - 20 + 4 - 7 = \mathbf{1}$.
Question 2 Tier 1: Standard 3 Marks

Use the Factor Theorem to determine whether $(x + 3)$ is a factor of $P(x) = x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x - 6$.

View Full Method Markscheme
[M1] Test $x = -3$: Evaluate $P(-3)$.
[M1] $P(-3) = (-3)^3 + 2(-3)^2 - 5(-3) - 6 = -27 + 2(9) + 15 - 6 = -27 + 18 + 15 - 6 = 0$.
[A1] Since $P(-3) = 0$, by the Factor Theorem, $\mathbf{(x + 3) \text{ is indeed a factor}}$.
Question 3 Tier 1: Standard 3 Marks

Perform polynomial division to find the quotient and remainder: $(2x^3 - 7x^2 + 9x - 5) \div (x - 2)$.

View Full Method Markscheme
[M1] Divide leading terms: $\frac{2x^3}{x} = 2x^2$. Subtract $2x^2(x - 2) = 2x^3 - 4x^2$, leaving $-3x^2 + 9x$.
[M1] Next term: $\frac{-3x^2}{x} = -3x$. Subtract $-3x(x - 2) = -3x^2 + 6x$, leaving $3x - 5$. Next term: $+3$. Subtract $3(x - 2) = 3x - 6$, leaving $+1$.
[A1] Quotient: $\mathbf{Q(x) = 2x^2 - 3x + 3}$, Remainder: $\mathbf{R = 1}$.
Question 4 Tier 2: Intermediate 4 Marks

Find the value of $a$ such that $(2x - 1)$ is a factor of $P(x) = 4x^3 + ax^2 - 5x + 2$.

View Full Method Markscheme
[M1] For $(2x - 1)$ to be a factor, $P\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = 0$.
[M1] Substitute $x = \frac{1}{2}$: $$P\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = 4\left(\frac{1}{8}\right) + a\left(\frac{1}{4}\right) - 5\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) + 2 = 0$$
[M1] Simplify: $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{a}{4} - \frac{5}{2} + 2 = 0 \implies \frac{a}{4} + (0) = 0$.
[A1] Thus $\frac{a}{4} = 0 \implies \mathbf{a = 0}$.
Question 5 Tier 2: Intermediate 4 Marks

Factorise completely: $P(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 - 11x + 12$.

View Full Method Markscheme
[M1] Test $x = 1$: $P(1) = 1 - 2 - 11 + 12 = 0 \implies (x - 1)$ is a factor.
[M1] Divide by $(x - 1)$: $x^3 - 2x^2 - 11x + 12 = (x - 1)(x^2 - x - 12)$.
[M1] Factorise quadratic $x^2 - x - 12$: factors of $-12$ summing to $-1$ are $-4$ and $+3$.
[A1] Full factorisation: $\mathbf{(x - 1)(x - 4)(x + 3)}$.
Question 6 Tier 2: Intermediate 4 Marks

When polynomial $P(x) = x^4 - 3x^2 + ax + b$ is divided by $(x - 1)$, the remainder is $4$. When divided by $(x + 2)$, the remainder is $-8$. Find the values of $a$ and $b$.

View Full Method Markscheme
[M1] From $(x - 1)$: $P(1) = 1 - 3 + a + b = 4 \implies a + b = 6$. (Eq 1)
[M1] From $(x + 2)$: $P(-2) = (-2)^4 - 3(-2)^2 + a(-2) + b = 16 - 12 - 2a + b = 4 - 2a + b = -8 \implies -2a + b = -12$. (Eq 2)
[M1] Subtract Eq 2 from Eq 1: $(a - (-2a)) + (b - b) = 6 - (-12) \implies 3a = 18 \implies a = 6$.
[A1] Find $b$: $6 + b = 6 \implies b = 0$. Solutions: $\mathbf{a = 6, \; b = 0}$.
Question 7 Tier 2: Intermediate 4 Marks

Find all real solutions to the cubic equation: $2x^3 + 3x^2 - 8x + 3 = 0$.

View Full Method Markscheme
[M1] Test $x = 1$: $2(1) + 3(1) - 8(1) + 3 = 0 \implies (x - 1)$ is a factor.
[M1] Divide by $(x - 1)$: $2x^3 + 3x^2 - 8x + 3 = (x - 1)(2x^2 + 5x - 3)$.
[M1] Factorise quadratic: $2x^2 + 5x - 3 = (2x - 1)(x + 3)$.
[A1] Roots are $\mathbf{x = 1, \quad x = \frac{1}{2}, \quad x = -3}$.
Question 8 Tier 3: Extended 5 Marks

A polynomial $P(x)$ leaves a remainder of $3$ when divided by $(x - 1)$, and a remainder of $7$ when divided by $(x - 3)$.
(a) State the maximum possible degree of the remainder when $P(x)$ is divided by $(x - 1)(x - 3)$.
(b) Determine this remainder in the form $R(x) = Ax + B$.

View Full Method Markscheme
[M1] Part (a): Since the divisor is quadratic ($\deg = 2$), the remainder must have degree at most $1$: $\mathbf{\deg(R) \le 1}$.
[M1] Write $P(x) = (x - 1)(x - 3)Q(x) + (Ax + B)$.
[M1] Apply $P(1) = 3$: $(1 - 1)(1 - 3)Q(1) + (A(1) + B) = 3 \implies A + B = 3$.
[M1] Apply $P(3) = 7$: $3A + B = 7$.
[A1] Subtract equations: $2A = 4 \implies A = 2$. Then $2 + B = 3 \implies B = 1$. The remainder is $\mathbf{R(x) = 2x + 1}$.
Question 9 Tier 3: Extended 5 Marks

Criterion B Investigation: Consider the polynomial $P_n(x) = x^n - 1$ where $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.
(a) Prove using the Factor Theorem that $(x - 1)$ is a factor of $x^n - 1$ for all integers $n \ge 1$.
(b) Prove that $(x + 1)$ is a factor of $x^n - 1$ if and only if $n$ is an even integer.

View Full Method Markscheme
[M1] Part (a): Substitute $x = 1$ into $P_n(x)$: $P_n(1) = 1^n - 1 = 1 - 1 = 0$ for any integer $n \ge 1$.
[A1] By the Factor Theorem, because $P_n(1) = 0$, $(x - 1)$ is always a factor for all $n \ge 1$. [Proved]
[M1] Part (b): By the Factor Theorem, $(x + 1)$ is a factor if and only if $P_n(-1) = 0$.
[M1] Evaluate: $P_n(-1) = (-1)^n - 1$.
[R1] If $n$ is even, $(-1)^n = +1$, so $1 - 1 = 0$, confirming $(x + 1)$ is a factor. If $n$ is odd, $(-1)^n = -1$, giving $-1 - 1 = -2 \ne 0$. Thus $(x + 1)$ is a factor if and only if $n$ is even. [Proved]
Question 10 Tier 3: Extended 5 Marks

A cubic polynomial $P(x)$ has a turning point at $(2, 0)$, a root at $x = -1$, and passes through the point $(0, 12)$.
(a) Write down the general factorised form of $P(x)$ using its roots and repeated root.
(b) Determine the equation of $P(x)$ in expanded polynomial form.

View Full Method Markscheme
[M1] Since the turning point lies on the $x$-axis at $(2, 0)$, the line $y = 0$ is tangent to the curve, meaning $(x - 2)$ is a repeated factor of multiplicity $2$.
[M1] The root at $x = -1$ gives factor $(x + 1)$. General form: $P(x) = a(x - 2)^2(x + 1)$.
[M1] Substitute point $(0, 12)$: $12 = a(0 - 2)^2(0 + 1) = a(4)(1) \implies 4a = 12 \implies a = 3$.
[M1] Expand: $P(x) = 3(x^2 - 4x + 4)(x + 1) = 3(x^3 + x^2 - 4x^2 - 4x + 4x + 4) = 3(x^3 - 3x^2 + 4)$.
[A1] Expanded polynomial: $\mathbf{P(x) = 3x^3 - 9x^2 + 12}$.

Extended Polynomial Formulas

  • Division Algorithm: $P(x) = D(x) \cdot Q(x) + R(x)$
  • Remainder Theorem: Remainder upon division by $(x - c)$ is $R = P(c)$
  • Generalised Remainder: Division by $(ax - b)$ yields $R = P\left(\dfrac{b}{a}\right)$
  • Factor Theorem: $(x - c) \text{ is a factor of } P(x) \iff P(c) = 0$
  • Rational Root Theorem: If $\frac{p}{q}$ is a root, $p \mid a_0$ and $q \mid a_n$
  • Sum of Cubes: $a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)$
  • Difference of Cubes: $a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)$

Top Common MYP Pitfalls

1. Sign Error in Divisor Substitution

When dividing by $(x + 4)$, evaluating $P(+4)$ instead of $P(-4)$. Setting $x + 4 = 0$ gives $x = -4$!

2. Missing Terms in Long Division

Dividing $x^3 - 7x + 6$ without inserting a $0x^2$ placeholder column. Always align terms by descending powers!

3. Subtraction Sign Error in Long Division

Forgetting to subtract the entire line during long division, turning $(-7x^2 - (-4x^2))$ into $-11x^2$ instead of $-3x^2$.

4. Not Testing Fractional Candidates

When factoring non-monic cubics like $2x^3 + \dots$, forgetting that roots can be fractions such as $\pm \frac{1}{2}$.

Question 1 of 5 Remainder Theorem Calculation

What is the remainder when $P(x) = 2x^3 - 3x + 5$ is divided by $(x + 1)$?

Explanation: Set $x + 1 = 0 \implies x = -1$. By the Remainder Theorem, $R = P(-1) = 2(-1)^3 - 3(-1) + 5 = -2 + 3 + 5 = 6$.

Question 2 of 5 Factor Theorem Application

If $(x - 2)$ is a factor of $P(x) = x^3 - kx^2 + 4$, what is the value of $k$?

Explanation: By Factor Theorem, $P(2) = 0 \implies (2)^3 - k(2)^2 + 4 = 0 \implies 8 - 4k + 4 = 0 \implies 12 = 4k \implies k = 3$.

Question 3 of 5 Degree of Quotient

When a degree-5 polynomial is divided by a degree-2 polynomial, what is the degree of the quotient?

Explanation: The degree of the quotient is $\deg(P) - \deg(D) = 5 - 2 = 3$.

Question 4 of 5 Linear Divisor with Coefficient

To find the remainder when $P(x)$ is divided by $(3x - 2)$, which value must be substituted into $P(x)$?

Explanation: Set the divisor to zero: $3x - 2 = 0 \implies 3x = 2 \implies x = \frac{2}{3}$. The remainder is $P\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)$.

Question 5 of 5 Cubic Curve Feature

If a cubic polynomial has a factor $(x - 4)^2$, how does its graph interact with the $x$-axis at $x = 4$?

Explanation: A repeated root of even multiplicity (such as $(x - 4)^2$) means the graph touches the $x$-axis at $(4, 0)$ without crossing it (the $x$-axis is tangent to the curve).
