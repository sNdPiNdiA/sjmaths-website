Levels

Standard and Extended Mathematics

IB MYP Mathematics is offered at two levels. Both are taught within the same four branches; Extended adds greater depth and complexity. Topic pages on this site clearly indicate which content applies to each level.

Standard

Mathematics: Standard Level

Covers the core MYP syllabus accessible to all students. Suitable for those planning DP Mathematics: Applications and Interpretation at Standard Level or as a solid general-education mathematics qualification.

  • Core concepts across all four branches
  • Appropriate for a broad range of future DP pathways
  • Emphasis on understanding and application
  • Linked to the IB Mathematical Practices
Extended

Mathematics: Extended Level

Includes all Standard content plus additional depth and complexity. Designed for students intending to pursue DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches HL, or who want a more rigorous preparation for science and engineering.

  • All Standard content plus extended topics
  • Stronger preparation for DP AA HL or AI HL
  • Greater emphasis on abstract reasoning and proof
  • Recommended for mathematics-intensive DP programmes

About

About IB MYP Mathematics

The IB Middle Years Programme (MYP) Mathematics course is designed for students aged 11–16 (MYP Years 1–5). It develops mathematical knowledge and skills within four interconnected branches: Number, Algebra, Geometry and Trigonometry, and Statistics and Probability.

MYP Mathematics aims to cultivate mathematical thinking rather than rote calculation. Students develop the ability to recognise patterns, construct arguments, communicate mathematically, and apply mathematics in real-world contexts. These skills are central to the IB’s broader educational philosophy.

The programme offers two levels — Standard and Extended — allowing schools to meet students’ diverse needs. Both levels cover the same four branches; Extended provides greater depth and rigour, preparing students well for the Diploma Programme.

Assessment in MYP Mathematics uses four criteria: Knowing and Understanding; Investigating Patterns; Communicating; and Applying Mathematics in Real-Life Contexts. Understanding these criteria is essential for performing well in school-assessed tasks.
