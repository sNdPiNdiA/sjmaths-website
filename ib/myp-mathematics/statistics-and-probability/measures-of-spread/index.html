Measures of Spread: Dispersion & Boxplots

Quantify the variability and consistency of data. Master the range, quartiles, interquartile range (IQR), the rigorous $1.5 \times \text{IQR}$ outlier rule, five-number summary box-and-whisker plots, variance, and standard deviation.

Range, Quartiles & Interquartile Range (IQR)

Two datasets can share identical means and medians while having completely different levels of variation. Measures of spread quantify this dispersion.

The Range: $$\text{Range} = \text{Maximum Value} - \text{Minimum Value}$$ Limitation: Highly sensitive to extreme single values; tells us nothing about the clustering of intermediate data.
Quartiles & Interquartile Range:
Quartiles divide an ordered dataset into four equal segments ($25\%$ each):
  • Lower Quartile ($Q_1$): The median of the lower half of the data ($25\text{th percentile}$).
  • Median ($Q_2$): The middle of the entire dataset ($50\text{th percentile}$).
  • Upper Quartile ($Q_3$): The median of the upper half of the data ($75\text{th percentile}$).
  • Interquartile Range ($\text{IQR}$): The range of the central $50\%$ of the observations: $$\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$$
  • Semi-Interquartile Range: $\text{SIQR} = \frac{Q_3 - Q_1}{2}$.
2

Box-and-Whisker Plots & The 1.5×IQR Outlier Fence Rule

A box-and-whisker plot visually encapsulates the Five-Number Summary: $$\{\text{Minimum}, \; Q_1, \; Q_2, \; Q_3, \; \text{Maximum}\}$$

The Mathematical Outlier Rule ($1.5 \times \text{IQR}$):
A data point $x$ is formally classified as an outlier if it lies outside the inner fences: $$\text{Lower Fence} = Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$$ $$\text{Upper Fence} = Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$$ $$\text{Outlier Condition: } x < Q_1 - 1.5(\text{IQR}) \quad \text{or} \quad x > Q_3 + 1.5(\text{IQR})$$

Boxplot Drawing Conventions:

  • The central rectangular box extends from $Q_1$ to $Q_3$, with a vertical line marking the median $Q_2$.
  • Outliers are marked as individual points ($\times$ or $\bullet$).
  • The whiskers extend to the smallest and largest non-outlier values in the dataset.

Five-number summary boxplot: showing lower quartile ($Q_1$), median ($Q_2$), upper quartile ($Q_3$), whiskers, and an isolated outlier beyond the fence.

3

Variance & Standard Deviation (Population vs. Sample)

Standard deviation ($\sigma$) measures the typical average distance of data values from the arithmetic mean ($\bar{x}$).

Population Standard Deviation ($\sigma$): $$\text{Variance: } \sigma^2 = \frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^2}{N} = \frac{\sum x^2}{N} - \bar{x}^2$$ $$\text{Standard Deviation: } \sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^2}{N}} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum x^2}{N} - \bar{x}^2}$$
Sample Standard Deviation ($s$ or $s_{n-1}$):
When estimating the spread of a population from a smaller sample, Bessel's correction divides by $n - 1$: $$s = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1}}$$
Frequency Table Standard Deviation: $$\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum f(x - \bar{x})^2}{\sum f}} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum fx^2}{\sum f} - \bar{x}^2}$$
4

Linear Transformations & Comparative Analysis

When data is converted via $y = ax + b$, measures of spread behave very differently from measures of central tendency.

Effect of $y = ax + b$ on Spread:
  • Adding a constant ($+b$) shifts every point equally without altering their spacing: $$\text{Range}(y) = |a| \times \text{Range}(x)$$ $$\text{IQR}(y) = |a| \times \text{IQR}(x)$$ $$\sigma(y) = |a| \times \sigma(x)$$ $$\sigma^2(y) = a^2 \times \sigma^2(x)$$
  • Crucial insight: Adding a constant has zero effect on any measure of spread! Only multiplying by $|a|$ scales the spread.

Worked Example 1: Five-Number Summary & Outlier Test

The resting heart rates (in beats per minute) of 13 athletes are: $$42, 45, 48, 49, 51, 52, 54, 55, 58, 60, 62, 65, 88$$
a) Determine the five-number summary: Min, $Q_1$, $Q_2$, $Q_3$, Max.
b) Calculate the Interquartile Range (IQR).
c) Apply the $1.5 \times \text{IQR}$ rule to determine whether $88\text{ bpm}$ is an outlier.

Step-by-step Solution:

Part a: Five-Number Summary:
Data is already ordered ($n = 13$).
Median ($Q_2$): Position is $\frac{13+1}{2} = 7$th value $\implies Q_2 = 54$. [M1]
Lower half (first 6 values: $42, 45, 48, 49, 51, 52$):
$Q_1 = \frac{48 + 49}{2} = 48.5$. [A1]
Upper half (last 6 values: $55, 58, 60, 62, 65, 88$):
$Q_3 = \frac{60 + 62}{2} = 61$. [A1]
$\text{Min} = 42$, $\text{Max} = 88$.
Summary: $\{42, \; 48.5, \; 54, \; 61, \; 88\}$.

Part b: Interquartile Range:
$\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1 = 61 - 48.5 = 12.5\text{ bpm}$. [A1]

Part c: Outlier Fence Test:
$\text{Upper Fence} = Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR} = 61 + 1.5(12.5) = 61 + 18.75 = 79.75\text{ bpm}$. [M1]
Since $88 > 79.75$, the heart rate of $88\text{ bpm}$ is a mathematically confirmed outlier. [A1][R1: Criterion C Communication]

Worked Example 2: Standard Deviation Calculation

Calculate the exact mean ($\bar{x}$) and population standard deviation ($\sigma$) of the following 5 scores: $$3, 5, 8, 11, 13$$

Step-by-step Solution:

Step 1: Calculate the Mean
$\bar{x} = \frac{3 + 5 + 8 + 11 + 13}{5} = \frac{40}{5} = 8$. [A1]

Step 2: Deviations and Squared Deviations
$(3 - 8)^2 = (-5)^2 = 25$
$(5 - 8)^2 = (-3)^2 = 9$
$(8 - 8)^2 = 0^2 = 0$
$(11 - 8)^2 = 3^2 = 9$
$(13 - 8)^2 = 5^2 = 25$ [M1]
$\sum (x - \bar{x})^2 = 25 + 9 + 0 + 9 + 25 = 68$.

Step 3: Variance & Standard Deviation
$\sigma^2 = \frac{68}{5} = 13.6$. [M1]
$\sigma = \sqrt{13.6} \approx 3.69$ (3 s.f.). [Criterion A: Knowledge & Understanding]

Tier 1: Standard Exercise 1

Find the range and interquartile range (IQR) for the following ordered dataset: $$10, 12, 14, 15, 17, 19, 21, 24, 28$$

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

Range $= 28 - 10 = 18$. [A1]
$n = 9$. Median $Q_2 = 17$ (5th value).
Lower half ($10, 12, 14, 15$): $Q_1 = \frac{12 + 14}{2} = 13$. [M1]
Upper half ($19, 21, 24, 28$): $Q_3 = \frac{21 + 24}{2} = 22.5$. [M1]
$\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1 = 22.5 - 13 = 9.5$. [A1]

Tier 1: Standard Exercise 2

A dataset has $Q_1 = 34$ and $Q_3 = 58$.
a) Calculate the IQR.
b) Calculate the lower and upper outlier fences.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

a) $\text{IQR} = 58 - 34 = 24$. [A1]
b) Lower Fence $= Q_1 - 1.5(\text{IQR}) = 34 - 1.5(24) = 34 - 36 = -2$. [A1]
Upper Fence $= Q_3 + 1.5(\text{IQR}) = 58 + 1.5(24) = 58 + 36 = 94$. [A1]

Tier 1: Standard Exercise 3

The standard deviation of test scores in a class is $8.4$. If the teacher decides to add $5$ bonus marks to every student's score, what is the new standard deviation? Explain.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

The new standard deviation is still $8.4$. [A1]
Explanation: Adding a constant value shifts every data point by $+5$ without changing the distances between scores or their deviations from the new mean. Measures of spread are invariant under addition of a constant. [R1]

Tier 2: Intermediate Exercise 4

The masses (in grams) of six chemistry samples are: $12, 14, 15, 18, 19, 22$.
a) Find the mean mass $\bar{x}$.
b) Calculate the population variance $\sigma^2$ and standard deviation $\sigma$.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

a) $\bar{x} = \frac{12 + 14 + 15 + 18 + 19 + 22}{6} = \frac{100}{6} \approx 16.67\text{ g}$. [M1][A1]
Using $\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum x^2}{n} - \bar{x}^2$:
$\sum x^2 = 144 + 196 + 225 + 324 + 361 + 484 = 1734$.
$\sigma^2 = \frac{1734}{6} - \left(\frac{100}{6}\right)^2 = 289 - 277.78 = 11.22\text{ g}^2$. [A1]
$\sigma = \sqrt{11.22} \approx 3.35\text{ g}$. [A1]

Tier 2: Intermediate Exercise 5

Two runners record their race times (in seconds) over 8 training runs:
Runner A: $\text{Mean} = 12.2\text{ s}, \; \text{SD} = 0.15\text{ s}$.
Runner B: $\text{Mean} = 12.2\text{ s}, \; \text{SD} = 0.65\text{ s}$.
a) Which runner is faster?
b) Which runner is more consistent? Justify using statistical concepts.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

a) Both runners have the exact same average speed since their means are identical ($12.2\text{ s}$). [A1]
b) Runner A is significantly more consistent. A lower standard deviation ($0.15 < 0.65$) indicates that Runner A's race times cluster much more tightly around the mean, with minimal variation between runs. Runner B has greater variability. [R1: Criterion C Communication]

Tier 2: Intermediate Exercise 6

For a dataset, the five-number summary is: $\text{Min} = 12, Q_1 = 28, Q_2 = 45, Q_3 = 60, \text{Max} = 115$.
a) Determine whether the maximum value $115$ is an outlier.
b) Determine whether the minimum value $12$ is an outlier.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

$\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1 = 60 - 28 = 32$. [M1]
$1.5 \times \text{IQR} = 1.5 \times 32 = 48$.
$\text{Upper Fence} = 60 + 48 = 108$.
$\text{Lower Fence} = 28 - 48 = -20$.
a) Since $\text{Max} = 115 > 108$, $115$ is an outlier. [A1]
b) Since $\text{Min} = 12 > -20$, $12$ is within the lower fence and is NOT an outlier. [A1]

Tier 3: Extended Exercise 8

Prove algebraically that for any set of numbers $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ with mean $\bar{x}$, the variance can be calculated via the alternate formula: $$\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum x^2}{n} - \bar{x}^2$$

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

Start from the definition: $\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{x})^2$. [M1]
Expand the squared binomial: $(x_i - \bar{x})^2 = x_i^2 - 2\bar{x}x_i + \bar{x}^2$. [M1]
Distribute summation: $\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{n}\left[\sum x_i^2 - 2\bar{x}\sum x_i + \sum \bar{x}^2\right]$.
Note that $\sum \bar{x}^2 = n\bar{x}^2$ and $\sum x_i = n\bar{x}$: [M1]
$\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{n}\left[\sum x_i^2 - 2\bar{x}(n\bar{x}) + n\bar{x}^2\right] = \frac{1}{n}\left[\sum x_i^2 - 2n\bar{x}^2 + n\bar{x}^2\right] = \frac{1}{n}\left[\sum x_i^2 - n\bar{x}^2\right]$.
$\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum x^2}{n} - \bar{x}^2$. $\blacksquare$ [A1][R1: Rigorous proof]

Tier 3: Extended Exercise 9

The heights of 50 male students have mean $175\text{ cm}$ and standard deviation $6\text{ cm}$. The heights of 50 female students have mean $165\text{ cm}$ and standard deviation $5\text{ cm}$. Calculate the overall standard deviation of all 100 students combined.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

Combined mean: $\bar{x}_c = \frac{50(175) + 50(165)}{100} = 170\text{ cm}$. [M1]
For males: $\sigma_m^2 = \frac{\sum x_m^2}{50} - 175^2 \implies \frac{\sum x_m^2}{50} = 6^2 + 175^2 = 36 + 30625 = 30661 \implies \sum x_m^2 = 1{,}533{,}050$. [M1]
For females: $\frac{\sum x_f^2}{50} = 5^2 + 165^2 = 25 + 27225 = 27250 \implies \sum x_f^2 = 1{,}362{,}500$. [M1]
Combined sum of squares: $\sum x_c^2 = 1{,}533{,}050 + 1{,}362{,}500 = 2{,}895{,}550$.
Combined variance: $\sigma_c^2 = \frac{2{,}895{,}550}{100} - 170^2 = 28955.5 - 28900 = 55.5$. [A1]
Combined standard deviation: $\sigma_c = \sqrt{55.5} \approx 7.45\text{ cm}$. [A1]

Tier 3: Extended Exercise 10

Criterion B & D Quality Control Investigation: An automated factory produces ball bearings with a target diameter of $10.00\text{ mm}$. The factory operates two machines (Machine X and Machine Y). Random samples of 100 bearings from each machine yield:
Machine X: $\bar{x} = 10.02\text{ mm}, \; \sigma = 0.08\text{ mm}$, with no outliers.
Machine Y: $\bar{x} = 10.00\text{ mm}, \; \sigma = 0.22\text{ mm}$, with 4 outliers beyond $3\sigma$.
a) Compare the accuracy and precision of both machines.
b) If quality specifications require all bearings to be within $10.00 \pm 0.15\text{ mm}$, recommend which machine should be prioritized for high-precision aerospace contracts. Justify using measures of spread.

View Solution & Markscheme

Method & Marks:

Part a: Accuracy vs. Precision:
- Accuracy is proximity of the mean to the target: Machine Y has higher accuracy ($\bar{x} = 10.00\text{ mm}$ exactly on target vs. $10.02\text{ mm}$).
- Precision is repeatability and closeness of data: Machine X has far higher precision due to a significantly smaller standard deviation ($\sigma = 0.08\text{ mm}$ vs $0.22\text{ mm}$) and zero outliers. [R1: Criterion C]
Part b: Industrial Recommendation:
Machine X should be prioritized. For Machine X, the entire $2\sigma$ spread ($10.02 \pm 0.16\text{ mm}$) lies comfortably within or near tolerance, and a simple calibration adjustment can shift the mean down by $0.02\text{ mm}$. Machine Y's large spread ($\sigma = 0.22\text{ mm}$) means a substantial fraction of bearings will fail the $\pm 0.15\text{ mm}$ specification, causing high scrap rates and safety hazards in aerospace applications. [A1][R1: Criterion D Evaluation]

Measures of Spread Quick Reference

Range

$$\text{Range} = \text{Max} - \text{Min}$$

Full span of dataset.

Interquartile Range

$$\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$$

Spread of middle 50%.

Outlier Rule

$$x < Q_1 - 1.5\text{IQR} \text{ or } x > Q_3 + 1.5\text{IQR}$$

Inner fence boundaries.

Standard Deviation

$$\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum x^2}{N} - \bar{x}^2}$$

Root mean square deviation.

Sample SD

$$s = \sqrt{\frac{\sum(x - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1}}$$

Bessel's correction factor.

Linear Shift Invariance

$$y = ax + b \implies \sigma(y) = |a|\sigma(x)$$

Constant $+b$ does NOT alter spread.

Top 5 Common MYP Spread Pitfalls

  • Changing Spread When Adding a Constant: Students often think adding 10 to every score increases the standard deviation by 10. Spread measures the distances between values, which stay completely identical when shifted!
  • Squaring the Multiplier for Standard Deviation: Under $y = ax + b$, the variance multiplies by $a^2$, but the standard deviation multiplies by $|a|$. Do NOT square $a$ when finding the new standard deviation.
  • Extending Whiskers to Outliers: Whiskers on a modified boxplot should NOT extend to outliers. Outliers are plotted as individual detached symbols, and whiskers stop at the nearest non-outlier value.
  • Using Range for Outlier-Prone Data: Range relies on the two extreme endpoints. A single data entry error of 1000 ruins the range. Quote the IQR instead.
  • Confusing Population ($\sigma$) with Sample ($s$): Pay attention to whether the problem involves the entire population ($N$) or a small sample survey ($n - 1$).

Self-Grading Mini Test

5 interactive questions testing your mastery of IQR, outlier rules, standard deviation, and boxplots.

Question 1 of 5 Interquartile Range

In a boxplot, $Q_1 = 22$ and $Q_3 = 38$. What is the IQR?

Explanation: $\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1 = 38 - 22 = 16$.

Question 2 of 5 Outlier Detection

Given $Q_1 = 20$ and $Q_3 = 40$, which of the following scores is an outlier?

Explanation: $\text{IQR} = 20$. Upper fence $= 40 + 1.5(20) = 70$. Lower fence $= 20 - 1.5(20) = -10$. Since $75 > 70$, $75$ is an outlier.

Question 3 of 5 Standard Deviation Scaling

A dataset has standard deviation $\sigma = 4$. If each value is multiplied by $3$ and then has $7$ added to it, what is the new standard deviation?

Explanation: For $y = 3x + 7$, $\sigma_y = |3|\sigma_x = 3 \times 4 = 12$. The addition of $7$ does not affect standard deviation.

Question 4 of 5 Consistency Comparison

Which statistic is the best indicator of consistency in experimental trials?

Explanation: Consistency is measured by dispersion/spread. A smaller standard deviation indicates higher consistency.

Question 5 of 5 Variance from Standard Deviation

If the variance of a dataset is $25$, what is its standard deviation?

Explanation: Standard deviation is the square root of variance: $\sigma = \sqrt{25} = 5$.
