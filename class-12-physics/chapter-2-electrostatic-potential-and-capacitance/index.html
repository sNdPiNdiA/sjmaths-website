Electrostatic Potential Energy & Potential

Core Fundamentals & Conservation

2.1.1 Conservative Nature, Additive Constant & Potential Difference

  • Analogy with Gravity & Springs: Just as work done against spring or gravitational force is stored as potential energy ($K + U = constant$), electrostatic work done against Coulomb force is stored as electrostatic potential energy. Both forces follow inverse-square laws and are conservative.
  • Test Charge Conditions: Test charge $q$ must be infinitesimally small ($q \to 0$) so as not to disturb the source charge configuration, and moved with external force $\mathbf{F}ext = -\mathbf{F}_E$ (no net acceleration, constant slow speed).
  • Work Done: Moving test charge $q$ from $R$ to $P$:
$$WRP = Δ U = U_P - U_R = - ∫_R^P \mathbf{F}_E · d\mathbf{r} = ∫_R^P \mathbf{F}ext · d\mathbf{r}$$
  • Physical Significance of Potential Difference: Actual absolute potential has an arbitrary additive constant $α$ ($U \to U + α$ leaves $Δ U$ unchanged). Only the difference $(U_P - U_R)$ is physically meaningful.
  • Reference Zero at Infinity: Setting $U_∞ = 0 \implies U_P = W∞ P$.
  • Electrostatic Potential ($V$): Work done per unit positive charge from infinity ($V = W∞ P/q$):
$$V_P - V_R = WRPq = U_P - U_Rq, \qquad V_P = W∞ Pq$$
SI Unit & Dimensions: Volt ($V$) $\equiv J C-1 \equiv N m C-1$. Dimensional formula: $[M L^2 T-3 A-1]$. It is a scalar field.

Potential Due to a Point Charge

Point Charge Potential & Derivation

2.3.1 Potential at Distance $r$ from Charge $Q$ (Step-by-Step Derivation)

Consider charge $Q$ at the origin. Work done in bringing unit positive test charge ($+1 C$) from $∞$ along radial direction to point $P(r)$:

  • Intermediate Force at $P'(r')$: $\mathbf{F} = 14π\varepsilon_0Qr'^2\,\hat{\mathbf{r}}'$
  • Infinitesimal Work for Displacement $Δ r'$: $Δ W = -Q4π\varepsilon_0 r'^2\,Δ r'$ (Negative sign because $Δ r' < 0$ as $r'$ decreases).
  • Total Work Integral:
$$W = -∫_∞^r Q4π\varepsilon_0 r'^2\,dr' = [ Q4π\varepsilon_0 r' ]_∞^r = Q4π\varepsilon_0 r$$
$$V(r) = 14π\varepsilon_0 Qr$$
  • Sign of Potential:
    • For $Q > 0$: $V > 0$. Work done by external agency against repulsion is positive.
    • For $Q < 0$: $V < 0$. Coulomb force is attractive, so displacement and electric force are in the same direction. External force must be directed outward, doing negative work.
  • Graphic Variation ($V$ vs $r$ and $E$ vs $r$): Potential falls as $1/r$ (slower decay), while electric field falls as $1/r^2$ (steeper decay).
Example 2.1

Potential & Work Done by External Agency

(a) Calculate potential at point P due to charge $4 × 10-7 C$ located $9 cm$ away. (b) Obtain work done in bringing charge $2 × 10-9 C$ from infinity to P. Does the answer depend on path?

(a) Potential Calculation:
$V = 14π\varepsilon_0Qr = 9 × 10^9 × (4 × 10-7)0.09 = 4 × 10^4 V$.
(b) Work Done ($W$):
$W = q V = (2 × 10-9 C) × (4 × 10^4 V) = 8 × 10-5 J$.
Answer: $V = 4 × 10^4 V$, $W = 8 × 10-5 J$. No, work done is completely independent of the path.

Potential Due to an Electric Dipole

Dipole Potential & Derivation

2.4.1 General Formula, Geometry & Contrast with Monopole

For an electric dipole of charges $± q$ separated by $2a$ ($\mathbf{p} = 2qa\,\hat{\mathbf{p}}$), potential at point $P(r, θ)$ with $r \gg a$:

  • Geometry & Binomial Expansion:
    • $r_1^2 = r^2 + a^2 - 2ar\cosθ \implies 1r_1 ≈ 1r(1 + ar\cosθ)$
    • $r_2^2 = r^2 + a^2 + 2ar\cosθ \implies 1r_2 ≈ 1r(1 - ar\cosθ)$
    • $V = q4π\varepsilon_0(1r_1 - 1r_2) = q4π\varepsilon_0(2a\cosθr^2) = p\cosθ4π\varepsilon_0 r^2$
$$V(r, θ) = 14π\varepsilon_0 p \cosθr^2 = 14π\varepsilon_0 \mathbf{p} · \hat{\mathbf{r}}r^2 \quad (r \gg a)$$
  • Axial Line ($θ = 0^°, 180^°$): $V = ± 14π\varepsilon_0 pr^2$ ($+$ for $θ = 0^°$, $-$ for $θ = 180^°$).
  • Equatorial Plane ($θ = 90^°$): $V = 0$ identically everywhere on the equatorial plane.
  • Axial Symmetry: Rotating position vector $\mathbf{r}$ around dipole moment vector $\mathbf{p}$ at a constant cone angle $θ$ yields identical potential.
  • Dipole vs Single Point Charge:
    1. Point charge: $V ∝ 1/r$ (spherically symmetric); Dipole: $V ∝ 1/r^2$ (depends on both distance $r$ and angle $θ$).
    2. Point dipole formula is exact at all distances for an ideal point dipole ($\lim a \to 0, q \to ∞$ with $p = const$).

Potential Due to a System of Charges

Superposition Principle & Shell Potential

2.5.1 Discrete, Continuous & Spherical Shell Potentials

  • Discrete Charges (Algebraic Superposition): $V = ∑i=1^n 14π\varepsilon_0 q_iriP$. (Scalars add directly with algebraic signs, much simpler than vector summation for field).
  • Continuous Charge Distribution ($ρ$): For volume charge density $ρ(\mathbf{r})$, total potential is obtained by volume integration: $$V = 14π\varepsilon_0 ∫ ρ\,dvr$$
  • Uniformly Charged Spherical Shell (Radius $R$, Total Charge $q$):
    • Outside ($r ≥ R$): $V = 14π\varepsilon_0 qr$ (Charge acts as if concentrated at center).
    • Inside & On Surface ($r ≤ R$): $V = 14π\varepsilon_0 qR$ (Since $E = 0$ inside, no work is done in moving charge inside, so $V$ remains constant throughout).
Example 2.2

Null Potential Points on Line Joining Charges

Two charges $3 × 10-8 C$ and $-2 × 10-8 C$ are located $15 cm$ apart. At what points on the line joining them is electric potential zero?

Case 1: Point between charges ($0 < x < 15 cm$):
$3 × 10-8x + -2 × 10-815 - x = 0 \implies 3x = 215 - x \implies 3(15-x) = 2x \implies x = 9 cm$.
Case 2: Point outside on negative charge side ($x > 15 cm$):
$3x = 2x - 15 \implies 3x - 45 = 2x \implies x = 45 cm$.
Answer: Potential is zero at $9 cm$ (between charges) and $45 cm$ (outside) from the positive charge.
Example 2.3

Signs of Potential Difference & Work Done

Field lines of positive and negative point charges: Determine signs of (a) $V_P - V_Q$; $V_B - V_A$, (b) $Δ U$ of small negative charge between Q and P; A and B, (c) Work done by field moving small $+q$ from Q to P, (d) Work done by external agency moving $-q$ from B to A, (e) Does kinetic energy of small negative charge increase or decrease from B to A?

(a) Potential differences: $V_P > V_Q \implies (V_P - V_Q) > 0$ (Positive). Also $V_B$ is less negative than $V_A \implies (V_B - V_A) > 0$ (Positive).
(b) Potential energy difference: Small negative charge is attracted towards positive charge, so moving $Q \to P$ reduces its P.E. $\implies U_Q - U_P > 0$ (Positive difference). Similarly, $(P.E.)_A > (P.E.)_B \implies$ Positive.
(c) Work by field: Moving $+q$ from $Q \to P$ opposes repulsive force $\implies$ Work done by field is Negative.
(d) Work by external agency: Moving $-q$ towards negative source ($B \to A$) requires pushing against repulsion $\implies$ Work by external agency is Positive.
(e) Kinetic Energy: Due to electric repulsive force on the negative charge opposing motion towards A, velocity decreases $\implies$ Kinetic energy decreases.
Summary: (a) Positive, Positive; (b) Positive; (c) Negative; (d) Positive; (e) Decreases.

Equipotential Surfaces & Relation with Field

Geometry, Work & Gradient

2.6.1 Equipotential Properties & 4 Geometric Shapes

  • Definition: A surface with a constant value of potential at all points ($V = const$).
  • Zero Work Property: Work done in moving test charge $q$ between any two points on an equipotential surface is strictly zero ($W = q(V_A - V_B) = 0$).
  • Proof that $\mathbf{E} \perp$ Equipotential Surface: If $\mathbf{E}$ were not normal, it would have a non-zero tangential component along the surface. Work would then be required to move a charge against this component, which contradicts the definition of an equipotential surface ($W = 0$). Hence, $\mathbf{E}$ is always perpendicular to equipotential surfaces.
  • Shapes of Equipotential Surfaces for 4 Standard Configurations:
    1. Single Point Charge: Concentric spherical surfaces centered at the charge (Fig. 2.9).
    2. Uniform Electric Field (along x-axis): Equidistant planes normal to the x-axis, i.e. planes parallel to the y-z plane (Fig. 2.10).
    3. Electric Dipole ($+q, -q$): Concentrated/closer together in the region between the charges where field is strong, flattening out in equatorial plane where $V = 0$ (Fig. 2.11a).
    4. Two Identical Positive Charges ($+q, +q$): Repelled in the center, surrounding both charges at large distances as single outer envelopes (Fig. 2.11b).
$$E = - dVdr \qquad or \qquad |\mathbf{E}| = |\delta V|\delta l$$
  • 2 Core Deductions (Eq. 2.21):
    1. Electric field points in the direction in which electric potential decreases steepest.
    2. Magnitude of $\mathbf{E}$ equals potential gradient (change in potential per unit displacement normal to the equipotential surface).

Potential Energy of System & External Field

Energy Configurations & Derivations

2.7.1 System of Charges, External Fields & Dipole Torque Derivation

  • Two Point Charges: $U = 14π\varepsilon_0 q_1 q_2r12$ (Independent of path or order of assembly).
  • Three Point Charges: $U = 14π\varepsilon_0 [ q_1 q_2r12 + q_1 q_3r13 + q_2 q_3r23 ]$ (Sum over all unique pairs $∑i$).
  • Single Charge in External Field $V(\mathbf{r})$: $U = q V(\mathbf{r})$.
  • Electron-Volt ($eV$): Kinetic energy gained by an electron ($q = 1.6 × 10-19 C$) accelerated through $Δ V = 1 V$:
    $1 eV = 1.6 × 10-19 J \quad | \quad 1 keV = 10^3 eV \quad | \quad 1 MeV = 10^6 eV \quad | \quad 1 GeV = 10^9 eV$.
  • Two Charges in External Field: $U = q_1 V(\mathbf{r}_1) + q_2 V(\mathbf{r}_2) + 14π\varepsilon_0q_1 q_2r12$.
  • Electric Dipole in Uniform Field $\mathbf{E}$:
    Torque $\boldsymbol{τ} = \mathbf{p} × \mathbf{E} = pE\sinθ$. Work done by external torque to rotate from $θ_0$ to $θ_1$: $$W = ∫θ_0θ_1 τext dθ = ∫θ_0θ_1 pE\sinθ dθ = pE(\cosθ_0 - \cosθ_1)$$ Taking natural reference zero at $θ_0 = π/2$ (where work in bringing $+q$ and $-q$ in external field cancel out):
$$U(θ) = - \mathbf{p} · \mathbf{E} = - pE \cosθ$$
  • $θ = 0^°$: Minimum energy $U = -pE$ (Stable Equilibrium).
  • $θ = 180^°$: Maximum energy $U = +pE$ (Unstable Equilibrium).
  • $θ = 90^°$: Zero potential energy reference $U = 0$.
Example 2.4

Work to Assemble 4 Charges at Square Corners

Four charges $+q, -q, +q, -q$ are arranged at corners of square ABCD of side $d$. (a) Find work required to assemble arrangement. (b) Find extra work to bring charge $q_0$ to centre E.

(a) Total electrostatic energy:
$U = 14π\varepsilon_0[ 4 side pairs + 2 diagonal pairs ] = q^24π\varepsilon_0 d[-4 + 2√2] = -q^24π\varepsilon_0 d(4 - √2)$.
(b) Extra work for charge $q_0$ at center:
Potential at center $V_E = 0$ (due to pairwise cancellation). $W = q_0 V_E = 0$.
Answers: (a) $W = -q^24π\varepsilon_0 d(4 - √2)$, (b) $Wextra = 0$.
Example 2.5

Two Charges in External Field $E = A/r^2$

Charges $7\,μC$ and $-2\,μC$ at $(-9 cm, 0, 0)$ and $(9 cm, 0, 0)$: (a) Potential energy with no field, (b) Work to separate to infinity, (c) Net energy in external field $E = A/r^2$ ($A = 9 × 10^5 N C-1 m^2$).

(a) Mutual Potential Energy: $U = 9 × 10^9 × (7 × 10-6) × (-2 × 10-6)0.18 m = -0.7 J$.
(b) Work to separate infinitely: $W = 0 - U = +0.7 J$.
(c) In External Field: $V(r) = A/r$.
$Unet = q_1 V(r_1) + q_2 V(r_2) + Umutual = 7μC × A0.09 + -2μC × A0.09 - 0.7 J = 70 - 20 - 0.7 = 49.3 J$.
Answers: (a) $-0.7 J$, (b) $+0.7 J$, (c) $49.3 J$.
Example 2.6

Heat Released on Dipole Re-alignment

1 mole of dipole molecules ($p = 10-29 C m$ each) is polarised in $E = 10^6 V m-1$. Field direction suddenly changes by $60^°$. Estimate heat released.

Total dipole moment ($ptot$): $ptot = 6 × 1023 × 10-29 C m = 6 × 10-6 C m$.
Initial & Final Energy:
$U_i = -pE\cos 0^° = -(6 × 10-6) × 10^6 × 1 = -6 J$.
$U_f = -pE\cos 60^° = -6 × 0.5 = -3 J$.
Heat released: $Δ H = -Δ U = U_i - U_f = -6 - (-3) = -3 J$ (Loss in P.E. $= 3 J$).
Answer: Heat released $= 3 J$.

Electrostatics of Conductors

6 Board-Exam Results & Derivations

2.9.1 Six Fundamental Properties of Conductors & Pill-Box Derivation

  1. Interior Electrostatic Field is Zero ($\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{0}$): Free electrons drift under external field until surface charges develop opposing internal field, cancelling external field completely in static equilibrium.
  2. Field at Surface is Strictly Normal: If $\mathbf{E}$ had a tangential component, surface charges would experience tangential force and flow, violating static condition. Thus $\mathbf{E}tangential = 0$.
  3. No Excess Charge in Interior: By Gauss's Law ($\oint \mathbf{E}· d\mathbf{S} = qenc/\varepsilon_0$), since $\mathbf{E} = 0$ over any Gaussian surface inside the conductor, $qenc = 0$. All excess charge resides on outer surface.
  4. Constant Potential Throughout: Since $\mathbf{E} = 0$ inside and along surface, work done in moving a test charge is zero ($Vinside = Vsurface = constant$).
  5. Surface Field ($\mathbf{E} = σ\varepsilon_0\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}$):
    Pill-box Derivation: Construct a cylindrical Gaussian pill-box of cross-section $\delta S$ partly inside and partly outside. Flux through interior and curved sides is zero. Outer circular face flux is $E\,\delta S$. Charge enclosed is $σ\,\delta S$. By Gauss's Law: $E\,\delta S = σ\,\delta S\varepsilon_0 \implies \mathbf{E} = σ\varepsilon_0\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ (Eq. 2.35).
  6. Electrostatic Shielding & Cavities: Field inside any charge-free cavity is identically zero ($\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{0}$), protecting sensitive electronic apparatus. Note: Shielding does not work if charges are placed inside the cavity.
Example 2.7

Conceptual Electrostatic Applications

Explain: (a) Why dry comb attracts bits of paper, (b) Why aircraft tires are slightly conducting, (c) Why fuel trucks have metallic ropes touching ground, (d) Why bird on high-voltage wire is safe.

(a) Comb: Charges by friction and polarizes neutral paper molecules, exerting net attraction.
(b) Aircraft Tires: Dissipates accumulated frictional static charge safely to the ground on landing to prevent sparks.
(c) Fuel Trucks: Conducts static charge to Earth preventing sparks near inflammable vapors.
(d) Bird on Wire: Both feet are at the same electric potential ($Δ V = 0$), so no current flows through the bird.

Dielectrics and Polarisation

Polarisation, Susceptibility & Bound Charges

2.10.1 Polar vs Non-Polar Dielectrics & Microscopic Mechanism

  • Non-polar Molecules (e.g. $H_2, O_2, CO_2$): Centers of positive and negative charges coincide; zero intrinsic dipole moment. External field displaces charges until balanced by molecular restoring forces $\implies$ develops induced dipole moment.
  • Polar Molecules (e.g. $HCl, H_2O$): Centers of $+$ and $-$ charges separated; permanent dipole moment. Thermal agitation keeps dipoles randomly oriented ($pnet = 0$). External field aligns permanent dipoles against thermal agitation.
  • Linear Isotropic Dielectrics: Substances where induced dipole moment is directly in the direction of and proportional to applied field.
  • Polarisation Vector ($\mathbf{P}$): Total dipole moment per unit volume:
$$\mathbf{P} = \varepsilon_0 \chi_e \mathbf{E}$$
  • Electric Susceptibility ($\chi_e$): Dimensionless characteristic constant of dielectric medium ($K = 1 + \chi_e$).
  • Net Bound Surface Charges ($\pm \sigma_p$) & Zero Volume Charge: Inside any macroscopic volume element $\Delta v$, positive and negative dipole ends sit adjacent and neutralize ($\rho_{\text{bound}} = 0$). At the outer surfaces perpendicular to $\mathbf{E}_0$, unneutralized dipole ends appear as bound surface charge density $\pm \sigma_p = P$.
  • Net Reduced Field ($E$): Induced surface charge field $E_p = σ_p/\varepsilon_0$ opposes external field $E_0$:
$$E = E_0 - E_p = σ - σ_p\varepsilon_0 = E_0K$$

Capacitance & Parallel Plate Capacitor

Capacitance & Derivations

2.11.1 Definition & Parallel Plate Capacitor Derivation

  • Capacitance ($C = Q/V$): Ratio of charge $Q$ on either conductor to potential difference $V$. Depends only on geometry (shape, size, separation) and dielectric medium. (SI unit: Farad, $1 F = 1 C V-1$).
  • Electric Field in 3 Regions:
    • Outer Region I (above plate 1): $E_I = σ2\varepsilon_0 - σ2\varepsilon_0 = 0$.
    • Outer Region II (below plate 2): $EII = σ2\varepsilon_0 - σ2\varepsilon_0 = 0$.
    • Inner Region (between plates): $E = σ2\varepsilon_0 + σ2\varepsilon_0 = σ\varepsilon_0 = Q\varepsilon_0 A$.
  • Potential Difference & Vacuum Capacitance: $V = E d = Q d\varepsilon_0 A \implies C_0 = \varepsilon_0 Ad$.
  • Fringing of Field: Near the outer boundaries of finite plates, field lines bend outward (fringing). For $d^2 \ll A$, fringing is negligible and field is strictly uniform.
  • Dielectric Slab of Thickness $t < d$: $V = E_0(d - t) + E_0Kt \implies C = \varepsilon_0 Ad - t(1 - 1K)$.
$$C = QV, \qquad C_0 = \varepsilon_0 Ad, \qquad C = K C_0 = K\varepsilon_0 Ad$$

Example 2.8

Capacitor with Dielectric Slab of Thickness $3d/4$

A dielectric slab of constant $K$ and thickness $t = 3d/4$ is inserted into a capacitor of plate separation $d$. Find the new capacitance $C$.

Potential Difference: $V = E_0(d - 34d) + E_0K(34d) = E_0 d(14 + 34K) = V_0 (K + 34K)$.
New Capacitance ($C$): $C = QV = Q_0V_0(4KK+3) = 4KK+3 C_0$.
Answer: $C = (4KK+3) C_0$.

Combinations & Energy Stored in Capacitor

Series, Parallel & Energy Formulas

2.14.1 Series & Parallel Equivalent Capacitance

  • Series Combination (Charge $Q$ same on all):
$$1Ceq = 1C_1 + 1C_2 + \dots + 1C_n, \qquad Vtotal = V_1 + V_2 + \dots + V_n$$
  • Parallel Combination (Voltage $V$ same across all):
$$Ceq = C_1 + C_2 + \dots + C_n, \qquad Qtotal = Q_1 + Q_2 + \dots + Q_n$$

2.15.1 Energy Stored & Energy Density Derivations

  • Integral Derivation (Building charge bit-by-bit): Transferring infinitesimal charge $\delta Q'$ from conductor 2 to 1 at intermediate potential $V' = Q'/C$: $$\delta W = V'\,\delta Q' = Q'C\,\delta Q' \implies W = ∫_0^Q Q'C\,dQ' = Q^22C$$
$$U = Q^22C = 12 C V^2 = 12 Q V$$
  • Energy Density in Electric Field ($u = 12\varepsilon_0 E^2$):
    For parallel plate capacitor: $U = 12Q^2C = 12(σ A)^2(\varepsilon_0 A / d) = 12(σ\varepsilon_0)^2 \varepsilon_0 (Ad) = 12\varepsilon_0 E^2 (Ad)$.
    Since volume of region where field exists is $Vol = Ad$, energy density $u = UVol$:
$$u = 12 \varepsilon_0 E^2 \quad (SI Unit: J m-3)$$
  • Common Potential on Connecting Two Charged Capacitors: $Vcommon = C_1 V_1 + C_2 V_2C_1 + C_2$.
  • Energy Loss during Sharing of Charges:
$$Δ Uloss = C_1 C_22(C_1 + C_2)(V_1 - V_2)^2$$

Note: The lost energy is dissipated as heat in connecting wires and electromagnetic radiation during transient current flow.

Example 2.9

Network of Four $10\,μF$ Capacitors at $500 V$

Four $10\,μF$ capacitors: three in series ($C_1, C_2, C_3$) in parallel with $C_4$, connected to $500 V$. Find: (a) Equivalent capacitance, (b) Charge on each capacitor.

(a) Equivalent Capacitance: Three series branch $C' = 10/3\,μF$.
$Ceq = C' + C_4 = 103 + 10 = 403\,μF ≈ 13.3\,μF$.
(b) Charges:
$Q_1 = Q_2 = Q_3 = C' V = (103 × 10-6) × 500 = 1.7 × 10-3 C = 1.7 mC$.
$Q_4 = C_4 V = (10 × 10-6) × 500 = 5.0 × 10-3 C = 5.0 mC$.
Answers: (a) $Ceq = 13.3\,μF$, (b) $Q1,2,3 = 1.7 mC$, $Q_4 = 5.0 mC$.
Example 2.10

Energy Stored and Energy Loss on Sharing

(a) A $900 pF$ capacitor is charged by $100 V$ battery. How much electrostatic energy is stored? (b) It is disconnected and connected to another uncharged $900 pF$ capacitor. What is final energy?

(a) Initial Energy ($U_i$):
$U_i = 12 C V^2 = 12 × (900 × 10-12 F) × (100 V)^2 = 4.5 × 10-6 J = 4.5\,μJ$.
(b) Final Energy ($U_f$):
Common potential $V' = V/2 = 50 V$.
$U_f = 2 × (12 C V'^2) = C (V/2)^2 = 14 C V^2 = U_i2 = 2.25 × 10-6 J$.
Where did the remaining $2.25\,μJ$ go? Dissipated as heat and electromagnetic radiation during charge redistribution.
Answers: (a) $U_i = 4.5\,μJ$, (b) $U_f = 2.25\,μJ$ (50% energy loss).

Points to Ponder

  1. Charges at Rest: In electrostatic equilibrium, each charge is held at rest by internal or external opposing forces balancing Coulomb interactions.
  2. Field Confinement in Capacitors: A capacitor is geometrically configured to confine strong field lines to a small volume, allowing large charge storage at safe, small potential differences.
  3. Discontinuity vs Continuity: Across a spherical shell surface, electric field is discontinuous ($0$ inside to $σ/\varepsilon_0$ outside), while electric potential is continuous ($V = 14π\varepsilon_0qR$).
  4. Dipole Oscillation: Torque $\boldsymbol{τ} = \mathbf{p} × \mathbf{E}$ causes the dipole to oscillate about $\mathbf{E}$; alignment occurs only when dissipative damping is present.
  5. Self-Potential Singularity: Potential due to a point charge at its exact own coordinate is undefined (infinite).
  6. External Potential Definition: In the potential energy expression $q V(\mathbf{r})$, $V(\mathbf{r})$ is produced strictly by external charges, not by charge $q$ itself.
  7. One-Way Shielding: Electrostatic shielding protects cavity interiors from outside fields. However, charges placed inside the cavity will produce external electric fields outside the conductor.

$$V = C_1 V_1C_1 + C_2 = 2 × 1002 + 8 = 20 V$$

93. (a) Explain briefly how a parallel plate capacitor is formed and define its capacitance. (b) Derive an expression for the energy stored in a fully charged capacitor. (c) The energy stored in a parallel plate capacitor is E. What will the energy stored be when the plate separation is (i) doubled, (ii) halved, with battery remaining connected?

Final Answer: Derivations, (i) E/2, (ii) 2E.
Step 1: Part (a) Formation and Capacitance
A parallel plate capacitor is formed by placing two conducting plates parallel to each other, separated by a thin dielectric medium. When charged, they hold equal and opposite charges. Capacitance is the charge required to raise the potential difference between the plates by 1 volt: $C = Q/V$.
Step 2: Part (b) Energy Derivation
Let the capacitor be charged to charge $q$ with potential $v = q/C$. The work done in adding charge $dq$ is:
$$dW = v dq = qC dq$$
Total work in charging from $0$ to $Q$:
$$W = ∫_0^Q qC dq = 1C [ q^22 ]_0^Q = Q^22C$$
This work is stored as potential energy: $U = Q^22C = 12 C V^2$.
Step 3: Part (c) Battery remaining connected (Voltage is constant)
Since battery is connected, voltage is constant. Stored energy is $U = 12 C V^2$. Since $C = \varepsilon_0 Ad$, we have $U ∝ 1/d$.
• (i) Separation doubled ($d' = 2d$): Capacitance is halved, so energy is halved: $U' = E/2$.
• (ii) Separation halved ($d' = d/2$): Capacitance is doubled, so energy is doubled: $U' = 2E$.
