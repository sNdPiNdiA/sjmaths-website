Oersted Discovery, Magnetic Field & Lorentz Force

Fundamental Interaction

4.2.1 Lorentz Force & Force on Current-Carrying Conductor

  • Oersted's Discovery (1820): Moving charges or steady currents produce magnetic fields in surrounding space. Field lines form concentric circles centered on the wire.
  • Lorentz Force ($\mathbf{F}$): Total electromagnetic force on charge $q$ moving with velocity $\mathbf{v}$ in presence of $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$:
$$\mathbf{F} = q[\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B}] = \mathbf{F}_e + \mathbf{F}_m$$
  • Key Characteristics of Magnetic Force ($\mathbf{F}_m = q(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B})$):
    1. Acts perpendicular to both $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ ($\mathbf{F}_m \perp \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{F}_m \perp \mathbf{B}$).
    2. No work is done by magnetic force on a charged particle ($W = \int \mathbf{F}_m \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0$), so particle kinetic energy and speed remain constant.
    3. Vanishes if charge is stationary ($v = 0$) or moving parallel/antiparallel to field ($\theta = 0^\circ, 180^\circ \implies \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{0}$).
  • Magnetic Field SI Unit: Tesla ($\text{T} \equiv \text{N s C}^{-1}\text{ m}^{-1} \equiv \text{N A}^{-1}\text{ m}^{-1}$). CGS unit: Gauss ($1\text{ G} = 10^{-4}\text{ T}$). Earth's field $\approx 3.6 \times 10^{-5}\text{ T}$.
  • Force on Current-Carrying Straight Conductor:
    $\mathbf{F} = (n l A) q (\mathbf{v}_d \times \mathbf{B}) = (j A l)\,\hat{\mathbf{j}} \times \mathbf{B}$:
$$\mathbf{F} = I (\mathbf{l} \times \mathbf{B}) = I l B \sin\theta\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}$$
Example 4.1

Mid-Air Suspension of Current-Carrying Wire

A straight wire of mass $200\text{ g}$ and length $1.5\text{ m}$ carries a current of $2\text{ A}$. It is suspended in mid-air by a uniform horizontal magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$. What is the magnitude of $\mathbf{B}$?

Equilibrium Condition: Upward magnetic force balances downward gravity: $F_m = m g \implies I l B = m g \implies B = \frac{m g}{I l}$
Calculation: $B = \frac{0.2\text{ kg} \times 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}}{2\text{ A} \times 1.5\text{ m}} = \frac{1.96}{3.0} \approx 0.65\text{ T}$.
Answer: $B \approx 0.65\text{ T}$.

Motion in a Magnetic Field & Helical Paths

Circular & Helical Motion

4.3.1 Radius, Cyclotron Frequency & Pitch

  • Case 1: $\mathbf{v} \perp \mathbf{B}$ (Circular Motion):
    Magnetic force provides centripetal force: $\frac{m v^2}{r} = q v B$:
$$r = \frac{m v}{q B} = \frac{p}{q B} = \frac{\sqrt{2 m K}}{q B}$$
  • Cyclotron Frequency ($\nu_c$) & Time Period ($T$):
    $\omega = \frac{v}{r} = \frac{q B}{m} \implies \nu_c = \frac{\omega}{2\pi} = \frac{q B}{2\pi m}, \qquad T = \frac{2\pi m}{q B}$
    Crucial Property: Frequency $\nu_c$ and period $T$ are independent of particle speed $v$ and radius $r$.
  • Case 2: Velocity at Angle $\theta$ to $\mathbf{B}$ (Helical Motion):
    • Perpendicular component $v_\perp = v\sin\theta$ drives circular motion with radius $r = \frac{m v_\perp}{q B}$.
    • Parallel component $v_\parallel = v\cos\theta$ is unaffected by $\mathbf{B}$, driving uniform translation along field lines.
    • Pitch ($p$): Linear distance traveled along field during one complete revolution:
$$p = v_\parallel T = \frac{2\pi m v_\parallel}{q B} = \frac{2\pi m v \cos\theta}{q B}$$
Example 4.3

Electron Orbit Radius, Frequency & Energy

An electron ($m = 9 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}, q = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$) moves at $3 \times 10^7\text{ m s}^{-1}$ perpendicular to $B = 6 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$. Find orbit radius, frequency, and kinetic energy in keV.

Radius ($r$): $r = \frac{m v}{q B} = \frac{(9 \times 10^{-31}) \times (3 \times 10^7)}{(1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \times (6 \times 10^{-4})} = 0.281\text{ m} \approx 28\text{ cm}$.
Frequency ($\nu$): $\nu = \frac{q B}{2\pi m} = \frac{(1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \times (6 \times 10^{-4})}{2\pi \times (9 \times 10^{-31})} \approx 1.7 \times 10^7\text{ Hz} = 17\text{ MHz}$.
Energy ($E$): $E = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 = 0.5 \times (9 \times 10^{-31}) \times (9 \times 10^{14}) = 4.05 \times 10^{-16}\text{ J} = \frac{4.05 \times 10^{-16}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}}\text{ eV} \approx 2.5\text{ keV}$.
Answers: $r = 28\text{ cm}$, $\nu = 17\text{ MHz}$, $E = 2.5\text{ keV}$.

Biot-Savart Law & Permeability of Free Space

Fundamental Field Law

4.4.1 Biot-Savart Formulation & Comparison with Coulomb's Law

The magnetic field $d\mathbf{B}$ due to an infinitesimal current element $I d\mathbf{l}$ at displacement $\mathbf{r}$:

$$d\mathbf{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I (d\mathbf{l} \times \mathbf{r})}{r^3} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I (d\mathbf{l} \times \hat{\mathbf{r}})}{r^2}$$
$$|d\mathbf{B}| = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I dl \sin\theta}{r^2}, \qquad \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} = 10^{-7}\text{ T m A}^{-1}$$
  • Permeability of Free Space ($\mu_0$): $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}\text{ T m A}^{-1}$ (or $\text{N A}^{-2}$).
  • Relation with Speed of Light ($c$): $\mu_0 \varepsilon_0 = (4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times \left(\frac{1}{4\pi \times 9 \times 10^9}\right) = \frac{1}{(3 \times 10^8)^2} = \frac{1}{c^2} \implies c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0}}$
  • Biot-Savart vs Coulomb Law Comparison:
    1. Both are inverse-square ($1/r^2$) long-range laws obeying linear superposition.
    2. Electrostatic field has a scalar source ($q$), directed radially. Magnetic field has a vector source ($I d\mathbf{l}$), directed perpendicular to the plane of $d\mathbf{l}$ and $\mathbf{r}$.
    3. Biot-Savart field has angle dependence ($\sin\theta$) and vanishes along the axis of current element ($\theta = 0$).
Example 4.4

Magnetic Field of Current Element on Y-Axis

An element $\Delta\mathbf{l} = \Delta x\,\hat{\mathbf{i}}$ ($\Delta x = 1\text{ cm}$) carries current $I = 10\text{ A}$ at origin. Find $\mathbf{B}$ at $y = 0.5\text{ m}$ on y-axis.

Vector Cross Product: $d\mathbf{l} \times \mathbf{r} = (\Delta x\,\hat{\mathbf{i}}) \times (y\,\hat{\mathbf{j}}) = (\Delta x y)\,\hat{\mathbf{k}}$. ($\theta = 90^\circ, \sin 90^\circ = 1$).
Magnitude: $B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I \Delta x}{y^2} = 10^{-7} \times \frac{10 \times 10^{-2}}{(0.5)^2} = 10^{-7} \times \frac{0.1}{0.25} = 4 \times 10^{-8}\text{ T}$.
Answer: $\mathbf{B} = 4 \times 10^{-8}\text{ T}\,\hat{\mathbf{k}}$ (along $+z$ direction).

Magnetic Field on the Axis of a Circular Current Loop

Complete Derivation

4.5.1 Axial Field Derivation & Centre Formula

Consider circular loop of radius $R$ carrying steady current $I$. Point $P$ is on axis at distance $x$ from center:

  • Distance from Element: $r = \sqrt{x^2 + R^2}$. Current element $d\mathbf{l} \perp \mathbf{r} \implies |d\mathbf{l} \times \mathbf{r}| = r\,dl$.
  • Cancellation of Perpendicular Components: Diametrically opposite elements produce components $dB_\perp$ that cancel pairwise. Only axial components $dB_x = dB\cos\theta$ add up, where $\cos\theta = \frac{R}{\sqrt{x^2 + R^2}}$.
  • Integration over Loop ($\oint dl = 2\pi R$): $B = \int dB_x = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi(x^2 + R^2)} \frac{R}{\sqrt{x^2 + R^2}} \oint dl = \frac{\mu_0 I R}{4\pi(x^2 + R^2)^{3/2}} (2\pi R)$
$$\mathbf{B}(x) = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(x^2 + R^2)^{3/2}}\,\hat{\mathbf{i}} \qquad \text{or for } N \text{ turns: } \quad B(x) = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2(x^2 + R^2)^{3/2}}$$
  • Special Case: Field at Centre of Loop ($x = 0$):
$$B_{\text{centre}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2 R} \qquad (N \text{ turns: } B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2 R})$$
  • Right-Hand Thumb Rule for Circular Loop: Curl palm fingers along the direction of current in loop; extended thumb points in the direction of axial magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$.
Example 4.6

Field at Centre of 100-Turn Circular Coil

A tightly wound 100-turn coil of radius $10\text{ cm}$ carries current $1\text{ A}$. What is magnetic field at centre?

Formula: $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2 R} = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 100 \times 1}{2 \times 0.1} = 2\pi \times 10^{-4}\text{ T} \approx 6.28 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$.
Answer: $B = 6.28 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$.

Ampere's Circuital Law & The Solenoid

Circuital Integral & Solenoids

4.6.1 Ampere's Law & Field of Long Straight Wire & Solenoid

Ampere's Circuital Law states that line integral of magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$ around any closed closed loop (Amperian loop) equals $\mu_0$ times the total steady current $I_{\text{enclosed}}$ threaded through loop:

$$\oint_C \mathbf{B} \cdot d\mathbf{l} = \mu_0 I_{\text{enclosed}}$$
  • Magnetic Field of Infinitely Long Straight Wire:
    For circular Amperian loop of radius $r$: $B(2\pi r) = \mu_0 I \implies B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$.
  • Solid Cylindrical Wire of Radius $a$ Carrying Uniform Current $I$:
    • Outside ($r \ge a$): $I_{\text{enc}} = I \implies B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r} \propto \frac{1}{r}$.
    • Inside ($r \le a$): $I_{\text{enc}} = I\left(\frac{\pi r^2}{\pi a^2}\right) = \frac{I r^2}{a^2} \implies B(2\pi r) = \mu_0 \frac{I r^2}{a^2} \implies B = \frac{\mu_0 I r}{2\pi a^2} \propto r$.
  • Field Inside Ideal Long Solenoid ($n = N/L$ turns/metre):
    Rectangular loop $abcd$ of length $h$: $\oint \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = B h = \mu_0 (n h I)$:
$$B = \mu_0 n I = \mu_0 \frac{N}{L} I$$
  • Field outside ideal long solenoid is identically zero ($B_{\text{out}} = 0$). At the ends of a finite solenoid: $B_{\text{end}} = \frac{1}{2}\mu_0 n I$.
Example 4.8

Field Inside Solenoid of 500 Turns

A solenoid of length $0.5\text{ m}$ has radius $1\text{ cm}$ and 500 turns. It carries current of $5\text{ A}$. What is magnetic field inside?

Turns per unit length: $n = \frac{500}{0.5} = 1000\text{ turns/m}$.
Field: $B = \mu_0 n I = (4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 1000 \times 5 = 2\pi \times 10^{-3}\text{ T} \approx 6.28 \times 10^{-3}\text{ T}$.
Answer: $B = 6.28 \times 10^{-3}\text{ T}$.

Force Between Two Parallel Current-Carrying Conductors

Ampere Standard Definition

4.8.1 Parallel Currents & Definition of the Ampere (SI Base Unit)

Two long parallel conductors separated by distance $d$ carrying currents $I_a, I_b$:

  • Conductor $a$ sets up field $B_a = \frac{\mu_0 I_a}{2\pi d}$ at conductor $b$.
  • Force on length $L$ of conductor $b$ is $F_{ba} = I_b L B_a = \frac{\mu_0 I_a I_b L}{2\pi d}$.
  • Force Per Unit Length ($f_{ba} = F_{ba}/L$):
$$f = \frac{\mu_0 I_a I_b}{2\pi d} \quad (\text{SI Unit: }\text{N m}^{-1})$$
  • Newton's Third Law: $\mathbf{F}_{ba} = -\mathbf{F}_{ab}$ (equal and opposite mutual forces).
  • Interaction Rule:
    • Parallel currents (same direction): ATTRACT each other.
    • Antiparallel currents (opposite direction): REPEL each other.
  • Official Definition of 1 Ampere (1946): That steady current which, when maintained in each of two infinitely long straight parallel conductors of negligible cross-section, placed $1\text{ metre}$ apart in vacuum, produces on each conductor a force of $2 \times 10^{-7}\text{ N per metre of length}$.

Torque on Current Loop & Magnetic Dipole

Magnetic Dipole Moment

4.9.1 Torque Derivation ($\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{m} \times \mathbf{B}$) & Dipole Analogy

  • Magnetic Dipole Moment ($\mathbf{m}$): For a loop of $N$ turns carrying current $I$ and area $A$: $\mathbf{m} = N I \mathbf{A} \quad (\text{SI Unit: }\text{A m}^2 \equiv \text{J T}^{-1})$
  • Torque on Planar Loop in Uniform $\mathbf{B}$: Net force is zero ($\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}} = \mathbf{0}$), but forces on opposite arms form a couple:
$$\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{m} \times \mathbf{B} = N I A B \sin\theta\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}$$
  • $\theta = 0^\circ$ ($\mathbf{m} \parallel \mathbf{B}$): Torque is zero $\implies$ Stable Equilibrium.
  • $\theta = 180^\circ$ ($\mathbf{m} \parallel -\mathbf{B}$): Torque is zero $\implies$ Unstable Equilibrium.
  • Dipole Field Comparison ($x \gg R$):
    • Axial line: $B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{2m}{x^3}$ (Exact analog of electric dipole $E_{\text{axial}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{2p}{x^3}$).
    • Equatorial line: $B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{m}{x^3}$ (Analog of $E_{\text{eq}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{p}{x^3}$).

The Moving Coil Galvanometer (MCG)

Instrument Physics & Conversions

4.10.1 Principle, Radial Field, Ammeter & Voltmeter Conversions

  • Principle: Current-carrying coil placed in magnetic field experiences deflecting torque $\tau = N I A B$. Soft iron cylindrical core produces radial magnetic field (ensuring $\theta = 90^\circ, \sin\theta = 1$ in all coil orientations).
  • Equilibrium Deflection ($\phi$): Deflecting torque balanced by spring restoring torque ($k\phi$):
$$N I A B = k \phi \implies \phi = \left(\frac{N A B}{k}\right) I \implies \phi \propto I$$
  • Current Sensitivity ($S_i = \phi/I$): $S_i = \frac{N A B}{k}$ ($\text{rad A}^{-1}$).
  • Voltage Sensitivity ($S_v = \phi/V$): $S_v = \frac{\phi}{I R_G} = \frac{N A B}{k R_G}$ ($\text{rad V}^{-1}$).
    Note: Doubling turns ($N \to 2N$) doubles coil resistance ($R_G \to 2R_G$), so $S_i$ doubles but $S_v$ remains unchanged!
  • Conversion into Ammeter (Shunt $r_s$ in parallel): $I_g R_G = (I - I_g) r_s \implies r_s = \frac{I_g R_G}{I - I_g}$
  • Conversion into Voltmeter (High resistance $R$ in series): $V = I_g(R_G + R) \implies R = \frac{V}{I_g} - R_G$
Example 4.12

Galvanometer Resistance in Circuit Measurement

A circuit has $3\text{ V}$ battery and $3\,\Omega$ resistor. Find measured current if ammeter is: (a) Galvanometer $R_G = 60\,\Omega$, (b) Galvanometer with shunt $r_s = 0.02\,\Omega$, (c) Ideal ammeter with zero resistance.

(a) Plain Galvanometer: Total $R = 60 + 3 = 63\,\Omega \implies I = \frac{3\text{ V}}{63\,\Omega} \approx 0.048\text{ A}$.
(b) Converted Ammeter: $R_A = \frac{60 \times 0.02}{60 + 0.02} \approx 0.02\,\Omega$. Total $R = 3.02\,\Omega \implies I = \frac{3}{3.02} \approx 0.99\text{ A}$.
(c) Ideal Ammeter: $R = 3\,\Omega \implies I = \frac{3}{3} = 1.00\text{ A}$.
Answers: (a) $0.048\text{ A}$, (b) $0.99\text{ A}$, (c) $1.00\text{ A}$.

Q1 UNANSWERED

Work done by a magnetic force on a moving charged particle is always:

Option A is correct. Magnetic force $\mathbf{F}_m = q(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B})$ is always perpendicular to velocity $\mathbf{v}$ ($\mathbf{F}_m \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0$). Hence power $P = \mathbf{F}_m \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0$ and work done $W = 0$.
Option B is incorrect. Electric force can do work, but magnetic force cannot.
Option C is incorrect. Dot product with displacement is identically zero.
Option D is incorrect. Kinetic energy remains constant throughout.
Core Rule
Magnetic force changes the direction of motion, never the speed or kinetic energy ($W = 0$).
Q2 UNANSWERED

The cyclotron frequency of a charged particle of mass $m$ and charge $q$ in magnetic field $B$ is independent of:

Option A is correct. $\nu_c = \frac{q B}{2\pi m}$, which depends strictly on $q/m$ and $B$, completely independent of velocity $v$, energy, or orbital radius $r$.
Option B is incorrect. $\nu_c$ is directly proportional to $B$.
Option C is incorrect. $\nu_c$ is proportional to charge $q$.
Option D is incorrect. $\nu_c$ is inversely proportional to mass $m$.
Core Rule
Cyclotron resonance condition: Period $T = \frac{2\pi m}{q B}$ is invariant with particle energy.
Q3 UNANSWERED

Two long parallel wires carrying currents in the same direction:

Option A is correct. By right-hand thumb rule and Fleming's left-hand rule, parallel currents attract with force per unit length $f = \frac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2}{2\pi d}$.
Option B is incorrect. Antiparallel currents repel; parallel currents attract.
Option C is incorrect. Mutual magnetic force is non-zero.
Option D is incorrect. Parallel wires experience pure attractive translation.

Parallel currents attract; antiparallel currents repel (opposite to electrostatic charge sign rules).
Q4 UNANSWERED

To convert a galvanometer into an ammeter of range $I$, we connect:

Option A is correct. A low resistance shunt $r_s = \frac{I_g R_G}{I - I_g}$ in parallel bypasses excess current and ensures overall ammeter resistance is negligible.
Option B is incorrect. High resistance in series is used to convert galvanometer to a voltmeter.
Option C is incorrect. Shunts must be connected in parallel.
Option D is incorrect. Parallel resistance must be very small.

Ammeter $\to$ Small shunt in parallel ($r_s \ll R_G$). Voltmeter $\to$ Large multiplier in series ($R \gg R_G$).
Q5 UNANSWERED

If the number of turns $N$ of a galvanometer coil is doubled ($N \to 2N$), its voltage sensitivity will:

Option A is correct. $S_v = \frac{N A B}{k R_G}$. When $N \to 2N$, wire length doubles so resistance also doubles ($R_G \to 2R_G$). Thus $S_v$ remains invariant even though current sensitivity doubles.
Option B is incorrect. Current sensitivity doubles, but voltage sensitivity does not.
Option C is incorrect. The factor of 2 cancels out.
Option D is incorrect. $S_v$ is invariant.
Core Rule
Increasing current sensitivity by adding turns does not increase voltage sensitivity because coil resistance increases proportionally.

Ex 4.1 Centre of Circular Coil

4.1 A circular coil of wire consisting of 100 turns, each of radius $8.0\text{ cm}$ carries a current of $0.40\text{ A}$. What is the magnitude of the magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$ at the centre of the coil?

$B = 3.14 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T} = \pi \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$.
Formula: Field at centre of circular coil of $N$ turns: $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2 R}$
Calculation: $B = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 100 \times 0.40}{2 \times (8.0 \times 10^{-2})} = \frac{160\pi \times 10^{-7}}{0.16} = \pi \times 10^{-4}\text{ T} \approx 3.14 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$.
Ex 4.2 Straight Wire Field

4.2 A long straight wire carries a current of $35\text{ A}$. What is the magnitude of the field $\mathbf{B}$ at a point $20\text{ cm}$ from the wire?

$B = 3.5 \times 10^{-5}\text{ T}$.
Formula: $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$.
Calculation: $B = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 35}{2\pi \times 0.20} = \frac{2 \times 10^{-7} \times 35}{0.20} = 3.5 \times 10^{-5}\text{ T}$.
Ex 4.3 Direction & Magnitude

4.3 A long straight wire in the horizontal plane carries a current of $50\text{ A}$ in north to south direction. Give the magnitude and direction of $\mathbf{B}$ at a point $2.5\text{ m}$ east of the wire.

$B = 4.0 \times 10^{-6}\text{ T}$, directed vertically upwards.
Magnitude: $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r} = \frac{2 \times 10^{-7} \times 50}{2.5} = 4.0 \times 10^{-6}\text{ T}$.
Direction (Right-Hand Rule): Thumb pointing North $\to$ South, fingers curl out of horizontal plane at point east $\implies$ Vertically Upwards.
Ex 4.4 Power Line Field

4.4 A horizontal overhead power line carries a current of $90\text{ A}$ in east to west direction. What is the magnitude and direction of the magnetic field due to the current $1.5\text{ m}$ below the line?

$B = 1.2 \times 10^{-5}\text{ T}$, directed horizontally towards South.
Magnitude: $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r} = \frac{2 \times 10^{-7} \times 90}{1.5} = 1.2 \times 10^{-5}\text{ T}$.
Direction: Current flows East $\to$ West. By right-hand thumb rule, below the wire fingers point towards Geographic South.
Ex 4.5 Force per Unit Length

4.5 What is the magnitude of magnetic force per unit length on a wire carrying a current of $8\text{ A}$ and making an angle of $30^\circ$ with the direction of a uniform magnetic field of $0.15\text{ T}$?

$f = 0.6\text{ N m}^{-1}$.
Formula: $f = \frac{F}{l} = I B \sin\theta$.
Calculation: $f = 8\text{ A} \times 0.15\text{ T} \times \sin 30^\circ = 1.2 \times 0.5 = 0.6\text{ N m}^{-1}$.
Ex 4.6 Wire Inside Solenoid

4.6 A $3.0\text{ cm}$ wire carrying a current of $10\text{ A}$ is placed inside a solenoid perpendicular to its axis. The magnetic field inside the solenoid is $0.27\text{ T}$. What is the magnetic force on the wire?

$F = 8.1 \times 10^{-2}\text{ N}$.
Calculation: $\theta = 90^\circ \implies F = I l B = 10\text{ A} \times (3.0 \times 10^{-2}\text{ m}) \times 0.27\text{ T} = 8.1 \times 10^{-2}\text{ N}$.
Ex 4.7 Parallel Wire Mutual Force

4.7 Two long and parallel straight wires A and B carrying currents of $8.0\text{ A}$ and $5.0\text{ A}$ in the same direction are separated by $4.0\text{ cm}$. Estimate the force on a $10\text{ cm}$ section of wire A.

$F = 2.0 \times 10^{-5}\text{ N}$ (Attractive force towards wire B).
Formula: $F = \frac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2 L}{2\pi d} = \frac{(2 \times 10^{-7}) \times 8.0 \times 5.0 \times 0.10}{0.04} = \frac{8.0 \times 10^{-7}}{0.04} = 2.0 \times 10^{-5}\text{ N}$.
Ex 4.8 Multi-Layer Solenoid

4.8 A closely wound solenoid $80\text{ cm}$ long has 5 layers of windings of 400 turns each. Diameter is $1.8\text{ cm}$. If current is $8.0\text{ A}$, estimate $B$ inside near centre.

$B \approx 2.5 \times 10^{-2}\text{ T} = 2.51 \times 10^{-2}\text{ T}$.
Total turns: $N = 5 \times 400 = 2000$. Turns per unit length $n = \frac{2000}{0.80\text{ m}} = 2500\text{ turns/m}$.
Field: $B = \mu_0 n I = (4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 2500 \times 8.0 = 8\pi \times 10^{-3}\text{ T} \approx 2.51 \times 10^{-2}\text{ T}$.
Ex 4.9 Torque on Square Coil

4.9 A square coil of side $10\text{ cm}$ consists of 20 turns and carries current of $12\text{ A}$. Suspended vertically, normal to coil makes $30^\circ$ with uniform horizontal $B = 0.80\text{ T}$. Find torque.

$\tau = 0.96\text{ N m}$.
Area: $A = (0.10)^2 = 0.01\text{ m}^2$.
Torque: $\tau = N I A B \sin\theta = 20 \times 12 \times 0.01 \times 0.80 \times \sin 30^\circ = 1.92 \times 0.5 = 0.96\text{ N m}$.
Ex 4.10 Galvanometer Sensitivity Ratios

4.10 Two moving coil meters $M_1$ and $M_2$: ($R_1 = 10\,\Omega, N_1 = 30, A_1 = 3.6 \times 10^{-3}, B_1 = 0.25\text{ T}$) and ($R_2 = 14\,\Omega, N_2 = 42, A_2 = 1.8 \times 10^{-3}, B_2 = 0.50\text{ T}$). Identical spring constants. Find ratio of (a) Current sensitivity, (b) Voltage sensitivity of $M_2$ to $M_1$.

(a) $\frac{S_{i2}}{S_{i1}} = 1.4$ • (b) $\frac{S_{v2}}{S_{v1}} = 1.0$.
(a) Current Sensitivity Ratio:
$\frac{S_{i2}}{S_{i1}} = \frac{N_2 A_2 B_2}{N_1 A_1 B_1} = \frac{42 \times (1.8 \times 10^{-3}) \times 0.50}{30 \times (3.6 \times 10^{-3}) \times 0.25} = \frac{37.8}{27.0} = 1.4$.
(b) Voltage Sensitivity Ratio:
$\frac{S_{v2}}{S_{v1}} = \frac{S_{i2}}{S_{i1}} \times \frac{R_1}{R_2} = 1.4 \times \frac{10}{14} = 1.0$.
Ex 4.11 Electron in Magnetic Chamber

4.11 In a chamber, $B = 6.5\text{ G} = 6.5 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$. Electron shot with speed $4.8 \times 10^6\text{ m s}^{-1}$ normal to field. Explain why path is circle and find radius. ($e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}, m = 9.1 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}$).

Path is circular as $\mathbf{F}_m \perp \mathbf{v}$ acts as centripetal force • $r = 4.2\text{ cm}$.
Calculation: $r = \frac{m v}{e B} = \frac{(9.1 \times 10^{-31}) \times (4.8 \times 10^6)}{(1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \times (6.5 \times 10^{-4})} = \frac{4.368 \times 10^{-24}}{1.04 \times 10^{-22}} = 0.042\text{ m} = 4.2\text{ cm}$.
Ex 4.12 Electron Cyclotron Frequency

4.12 In Exercise 4.11, obtain the frequency of revolution of the electron. Does the answer depend on the speed of the electron?

$\nu = 1.82 \times 10^7\text{ Hz} = 18.2\text{ MHz}$ • No, frequency is completely independent of electron speed.
Calculation: $\nu = \frac{e B}{2\pi m} = \frac{(1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \times (6.5 \times 10^{-4})}{2\pi \times (9.1 \times 10^{-31})} \approx 1.82 \times 10^7\text{ Hz} = 18.2\text{ MHz}$.
Ex 4.13 Counter Torque on Suspended Coil

4.13 (a) Circular coil of 30 turns and radius $8.0\text{ cm}$ carrying current $6.0\text{ A}$ is suspended vertically in uniform horizontal $B = 1.0\text{ T}$. Field lines make $60^\circ$ with normal. Find counter torque. (b) Would answer change for planar coil of irregular shape enclosing same area?

(a) $\tau = 3.13\text{ N m}$ • (b) No, torque depends solely on area $A$, not shape.
(a) Torque: $A = \pi R^2 = \pi \times (0.08)^2 \approx 0.0201\text{ m}^2$.
$\tau = N I A B \sin 60^\circ = 30 \times 6.0 \times 0.0201 \times 1.0 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 3.13\text{ N m}$.
(b) Irregular Shape: Torque $\boldsymbol{\tau} = I \mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B}$ depends only on the total enclosed area vector $\mathbf{A}$ and is strictly independent of the geometric perimeter shape.

Points to Ponder

  1. Continuous Closed Loops: Electrostatic field lines originate on positive charges and terminate on negative charges. Magnetic field lines always form continuous closed loops without start or end points (reflecting non-existence of magnetic monopoles).
  2. Momentum of Electromagnetic Fields: In steady current circuits, Newton's third law holds directly for mutual forces ($\mathbf{F}_{ba} = -\mathbf{F}_{ab}$). For time-varying fields, conservation of momentum holds only when the momentum carried by electromagnetic fields is included.
  3. Lorentz Force & Relativity: The magnetic force $\mathbf{F}_m = q(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B})$ depends on particle velocity $\mathbf{v}$. If we switch to a reference frame moving with velocity $\mathbf{v}$, the magnetic force vanishes and the acceleration is explained entirely by an electric field in that frame, showing electricity and magnetism are linked relativistic phenomena.
  4. Ampere's vs Biot-Savart Law: Ampere's circuital law is not independent of Biot-Savart law; it can be derived from it. Their relationship is identical to that between Gauss's law and Coulomb's law in electrostatics.
  5. Intrinsic Dipole Moments: Elementary particles like electrons and protons possess intrinsic magnetic dipole moments and spin, beyond circulating currents.
