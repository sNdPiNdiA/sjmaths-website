Discounts & Successive Discounts

Medium Difficulty 25-30 Mins Read High Exam Weightage Updated 2026

📘 Introduction: Discounts & Successive Discounts

Discounts are a fundamental part of commercial transactions, representing a reduction in the Marked Price (MP) of an item to attract customers or clear stock. Understanding discounts is crucial for both sellers (profitability) and buyers (savings).

This microtopic covers the concepts of single discounts, Successive Discounts, and how to calculate the Equivalent Single Discount, equipping you with essential skills for real-world scenarios and competitive exams.

  • Direct Questions: Forms standalone questions in most exams.
  • Foundation: Essential for Profit & Loss, a broader topic.
  • Data Interpretation: Often appears in DI sets in Banking exams.
  • Problem Solving: Requires logical application of percentage concepts.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

  • Define and differentiate between Marked Price, Selling Price, and Discount.
  • Calculate Discount Amount and Discount Percentage accurately.
  • Understand and apply the concept of Successive Discounts.
  • Determine the Equivalent Single Discount for multiple successive discounts.
  • Solve exam-level problems involving discounts, including combinations with Profit & Loss.

💡 Core Concepts

1. Marked Price (MP) / List Price

  • Definition: The price at which an article is listed for sale, often printed on the label or tag.
  • Intuition: It's the original "sticker price" before any reductions.
  • Key Observation: Discount is always calculated on the MP.
  • Memory Tip: MP = Maximum Price (before discount).

2. Selling Price (SP)

  • Definition: The actual price at which an article is sold to the customer after any discount.
  • Intuition: It's the amount the customer pays.
  • Key Observation: \(SP = MP - \text{Discount}\).
  • Memory Tip: SP = Sale Price.

3. Discount (D)

  • Definition: The reduction offered on the Marked Price of an article.
  • Intuition: It's the amount of money saved by the customer or reduced by the seller.
  • Key Observation: Discount is expressed as an amount or a percentage of MP.
  • Memory Tip: D for Discount, D for Decrease in price.

4. Discount Percentage (%D)

  • Definition: The discount expressed as a percentage of the Marked Price.
  • Intuition: Shows how significant the price reduction is relative to the original listed price.
  • Key Observation: Always calculated on MP. If not specified, assume it's a percentage of MP.
  • Mini Example: An item marked at ₹100 is sold for ₹80. Discount = ₹20. \(\%D = \frac{20}{100} \times 100 = 20\%\).

5. Successive Discounts

  • Definition: When multiple discounts are applied one after another on an item. The second discount is calculated on the price *after* the first discount has been applied.
  • Intuition: Imagine a "Mega Sale" where you get 20% off, and then an additional 10% off the *reduced* price.
  • Key Observation: Successive discounts are NOT simply added up. The base for each subsequent discount changes.
  • Mini Example: MP = ₹100. First discount 20%. Price after 1st discount = \(100 - 20 = ₹80\). Second discount 10%. This 10% is on ₹80, not ₹100. So, \(10\% \text{ of } 80 = ₹8\). Final SP = \(80 - 8 = ₹72\).

6. Equivalent Single Discount

  • Definition: A single discount that results in the same Selling Price as a series of successive discounts.
  • Intuition: If you get 20% and then 10% successive discounts, what single percentage discount would give you the same final price? (It's not 30%!).
  • Key Observation: This helps simplify calculations and compare different discount offers.
  • Mini Example: For 20% and 10% successive discounts, final SP was ₹72 on MP ₹100. So the total discount is \(100 - 72 = ₹28\). Equivalent single discount \(\%D_{eq} = \frac{28}{100} \times 100 = 28\%\).
Comparison: Price Terms & Bases
Term Definition Calculation Base Relationship
Cost Price (CP) Price at which item is purchased by seller. N/A \(SP - \text{Profit}\) or \(SP + \text{Loss}\)
Marked Price (MP) Price listed on item; reference for discount. Discount always on MP. \(SP + \text{Discount}\)
Selling Price (SP) Actual price customer pays. Profit/Loss always on CP. \(MP - \text{Discount}\) or \(CP + \text{Profit}\) or \(CP - \text{Loss}\)

📏 Rules & Properties

1. Discount is always calculated on the Marked Price (MP).

  • Explanation: The discount is offered to reduce the advertised price, not the cost price or the final selling price.
  • Reason: This standard practice ensures transparency and allows customers to compare reductions directly from the original listed value.
  • Shortcut: \(SP = MP - \text{Discount}\).
  • Exception: None, this is a fundamental rule.
  • Example: A 25% discount on an item marked at ₹400 means a discount of \(25\% \text{ of } ₹400 = ₹100\), making the SP ₹300.

2. Successive Discounts are NOT additive.

  • Explanation: A 20% discount followed by a 10% discount does NOT equate to a 30% total discount. The second discount is applied to the price *after* the first discount.
  • Reason: The base for the percentage calculation changes with each applied discount.
  • Shortcut: Use the formula for Equivalent Single Discount \(D_{eq} = \left(d_1 + d_2 - \frac{d_1 d_2}{100}\right)\%\) for two discounts or the multiplying factor method for multiple discounts.
  • Example: MP = ₹1000.
    First discount 20% \(\rightarrow\) Price = \(1000 \times (1 - 0.20) = 1000 \times 0.80 = ₹800\).
    Second discount 10% \(\rightarrow\) Final SP = \(800 \times (1 - 0.10) = 800 \times 0.90 = ₹720\).
    Total discount = \(1000 - 720 = ₹280\), which is 28% of ₹1000, not 30%.

3. The order of successive discounts does not matter.

  • Explanation: Applying a 20% discount then a 10% discount yields the same final price as applying a 10% discount then a 20% discount.
  • Reason: Multiplication is commutative. \(MP \times (1 - d_1/100) \times (1 - d_2/100)\) is the same as \(MP \times (1 - d_2/100) \times (1 - d_1/100)\).
  • Shortcut: Always calculate with the easier discount first if it simplifies mental math, but the order won't change the result.
  • Example: MP = ₹1000.
    Case 1: 20% then 10%. SP = \(1000 \times 0.80 \times 0.90 = ₹720\).
    Case 2: 10% then 20%. SP = \(1000 \times 0.90 \times 0.80 = ₹720\).

📐 Formula Box

1. Discount Amount

$$D = MP - SP$$
  • Meaning: The absolute value of the price reduction.
  • Variables: D = Discount, MP = Marked Price, SP = Selling Price.
  • When to use: To find the actual money saved or reduced.
  • Memory Trick: Discount is the difference between what was asked (MP) and what was paid (SP).

2. Discount Percentage

$$\%D = \frac{D}{MP} \times 100$$
  • Meaning: Discount expressed as a percentage of the Marked Price.
  • Variables: %D = Discount Percentage, D = Discount Amount, MP = Marked Price.
  • When to use: To find the rate of discount offered.
  • Memory Trick: Discount is always "on" MP. Base for percentage is MP.

3. Selling Price after Single Discount

$$SP = MP \left(1 - \frac{\%D}{100}\right)$$
  • Meaning: The price after a single discount is applied.
  • Variables: SP = Selling Price, MP = Marked Price, %D = Discount Percentage.
  • When to use: To directly calculate SP from MP and %D.
  • Memory Trick: If \(D\%\) is the discount, you pay \((100-D)\%\) of MP.

4. Equivalent Single Discount for Two Successive Discounts

$$D_{eq} = \left(d_1 + d_2 - \frac{d_1 d_2}{100}\right)\%$$
  • Meaning: A single percentage discount that is equivalent to two successive discounts \(d_1\) and \(d_2\).
  • Variables: \(D_{eq}\) = Equivalent Single Discount, \(d_1\) = First discount percentage, \(d_2\) = Second discount percentage.
  • When to use: When two successive discounts are given, and you need to find the overall percentage discount or simplify calculations.
  • Memory Trick: "Add them, then subtract their product over 100". (Similar to percentage change formula).

5. Selling Price after Multiple Successive Discounts

$$SP = MP \left(1 - \frac{d_1}{100}\right) \left(1 - \frac{d_2}{100}\right) \left(1 - \frac{d_3}{100}\right) \dots$$
  • Meaning: The final selling price after applying three or more successive discounts.
  • Variables: SP = Selling Price, MP = Marked Price, \(d_1, d_2, d_3, \dots\) = Individual discount percentages.
  • When to use: For three or more successive discounts; also applicable for two discounts.
  • Memory Trick: Each discount factor \((1 - d/100)\) reduces the price multiplicatively.

🧠 Solved Examples

Easy

Example 1: Basic Discount Calculation

An article is marked at ₹500. A discount of 15% is offered. Find the discount amount and the selling price (SP).

View Step-by-Step Solution

Given: MP = ₹500, %D = 15%

  1. Calculate Discount Amount: $$D = \%D \text{ of } MP = \frac{15}{100} \times 500 = 15 \times 5 = ₹75$$
  2. Calculate Selling Price (SP): $$SP = MP - D = 500 - 75 = ₹425$$

Answer: The discount amount is ₹75, and the selling price is ₹425.

Medium

Example 2: Finding Marked Price

A shopkeeper sells an item for ₹840 after offering a 16% discount. What was the Marked Price of the item?

View Step-by-Step Solution

Given: SP = ₹840, %D = 16%

  1. Understand the relationship: If there's a 16% discount, the Selling Price is \((100 - 16)\%\) of the Marked Price. $$SP = (100 - 16)\% \text{ of } MP = 84\% \text{ of } MP$$
  2. Set up the equation: $$840 = \frac{84}{100} \times MP$$
  3. Solve for MP: $$MP = \frac{840 \times 100}{84} = 10 \times 100 = ₹1000$$

Answer: The Marked Price of the item was ₹1000.

Hard

Example 3: Successive Discounts

Find the Selling Price of an article marked at ₹1200 if two successive discounts of 20% and 10% are given.

View Step-by-Step Solution

Given: MP = ₹1200, \(d_1 = 20\%\), \(d_2 = 10\%\)

  1. Apply the first discount: $$ \text{Price after 20% discount} = 1200 \times \left(1 - \frac{20}{100}\right) = 1200 \times 0.80 = ₹960 $$
  2. Apply the second discount on the reduced price: $$ SP = 960 \times \left(1 - \frac{10}{100}\right) = 960 \times 0.90 = ₹864 $$

Alternate Method (Direct Formula): $$ SP = MP \left(1 - \frac{d_1}{100}\right) \left(1 - \frac{d_2}{100}\right) $$ $$ SP = 1200 \times \left(1 - \frac{20}{100}\right) \times \left(1 - \frac{10}{100}\right) $$ $$ SP = 1200 \times 0.80 \times 0.90 $$ $$ SP = 1200 \times 0.72 = ₹864 $$

Answer: The Selling Price of the article is ₹864.

Exam Level

Example 4: Equivalent Single Discount & Profit

A shopkeeper marks his goods 40% above the Cost Price. He offers two successive discounts of 10% and 15%. If the Cost Price of an item is ₹500, find its Selling Price and the shopkeeper's profit percentage.

View Step-by-Step Solution

Given: CP = ₹500, Mark-up = 40%, \(d_1 = 10\%\), \(d_2 = 15\%\)

  1. Calculate Marked Price (MP): $$ MP = CP \times \left(1 + \frac{\text{Mark-up}\%}{100}\right) = 500 \times \left(1 + \frac{40}{100}\right) = 500 \times 1.40 = ₹700 $$
  2. Calculate Selling Price (SP) after successive discounts: $$ SP = MP \times \left(1 - \frac{d_1}{100}\right) \times \left(1 - \frac{d_2}{100}\right) $$ $$ SP = 700 \times \left(1 - \frac{10}{100}\right) \times \left(1 - \frac{15}{100}\right) $$ $$ SP = 700 \times 0.90 \times 0.85 $$ $$ SP = 630 \times 0.85 = ₹535.50 $$
  3. Calculate Profit Amount: $$ \text{Profit} = SP - CP = 535.50 - 500 = ₹35.50 $$
  4. Calculate Profit Percentage: $$ \% \text{Profit} = \frac{\text{Profit}}{CP} \times 100 = \frac{35.50}{500} \times 100 = \frac{35.50}{5} = 7.1\% $$

Answer: The Selling Price is ₹535.50, and the shopkeeper's profit percentage is 7.1%.

⚡ Exam Tricks & Shortcuts

1. Equivalent Single Discount (A+B-AB/100 Method)

  • Method: For two successive discounts \(d_1\) and \(d_2\), the equivalent single discount \(D_{eq} = \left(d_1 + d_2 - \frac{d_1 d_2}{100}\right)\%\). This is very fast for 2 discounts.
  • Example: Discounts of 20% and 10%. $$ D_{eq} = \left(20 + 10 - \frac{20 \times 10}{100}\right)\% = (30 - 2)\% = 28\% $$
  • Use Case: Quickly find overall discount for two-step scenarios. For three or more, apply this formula twice (first two, then result with third).

2. Factor Method for Multiple Discounts

  • Method: The final Selling Price is \(MP \times (1 - \frac{d_1}{100}) \times (1 - \frac{d_2}{100}) \times \dots\). This directly calculates the SP.
  • Example: MP = ₹1000, discounts 20%, 10%, 5%. $$ SP = 1000 \times (1 - 0.20) \times (1 - 0.10) \times (1 - 0.05) $$ $$ SP = 1000 \times 0.80 \times 0.90 \times 0.95 = 1000 \times 0.684 = ₹684 $$
  • Use Case: Ideal for 3 or more successive discounts to avoid iterative calculations. Also useful for finding the equivalent single discount by \(100 - (100 \times \text{final factor})\). In the example, \(100 - (100 \times 0.684) = 100 - 68.4 = 31.6\%\).

3. Ratio Method (Fraction Equivalents)

  • Method: Convert percentage discounts to fractions. If discount is \(\frac{x}{y}\), then MP:SP = \(y : (y-x)\). Multiply ratios for successive discounts.
  • Example: Discounts of 20% and 25%.
    • 20% discount = \(\frac{1}{5}\) \(\rightarrow\) MP:SP = 5:4
    • 25% discount = \(\frac{1}{4}\) \(\rightarrow\) MP:SP = 4:3
    • Combined Ratio: \((5 \times 4) : (4 \times 3) = 20 : 12 = 5 : 3\)

    This means if MP is 5 units, SP is 3 units. Total discount is 2 units.
    Equivalent Single Discount = \(\frac{2}{5} \times 100 = 40\%\).

  • Use Case: Excellent for mental calculation and when discounts are simple fractions (20%, 25%, 10%, 33.33%, etc.).

4. Smart Percentage Calculation

  • Method: Break down percentages. E.g., for 15% discount, calculate 10% and then 5% (half of 10%).
  • Example: 15% discount on ₹600.
    • 10% of ₹600 = ₹60
    • 5% of ₹600 = ₹30
    • Total discount = \(60 + 30 = ₹90\).
  • Use Case: Quick mental calculation for common percentages, reduces calculator dependency.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Pitfalls

1. Calculating Discount on Selling Price (SP) instead of Marked Price (MP).

  • Mistake: Incorrectly assuming discount base is SP.
  • Avoidance: Always remember that discount is a reduction from the *original listed price* (MP). "Discount is ON MP."

2. Directly adding successive discounts.

  • Mistake: Believing 20% + 10% discount = 30% total discount.
  • Avoidance: Understand that the base price changes. Use the \(D_{eq} = (d_1 + d_2 - \frac{d_1 d_2}{100})\%\) formula or the multiplying factor method.

3. Confusing Discount % with Profit/Loss %.

  • Mistake: Using CP as the base for discount percentage, or MP as the base for profit/loss percentage.
  • Avoidance:
    • Discount % is always on MP.
    • Profit/Loss % is always on CP.
    Keep the bases clear.

4. Calculation Errors.

  • Mistake: Simple arithmetic mistakes, especially with decimals or large numbers.
  • Avoidance: Practice mental math, use approximations to check answers, and double-check calculations in a hurry.

📌 Summary

  • Marked Price (MP): The listed price; base for discount.
  • Selling Price (SP): Price after discount: \(SP = MP - D\).
  • Discount (D): Reduction on MP. \(D = MP - SP\).
  • Discount Percentage (%D): \(\frac{D}{MP} \times 100\). Always on MP.
  • Successive Discounts: Multiple discounts applied one after another on the *reduced* price. Not additive.
  • Equivalent Single Discount \(D_{eq}\): For \(d_1, d_2\), \(D_{eq} = \left(d_1 + d_2 - \frac{d_1 d_2}{100}\right)\%\).
  • Selling Price (Multiple Discounts): \(SP = MP \times (1 - d_1/100) \times (1 - d_2/100) \times \dots\).

🚀 Quick Revision: Discounts & Successive Discounts

  • MP: Listed Price.
  • SP: Price Paid.
  • Discount (D): \(MP - SP\). Always on MP.
  • \(\%D\): \(\frac{D}{MP} \times 100\).
  • SP (from MP, %D): \(MP \times (1 - \frac{\%D}{100})\).
  • Successive Discounts \(d_1, d_2\): NOT \(d_1 + d_2\).
  • Equivalent Single Discount (\(D_{eq}\)): \((d_1 + d_2 - \frac{d_1 d_2}{100})\%\).
  • SP (multiple discounts): \(MP \times (1 - \frac{d_1}{100}) \times (1 - \frac{d_2}{100}) \times \dots\).
  • Trick (Ratio Method): Convert % to fraction. MP:SP is \(y:(y-x)\) for discount \(\frac{x}{y}\). Multiply ratios.
  • Common Error: Discount base is MP, not SP. Profit/Loss base is CP, not MP/SP.

🚀 Key Takeaways

  • Discounts are always calculated on the Marked Price (MP). This is the cornerstone of all discount problems.
  • Successive discounts are applied sequentially, with each subsequent discount calculated on the *reduced price*, not the original MP.
  • The Equivalent Single Discount formula \((A + B - AB/100)\%\) is a powerful shortcut for two successive discounts.
  • Mastering the factor method \((1 - d/100)\) is essential for solving problems with multiple successive discounts efficiently.
  • Always differentiate between the bases for Discount % (on MP) and Profit/Loss % (on CP) to avoid fundamental errors.
