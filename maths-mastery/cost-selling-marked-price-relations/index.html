Cost Price, Selling Price & Marked Price Relations

Medium Difficulty 25-30 Mins Read High Exam Weightage Updated 2026

📘 Introduction

This microtopic lays the foundation for all Commercial Arithmetic problems, specifically Profit & Loss and Discount. Understanding the fundamental concepts of Cost Price, Selling Price, and Marked Price, along with their interrelations, is crucial for mastering these topics.

  • Core for Profit & Loss: Direct questions on CP, SP, MP, Profit, Loss, and Discount percentages.
  • Exam Relevance: Often 1-2 questions directly from this topic, forming the basis for more complex problems in SSC CGL, Banking, Railway, and State PCS exams.
  • High Weightage: Essential to score well in the quantitative aptitude section.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Identify and define Cost Price (CP), Selling Price (SP), and Marked Price (MP).
  • Understand the concepts of Profit, Loss, and Discount.
  • Accurately calculate Profit Percentage, Loss Percentage, and Discount Percentage.
  • Grasp the relationships connecting CP, SP, and MP in various scenarios.
  • Solve basic to exam-level problems involving these concepts efficiently.

📝 Prerequisites

  • Basic Arithmetic: Addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division.
  • Percentages: Understanding how to calculate percentages, percentage increase/decrease, and converting between fractions and percentages.
  • Ratios & Proportions: Basic understanding of comparing quantities.

💡 Core Concepts

Cost Price (CP)

  • Definition: The price at which an article is purchased or manufactured.
  • Intuition: Your initial investment to acquire the item.
  • Key Observation: Profit or Loss is always calculated with respect to CP (unless stated otherwise).
  • Memory Tip: C for "Cost to you."
  • Mini Example: If you buy a pen for ₹10, its CP is ₹10.

Selling Price (SP)

  • Definition: The price at which an article is sold to a customer.
  • Intuition: The revenue generated from selling the item.
  • Key Observation: Determines if there's a profit (SP > CP) or a loss (SP < CP).
  • Memory Tip: S for "Selling out."
  • Mini Example: If you sell the pen for ₹12, its SP is ₹12.

Marked Price (MP) / List Price

  • Definition: The price printed on an article or the price fixed by the shopkeeper, often higher than CP.
  • Intuition: The advertised price before any discounts are applied.
  • Key Observation: Discount is always calculated on MP. Also called List Price or Tag Price.
  • Memory Tip: M for "Marked on the tag."
  • Mini Example: If the pen has a price tag of ₹15, its MP is ₹15.

Profit

  • Definition: Occurs when the Selling Price (SP) is greater than the Cost Price (CP).
  • Intuition: You earned more than you spent.
  • Key Observation: Profit = SP - CP. Always positive.
  • Mini Example: If CP = ₹10, SP = ₹12, then Profit = ₹12 - ₹10 = ₹2.

Loss

  • Definition: Occurs when the Cost Price (CP) is greater than the Selling Price (SP).
  • Intuition: You spent more than you earned from the sale.
  • Key Observation: Loss = CP - SP. Always positive.
  • Mini Example: If CP = ₹10, SP = ₹8, then Loss = ₹10 - ₹8 = ₹2.

Discount

  • Definition: A reduction offered on the Marked Price (MP) of an article.
  • Intuition: A markdown from the advertised price to attract buyers.
  • Key Observation: Discount = MP - SP. Always calculated on MP.
  • Mini Example: If MP = ₹15, SP = ₹12, then Discount = ₹15 - ₹12 = ₹3.
Bases for Calculation
Concept Calculated On Formula
Profit Cost Price (CP) \( \text{SP} - \text{CP} \)
Loss Cost Price (CP) \( \text{CP} - \text{SP} \)
Profit % Cost Price (CP) \( \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 \)
Loss % Cost Price (CP) \( \frac{\text{Loss}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 \)
Discount Marked Price (MP) \( \text{MP} - \text{SP} \)
Discount % Marked Price (MP) \( \frac{\text{Discount}}{\text{MP}} \times 100 \)

📏 Rules & Properties

1. Profit/Loss Base

  • Explanation: Profit or Loss is always calculated on the Cost Price (CP) unless explicitly stated otherwise.
  • Reason: CP represents the actual expense; profit/loss indicates return on this investment.
  • Shortcut: To find SP when CP and P/L% are known:
    • If Profit: \( \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times (1 + \frac{\text{P%}}{100}) \)
    • If Loss: \( \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times (1 - \frac{\text{L%}}{100}) \)
  • Exception: Sometimes questions might ask for P/L% on SP, in which case the base changes.
  • Example: A shopkeeper buys an item for ₹200 and sells it at a 20% profit. Profit = 20% of ₹200 = ₹40. SP = ₹200 + ₹40 = ₹240.

2. Discount Base

  • Explanation: Discount is always calculated on the Marked Price (MP).
  • Reason: MP is the advertised price from which a reduction is given.
  • Shortcut: To find SP when MP and Discount% are known:
    • SP = MP \($ \times (1 - \frac{\text{D%}}{100}) \)
  • Exception: None. Discount always refers to MP.
  • Example: An item marked at ₹500 is sold at a 10% discount. Discount = 10% of ₹500 = ₹50. SP = ₹500 - ₹50 = ₹450.

3. Interrelation of CP, SP, MP

  • Explanation: These three prices are linked sequentially. CP is the start, SP is the transaction price, and MP is the advertised price.
  • Reason: The sequence is: CP (Cost) → (Profit/Loss) → SP (Selling Price) ← (Discount) ← MP (Marked Price).
  • Shortcut: A crucial direct relation exists: $$ \text{CP} \times (100 \pm \text{Profit/Loss %}) = \text{MP} \times (100 - \text{Discount %}) $$ (Use + for profit, - for loss)
  • Example: An article costs ₹400, is marked up by 25%, and sold at a 10% discount. MP = ₹400 \($ \times 1.25 = \) ₹500. SP = ₹500 \($ \times (1 - 0.10) = \) ₹500 \($ \times 0.90 = \) ₹450.

📐 Formula Box

1. Profit and Loss

  • Profit: $$ \text{Profit} = \text{SP} - \text{CP} \quad (\text{if SP > CP}) $$
  • Loss: $$ \text{Loss} = \text{CP} - \text{SP} \quad (\text{if CP > SP}) $$
  • Variables: SP = Selling Price, CP = Cost Price.
  • When to use: To find the absolute profit or loss amount.
  • Memory Trick: ProFit means SP is greater; Loss means CP is greater.

2. Profit/Loss Percentage

  • Profit %: $$ \text{Profit %} = \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 $$
  • Loss %: $$ \text{Loss %} = \frac{\text{Loss}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 $$
  • Variables: Profit/Loss = absolute amount, CP = Cost Price.
  • When to use: To express profit or loss as a percentage of the cost.
  • Memory Trick: Always remember the base is CP for P/L%.

3. Selling Price from CP & P/L%

  • With Profit %: $$ \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \left(1 + \frac{\text{Profit %}}{100}\right) \quad \text{or} \quad \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \frac{100 + \text{Profit %}}{100} $$
  • With Loss %: $$ \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \left(1 - \frac{\text{Loss %}}{100}\right) \quad \text{or} \quad \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \frac{100 - \text{Loss %}}{100} $$
  • Variables: SP = Selling Price, CP = Cost Price, Profit%/Loss% = percentage value.
  • When to use: When CP and the percentage gain/loss are known, and SP is required.
  • Memory Trick: Think of 100 as the base for percentage, add for profit, subtract for loss.

4. Discount and Discount Percentage

  • Discount: $$ \text{Discount} = \text{MP} - \text{SP} $$
  • Discount %: $$ \text{Discount %} = \frac{\text{Discount}}{\text{MP}} \times 100 $$
  • Variables: MP = Marked Price, SP = Selling Price, Discount = absolute amount.
  • When to use: To calculate the actual discount or discount percentage on the marked price.
  • Memory Trick: Discount is always on MP (advertised price).

5. Selling Price from MP & Discount%

  • $$ \text{SP} = \text{MP} \times \left(1 - \frac{\text{Discount %}}{100}\right) \quad \text{or} \quad \text{SP} = \text{MP} \times \frac{100 - \text{Discount %}}{100} $$
  • Variables: SP = Selling Price, MP = Marked Price, Discount% = percentage value.
  • When to use: When MP and the discount percentage are known, and SP is required.
  • Memory Trick: Discount always reduces the MP.

6. Relation between CP, MP, Profit/Loss % & Discount % (The Master Formula)

  • $$ \text{CP} \times (100 \pm \text{Profit/Loss %}) = \text{MP} \times (100 - \text{Discount %}) $$
  • Variables: CP = Cost Price, MP = Marked Price. Use '+' for Profit%, '-' for Loss%.
  • When to use: When there's a direct relation required between CP and MP involving profit/loss and discount, or when one of these quantities is unknown. Highly efficient for complex problems.
  • When NOT to use: If the question involves successive discounts/profits on different bases, or if intermediate values are required.
  • Memory Trick: CP-side gets P/L, MP-side gets Discount. (100 +/- P/L) for CP, (100 - D) for MP.

🧠 Solved Examples

Easy

Example 1: Basic Profit/Loss Calculation

A vendor buys a cycle for ₹1200 and sells it for ₹1500. Find the profit and profit percentage.

View Step-by-Step Solution

Given:

  • Cost Price (CP) = ₹1200
  • Selling Price (SP) = ₹1500

Step 1: Calculate Profit.

Since SP > CP, there is a profit.

Profit = SP - CP

Profit = ₹1500 - ₹1200 = ₹300

Step 2: Calculate Profit Percentage.

Profit % = \( \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 \)

Profit % = \( \frac{300}{1200} \times 100 \)

Profit % = \( \frac{1}{4} \times 100 = 25\% \)

Answer: The profit is ₹300, and the profit percentage is 25%.

Medium

Example 2: SP with Loss Percentage

An article is bought for ₹800. If it is sold at a loss of 15%, what is its selling price?

View Step-by-Step Solution

Given:

  • Cost Price (CP) = ₹800
  • Loss % = 15%

Step 1: Use the formula for SP with Loss %.

SP = \( \text{CP} \times \left(1 - \frac{\text{Loss %}}{100}\right) \)

SP = \( 800 \times \left(1 - \frac{15}{100}\right) \)

SP = \( 800 \times \left(1 - 0.15\right) \)

SP = \( 800 \times 0.85 \)

SP = ₹680

Alternative Step 1: Calculate the Loss amount first.

Loss = 15% of ₹800 = \( \frac{15}{100} \times 800 = 15 \times 8 = \) ₹120

Alternative Step 2: Calculate SP.

SP = CP - Loss

SP = ₹800 - ₹120 = ₹680

Answer: The selling price of the article is ₹680.

Hard

Example 3: MP, Discount, and Profit

A shopkeeper marks his goods 20% above the cost price. If he allows a discount of 10%, find his profit percentage.

View Step-by-Step Solution

Given:

  • Mark-up = 20% above CP
  • Discount = 10%

Step 1: Assume a convenient Cost Price (CP).

Let CP = ₹100.

Step 2: Calculate Marked Price (MP).

MP = CP + 20% of CP = \( 100 + 20 = \) ₹120

OR, MP = \( \text{CP} \times (1 + \frac{20}{100}) = 100 \times 1.20 = \) ₹120

Step 3: Calculate Selling Price (SP) after discount.

Discount = 10% of MP = 10% of ₹120 = ₹12

SP = MP - Discount = ₹120 - ₹12 = ₹108

OR, SP = \( \text{MP} \times (1 - \frac{10}{100}) = 120 \times 0.90 = \) ₹108

Step 4: Calculate Profit and Profit Percentage.

Profit = SP - CP = ₹108 - ₹100 = ₹8

Profit % = \( \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 = \frac{8}{100} \times 100 = 8\% \)

Using the Master Formula (Shortcut):

Let Profit % be P%.

MP is 20% above CP, so \( \text{MP} = \text{CP} \times (100+20)/100 \)

$$ \text{CP} \times (100 + P\%) = \text{MP} \times (100 - \text{Discount %}) $$

$$ \text{CP} \times (100 + P\%) = \text{CP} \times \frac{120}{100} \times (100 - 10) $$

$$ (100 + P\%) = 1.2 \times 90 $$

$$ (100 + P\%) = 108 $$

$$ P\% = 108 - 100 = 8\% $$

Answer: The profit percentage is 8%.

Exam Level

Example 4: Combined CP, MP, Discount & Profit (SSC CGL Tier-I)

A trader allows a discount of 15% on the marked price of an article. How much per cent above the cost price must he mark it so as to gain 19%?

View Step-by-Step Solution

Given:

  • Discount % = 15%
  • Profit % = 19%

Goal: Find (MP - CP) as a percentage of CP, i.e., \( \frac{\text{MP - CP}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 \).

Step 1: Apply the Master Formula relating CP, MP, Profit%, and Discount%.

$$ \text{CP} \times (100 + \text{Profit %}) = \text{MP} \times (100 - \text{Discount %}) $$

Substitute the given values:

$$ \text{CP} \times (100 + 19) = \text{MP} \times (100 - 15) $$

$$ \text{CP} \times 119 = \text{MP} \times 85 $$

Step 2: Find the ratio of MP to CP.

$$ \frac{\text{MP}}{\text{CP}} = \frac{119}{85} $$

Simplify the fraction (both are divisible by 17):

$$ \frac{\text{MP}}{\text{CP}} = \frac{17 \times 7}{17 \times 5} = \frac{7}{5} $$

This means MP is \( \frac{7}{5} \) times CP. If CP is 5 units, MP is 7 units.

Step 3: Calculate the percentage mark-up above CP.

Mark-up = MP - CP

Percentage Mark-up = \( \frac{\text{MP - CP}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 \)

Using the ratio: \( \frac{7 - 5}{5} \times 100 \)

Percentage Mark-up = \( \frac{2}{5} \times 100 = 40\% \)

Answer: He must mark the article 40% above the cost price.

⚡ Exam Tricks & Shortcuts

1. Ratio Method for Percentages

  • Concept: Convert percentages to fractions for quicker calculations.
  • Trick:
    • If 20% profit, ratio of SP:CP is 120:100 or 6:5. So \( \text{SP} = \frac{6}{5}\text{CP} \).
    • If 10% loss, ratio of SP:CP is 90:100 or 9:10. So \( \text{SP} = \frac{9}{10}\text{CP} \).
    • If 25% discount, ratio of SP:MP is 75:100 or 3:4. So \( \text{SP} = \frac{3}{4}\text{MP} \).
  • Benefit: Avoids decimal calculations, often simplifies to integers.
  • Example: An item bought for ₹400 makes a 15% profit. CP = ₹400. SP = \( 400 \times \frac{100+15}{100} = 400 \times \frac{115}{100} = 4 \times 115 = \) ₹460.

2. Direct CP-MP-P/L%-D% Relation

  • Concept: The master formula \( \text{CP} \times (100 \pm \text{P/L %}) = \text{MP} \times (100 - \text{D %}) \) is a powerful shortcut.
  • Trick: Directly plug in known values to find any one unknown.
  • Benefit: Bypasses intermediate SP calculations, saving time.
  • Example: A shopkeeper gains 20% after allowing a 10% discount. Find the % mark-up. Let CP = 100. \( 100 \times (100 + 20) = \text{MP} \times (100 - 10) \) \( 100 \times 120 = \text{MP} \times 90 \) \( \text{MP} = \frac{100 \times 120}{90} = \frac{100 \times 4}{3} = \) ₹133.33. Mark-up = \( \frac{\text{MP - CP}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 = \frac{133.33 - 100}{100} \times 100 = 33.33\% \).

3. Assuming CP = 100 or 1000

  • Concept: When only percentages are involved, assume a convenient base value for CP.
  • Trick: Set CP = 100 (or 1000 if decimals are expected) to simplify calculations.
  • Benefit: Converts percentage problems into simple arithmetic.
  • Example: Same as Example 3, Hard, in Solved Examples. Assuming CP=100 made calculations straightforward.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Pitfalls

1. Incorrect Base for Profit/Loss %

  • Mistake: Calculating Profit/Loss % on SP instead of CP.
  • How to Avoid: Always remember P/L % is on CP unless specifically mentioned. The Cost Price is the true investment base.
  • Example: Profit ₹20 on CP ₹100. P% = \( \frac{20}{100} \times 100 = 20\% \). NOT \( \frac{20}{120} \times 100 \).

2. Incorrect Base for Discount %

  • Mistake: Calculating Discount % on CP or SP instead of MP.
  • How to Avoid: Discount is always a reduction from the advertised (marked) price.
  • Example: Discount ₹10 on MP ₹100. D% = \( \frac{10}{100} \times 100 = 10\% \). NOT \( \frac{10}{90} \times 100 \) if SP is ₹90.

3. Confusing Mark-up and Profit

  • Mistake: Thinking a "20% mark-up" means 20% profit.
  • How to Avoid: Mark-up is on CP to reach MP. Profit is based on CP after discount is applied to MP. They are different if a discount is given.
  • Example: CP 100, Mark-up 20% -> MP 120. If 10% discount on MP, SP = 108. Profit = 8%, not 20%.

4. Arithmetic Errors

  • Mistake: Small calculation errors, especially with percentages or fractions.
  • How to Avoid: Practice mental math, use ratio methods carefully, and double-check calculations. Break complex calculations into smaller, manageable steps.

📊 Exam Strategy & Weightage

SSC CGL/CHSL/MTS/CPO/GD

  • Questions: 1-2 direct questions from Profit & Loss, often involving CP, SP, MP, and discounts.
  • Type: Word problems. Sometimes combined with successive transactions or dishonest dealer concepts (covered in next microtopic).
  • Strategy: Master the direct formula \( \text{CP} \times (100 \pm \text{P/L %}) = \text{MP} \times (100 - \text{D %}) \) for quick solutions. Practice questions assuming CP=100.
  • Time: Aim for 30-45 seconds per question.

Railway Exams (NTPC/Group D)

  • Questions: Typically 1-2 questions, generally straightforward.
  • Type: Basic calculations of profit/loss, percentage, or finding SP/CP/MP given other values.
  • Strategy: Focus on fundamental formulas and understanding the base for P/L and Discount.
  • Time: Aim for 20-40 seconds per question.

Banking Exams (IBPS/SBI PO/Clerk)

  • Questions: 1-2 direct questions, but more commonly integrated into Data Interpretation (DI) sets or caselets.
  • Type: Word problems (often slightly trickier than SSC), DI questions with P&L data.
  • Strategy: Strong conceptual clarity and quick calculation skills are vital. Practice DI sets. The Master Formula is very useful here.
  • Time: Aim for 45-60 seconds for standalone questions, be efficient when part of DI.

CDS/CAPF/State PCS/Police

  • Questions: 1-2 questions. CDS can have moderate difficulty, others usually basic to medium.
  • Type: Word problems. May involve slightly complex scenarios or multiple percentage changes.
  • Strategy: Comprehensive understanding of all formulas and their applications. Practice diverse problem types.
  • Time: Aim for 45-60 seconds per question.

📌 Summary

  • Cost Price (CP): Price paid by the merchant. Base for P/L calculation.
  • Selling Price (SP): Price at which item is sold.
  • Marked Price (MP): Advertised price. Base for Discount calculation.
  • Profit: SP > CP. Profit % = \( \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 \).
  • Loss: CP > SP. Loss % = \( \frac{\text{Loss}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 \).
  • Discount: Reduction on MP. Discount % = \( \frac{\text{Discount}}{\text{MP}} \times 100 \).
  • Key Relation: \( \text{CP} \times (100 \pm \text{Profit/Loss %}) = \text{MP} \times (100 - \text{Discount %}) \).

🚀 Quick Revision Box: CP, SP & MP Relations

  • CP: Your cost. SP: Your selling price. MP: Tag price.
  • Profit (P): SP - CP (if SP > CP)
  • Loss (L): CP - SP (if CP > SP)
  • P% = \($ \frac{P}{CP} \times 100 \)
  • L% = \($ \frac{L}{CP} \times 100 \)
  • Discount (D): MP - SP
  • D% = \($ \frac{D}{MP} \times 100 \)
  • SP from CP:
    • With P%: \($ \text{CP} \times \frac{100+P\%}{100} \)
    • With L%: \($ \text{CP} \times \frac{100-L\%}{100} \)
  • SP from MP: \($ \text{MP} \times \frac{100-D\%}{100} \)
  • Master Relation: \($ \text{CP} \times (100 \pm \text{P/L%}) = \text{MP} \times (100 - \text{D%}) \)
  • Key Rule: P/L % is on CP. D% is on MP.
  • Trick: Assume CP=100 for percentage-only problems.

🚀 Key Takeaways

  • Always identify the base (CP or MP) for percentage calculations.
  • The Master Formula relating CP, MP, Profit/Loss%, and Discount% is highly efficient.
  • Assuming CP=100 simplifies problems involving only percentages.
  • Practice converting percentages to fractions for faster calculations.
  • A clear understanding of these basics is fundamental to all advanced P&L topics.
