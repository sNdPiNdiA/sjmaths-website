Basic Arithmetic Operations on Fractions

Medium Difficulty 25-30 Mins Read High Exam Weightage Updated 2026

📘 1. Introduction

In competitive exams like SSC CGL, Banking (IBPS, SBI PO/Clerk), Railways, and CDS, speed and accuracy are the two pillars of success. At the heart of all quantitative calculations lies the arithmetic of Fractions.

Basic Arithmetic Operations on Fractions (Addition, Subtraction, Multiplication, and Division) form the absolute bedrock of numerical ability. Whether you are solving a direct simplification question, calculating ratios, determining efficiency in Time & Work, or parsing a complex Data Interpretation (DI) table, you will perform these operations repeatedly.

This module bypasses slow, conventional school-level methods and equips you with high-speed mental math techniques, shortcut rules (like the Butterfly Method), and exam-oriented strategies designed to save precious seconds under pressure.

🎯 2. Learning Objectives

  • 🔹 Master rapid Addition and Subtraction of like and unlike fractions using optimized LCM methods.
  • 🔹 Apply the "Butterfly Method" for instantaneous cross-multiplication of two fractions.
  • 🔹 Execute lightning-fast Multiplication and Division of proper, improper, and mixed fractions.
  • 🔹 Implement the "Integer-Fraction Splitting Technique" to solve mixed fraction equations without converting them to heavy improper forms.
  • 🔹 Recognize and avoid common calculation traps to ensure 100% accuracy in high-speed exam environments.

📝 3. Prerequisites

Before diving into arithmetic operations, ensure you are comfortable with these fundamental building blocks:

1. Prime Factorization & LCM

Quickly finding the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of denominators mentally (e.g., \(\text{LCM}(12, 18) = 36\)).

2. Fraction Inter-conversion

Seamlessly converting mixed fractions to improper fractions (e.g., \(4\frac{2}{3} \rightarrow \frac{14}{3}\)) and vice-versa.

3. HCF & Simplification

Reducing fractions to their lowest terms by cancelling out the Highest Common Factor (HCF).

💡 4. Core Concepts

Sub-concept 1: Addition & Subtraction of Like Fractions

Definition: Like fractions are fractions that share the exact same denominator.

Explanation & Intuition: Because the units (denominators) are identical, you simply add or subtract the numerators while keeping the denominator constant. Think of it as adding similar objects: 2 apples + 3 apples = 5 apples. Similarly, 2 sevenths + 3 sevenths = 5 sevenths.

$$\frac{a}{d} \pm \frac{b}{d} = \frac{a \pm b}{d}$$

Important Observation: Always check if the final resulting fraction can be simplified to its lowest terms.

Memory Tip: "Keep the base, add/sub the face!" (Base = Denominator, Face = Numerator).
Common Mistake: NEVER add or subtract the denominators. \(\frac{1}{5} + \frac{2}{5} \neq \frac{3}{10}\).
Mini Example: Simplify \(\frac{7}{12} + \frac{11}{12} - \frac{5}{12}\).
Solution: Since denominators are identical, merge the numerators: \(\frac{7 + 11 - 5}{12} = \frac{13}{12} = 1\frac{1}{12}\).

Sub-concept 2: Addition & Subtraction of Unlike Fractions (LCM Method)

Definition: Unlike fractions are fractions that have different denominators.

Explanation & Intuition: You cannot directly add or subtract fractions of different sizes (e.g., half an apple plus a third of an apple). You must first convert them into equivalent fractions with a common denominator, which is the LCM of the original denominators.

Step-by-Step Process:

  1. Find the LCM of the denominators.
  2. Convert each fraction to an equivalent fraction with the LCM as the denominator.
  3. Add or subtract the new numerators.
  4. Simplify the final fraction.
$$\text{To calculate } \frac{3}{4} + \frac{5}{6}, \text{ find } \text{LCM}(4, 6) = 12.$$ $$\text{Convert: } \frac{3 \times 3}{4 \times 3} = \frac{9}{12}, \quad \frac{5 \times 2}{6 \times 2} = \frac{10}{12}$$ $$\text{Add: } \frac{9 + 10}{12} = \frac{19}{12} = 1\frac{7}{12}$$
Memory Tip: The LCM acts as a "common ground" where fractions of different sizes can speak the same language.
Mini Example: Evaluate \(\frac{5}{8} - \frac{1}{6}\).
Solution: \(\text{LCM}(8, 6) = 24\). Convert: \(\frac{5 \times 3}{24} - \frac{1 \times 4}{24} = \frac{15 - 4}{24} = \frac{11}{24}\).

Sub-concept 3: Multiplication of Fractions

Definition: Multiplication involves taking a fractional part of another fraction.

Explanation & Intuition: When you multiply \(\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{3}\), you are literally asking: "What is half of one-third?" The result is naturally smaller: \(\frac{1}{6}\). Unlike addition, you do not need a common denominator.

$$\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \times c}{b \times d}$$

Strategic Action (Cross-Cancellation): Before multiplying the numerators and denominators, always look diagonally and vertically to cancel out common factors. This keeps the numbers small and easy to manage mentally.

Common Mistake: Multiplying mixed fractions directly (e.g., \(2\frac{1}{2} \times 3\frac{1}{3} \neq 6\frac{1}{6}\)). You MUST convert mixed fractions to improper fractions before multiplying!
Mini Example: Multiply \(\frac{14}{15} \times \frac{25}{21}\).
Solution: Instead of doing \(14 \times 25\), cross-cancel:
  • \(14\) and \(21\) share factor \(7 \rightarrow\) becomes \(2\) and \(3\).
  • \(25\) and \(15\) share factor \(5 \rightarrow\) becomes \(5\) and \(3\).
Result: \(\frac{2 \times 5}{3 \times 3} = \frac{10}{9} = 1\frac{1}{9}\).

Sub-concept 4: Division of Fractions (Keep-Change-Flip)

Definition: Dividing by a fraction is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal.

Explanation & Intuition: Dividing a number by \(\frac{1}{2}\) answers the question: "How many halves are in this number?" There are exactly 2 halves in 1, so dividing by \(\frac{1}{2}\) is the same as multiplying by 2. Thus, division becomes multiplication once you reciprocal the divisor.

$$\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c} = \frac{a \times d}{b \times c}$$
Memory Tip (KCF):
  • Keep the first fraction.
  • Change division to multiplication.
  • Flip the second fraction (reciprocal).
Mini Example: Evaluate \(\frac{9}{16} \div \frac{27}{20}\).
Solution: Apply KCF: \(\frac{9}{16} \times \frac{20}{27}\). Cancel common terms:
  • \(9\) and \(27\) cancel to \(1\) and \(3\).
  • \(20\) and \(16\) cancel to \(5\) and \(4\) (divided by 4).
Result: \(\frac{1 \times 5}{4 \times 3} = \frac{5}{12}\).

📏 5. Rules & Properties

Rule 1: Commutative & Associative Laws

Explanation: Addition and multiplication of fractions are commutative and associative. Subtraction and division are NOT.

$$\frac{a}{b} + \frac{c}{d} = \frac{c}{d} + \frac{a}{b} \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{c}{d} \times \frac{a}{b}$$

Shortcut Application: In a long simplification expression, reorder additions and multiplications to group fractions with common denominators or easily cancelable terms first.

Rule 2: Separating Whole and Fractional Parts in Mixed Fractions

Explanation: A mixed fraction \(A\frac{b}{c}\) is mathematically defined as \(A + \frac{b}{c}\). Therefore, when adding or subtracting mixed fractions, you can perform operations on the whole numbers and fractional parts separately.

$$A\frac{b}{c} + X\frac{y}{z} = (A + X) + \left(\frac{b}{c} + \frac{y}{z}\right)$$

Why use this? It prevents you from converting mixed fractions into massive, calculation-heavy improper fractions, drastically cutting down computation time.

Example: \(105\frac{2}{3} + 95\frac{1}{4} = (105 + 95) + \left(\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4}\right) = 200 + \left(\frac{8+3}{12}\right) = 200\frac{11}{12}\).

📐 6. Formula Box

Essential Operational Formulas

$$\text{1. Addition/Subtraction (Butterfly Cross): } \frac{a}{b} \pm \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ad \pm bc}{bd}$$ $$\text{2. Multiplication: } \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ac}{bd}$$ $$\text{3. Division: } \frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ad}{bc}$$

Variables: \(a, b, c, d\) are integers where denominators \(b, c, d \neq 0\).

When to use: Use the Butterfly Cross formula primarily when denominators are small, coprime, or when finding the LCM is slower than direct cross-multiplication.

When NOT to use: Do not use cross-multiplication for three or more fractions; instead, use the standard LCM method to avoid giant numbers in the denominator.

🧠 7. Solved Examples

Easy

Example 1: Basic Mixed Fraction Simplification

Question: Evaluate \(5\frac{2}{3} + 3\frac{1}{2} - 4\frac{5}{6}\).

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Separate the whole numbers from the fractions.

$$\text{Expression} = (5 + 3 - 4) + \left(\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{2} - \frac{5}{6}\right)$$

Step 2: Solve the integer part.

$$5 + 3 - 4 = 4$$

Step 3: Solve the fractional part using LCM of denominators (3, 2, 6) which is 6.

$$\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{2} - \frac{5}{6} = \frac{2 \times 2}{6} + \frac{1 \times 3}{6} - \frac{5}{6} = \frac{4 + 3 - 5}{6} = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$$

Step 4: Combine the parts.

$$4 + \frac{1}{3} = 4\frac{1}{3}$$

Correct Answer: \(4\frac{1}{3}\)

Medium

Example 2: Arithmetic Sequence of Operations

Question: Simplify the expression: \(\left( \frac{5}{14} \times \frac{7}{10} \right) \div \frac{3}{8} + \frac{1}{3}\).

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Execute the multiplication inside the parenthesis first (BODMAS).

$$\frac{5}{14} \times \frac{7}{10} = \frac{\not{5}^1}{\not{14}_2} \times \frac{\not{7}^1}{\not{10}_2} = \frac{1 \times 1}{2 \times 2} = \frac{1}{4}$$

Step 2: Now substitute and perform the division: \(\frac{1}{4} \div \frac{3}{8}\).

$$\text{Apply KCF: } \frac{1}{4} \times \frac{8}{3} = \frac{\not{8}^2}{\not{4}_1 \times 3} = \frac{2}{3}$$

Step 3: Perform the final addition: \(\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{3}\).

$$\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2+1}{3} = \frac{3}{3} = 1$$

Correct Answer: 1

Hard

Example 3: Mixed Fraction with Negative Fractional Remainder

Question: Simplify: \(12\frac{1}{4} - 5\frac{2}{3} - 2\frac{5}{12}\).

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Separate integers and fractions.

$$\text{Expression} = (12 - 5 - 2) + \left(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{2}{3} - \frac{5}{12}\right)$$ $$\text{Integer Part} = 5$$

Step 2: Solve the fractional part. The LCM of 4, 3, and 12 is 12.

$$\text{Fractional Part} = \frac{1 \times 3}{12} - \frac{2 \times 4}{12} - \frac{5}{12} = \frac{3 - 8 - 5}{12} = \frac{-10}{12} = -\frac{5}{6}$$

Step 3: Combine the parts: \(5 + \left(-\frac{5}{6}\right) = 5 - \frac{5}{6}\).

Trick: Borrow 1 unit from the integer 5 to resolve the negative fraction:

$$5 - \frac{5}{6} = 4 + \left(1 - \frac{5}{6}\right) = 4 + \frac{1}{6} = 4\frac{1}{6}$$

Correct Answer: \(4\frac{1}{6}\)

Exam Level

Example 4: High-Yield Exam Simplification Pattern

Question: Find the value of \(X\) if: \(X + 2\frac{1}{3} \text{ of } \frac{3}{14} = 3\frac{1}{6} \div \frac{19}{12}\).

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Simplify the "of" term first. Recall that "of" is treated as multiplication but with higher precedence than division in standard BODMAS/VBODMAS.

$$2\frac{1}{3} = \frac{7}{3}$$ $$\frac{7}{3} \text{ of } \frac{3}{14} = \frac{\not{7}^1}{\not{3}^1} \times \frac{\not{3}^1}{\not{14}_2} = \frac{1}{2}$$

So, LHS becomes \(X + \frac{1}{2}\).

Step 2: Simplify the RHS: \(3\frac{1}{6} \div \frac{19}{12}\).

$$3\frac{1}{6} = \frac{19}{6}$$ $$\frac{19}{6} \div \frac{19}{12} = \frac{\not{19}^1}{\not{6}_1} \times \frac{\not{12}^2}{\not{19}_1} = 2$$

Step 3: Equate LHS and RHS to solve for \(X\).

$$X + \frac{1}{2} = 2 \implies X = 2 - \frac{1}{2} = 1\frac{1}{2} \text{ or } 1.5$$

Correct Answer: \(1\frac{1}{2}\) (or \(1.5\))

⚡ 8. Exam Tricks & Shortcuts

The "Butterfly Method" for Fast 2-Fraction Operations

To add or subtract exactly two unlike fractions in seconds without explicitly finding the LCM, use cross-multiplication:

$$\frac{a}{b} \nwarrow \nearrow \frac{c}{d} \implies \frac{(a \times d) \pm (b \times c)}{b \times d}$$

Mental Workflow: Multiply the diagonals, add/subtract the products, and put them over the product of the bottom numbers.

Example: \(\frac{2}{5} + \frac{3}{7} = \frac{(2 \times 7) + (3 \times 5)}{35} = \frac{14 + 15}{35} = \frac{29}{35}\). Done in 3 seconds!

The "Borrowing Trick" for Mixed Subtraction

When subtracting mixed fractions, sometimes you get a negative fractional part (as seen in Example 3). Use this rapid borrowing formula instead of converting back to improper fractions:

$$A - \frac{x}{y} \implies (A - 1) + \left(\frac{y - x}{y}\right)$$

Example: \(83 - \frac{4}{11} = 82 + \left(\frac{11 - 4}{11}\right) = 82\frac{7}{11}\).

⚠️ 9. Common Mistakes & Pitfalls

1. Direct Denominator Addition

Mistake: Writing \(\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{7}\).

How to avoid: Remember that fractions cannot be combined unless they are converted to equal units (common denominator). Always use the Butterfly or LCM method.

2. Incorrect Divisor Reciprocation

Mistake: In \(\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{2}{5}\), flipping the first fraction to get \(\frac{4}{3} \times \frac{2}{5}\).

How to avoid: Always keep the first term constant (Keep-Change-Flip). Only flip the divisor (the term after the division sign).

3. Misinterpreting \(A\frac{b}{c}\) as \(A \times \frac{b}{c}\)

Mistake: Treating \(3\frac{1}{2}\) as \(3 \times \frac{1}{2} = 1.5\).

How to avoid: A mixed fraction represents addition: \(3\frac{1}{2} = 3 + \frac{1}{2} = 3.5\). Only parentheses imply multiplication: \(3(\frac{1}{2}) = 1.5\).

📌 11. Summary

  • Like fractions are combined by direct addition/subtraction of their numerators over the same denominator.
  • Unlike fractions require standardizing their denominators via the LCM of the denominators.
  • For rapid calculation of two fractions, bypass the LCM and execute the Butterfly Method.
  • When multiplying, prioritize cross-cancellation of numerators and denominators to maintain small numbers.
  • To divide, execute Keep-Change-Flip (KCF).
  • Mixed fractions are additive structures: \(A\frac{b}{c} = A + \frac{b}{c}\). Always process integers and fractions separately to bypass cumbersome calculations.

🚀 12. Key Takeaways

  • ✔️ Fraction arithmetic is not about calculation; it is about decomposition. Always seek to separate big terms into smaller components.
  • ✔️ Always reduce fractions as early as possible in a multi-step calculation.
  • ✔️ Convert to improper fractions only for multiplication and division; never for addition and subtraction.
  • ✔️ Watch your brackets (BODMAS)—always resolve "of" before starting division or multiplication.
