BODMAS Rule & Order of Operations (Brackets & Mixed Operations)

Medium Difficulty 25-30 Mins Read High Exam Weightage Updated 2026

📘 Introduction: The Golden Rules of Calculation

In competitive examinations like SSC CGL, Banking, Railways, and State PCS, VBODMAS & Simplification is the ultimate gateway to high-speed mathematics. Often, aspirants look at basic calculations and think, "This is just simple arithmetic, I can do this instantly!" However, without a systematic protocol, nested mathematical operations inevitably lead to calculation traps and negative marking.

The VBODMAS Rule is the operational hierarchy or "traffic control system" of mathematical calculations. It defines precisely which mathematical operator gets evaluated first when multiple operators compete for priority in an expression. Mastering this microtopic ensures you can solve complex expressions with absolute accuracy in under 30 seconds.

🎯 Exam Relevance:
  • SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO): 2 to 3 questions directly on VBODMAS / complex fraction simplifications.
  • Banking (IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI PO/Clerk): 5 to 10 questions of pure simplification where speed-solving nested expressions is the differentiator.
  • Railways & Police Exams: 3 to 4 direct, high-scoring questions.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this study module, you will be able to:

  • Deconstruct and sequence any complex mathematical expression using the strict VBODMAS order without hesitation.
  • Eliminate sign errors and structural mistakes associated with nested brackets: circular \( ( ) \), curly \( \{ \} \), and square \( [ ] \).
  • Correctly decode and solve the highly misunderstood Vinculum (Bar bracket) and "Of" (का) operators.
  • Execute multi-stage, continuous divisions sequentially from left to right without falling into reciprocal traps.
  • Apply elite mental math shortcuts, options elimination, and unit-digit analysis to crack exam-level simplification problems in under 45 seconds.

📋 Prerequisites

Before diving deep into the core concepts, ensure you are comfortable with the following foundational tools:

1. Basic Sign Rules

\( (+) \times (+) = (+) \)
\( (-) \times (-) = (+) \)
\( (+) \times (-) = (-) \)
\( (-) \times (+) = (-) \)

2. Fraction Conversions

Converting mixed fractions to improper fractions:
\( a\frac{b}{c} = \frac{a \times c + b}{c} \)
Example: \( 3\frac{2}{5} = \frac{3 \times 5 + 2}{5} = \frac{17}{5} \)

3. Multiplicative Reciprocals

Dividing by a fraction is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal:
\( A \div \frac{B}{C} = A \times \frac{C}{B} \)

🧠 Core Concepts

Concept 1: The VBODMAS Hierarchy Decoded

To ensure uniform calculations, the hierarchy of operators runs strictly from top to bottom. Let's understand each component of V-B-O-D-M-A-S:

Letter Stands For Symbol / Meaning Execution Priority
V Vinculum (Bar) \( \overline{x - y} \) 1st (Highest)
B Brackets \( ( ) \rightarrow \{ \} \rightarrow [ ] \) 2nd
O Of (का) Multiplication (\( \times \)) with high priority 3rd
D Division \( \div \) or \( / \) 4th
M Multiplication \( \times \) 5th
A Addition \( + \) 6th
S Subtraction \( - \) 7th (Lowest)

Intuition: Think of the hierarchy as layers of packaging. You cannot access the candy inside (Addition/Subtraction) until you have unwrapped the external boxes (Vinculum, Brackets) and handled the high-level operators (Of, Division).

Memory Tip: Remember the phrase: "Very Bad Operations Destroy Maths Accuracy Swiftly" to remember the exact sequence!

⚠️ Common Mistake: Confusing the operation order of 'Of' and 'Division'.
For example, in \( 10 \div 2 \text{ of } 5 \), many students divide first: \( 5 \text{ of } 5 = 25 \). This is incorrect. You must evaluate 'Of' first: \( 10 \div (2 \text{ of } 5) = 10 \div 10 = 1 \).

Mini Example: Evaluate \( 12 + 6 \div 3 \times 2 \)
Step 1 (Division): \( 6 \div 3 = 2 \). The expression becomes \( 12 + 2 \times 2 \).
Step 2 (Multiplication): \( 2 \times 2 = 4 \). The expression becomes \( 12 + 4 \).
Step 3 (Addition): \( 12 + 4 = 16 \).

Concept 2: Vinculum (Bar Bracket) — The Silent Trap

The Vinculum (\( \overline{\text{bar}} \)) is written above two or more numbers. It behaves exactly like an invisible, high-priority grouping bracket around the terms underneath it.

Crucial Rule: The negative sign outside the bar bracket does not belong to the first term under the bar. It acts outside the entire grouped expression.

Visual Comparison:
\( a - \overline{b - c} \quad \text{is mathematically identical to} \quad a - (b - c) \)
It is NOT equal to \( a - b - c \) or \( (a - b) - c \).

Mini Example: Evaluate \( 15 - \overline{7 - 4} \)
Correct Method: Solve the bar first: \( \overline{7 - 4} = 3 \). Expression becomes \( 15 - 3 = 12 \).
Incorrect Trap: Distributing negative sign immediately: \( 15 - 7 - 4 = 4 \) (Wrong!).

Concept 3: Types of Brackets & Inside-Out Evaluation

When multiple brackets are nested within each other, the order of removal is strictly inside-out:

  1. Small / Round Brackets \( ( ) \): Resolved first.
  2. Curly / Braces \( \{ \} \): Resolved second, after all inside terms are reduced.
  3. Square / Box Brackets \( [ ] \): Resolved last.

The Golden Strategy: Always scan the expression visually to locate the innermost parentheses, simplify them completely, and then peel back the layers step-by-step.

Mini Example: Evaluate \( 20 - [ 8 - \{ 6 - (5 - 3) \} ] \)
Step 1 (Innermost circular bracket): \( 5 - 3 = 2 \). Expression: \( 20 - [ 8 - \{ 6 - 2 \} ] \)
Step 2 (Curly braces): \( 6 - 2 = 4 \). Expression: \( 20 - [ 8 - 4 ] \)
Step 3 (Square bracket): \( 8 - 4 = 4 \). Expression: \( 20 - 4 \)
Step 4 (Final subtraction): \( 20 - 4 = 16 \).

📏 Rules & Properties

Rule 1: Consecutive Divisions (The Left-to-Right Rule)

When an expression contains consecutive division symbols without any grouping brackets, evaluate strictly from Left to Right. Alternatively, convert all divisions into multiplications by taking the reciprocal of the terms following the division symbol.

Mathematical Identity:
$$ a \div b \div c \div d = \left( (a \div b) \div c \right) \div d = a \times \frac{1}{b} \times \frac{1}{c} \times \frac{1}{d} = \frac{a}{b \cdot c \cdot d} $$

Shortcut: Just keep the first number in the numerator and pull all subsequent dividing numbers into the denominator with multiplication signs!

Example: Evaluate \( 64 \div 8 \div 4 \div 2 \)
Using the shortcut: \( \frac{64}{8 \times 4 \times 2} = \frac{64}{64} = 1 \).
(If evaluated randomly or right-to-left, you get an incorrect answer of \( 64 \)).

Rule 2: "Of" vs "Multiplication" Operator

While both "Of" and "Multiplication" (\( \times \)) translate to standard arithmetic multiplication, they are placed at different priority levels in VBODMAS:

  • Of is calculated before Division.
  • Multiplication (\( \times \)) is calculated after Division.

Comparative Analysis:
Case A: \( 24 \div 4 \text{ of } 3 \implies 24 \div (4 \times 3) = 24 \div 12 = 2 \).
Case B: \( 24 \div 4 \times 3 \implies (24 \div 4) \times 3 = 6 \times 3 = 18 \).

📐 Formula Box

💡 Standard VBODMAS Priority Formula Sheet

When evaluating algebraic or numerical expressions, resolve terms in this absolute sequence:

$$ \text{Expression} = \overline{\text{Vinculum}} \rightarrow \mathbf{( ~ )} \rightarrow \mathbf{\{ ~ \}} \rightarrow \mathbf{[ ~ ]} \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{Of}} \rightarrow \mathbf{\div} \rightarrow \mathbf{\times} \rightarrow \mathbf{+} \rightarrow \mathbf{-} $$

Key Identities for Fast Tracking:

  • Sequential Division: \( a \div \frac{b}{c} = a \times \frac{c}{b} \)
  • Double Division Grouping: \( \frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c} = \frac{ad}{bc} \)
  • Distributive Law (Safe Bracket Opening): \( a(b \pm c) = ab \pm ac \)

⚠️ When NOT to use: Do not skip steps in sequential division if they are interspersed with other operators (like '+' or '-'). Apply the left-to-right rule step-by-step in those segments first.

📝 Solved Examples

Example 1: Basic VBODMAS Execution with Nested Brackets

Easy

Question: Evaluate the expression:
$$ 18 - \left[ 6 - \left\{ 4 - \left( 8 - \overline{6 - 3} \right) \right\} \right] $$

View Step-by-Step Solution

Follow the order of VBODMAS strictly:

Step 1: Simplify the Vinculum (Bar bracket) first:
\( \overline{6 - 3} = 3 \).
The expression reduces to: \( 18 - [6 - \{4 - (8 - 3)\}] \)

Step 2: Simplify the innermost Circular Bracket \( ( ) \):
\( 8 - 3 = 5 \).
The expression reduces to: \( 18 - [6 - \{4 - 5\}] \)

Step 3: Simplify the Curly Braces \( \{ \} \):
\( 4 - 5 = -1 \).
The expression reduces to: \( 18 - [6 - (-1)] \)
Since \( -(-1) = +1 \), it becomes: \( 18 - [6 + 1] \)

Step 4: Simplify the Square Bracket \( [ ] \):
\( 6 + 1 = 7 \).
The expression reduces to: \( 18 - [7] \)

Step 5: Final Subtraction:
\( 18 - 7 = 11 \).

Answer: 11

Example 2: Fractions and the Priority of "Of"

Medium

Question: Find the value of:
$$ \frac{3}{4} \div \frac{5}{6} \text{ of } \frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{5} $$

View Step-by-Step Solution

Apply VBODMAS hierarchy: Of is calculated before Division.

Step 1: Solve the "Of" operator:
\( \frac{5}{6} \text{ of } \frac{2}{3} = \frac{5}{6} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{5 \times 2}{6 \times 3} = \frac{10}{18} = \frac{5}{9} \).
The expression now is: \( \frac{3}{4} \div \frac{5}{9} + \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{5} \)

Step 2: Solve the Division operator:
Multiply by the reciprocal of \( \frac{5}{9} \):
\( \frac{3}{4} \div \frac{5}{9} = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{9}{5} = \frac{27}{20} \).
The expression now is: \( \frac{27}{20} + \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{5} \)

Step 3: Solve the Multiplication operator:
\( \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{5} = \frac{3}{10} \).
The expression now is: \( \frac{27}{20} + \frac{3}{10} \)

Step 4: Perform final Addition:
Make the denominators equal (LCM of 20 and 10 is 20):
\( \frac{3}{10} = \frac{6}{20} \)
\( \frac{27}{20} + \frac{6}{20} = \frac{33}{20} = 1\frac{13}{20} \).

Answer: \( \frac{33}{20} \) or \( 1\frac{13}{20} \)

Example 3: High-Level SSC CGL Mains Nested Mixed Fractions Practice

Exam Level (Hard)

Question: Simplify:
$$ 7\frac{1}{2} - \left[ 2\frac{1}{4} \div \left\{ 1\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{2} \left( 2\frac{1}{2} - \overline{\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{6}} \right) \right\} \right] $$

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Convert all mixed fractions to improper fractions:
\( 7\frac{1}{2} = \frac{15}{2} \)
\( 2\frac{1}{4} = \frac{9}{4} \)
\( 1\frac{1}{4} = \frac{5}{4} \)
\( 2\frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{2} \)
The expression is: \( \frac{15}{2} - \left[ \frac{9}{4} \div \left\{ \frac{5}{4} - \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{5}{2} - \overline{\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{6}} \right) \right\} \right] \)

Step 2: Solve the Vinculum (Bar):
\( \overline{\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{6}} = \frac{3 - 2}{12} = \frac{1}{12} \).
Expression: \( \frac{15}{2} - \left[ \frac{9}{4} \div \left\{ \frac{5}{4} - \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{5}{2} - \frac{1}{12} \right) \right\} \right] \)

Step 3: Solve the parentheses \( ( ) \):
\( \frac{5}{2} - \frac{1}{12} = \frac{30 - 1}{12} = \frac{29}{12} \).
Now the expression inside curly braces becomes: \( \left\{ \frac{5}{4} - \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{29}{12} \right\} = \left\{ \frac{5}{4} - \frac{29}{24} \right\} \)

Step 4: Solve the curly braces \( \{ \} \):
\( \frac{5}{4} - \frac{29}{24} = \frac{30 - 29}{24} = \frac{1}{24} \).
The square bracket term now becomes: \( \left[ \frac{9}{4} \div \frac{1}{24} \right] \)

Step 5: Solve the square bracket \( [ ] \):
\( \frac{9}{4} \div \frac{1}{24} = \frac{9}{4} \times 24 = 9 \times 6 = 54 \).
The entire expression reduces to: \( \frac{15}{2} - 54 \)

Step 6: Final calculation:
\( \frac{15}{2} - 54 = 7.5 - 54 = -46.5 = -46\frac{1}{2} = -\frac{93}{2} \).

Answer: \( -46\frac{1}{2} \) or \( -\frac{93}{2} \)

⚡ Exam Tricks & Shortcuts

🚀 Trick 1: The "Split-and-Solve" Method for Complex Fractions

If you face a giant fractional expression with separate complex numerators and denominators, do not solve them together! Treat them as two completely independent problems: Problem A (Numerator) and Problem B (Denominator). Simplify them in parallel sections of your scratch sheet and put them together at the final step. This prevents mixed-line calculation pollution.

🚀 Trick 2: Conversion of Division to Reciprocal Multiplication

Whenever you see fractional divisions, immediately replace \( \div \) with \( \times \) and invert the next fraction. This allows you to cancel terms across multiple fractions in a single step rather than computing intermediate divisions.
Formula: \( \frac{A}{B} \div \frac{C}{D} \div \frac{E}{F} = \frac{A}{B} \times \frac{D}{C} \times \frac{F}{E} \).

🚀 Trick 3: Digital Root & Unit Digit Elimination (Cautionary Tip)

For Bank/SSC exams with multiple-choice questions, you can check the last digit of the final step before solving. However, never use digital root methods directly while division operations are pending, as dividing decimal fractions changes digital roots unpredictably. Once you resolve division and are left with only \( +, - \) or \( \times \), digital roots are 100% safe to find the correct option instantly!

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Pitfalls

⛔ Pitfall 1: Incorrect Sign Multiplication on Bracket Opening

The Mistake: Writing \( -(x - y) = -x - y \).

How to Avoid: The negative sign outside a bracket multiplies every term inside. Always remember that opening the bracket flips all internal signs: \( -(x - y) = -x + y \).

⛔ Pitfall 2: Treating "Of" as standard multiplication under Division

The Mistake: Evaluating \( 15 \div 5 \times 3 \) the same way as \( 15 \div 5 \text{ of } 3 \).

How to Avoid: In the first case, divide first: \( (15 \div 5) \times 3 = 9 \). In the second case, multiply the 'of' terms first: \( 15 \div (5 \times 3) = 15 \div 15 = 1 \).

⛔ Pitfall 3: Evaluating sequential divisions from Right-to-Left

The Mistake: For \( 100 \div 10 \div 2 \), calculating \( 10 \div 2 = 5 \) first, then \( 100 \div 5 = 20 \).

How to Avoid: Go left-to-right: \( (100 \div 10) \div 2 = 10 \div 2 = 5 \).

📌 Summary

The VBODMAS rule is an unambiguous protocol designed to resolve operator conflicts. A bar bracket (vinculum) behaves as the innermost bracket. Brackets must be resolved inside-out: \( ( ) \rightarrow \{ \} \rightarrow [ ] \). The operator 'Of' is a multiplication executed before division, whereas standard multiplication (\( \times \)) is evaluated after division. Division of continuous numbers must be solved from left to right.

🚀 Quick Revision Cheatsheet

The Hierarchy:
1. Vinculum (\( \overline{\text{Bar}} \))
2. Brackets: \( ( ) \rightarrow \{ \} \rightarrow [ ] \)
3. Of (\( \text{ka} \))
4. Division (\( \div \))
5. Multiply (\( \times \))
6. Add (\( + \))
7. Subtract (\( - \))

Signs & Inversions:
\( - (a - b) = -a + b \)
\( a \div b \div c = \frac{a}{b \times c} \)
\( \frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \times d}{b \times c} \)

Pro-Exam Tip:
Always solve the Numerator and Denominator of fraction-heavy problems as two independent calculations, joining them at the final step to keep working space organized.

🧠 Key Takeaways

  • A Vinculum has supreme authority; calculate whatever sits under the bar first.
  • Never treat \( \text{'Of'} \) as a simple \( \times \) sign if division exists in the same expression. Solve it in its designated step first.
  • When operations of equal priority occur concurrently (like multiple additions/subtractions or multiple multiplications/divisions), calculate strictly from left to right.
  • Take extra care with brackets prefixed with a negative sign; they change the sign of every term inside when opened.
