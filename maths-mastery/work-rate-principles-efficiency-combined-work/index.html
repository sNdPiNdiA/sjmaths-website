Work-rate Principles, Efficiency & Combined Work

Medium Difficulty 25-30 Mins Read High Exam Weightage Updated 2026

📘 Introduction to Work-rate Principles, Efficiency & Combined Work

This microtopic lays the foundation for solving problems involving work done by individuals or groups over time. Understanding Work-rate (Efficiency), its relation to total Work, and Time is crucial for mastering Time & Work concepts.

  • Exam Relevance: A fundamental topic, questions regularly appear in SSC CGL/CHSL, Banking exams (IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI PO/Clerk), Railway (NTPC, Group D), CDS, and State PCS.
  • Question Type: Typically involves calculating time taken, work done, or efficiency of individuals working alone or together.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Define Work, Time, and Work-rate (Efficiency) and understand their interrelationship.
  • Calculate the efficiency of individuals and groups.
  • Apply the LCM Method to determine Total Work.
  • Solve problems involving combined work of multiple individuals.
  • Identify and use direct and inverse proportionalities between Work, Time, and Efficiency.

📝 Prerequisites

  • Basic Arithmetic Operations (Addition, Subtraction, Multiplication, Division).
  • Understanding of Fractions and Percentages.
  • Ability to calculate LCM (Least Common Multiple) of numbers.

💡 Core Concepts

Work

  • Definition: The total task or job to be completed. It's often considered a measurable quantity.
  • Intuition: Think of "work" as the number of pages to type, units to produce, or distance to cover.
  • Key Observation: Work is often represented as '1 unit' (complete work) or a numerical value derived using the LCM method.
  • Memory Tip: Work is the "output" you aim for.
  • Mini Example: Painting a wall is "work". Building a house is "work".

Time

  • Definition: The duration required to complete the work.
  • Intuition: The number of days, hours, or minutes spent on a task.
  • Key Observation: Time units must be consistent throughout a problem (e.g., all in days or all in hours).
  • Memory Tip: Time is the "duration" taken.
  • Mini Example: A completes a task in 10 days. Here, "10 days" is the time.

Work-rate (Efficiency)

  • Definition: The amount of work done per unit of time. It measures how fast someone or something can complete a task.
  • Intuition: If you can type 50 words per minute, your typing rate is 50 words/minute. This is your efficiency.
  • Key Observation: Higher efficiency means less time to complete the same work.
  • Memory Tip: Efficiency = Work / Time. It's "Work per unit Time".
  • Mini Example: If A paints 1/10th of a wall per day, A's work-rate is $1/10$ wall/day.

Total Work (LCM Method)

  • Definition: A convenient assumed value for the total work, usually the LCM of the times taken by individuals.
  • Intuition: Instead of dealing with fractions, assume a total work that is easily divisible by all individual times.
  • Key Observation: This method simplifies calculations by converting fractional rates into integer "units per day/hour".
  • Memory Tip: Total Work = LCM (times given). This is your "common ground" for calculations.
  • Mini Example: A takes 10 days, B takes 15 days. Total Work = LCM(10, 15) = 30 units.

Combined Work (Total Work Done Together)

  • Definition: The sum of individual work rates when multiple people or entities work together on the same task.
  • Intuition: If A does 2 units/day and B does 3 units/day, together they do $2 + 3 = 5$ units/day.
  • Key Observation: Individual efficiencies are added to find the combined efficiency.
  • Memory Tip: "More hands make lighter work" – combined efficiency is always greater than individual efficiencies.
  • Mini Example: If A's rate is 2 units/day and B's rate is 3 units/day, their combined rate is $2+3=5$ units/day.

📏 Rules & Properties

Inverse Relationship between Time and Efficiency

  • Explanation: For a fixed amount of work, if efficiency increases, the time taken to complete the work decreases, and vice-versa.
  • Reason: Since $$ \text{Time} = \frac{\text{Work}}{\text{Efficiency}} $$ for constant Work, Time is inversely proportional to Efficiency.
  • Shortcut: If the ratio of efficiencies of A and B is $E_A : E_B$, then the ratio of times taken by them for the same work is $T_A : T_B = 1/E_A : 1/E_B = E_B : E_A$.
  • Exception: This holds true only when the amount of work is constant for both individuals/entities.
  • Example: If A is twice as efficient as B ($E_A : E_B = 2:1$), then A will take half the time B takes ($T_A : T_B = 1:2$).

Direct Relationship between Work and Efficiency

  • Explanation: For a fixed amount of time, if efficiency increases, the amount of work done also increases, and vice-versa.
  • Reason: Since $$ \text{Work} = \text{Efficiency} \times \text{Time} $$ for constant Time, Work is directly proportional to Efficiency.
  • Shortcut: If A and B work for the same duration, their work done will be in the ratio of their efficiencies. $W_A : W_B = E_A : E_B$.
  • Exception: This holds true only when the time spent is constant for both individuals/entities.
  • Example: If A and B work for 5 hours, and A is thrice as efficient as B, A will complete thrice the work B does.

LCM Method (Unitary Method for Time & Work)

  • Explanation: Assume the Total Work to be the LCM of the days/hours given for each person. This converts fractional daily work into whole units.
  • Reason: It eliminates fractions, making calculations easier and less prone to errors. It effectively creates a "common denominator" for work.
  • Shortcut: Step 1: Find LCM of times. Step 2: Calculate individual efficiencies (LCM / individual time). Step 3: Add efficiencies for combined work.
  • Exception: Best suited when discrete times are given for individuals. Less useful for problems involving percentages of work or complex variable equations.
  • Example: A takes 10 days, B takes 12 days. Total Work = LCM(10, 12) = 60 units. A's efficiency = $60/10 = 6$ units/day. B's efficiency = $60/12 = 5$ units/day.

📐 Formula Box

$$ \text{Work} = \text{Rate (Efficiency)} \times \text{Time} $$
  • Meaning: The total amount of task completed is the product of how fast it's done and for how long.
  • Variables: Work (W), Rate (R), Time (T).
  • When to use: To find total work when rate and time are known. Also, fundamental for deriving other formulas.
  • When NOT to use: When directly comparing efficiencies without knowing time or work.
  • Memory Trick: W = R × T (Work = Runs × Time, like in cricket!).
$$ \text{Rate (Efficiency)} = \frac{\text{Work}}{\text{Time}} $$
  • Meaning: Efficiency is the work done per unit of time.
  • Variables: Rate (R), Work (W), Time (T).
  • When to use: To find the efficiency of a person or machine given their work and time.
  • When NOT to use: If you only have time and need total work, use the Work = Rate × Time formula.
  • Memory Trick: R = W / T (Rate = Work / Time, simple rearrangement).
$$ \text{Time} = \frac{\text{Work}}{\text{Rate (Efficiency)}} $$
  • Meaning: The duration needed to complete a task is total work divided by the work done per unit time.
  • Variables: Time (T), Work (W), Rate (R).
  • When to use: To find the time taken to complete a specific amount of work, especially useful for combined work problems.
  • When NOT to use: If efficiency is the unknown, use Rate = Work / Time.
  • Memory Trick: T = W / R (Time = Work / Rate, another rearrangement).
$$ \text{Combined Rate} = R_1 + R_2 + R_3 + \dots $$
  • Meaning: When multiple individuals or entities work together, their individual rates of work add up to form a combined rate.
  • Variables: Combined Rate ($R_{total}$), Individual Rates ($R_1, R_2, \dots$).
  • When to use: To find the total efficiency when people work simultaneously. This combined rate is then used to find combined time.
  • When NOT to use: If people work alternately or stop/start work at different times, direct summation is incorrect for the entire duration.
  • Memory Trick: "Teamwork makes the dream work!" – sum up individual efforts.

🧠 Solved Examples

Easy

Example 1: Basic Efficiency Calculation

A can complete a piece of work in 20 days. What is A's daily work rate (efficiency)?

View Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Identify Given: Time taken by A = 20 days.
  2. Assume Total Work: Let the total work be 1 unit (or 20 units for LCM method simplicity, but 1 unit is common for single-person efficiency).
  3. Apply Formula: Work-rate = Work / Time.
  4. Calculate: A's daily work rate = $1 \text{ work} / 20 \text{ days} = 1/20 \text{ per day}$.
  5. Answer: A's daily efficiency is $1/20$ of the work per day.
Medium

Example 2: Combined Work (LCM Method)

A can do a piece of work in 12 days and B can do the same work in 15 days. In how many days will they complete the work if they work together?

View Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Identify Given: A's time = 12 days, B's time = 15 days.
  2. Determine Total Work (LCM Method): Find the LCM of 12 and 15.
    LCM(12, 15) = 60 units. (Assume Total Work = 60 units)
  3. Calculate Individual Efficiencies:
    • A's efficiency = Total Work / A's Time = $60 / 12 = 5$ units/day.
    • B's efficiency = Total Work / B's Time = $60 / 15 = 4$ units/day.
  4. Calculate Combined Efficiency:
    Combined efficiency of A and B = A's efficiency + B's efficiency = $5 + 4 = 9$ units/day.
  5. Calculate Time for Combined Work:
    Time = Total Work / Combined Efficiency = $60 / 9 = 20/3 = 6 \frac{2}{3}$ days.
  6. Answer: A and B will complete the work together in $6 \frac{2}{3}$ days.
Exam Level

Example 3: Efficiency Ratios and Combined Time

A is thrice as efficient as B. If A and B together can complete a work in 15 days, in how many days can A alone complete the work?

View Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Identify Given:
    • Efficiency ratio: A is thrice as efficient as B. So, $E_A : E_B = 3:1$.
    • Combined time ($T_{A+B}$) = 15 days.
  2. Assume Individual Efficiencies: Let B's efficiency be $E_B = 1$ unit/day.
    Then A's efficiency $E_A = 3$ units/day.
  3. Calculate Combined Efficiency:
    $E_{A+B} = E_A + E_B = 3 + 1 = 4$ units/day.
  4. Calculate Total Work:
    Total Work = Combined Efficiency × Combined Time = $4 \text{ units/day} \times 15 \text{ days} = 60$ units.
  5. Calculate Time for A Alone:
    Time for A = Total Work / A's Efficiency = $60 \text{ units} / 3 \text{ units/day} = 20$ days.
  6. Answer: A alone can complete the work in 20 days.

⚡ Exam Tricks & Shortcuts

1. Direct Formula for Combined Time (Two Persons)

  • Trick: If A completes work in $x$ days and B in $y$ days, working together they complete it in $\frac{x \times y}{x + y}$ days.
  • Why it works: Combined rate is $1/x + 1/y = (y+x)/xy$. Time = Work / Rate = $1 / ((x+y)/xy) = xy/(x+y)$.
  • Example: A in 12 days, B in 15 days. Together = $(12 \times 15) / (12 + 15) = 180 / 27 = 20/3$ days.

2. Ratio Method for Time & Efficiency

  • Trick: If $E_A : E_B = a:b$, then $T_A : T_B = b:a$ (for the same work). Use this to find unknown times or efficiencies.
  • Why it works: Inverse proportionality of Time and Efficiency.
  • Example: A is 50% more efficient than B. $E_A : E_B = 1.5 : 1 = 3:2$. So, $T_A : T_B = 2:3$. If B takes 30 days, A takes 20 days.

3. Mental LCM Calculation for Total Work

  • Trick: For 2-3 numbers, often you can quickly find the LCM. Pick the largest number and check its multiples until you find one divisible by all others.
  • Why it works: Speeds up the initial step of the LCM method.
  • Example: A in 6 days, B in 8 days, C in 12 days. Largest is 12. Multiples: 12 (not div by 8), 24 (div by 6, 8, 12). LCM = 24.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Pitfalls

  • Adding Times Directly: Do NOT add the individual times to find combined time. If A takes 10 days and B takes 15 days, they do NOT take $10+15=25$ days together. Always add their *rates* or *work done*.
  • Confusing Work and Rate: $1/10$ could be "1 unit of work in 10 days" OR "1/10th of the work per day." Understand context. Work-rate is *per unit time*.
  • Inconsistent Units: Ensure all time units (days, hours, minutes) are consistent throughout the problem. Convert everything to a single unit before calculation.
  • Incorrectly Applying Ratios: Remember that Time and Efficiency are inversely proportional. If A is twice as efficient, A takes half the time, not twice.
  • Neglecting Leftover Work: In complex problems, ensure you calculate the remaining work correctly after a person leaves or joins.
  • Assuming 100% Efficiency: Not all problems assume full efficiency. Efficiency may be given as a percentage or fraction.

📊 Exam Strategy & Weightage

SSC CGL/CHSL/MTS/CPO/GD

  • Weightage: 1-2 questions from Time & Work (includes Pipes & Cisterns). Typically 1-2 questions directly from Work-rate/Efficiency.
  • Question Types: Combined work, efficiency ratios, individual time calculation, sometimes involving 3 persons.
  • Strategy: Master the LCM method and direct/inverse proportionality. Practice 2-person and 3-person combined work problems.

Railway (NTPC, Group D)

  • Weightage: 1-2 questions. Often simpler, direct application of formulas.
  • Question Types: Basic combined work, finding individual efficiency, inverse relation between time and men.
  • Strategy: Focus on foundational concepts and the LCM method. Speed and accuracy are key for easier questions.

Banking (IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI PO/Clerk)

  • Weightage: 1-2 questions, potentially part of a Data Interpretation (DI) set.
  • Question Types: Combined work with varying efficiencies, sometimes involving men, women, children, or percentage-based efficiency changes.
  • Strategy: Emphasize efficiency ratios and percentage-based problems. Be prepared for slightly more complex scenarios or multi-step questions.

📌 Summary

  • Work-rate (Efficiency) is work done per unit time: \( \text{Rate} = \text{Work} / \text{Time} \).
  • Total Work can be assumed as 1 unit or, preferably, the LCM of individual times.
  • Time and Efficiency are inversely proportional for constant work: If $E_A : E_B = a:b$, then $T_A : T_B = b:a$.
  • Work and Efficiency are directly proportional for constant time.
  • Combined Efficiency of individuals working together is the sum of their individual efficiencies.
  • The LCM Method simplifies calculations by avoiding fractions.

🚀 Quick Revision Box: Work-rate & Efficiency

  • Fundamental Relation: $$ \text{Work} = \text{Efficiency} \times \text{Time} $$
  • Efficiency: $$ E = \frac{W}{T} \quad (\text{Work per unit Time}) $$
  • Inverse Proportionality: For constant Work, \( T \propto 1/E \).
    • If $E_1:E_2 = a:b$, then $T_1:T_2 = b:a$.
  • Direct Proportionality: For constant Time, \( W \propto E \).
    • If $E_1:E_2 = a:b$, then $W_1:W_2 = a:b$ (when time is same).
  • LCM Method: Assume Total Work = LCM of individual times.
    • Individual Efficiency = Total Work / Individual Time.
  • Combined Efficiency: For people $P_1, P_2, \dots$ working together: $$ E_{total} = E_1 + E_2 + \dots $$
  • Combined Time (2 people): If A in $x$ days, B in $y$ days, together in $$ \frac{xy}{x+y} $$ days.

✅ Key Takeaways

  • Always assume a Total Work using the LCM of given times to simplify calculations.
  • Efficiency is your fundamental building block for all Time & Work problems. Calculate it first.
  • Understand the inverse relationship between Time and Efficiency, and the direct relationship between Work and Efficiency.
  • For combined work, add efficiencies, not times.
  • Practice with varying scenarios (2 people, 3 people, efficiency ratios) to build confidence.
