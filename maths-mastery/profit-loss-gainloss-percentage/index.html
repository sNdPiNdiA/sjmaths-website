Profit, Loss & Gain/Loss Percentage

Medium Difficulty 25-30 Mins Read High Exam Weightage Updated 2026

📘 Introduction to Profit, Loss & Gain/Loss Percentage

This microtopic is fundamental to Commercial Arithmetic, dealing with the financial outcomes of buying and selling goods. It's about understanding whether a transaction results in a gain (profit) or a deficit (loss) relative to the initial cost, and quantifying this as a percentage.

  • Exam Relevance: A high-yield topic, consistently featuring 1-3 questions in almost all government exams like SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, GD, Railway, Banking (IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI PO/Clerk), CDS, CAPF, State PCS, and Police.
  • Foundation: Essential for understanding advanced topics like Discount, Successive P/L, and Partnership.
  • Scoring Potential: Questions are generally formula-based and conceptual, making them scoring if basics are strong.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

  • Define and differentiate between Cost Price (CP) and Selling Price (SP).
  • Identify conditions for Profit and Loss in a transaction.
  • Calculate Profit Percentage (Profit %) and Loss Percentage (Loss %) accurately.
  • Apply relevant formulas and conceptual shortcuts to solve a variety of problems.
  • Efficiently tackle exam-level questions involving profit and loss calculations.

📝 Prerequisites

Before diving into Profit & Loss, ensure you are comfortable with:

  • Basic Arithmetic Operations: Addition, Subtraction, Multiplication, and Division.
  • Fractions and Decimals: Converting between them and performing operations.
  • Percentage Basics: Calculating a percentage of a number, converting fractions to percentages, and understanding percentage increase/decrease concepts.

💡 Core Concepts

Cost Price (CP)

  • Definition: The price at which an article is purchased or the cost incurred to produce it.
  • Intuition: It's the initial investment or the 'starting point' for the seller.
  • Key Observation: This is the base value upon which profit or loss is calculated.
  • Memory Tip: CP is what it Costs you.
  • Mini Example: If you buy a book for ₹200, its CP is ₹200.

Selling Price (SP)

  • Definition: The price at which an article is sold to a customer.
  • Intuition: It's the revenue generated from the sale.
  • Key Observation: Determines if there's a profit or loss when compared with CP.
  • Memory Tip: SP is what you Sell it for.
  • Mini Example: If you sell the book for ₹250, its SP is ₹250.

Profit (Gain)

  • Definition: The financial gain when the Selling Price (SP) is greater than the Cost Price (CP).
  • Intuition: You earned more than you spent.
  • Key Observation: Occurs when \( \text{SP} > \text{CP} \).
  • Memory Tip: Profit is Positive outcome.
  • Mini Example: Book CP = ₹200, SP = ₹250. Profit = ₹250 - ₹200 = ₹50.

Loss

  • Definition: The financial deficit incurred when the Cost Price (CP) is greater than the Selling Price (SP).
  • Intuition: You spent more than you earned.
  • Key Observation: Occurs when \( \text{CP} > \text{SP} \).
  • Memory Tip: Loss is Lower outcome.
  • Mini Example: Book CP = ₹200, SP = ₹180. Loss = ₹200 - ₹180 = ₹20.

Profit Percentage (Profit %)

  • Definition: The profit expressed as a percentage of the Cost Price.
  • Intuition: How much gain per ₹100 of investment.
  • Key Observation: Always calculated on CP, unless explicitly stated otherwise.
  • Memory Tip: Profit % is Profit relative to Cost.
  • Mini Example: Profit = ₹50, CP = ₹200. Profit % = \( \frac{50}{200} \times 100\% = 25\% \).

Loss Percentage (Loss %)

  • Definition: The loss expressed as a percentage of the Cost Price.
  • Intuition: How much deficit per ₹100 of investment.
  • Key Observation: Always calculated on CP, unless explicitly stated otherwise.
  • Memory Tip: Loss % is Loss relative to Cost.
  • Mini Example: Loss = ₹20, CP = ₹200. Loss % = \( \frac{20}{200} \times 100\% = 10\% \).
Key Comparisons

📏 Rules & Properties

Rule 1: Profit/Loss Percentage is Always Calculated on Cost Price (CP)

  • Explanation: Unless explicitly mentioned, the base for calculating profit or loss percentage is always the Cost Price (CP).
  • Reason: CP represents the actual investment. Percentage gain/loss indicates the return on that investment.
  • Shortcut: Think of CP as 100%. If 20% Profit, SP is 120% of CP. If 10% Loss, SP is 90% of CP.
  • Exception: Very rarely, a question might ask for P/L % "on SP". In such cases, the calculation base changes.
  • Example: An item bought for ₹400 and sold for ₹500. Profit = ₹100. Profit % = \( \frac{100}{400} \times 100\% = 25\% \). (Not \( \frac{100}{500} \times 100\% = 20\% \)).

Rule 2: Direct Relation between CP, SP, and P/L % (Multiplier Method)

  • Explanation: SP can be directly calculated from CP and P/L % using a multiplier, and vice-versa.
  • Reason: Profit increases CP, Loss decreases CP. The percentage acts as a scaling factor.
  • Shortcut:
    • If Profit % = P%, then \( \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \left( \frac{100 + P}{100} \right) \)
    • If Loss % = L%, then \( \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \left( \frac{100 - L}{100} \right) \)
    • Similarly for CP: \( \text{CP} = \text{SP} \times \left( \frac{100}{100 + P} \right) \) and \( \text{CP} = \text{SP} \times \left( \frac{100}{100 - L} \right) \)
  • Example: CP = ₹500, Profit % = 20%. SP = \( 500 \times \left( \frac{100+20}{100} \right) = 500 \times \frac{120}{100} = ₹600 \).

Rule 3: Understanding Profit/Loss as Fractions of CP

  • Explanation: Converting percentage P/L to fractions helps in quick ratio-based calculations.
  • Reason: Simplifies complex multiplications, especially with non-integer percentages.
  • Shortcut:
    • If Profit % = \( \frac{a}{b} \), then Profit = \( \frac{a}{b} \times \text{CP} \), and \( \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \left(1 + \frac{a}{b}\right) \).
    • If Loss % = \( \frac{a}{b} \), then Loss = \( \frac{a}{b} \times \text{CP} \), and \( \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \left(1 - \frac{a}{b}\right) \).
  • Example: Profit = \( 16\frac{2}{3}\% = \frac{1}{6} \). If CP = ₹600, Profit = \( \frac{1}{6} \times 600 = ₹100 \). SP = ₹700.

📐 Formula Box

1. Profit and Loss

$$ \text{Profit (P)} = \text{SP} - \text{CP} \quad (\text{if SP > CP}) $$

$$ \text{Loss (L)} = \text{CP} - \text{SP} \quad (\text{if CP > SP}) $$

  • Meaning: Direct monetary gain or deficit.
  • Variables: SP (Selling Price), CP (Cost Price).
  • When to use: To find the absolute profit or loss amount.
  • Memory Trick: Profit = Sale - Cost. Loss = Cost - Sale. Always bigger minus smaller.

2. Profit Percentage

$$ \text{Profit %} = \left( \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{CP}} \right) \times 100 $$

$$ \text{Profit %} = \left( \frac{\text{SP} - \text{CP}}{\text{CP}} \right) \times 100 $$

  • Meaning: Profit expressed as a percentage of the Cost Price.
  • Variables: Profit (P), CP (Cost Price), SP (Selling Price).
  • When to use: To quantify profit relative to the investment.
  • Memory Trick: Profit over Cost, then percent. 'P' always goes with 'CP' in denominator.

3. Loss Percentage

$$ \text{Loss %} = \left( \frac{\text{Loss}}{\text{CP}} \right) \times 100 $$

$$ \text{Loss %} = \left( \frac{\text{CP} - \text{SP}}{\text{CP}} \right) \times 100 $$

  • Meaning: Loss expressed as a percentage of the Cost Price.
  • Variables: Loss (L), CP (Cost Price), SP (Selling Price).
  • When to use: To quantify loss relative to the investment.
  • Memory Trick: Loss over Cost, then percent. 'L' always goes with 'CP' in denominator.

4. Selling Price (SP) from CP and P/L %

$$ \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \left( \frac{100 + \text{Profit %}}{100} \right) \quad (\text{for Profit}) $$

$$ \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \left( \frac{100 - \text{Loss %}}{100} \right) \quad (\text{for Loss}) $$

  • Meaning: Directly calculate the selling price when cost price and percentage profit/loss are known.
  • Variables: SP, CP, Profit %, Loss %.
  • When to use: Common scenario in many problems.
  • Memory Trick: (100 + P)/100 for profit, (100 - L)/100 for loss. SP is 'up' from CP with profit, 'down' with loss.

5. Cost Price (CP) from SP and P/L %

$$ \text{CP} = \text{SP} \times \left( \frac{100}{100 + \text{Profit %}} \right) \quad (\text{for Profit}) $$

$$ \text{CP} = \text{SP} \times \left( \frac{100}{100 - \text{Loss %}} \right) \quad (\text{for Loss}) $$

  • Meaning: Directly calculate the cost price when selling price and percentage profit/loss are known.
  • Variables: CP, SP, Profit %, Loss %.
  • When to use: To find original cost when final selling price and percentage result are given.
  • Memory Trick: Inverse of SP formula. If you want CP, divide by the percentage factor.

🧠 Solved Examples

Easy

Example 1: Basic Profit/Loss Calculation

A shopkeeper buys an article for ₹800 and sells it for ₹960. Find the profit and profit percentage.

View Step-by-Step Solution

Given:

  • Cost Price (CP) = ₹800
  • Selling Price (SP) = ₹960

Step 1: Calculate Profit.

Since SP > CP, there is a profit.

Profit = SP - CP = ₹960 - ₹800 = ₹160

Step 2: Calculate Profit Percentage.

Profit % = \( \left( \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{CP}} \right) \times 100 \)

Profit % = \( \left( \frac{160}{800} \right) \times 100 = \left( \frac{1}{5} \right) \times 100 = 20\% \)

Answer: The profit is ₹160, and the profit percentage is 20%.

Medium

Example 2: Finding SP with given CP and Loss %

An article is bought for ₹1200. If it is sold at a loss of 15%, find its selling price.

View Step-by-Step Solution

Given:

  • Cost Price (CP) = ₹1200
  • Loss Percentage (Loss %) = 15%

Step 1: Use the SP formula for Loss.

SP = \( \text{CP} \times \left( \frac{100 - \text{Loss %}}{100} \right) \)

SP = \( 1200 \times \left( \frac{100 - 15}{100} \right) \)

SP = \( 1200 \times \left( \frac{85}{100} \right) \)

Step 2: Calculate the Selling Price.

SP = \( 12 \times 85 = ₹1020 \)

Alternative Method (Calculate Loss amount first):

Loss amount = 15% of CP = \( \frac{15}{100} \times 1200 = 15 \times 12 = ₹180 \)

SP = CP - Loss = ₹1200 - ₹180 = ₹1020

Answer: The selling price of the article is ₹1020.

Hard

Example 3: Finding CP when SP and Profit % are known

By selling an article for ₹1440, a shopkeeper gains 20%. For how much should he sell it to gain 25%?

View Step-by-Step Solution

Given:

  • Selling Price (SP1) = ₹1440
  • Profit Percentage (P1 %) = 20%
  • Desired Profit Percentage (P2 %) = 25%

Step 1: Calculate the Cost Price (CP) from the first transaction.

Using the formula: CP = \( \text{SP} \times \left( \frac{100}{100 + \text{Profit %}} \right) \)

CP = \( 1440 \times \left( \frac{100}{100 + 20} \right) \)

CP = \( 1440 \times \left( \frac{100}{120} \right) \)

CP = \( 1440 \times \frac{5}{6} \)

CP = \( 240 \times 5 = ₹1200 \)

Step 2: Calculate the new Selling Price (SP2) for 25% profit.

Now, CP = ₹1200 and desired Profit % = 25%.

Using the formula: SP2 = \( \text{CP} \times \left( \frac{100 + \text{Profit %}}{100} \right) \)

SP2 = \( 1200 \times \left( \frac{100 + 25}{100} \right) \)

SP2 = \( 1200 \times \left( \frac{125}{100} \right) \)

SP2 = \( 12 \times 125 = ₹1500 \)

Answer: The shopkeeper should sell the article for ₹1500 to gain 25%.

Exam Level

Example 4: SSC CGL Type - Relative Profit/Loss

A man sells an article at 20% profit. If he had bought it for 10% less and sold it for ₹18 less, he would have gained 30%. Find the original Cost Price of the article.

View Step-by-Step Solution

Let's assume:

  • Original Cost Price (CP1) = \(x\)

Step 1: Calculate Original Selling Price (SP1).

Profit = 20%

SP1 = CP1 \( \times \left( \frac{100+20}{100} \right) = x \times \frac{120}{100} = 1.2x \)

Step 2: Calculate New Cost Price (CP2).

Bought for 10% less than original CP.

CP2 = CP1 \( \times \left( \frac{100-10}{100} \right) = x \times \frac{90}{100} = 0.9x \)

Step 3: Calculate New Selling Price (SP2) with 30% gain on CP2.

New Profit = 30% (on CP2)

SP2 = CP2 \( \times \left( \frac{100+30}{100} \right) = 0.9x \times \frac{130}{100} = 0.9x \times 1.3 = 1.17x \)

Step 4: Formulate equation using the given difference in SP.

He sold it for ₹18 less, meaning SP1 - SP2 = ₹18.

\( 1.2x - 1.17x = 18 \)

\( 0.03x = 18 \)

Step 5: Solve for \(x\) (Original CP).

\( x = \frac{18}{0.03} = \frac{18 \times 100}{3} = 6 \times 100 = 600 \)

Answer: The original Cost Price of the article is ₹600.

⚡ Exam Tricks & Shortcuts

1. Fraction Method for Percentages

  • Concept: Convert profit/loss percentages into their simplest fraction forms. This makes calculations faster, especially with common percentages like \(10\% = \frac{1}{10}\), \(20\% = \frac{1}{5}\), \(25\% = \frac{1}{4}\), \(16\frac{2}{3}\% = \frac{1}{6}\), \(14\frac{2}{7}\% = \frac{1}{7}\).
  • Application:
    • If Profit is \( \frac{1}{N} \), then CP = N parts, Profit = 1 part, SP = (N+1) parts.
    • If Loss is \( \frac{1}{N} \), then CP = N parts, Loss = 1 part, SP = (N-1) parts.
  • Example: An article sold at \(16\frac{2}{3}\%\) profit for ₹700. Find CP.
    • \(16\frac{2}{3}\% = \frac{1}{6}\). So, if CP = 6 units, Profit = 1 unit.
    • SP = 6 + 1 = 7 units.
    • Given SP = ₹700, so 7 units = ₹700.
    • 1 unit = ₹100.
    • CP = 6 units = \(6 \times 100 = ₹600\).

2. Ratio Method (CP : SP)

  • Concept: Directly establish a ratio between CP and SP based on profit/loss percentage.
  • Application:
    • If P% profit, \( \text{CP} : \text{SP} = 100 : (100 + P) \).
    • If L% loss, \( \text{CP} : \text{SP} = 100 : (100 - L) \).
  • Example: A shopkeeper sells an article for ₹1200, incurring a loss of 20%. Find CP.
    • Loss 20%, so \( \text{CP} : \text{SP} = 100 : (100-20) = 100 : 80 = 5 : 4 \).
    • 4 units (SP) = ₹1200.
    • 1 unit = ₹300.
    • 5 units (CP) = \(5 \times 300 = ₹1500\).

3. Equating SPs or CPs (Multi-condition Problems)

  • Concept: In problems with multiple scenarios (e.g., finding new SP for a different profit%), express CP in terms of SP or vice-versa for different conditions and equate them.
  • Application: Example 3 (Hard) demonstrates this. Instead of finding CP, then new SP:
    • Original SP = ₹1440 (20% profit). So, \( 1440 = \text{CP} \times \frac{120}{100} \).
    • New SP (let it be \(x\)) for 25% profit. So, \( x = \text{CP} \times \frac{125}{100} \).
    • Divide the two equations: \( \frac{1440}{x} = \frac{120/100}{125/100} = \frac{120}{125} = \frac{24}{25} \).
    • \( x = 1440 \times \frac{25}{24} = 60 \times 25 = ₹1500 \).

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Pitfalls

  • Calculating P/L % on SP instead of CP: Always remember that profit/loss percentage is fundamentally calculated on the Cost Price (CP), unless the question explicitly states "on selling price". This is the most frequent error.
  • Mixing up Profit and Loss Formulas: Ensure you use \( \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times (100 + P\%)/100 \) for profit and \( \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times (100 - L\%)/100 \) for loss. Don't swap plus and minus signs.
  • Incorrectly Applying Inverse Formulas: When finding CP from SP, remember the 100 goes in the numerator: \( \text{CP} = \text{SP} \times 100/(100 \pm \text{P/L %}) \).
  • Calculation Errors: Be careful with decimals and fractions when converting percentages or performing multiplications, especially under timed pressure.
  • Not Reading the Question Carefully: Sometimes questions introduce additional costs (overhead expenses) which should be added to the initial purchase price to get the effective CP.

📌 Summary

  • Cost Price (CP): Price at which an article is bought.
  • Selling Price (SP): Price at which an article is sold.
  • Profit: \( \text{SP} - \text{CP} \) (when \( \text{SP} > \text{CP} \)).
  • Loss: \( \text{CP} - \text{SP} \) (when \( \text{CP} > \text{SP} \)).
  • Profit/Loss %: Always calculated on CP.
  • \( \text{Profit %} = \left( \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{CP}} \right) \times 100 \)
  • \( \text{Loss %} = \left( \frac{\text{Loss}}{\text{CP}} \right) \times 100 \)
  • SP from CP: \( \text{CP} \times \frac{100 \pm \text{P/L %}}{100} \)
  • CP from SP: \( \text{SP} \times \frac{100}{100 \pm \text{P/L %}} \)
  • Key Shortcuts: Fraction method, Ratio method for quicker calculations.

Quick Revision Box: Profit, Loss & Percentage

Definitions:

  • CP: Cost Price (Investment)
  • SP: Selling Price (Revenue)

Outcomes:

  • Profit: SP > CP, P = SP - CP
  • Loss: CP > SP, L = CP - SP

Percentages (Always on CP):

  • $$ \text{Profit %} = \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 $$
  • $$ \text{Loss %} = \frac{\text{Loss}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 $$

SP & CP Relations:

  • $$ \text{SP} = \text{CP} \left( \frac{100 + \text{Profit %}}{100} \right) $$
  • $$ \text{SP} = \text{CP} \left( \frac{100 - \text{Loss %}}{100} \right) $$
  • $$ \text{CP} = \text{SP} \left( \frac{100}{100 + \text{Profit %}} \right) $$
  • $$ \text{CP} = \text{SP} \left( \frac{100}{100 - \text{Loss %}} \right) $$

Tricks:

  • Fraction Method: \(16\frac{2}{3}\% = \frac{1}{6}\). CP : SP = 6 : 7 (Profit).
  • Ratio Method: 20% loss \( \implies \) CP : SP = 100 : 80 = 5 : 4.

Mistake to Avoid: Calculating P/L % on SP instead of CP.

🚀 Key Takeaways

  • CP is King: Profit and Loss percentages are almost always referenced to the Cost Price.
  • Formulas are Tools: Understand the logic behind the formulas rather than just memorizing them.
  • Practice is Key: Master common percentage-to-fraction conversions for faster calculations.
  • Read Carefully: Pay close attention to keywords (e.g., "on selling price," "overhead expenses") that might alter standard calculations.
