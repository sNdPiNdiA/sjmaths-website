Differences between SI & CI, and Installment Basics

Medium Difficulty 25-30 Mins Read High Exam Weightage Updated 2026

📘 Introduction

This microtopic focuses on the crucial distinctions between Simple Interest (SI) and Compound Interest (CI), particularly the methods to calculate their differences over various periods. Additionally, we delve into the fundamentals of Installments, a practical application of interest concepts in loan repayments.

  • Exam Relevance: A high-scoring topic, questions on SI/CI differences and installments are common in SSC CGL, CHSL, Banking PO/Clerk, Railway, and State PCS exams.
  • Objective: Master these concepts to solve problems efficiently and accurately, securing easy marks.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand the fundamental conceptual differences between SI and CI.
  • Efficiently calculate the difference between SI and CI for 2 and 3 years.
  • Grasp the basics of loan installments and their types (SI vs. CI).
  • Apply specific formulas and methods to solve SI and CI installment problems.
  • Develop strategies to tackle complex exam questions related to these concepts.

📝 Prerequisites

  • Basic Percentage: Understanding percentage calculation and conversion.
  • Ratio & Proportion: Ability to work with ratios for distribution and comparison.
  • Simple Interest (SI): Knowledge of \( SI = \frac{P \cdot R \cdot T}{100} \) formula.
  • Compound Interest (CI): Knowledge of \( A = P\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^T \) and \( CI = A - P \) formulas.

💡 Core Concepts

Simple Interest (SI) vs. Compound Interest (CI) - Key Differences

Understanding the core difference is vital for effective problem-solving.

Feature Simple Interest (SI) Compound Interest (CI)
Definition Interest calculated only on the original principal amount for the entire period. Interest calculated on the principal amount and also on the accumulated interest of previous periods.
Basis of Interest Only on the initial Principal (P). On Principal + accumulated Interest (effectively, the Amount of the previous period).
Growth Linear growth: Interest earned per period remains constant. Exponential growth: Interest earned per period increases, leading to faster growth.
Practical Use Short-term loans, basic calculations, debentures, bonds. Long-term loans, savings accounts, investments, credit cards.
Formulas \( SI = \frac{PRT}{100} \) \( A = P\left(1+\frac{R}{100}\right)^T \), \( CI = A - P \)

Intuition: SI is "interest on principal", CI is "interest on interest".

Memory Tip: "Simple" is simple calculation; "Compound" means it compounds (adds up).

Mini Example: P=1000, R=10%, T=2 years.
SI: Year 1 = 100, Year 2 = 100. Total SI = 200.
CI: Year 1 = 100. New P for Year 2 = 1100. Year 2 CI = 110. Total CI = 210.

Installment Basics

Installment refers to periodic payments made to repay a loan or clear a debt over a specified period. Each payment includes a portion of the principal amount and the interest accrued.

  • Intuition: Instead of paying a lump sum, you break down the debt into manageable, regular payments.
  • Key Observation: The total amount paid in installments is always greater than the original principal due to the interest component.
  • Types: Installments can be calculated using either Simple Interest or Compound Interest principles.
  • Mini Example: A TV bought for ₹10,000 on installments might require 5 monthly payments of ₹2,200, totaling ₹11,000 (₹1,000 being the interest).

📏 Rules & Properties

Rule 1: CI - SI Difference for 2 Years

  • Explanation: The difference between CI and SI for 2 years at a given principal (P) and rate (R) is due to interest being charged on the first year's simple interest in the second year.
  • Reason: For the first year, SI = CI. For the second year, CI also calculates interest on the interest earned in the first year, which SI doesn't.
  • Shortcut: Difference \( = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 \).
  • Example: If P=₹5000, R=10% p.a., for 2 years. SI = \( \frac{5000 \cdot 10 \cdot 2}{100} = 1000 \). CI \( = 5000\left(1 + \frac{10}{100}\right)^2 - 5000 = 5000(1.1)^2 - 5000 = 5000(1.21) - 5000 = 6050 - 5000 = 1050 \). Difference \( = 1050 - 1000 = 50 \). Using shortcut: Difference \( = 5000 \left(\frac{10}{100}\right)^2 = 5000 \left(\frac{1}{10}\right)^2 = 5000 \cdot \frac{1}{100} = 50 \).

Rule 2: CI - SI Difference for 3 Years

  • Explanation: The difference for 3 years is more complex as it accounts for interest on interest for multiple periods.
  • Reason: For 3 years, CI charges interest on the first year's interest for 2 years, and on the second year's interest for 1 year, beyond what SI would.
  • Shortcut: Difference \( = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 \left(3 + \frac{R}{100}\right) \).
  • Example: If P=₹10000, R=10% p.a., for 3 years. SI = \( \frac{10000 \cdot 10 \cdot 3}{100} = 3000 \). CI \( = 10000\left(1 + \frac{10}{100}\right)^3 - 10000 = 10000(1.1)^3 - 10000 = 10000(1.331) - 10000 = 13310 - 10000 = 3310 \). Difference \( = 3310 - 3000 = 310 \). Using shortcut: Difference \( = 10000 \left(\frac{10}{100}\right)^2 \left(3 + \frac{10}{100}\right) = 10000 \left(\frac{1}{10}\right)^2 \left(3 + \frac{1}{10}\right) \) \( = 10000 \cdot \frac{1}{100} \cdot \left(\frac{31}{10}\right) = 100 \cdot \frac{31}{10} = 10 \cdot 31 = 310 \).

Rule 3: Simple Interest Installments

  • Explanation: In SI installments, each installment pays off a portion of the principal plus simple interest on that remaining principal (or principal components over time). Often, problems are framed as "equal annual installments" for a loan amount that becomes due after 'T' years.
  • Reason: The total amount due after 'T' years (Principal + SI) is covered by the sum of the future values of each installment.
  • Shortcut (for amount X paid annually to clear a debt D due after T years at R% SI): Let the amount of each installment be \( x \). The amount of debt \( D \) (Principal + Interest) paid off in \( T \) years is cleared by \( T \) installments of \( x \). Total Amount Paid = \( x + \left(x + \frac{x \cdot R \cdot 1}{100}\right) + \left(x + \frac{x \cdot R \cdot 2}{100}\right) + \dots + \left(x + \frac{x \cdot R \cdot (T-1)}{100}\right) \) is NOT correct for most competitive exams. Correct approach (Future Value perspective): If an amount D is due after T years, and it's paid in N equal annual installments of \( x \), then \( D = x \left(1 + \frac{R(T-1)}{100}\right) + x \left(1 + \frac{R(T-2)}{100}\right) + \dots + x \left(1 + \frac{R(0)}{100}\right) \). More commonly: Debt (Amount due at end) \( = n \times x + \frac{xR}{100} \left[ (n-1) + (n-2) + \dots + 1 + 0 \right] \) where \( n \) is number of installments and \( x \) is value of each installment. This simplifies to: Debt \( = nx + \frac{xR}{100} \cdot \frac{n(n-1)}{2} \). (Often, \( n \) is the number of installments, and the interest on the installment starts counting from the moment it is paid until the final due date, or the problem asks for the installment amount to clear a present loan). Alternative (more common for "debt paid in installments"): The present value of the debt is P. Each installment `x` paid annually for `n` years. Amount due = \( P + \frac{P \cdot R \cdot T}{100} \). If \( x \) is the annual payment, then the value of the \( 1^{st} \) installment at the end of \( T \) years is \( x\left(1 + \frac{R(T-1)}{100}\right) \). The value of the \( 2^{nd} \) installment at the end of \( T \) years is \( x\left(1 + \frac{R(T-2)}{100}\right) \), and so on. The last installment (paid at the end of T years) is simply \( x \). Sum of these future values equals the total amount due. So, Amount Due \( = x \left[ \left(1 + \frac{R(T-1)}{100}\right) + \left(1 + \frac{R(T-2)}{100}\right) + \dots + \left(1 + \frac{R \cdot 0}{100}\right) \right] \) \( = x \left[ T + \frac{R}{100} ( (T-1) + (T-2) + \dots + 0 ) \right] \) \( = x \left[ T + \frac{R}{100} \frac{T(T-1)}{2} \right] \)
  • Exception: Read the question carefully if installments are paid at the beginning or end of the period. The above assumes end-of-period payment or amount due calculation.
  • Example: A sum of ₹1100 is borrowed at 10% SI, to be paid back in 2 equal annual installments. What is each installment? Here, P = 1100, R = 10%, T = 2 years. Let each installment be \( x \). Amount due at end of 2 years (if not paid) \( = 1100 + \frac{1100 \cdot 10 \cdot 2}{100} = 1100 + 220 = 1320 \). Using the formula: Amount Due \( = x \left[ T + \frac{R}{100} \frac{T(T-1)}{2} \right] \) \( 1320 = x \left[ 2 + \frac{10}{100} \frac{2(2-1)}{2} \right] \) \( 1320 = x \left[ 2 + \frac{10}{100} \cdot 1 \right] = x \left[ 2 + 0.1 \right] = 2.1x \) \( x = \frac{1320}{2.1} = \frac{13200}{21} = \frac{4400}{7} \approx 628.57 \) (This formula assumes the loan amount is repaid as its future value) More common for exam questions: "What equal annual installment will discharge a debt of ₹X due in T years at R% per annum SI?" Debt due after T years = \( D = \text{Principal} + \text{SI} = P\left(1 + \frac{RT}{100}\right) \). Each installment, say \( x \), is paid. The first installment is paid after 1 year, its value at time T is \( x\left(1 + \frac{R(T-1)}{100}\right) \). The second installment is paid after 2 years, its value at time T is \( x\left(1 + \frac{R(T-2)}{100}\right) \). ... The last installment is paid after T years, its value at time T is \( x\left(1 + \frac{R(0)}{100}\right) = x \). Sum of future values of installments = Total Debt Due. Example from before (a debt of 1100 is paid off by 2 installments): This is generally P = 1100. Let's assume the question means a loan of 1100. A loan of ₹P is discharged in T annual installments of ₹x. In this case, the sum of present values of all installments should be equal to the loan amount P. \( P = \frac{x}{1 + \frac{R \cdot 1}{100}} + \frac{x}{1 + \frac{R \cdot 2}{100}} + \dots \) No, this is not standard. Most reliable exam method for SI Installments: Let each installment be ₹100. For T years, R% SI. Installment 1: \( 100 + 100 \cdot R \cdot (T-1)/100 \) Installment 2: \( 100 + 100 \cdot R \cdot (T-2)/100 \) ... Installment T: \( 100 + 100 \cdot R \cdot (0)/100 = 100 \) Sum these amounts. Let this sum be S. If actual Amount Due is A, then \( \text{Actual Installment} = \frac{A}{S} \times 100 \). Example: A debt of ₹1100 is to be cleared in 2 equal annual installments at 10% SI. Assume each installment is ₹100. First installment (paid after 1 year): Its value after 2 years would be \( 100(1 + \frac{10 \cdot (2-1)}{100}) = 100(1.1) = 110 \). Second installment (paid after 2 years): Its value after 2 years would be \( 100(1 + \frac{10 \cdot 0}{100}) = 100 \). Total "Assumed Amount Discharged" \( = 110 + 100 = 210 \). If 210 is discharged by 100, then 1100 will be discharged by \( \frac{1100}{210} \times 100 = \frac{11000}{21} \approx 523.81 \). This makes more sense.

Rule 4: Compound Interest Installments

  • Explanation: In CI installments, each payment reduces the principal, and the interest for the next period is calculated on the reduced principal. It's essentially summing the present values of each installment.
  • Reason: The present value of all future installments must equal the current loan amount.
  • Shortcut (for equal annual installments 'x' to repay a loan 'P' at R% CI for 'T' years): \( P = \frac{x}{\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^1} + \frac{x}{\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^2} + \dots + \frac{x}{\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^T} \) This can be simplified for 2 or 3 installments. For 2 installments: \( P = x \left[ \frac{1}{1+r} + \frac{1}{(1+r)^2} \right] \), where \( r = \frac{R}{100} \). For 3 installments: \( P = x \left[ \frac{1}{1+r} + \frac{1}{(1+r)^2} + \frac{1}{(1+r)^3} \right] \).
  • Example: A loan of ₹12,300 is to be paid back in two equal annual installments at 10% p.a. CI. Find each installment. Here, P = 12300, R = 10%, T = 2 years. Let each installment be \( x \). \( 12300 = \frac{x}{\left(1 + \frac{10}{100}\right)^1} + \frac{x}{\left(1 + \frac{10}{100}\right)^2} \) \( 12300 = \frac{x}{1.1} + \frac{x}{(1.1)^2} = x \left( \frac{1}{1.1} + \frac{1}{1.21} \right) \) \( 12300 = x \left( \frac{1.1 + 1}{1.21} \right) = x \left( \frac{2.1}{1.21} \right) \) \( x = 12300 \times \frac{1.21}{2.1} = 12300 \times \frac{121}{210} = 60 \times 121 = 7260 \). Each installment is ₹7260.

📐 Formula Box

Difference between CI and SI for 2 Years

$$ \text{CI} - \text{SI} = P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 $$
  • Meaning: The extra interest earned by compounding interest on the first year's SI.
  • Variables: \( P \) = Principal, \( R \) = Rate of interest per annum.
  • When to use: When finding the difference between CI and SI for exactly 2 years.
  • When NOT to use: For periods other than 2 years, or if interest is compounded half-yearly/quarterly (adjust R and T accordingly).
  • Memory Trick: For 2 years, it's \( P \) times \( (R/100)^2 \), like "square" of the rate factor.

Difference between CI and SI for 3 Years

$$ \text{CI} - \text{SI} = P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2\left(3 + \frac{R}{100}\right) $$
  • Meaning: The total extra interest earned due to compounding over three years.
  • Variables: \( P \) = Principal, \( R \) = Rate of interest per annum.
  • When to use: When finding the difference between CI and SI for exactly 3 years.
  • When NOT to use: For periods other than 3 years, or if compounding frequency is different.
  • Memory Trick: Similar to 2 years, but with an added \( (3 + R/100) \) factor, remember the "3" for three years.

Simple Interest Installment (to discharge a debt 'D' after 'T' years at R% SI by 'T' equal annual installments 'x')

$$ \text{Amount Due (D)} = x \left[ T + \frac{R}{100} \frac{T(T-1)}{2} \right] $$
  • Meaning: The sum of the future values of each installment equals the total amount due (Principal + SI on Principal) at the end of the term. This is the most common interpretation in exams.
  • Variables: \( D \) = Total Debt Amount due at the end of T years (Principal + SI on Principal), \( x \) = Value of each equal annual installment, \( R \) = Rate of interest per annum, \( T \) = Total number of years (which is also the number of installments).
  • When to use: When asked to find the equal annual installment to discharge a debt due after 'T' years at SI.
  • Memory Trick: \( x \times T \) (total cash paid) plus interest on the "unpaid" principal portions as installments are paid over time. \( \frac{T(T-1)}{2} \) is sum of arithmetic progression \( (T-1) + \dots + 1 + 0 \).

Compound Interest Installment (Loan 'P' repaid by 'T' equal annual installments 'x' at R% CI)

$$ P = \frac{x}{\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^1} + \frac{x}{\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^2} + \dots + \frac{x}{\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^T} $$
  • Meaning: The present value of all future installments equals the original loan amount (P).
  • Variables: \( P \) = Original Loan Amount (Present Value), \( x \) = Value of each equal annual installment, \( R \) = Rate of interest per annum, \( T \) = Total number of years (which is also the number of installments).
  • When to use: When asked to find the equal annual installment to repay a loan amount at CI.
  • Memory Trick: Discount each installment back to the present value and sum them up to equal the loan taken. Think of it as "reverse compounding".

🧠 Solved Examples

Easy

Example 1: Basic CI-SI Difference (2 Years)

Find the difference between CI and SI on ₹8000 for 2 years at 5% p.a.

View Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Identify Given Values: Principal (P) = ₹8000, Rate (R) = 5%, Time (T) = 2 years.
  2. Apply Formula for 2-Year Difference: \( \text{CI} - \text{SI} = P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 \)
  3. Substitute and Calculate: \( \text{CI} - \text{SI} = 8000 \left(\frac{5}{100}\right)^2 \) \( = 8000 \left(\frac{1}{20}\right)^2 \) \( = 8000 \times \frac{1}{400} \) \( = \frac{8000}{400} = 20 \)
  4. Result: The difference between CI and SI is ₹20.
Medium

Example 2: Basic CI-SI Difference (3 Years)

What is the difference between CI and SI on ₹10,000 for 3 years at 4% p.a.?

View Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Identify Given Values: Principal (P) = ₹10,000, Rate (R) = 4%, Time (T) = 3 years.
  2. Apply Formula for 3-Year Difference: \( \text{CI} - \text{SI} = P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2\left(3 + \frac{R}{100}\right) \)
  3. Substitute and Calculate: \( \text{CI} - \text{SI} = 10000 \left(\frac{4}{100}\right)^2\left(3 + \frac{4}{100}\right) \) \( = 10000 \left(\frac{1}{25}\right)^2\left(3 + \frac{1}{25}\right) \) \( = 10000 \times \frac{1}{625} \times \left(\frac{75 + 1}{25}\right) \) \( = 10000 \times \frac{1}{625} \times \frac{76}{25} \) \( = \frac{10000}{625} \times \frac{76}{25} \) \( = 16 \times \frac{76}{25} = \frac{1216}{25} = 48.64 \)
  4. Result: The difference between CI and SI is ₹48.64.
Exam Level

Example 3: Simple Interest Installment

A sum of ₹1560 is to be discharged by two equal annual installments at 10% p.a. simple interest. What is the value of each installment?

View Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Identify Given Values: Principal (P) = ₹1560, Rate (R) = 10%, Number of installments (T) = 2.
  2. Calculate Total Amount Due: First, calculate the total amount (P + SI on P) that would be due at the end of 2 years if no installments were paid. SI on P for 2 years = \( \frac{1560 \times 10 \times 2}{100} = 312 \). Total Amount Due (D) = \( 1560 + 312 = 1872 \).
  3. Apply SI Installment Logic (Unitary Method): Assume each installment is ₹100. The first installment is paid after 1 year. Its value at the end of 2 years (final payment date) would be \( 100 \left(1 + \frac{10 \times (2-1)}{100}\right) = 100(1.1) = 110 \). The second installment is paid after 2 years. Its value at the end of 2 years is \( 100 \left(1 + \frac{10 \times 0}{100}\right) = 100 \). Total "Assumed Amount Discharged" by two ₹100 installments = \( 110 + 100 = 210 \).
  4. Calculate Actual Installment: If ₹210 is discharged by an installment of ₹100, then ₹1872 will be discharged by an installment of \( \frac{1872}{210} \times 100 \). \( x = \frac{18720}{21} = \frac{6240}{7} = 891.428... \) (If the options are exact, look for exact fractions) Let's recheck with the formula: \( D = x \left[ T + \frac{R}{100} \frac{T(T-1)}{2} \right] \) \( 1872 = x \left[ 2 + \frac{10}{100} \frac{2(2-1)}{2} \right] \) \( 1872 = x \left[ 2 + \frac{1}{10} \cdot 1 \right] = x \left[ 2 + 0.1 \right] = 2.1x \) \( x = \frac{1872}{2.1} = \frac{18720}{21} = \frac{6240}{7} \approx 891.43 \). *Self-correction: Exam questions usually give values that yield integer installments. The logic is sound.*
  5. Result: Each installment is approximately ₹891.43.
Exam Level

Example 4: Compound Interest Installment

A loan of ₹2100 is to be repaid in two equal annual installments. If the rate of interest is 10% p.a. compounded annually, what is the amount of each installment?

View Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Identify Given Values: Loan Amount (P) = ₹2100, Rate (R) = 10%, Number of installments (T) = 2.
  2. Apply CI Installment Formula: \( P = \frac{x}{\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^1} + \frac{x}{\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^2} \) Let \( r = \frac{R}{100} = \frac{10}{100} = 0.1 \). So \( (1+r) = 1.1 \). \( 2100 = \frac{x}{1.1} + \frac{x}{(1.1)^2} \)
  3. Simplify and Solve for x: \( 2100 = x \left( \frac{1}{1.1} + \frac{1}{1.21} \right) \) \( 2100 = x \left( \frac{1.1 + 1}{1.21} \right) \) \( 2100 = x \left( \frac{2.1}{1.21} \right) \) \( x = 2100 \times \frac{1.21}{2.1} \) \( x = 2100 \times \frac{121}{210} \) (multiplying numerator and denominator by 100) \( x = 10 \times 121 \) \( x = 1210 \)
  4. Result: Each equal annual installment is ₹1210.

⚡ Exam Tricks & Shortcuts

Trick 1: Tree Method for CI-SI Difference (Visualizing Interest)

  • How it works: A visual representation of how interest is calculated in CI. For 2 years: 
    P
|--- R% of P (SI for 1st year)
|
|--- R% of P (SI for 2nd year)
     |--- R% of (R% of P) (Interest on 1st year's interest for 2nd year)
    CI - SI for 2 years is the bottom-most branch: \( R\% \text{ of } (R\% \text{ of } P) \). For 3 years: 
    P
|--- R% of P (A1)
|    |--- R% of A1 (B1)
|    |    |--- R% of B1 (C1)
|
|--- R% of P (A2)
|    |--- R% of A2 (B2)
|
|--- R% of P (A3)
    SI = A1+A2+A3 = 3 * (R% of P) CI = SI + 3 * B1 + C1 (where B1 is R% of 1st year's SI, and C1 is R% of B1) CI - SI = 3 * (R% of (R% of P)) + (R% of (R% of (R% of P))) \( = 3 \cdot P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 + P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^3 \) \( = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 \left(3 + \frac{R}{100}\right) \) (Matches formula!)
  • Benefit: Helps understand the underlying mechanism, useful for irregular rates or periods.
  • Example: P=10000, R=10%, T=3 years. 1st year SI: 1000. 2nd year SI: 1000. Interest on 1st year's interest: 10% of 1000 = 100. 3rd year SI: 1000. Interest on 1st year's interest: 10% of 1000 = 100. Interest on 2nd year's interest: 10% of (1000+100) = 110. No, simpler: Interest on 1st year's 100 is 10. Interest on 2nd year's 1000 is 100. Correct Tree for 3 years: Year 1: 1000 (SI) Year 2: 1000 (SI) + 100 (Interest on Year 1's SI) Year 3: 1000 (SI) + 100 (Interest on Year 1's SI) + 100 (Interest on Year 2's SI) + 10 (Interest on Year 1's interest from Year 2) SI = 1000+1000+1000 = 3000 CI-SI = 100 (from Y2) + 100 (from Y3) + 100 (from Y3) + 10 (from Y3) = 310. (This works!)

Trick 2: Ratio Method for CI-SI (Effective Rate)

  • How it works: Calculate the effective CI rate for T years, and compare it with T times the simple rate. Effective CI for 2 years at R%: \( R + R + \frac{R \cdot R}{100} = \left(2R + \frac{R^2}{100}\right)\% \) Effective SI for 2 years at R%: \( 2R\% \) Difference % \( = \left(2R + \frac{R^2}{100}\right)\% - 2R\% = \frac{R^2}{100}\% \) So, CI-SI = \( P \times \frac{R^2}{100 \times 100} = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 \).
  • Benefit: Good for quick mental calculations for 2 years. Extendable for 3 years (but complex).
  • Example: P=8000, R=5%, T=2 years. Difference % \( = \frac{5^2}{100}\% = \frac{25}{100}\% = 0.25\% \). CI - SI = \( 0.25\% \text{ of } 8000 = \frac{0.25}{100} \times 8000 = 0.25 \times 80 = 20 \).

Trick 3: Options Elimination for Installments

  • How it works: For CI installments, the installment amount (x) will always be greater than (Loan Amount / Number of Installments) but less than (Total Amount Due / Number of Installments). Use this to eliminate unrealistic options.
  • Benefit: Reduces options significantly, especially in MCQ tests.
  • Example: Loan ₹2100, 2 installments. Expected \( x \) would be greater than \( 2100/2 = 1050 \). Options like 900, 1000 can be eliminated. Total amount paid would be \( 2x \), which must be \( > 2100 \).

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Pitfalls

  • Confusing SI and CI Formulas: Applying SI formula where CI is needed, and vice-versa. Always double-check the question's interest type.
  • Incorrect Time Period (T): In installment problems, remember 'T' often refers to the number of installments, or total years for the debt to mature. Ensure consistency.
  • Rate Conversion Errors: Forgetting to divide 'R' by 100 when using formulas, or not adjusting 'R' and 'T' for half-yearly/quarterly compounding.
  • Applying 2-year Difference Formula for 3 Years: These formulas are distinct. Use the correct one.
  • Misinterpreting Installment Questions: Determine if the question asks for the installment amount to clear a *present loan* (CI installment formula) or to discharge a *future debt* (SI installment formula).
  • Calculation Mistakes: Be careful with fractions, decimals, and squaring/cubing numbers. Practice mental math and verification.

CDS/CAPF/State PCS/Police

  • SI vs CI: SI is on original principal, CI is on principal + accumulated interest.
  • CI - SI (2 years): \( P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 \)
  • CI - SI (3 years): \( P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2\left(3 + \frac{R}{100}\right) \)
  • SI Installment: Sum of future values of installments equals total amount due (Principal + SI on Principal).
  • CI Installment: Sum of present values of installments equals the original loan amount.
  • Tree Method: Excellent for visualizing and solving CI-SI difference problems, especially for 3 years.

🚀 Quick Revision: SI-CI Differences & Installments

  • SI: Interest on Principal only. \( SI = \frac{PRT}{100} \)
  • CI: Interest on (Principal + Previous Interest). \( A = P(1+R/100)^T \)
  • Diff (2Y): \( P(R/100)^2 \)
  • Diff (3Y): \( P(R/100)^2(3+R/100) \)
  • SI Installment: \( D = x \left[ T + \frac{R}{100} \frac{T(T-1)}{2} \right] \) (D = Total Amount Due)
  • CI Installment: \( P = \sum_{k=1}^{T} \frac{x}{(1+R/100)^k} \) (P = Loan Amount)
  • Tree Method: Visualize interest accumulation for CI-SI difference.
  • Key: Read questions carefully - SI vs CI, loan repayment vs debt discharge.

✅ Key Takeaways

  • The difference between CI and SI primarily stems from interest on interest in CI.
  • Formulas for 2 and 3-year differences are direct and high-yield. Memorize them.
  • Installments simplify large payments into periodic, manageable amounts.
  • SI and CI installment calculations use different underlying principles (future value vs. present value).
  • Practice is key to differentiating between problem types and applying the correct formula/method quickly.
