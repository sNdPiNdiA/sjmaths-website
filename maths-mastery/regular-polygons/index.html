📘 Introduction to Regular Polygons (Interior & Exterior Angles)

This module provides a comprehensive guide to understanding Regular Polygons, focusing on their interior and exterior angles. Mastering these concepts is fundamental for Geometry questions in various competitive exams.

You'll learn definitions, formulas, and practical application methods to solve problems efficiently and accurately.

  • Exam Relevance: Crucial for Geometry sections in SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, CPO, Railway (NTPC, Group D), Banking (conceptual base), CDS, CAPF, State PCS, and Police exams.
  • Question Types: Direct formula-based, finding number of sides from angles, ratio of angles, or combined with other polygon properties.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand the definition and characteristics of a Regular Polygon.
  • Define and differentiate between Interior and Exterior Angles of a polygon.
  • Master the formulas for calculating the sum of interior angles and each interior angle.
  • Master the formulas for calculating the sum of exterior angles and each exterior angle.
  • Solve exam-level problems involving angles of regular polygons quickly and accurately.

📝 Prerequisites

  • Basic understanding of polygons (sides, vertices).
  • Knowledge of straight lines and angles on a straight line (sum is 180°).
  • Basic algebraic manipulation (solving equations).

💡 Core Concepts

Regular Polygon

Definition: A Regular Polygon is a polygon that is both equiangular (all angles are equal) and equilateral (all sides are equal).

Intuition: Think of shapes like a square or an equilateral triangle – all sides are the same length, and all corners have the same angle. They are perfectly symmetrical.

Key Observations:

  • All sides have equal length.
  • All interior angles are equal.
  • All exterior angles are equal.
  • Can be inscribed in and circumscribed around a circle.

Memory Tip: "Regular" means "perfectly symmetrical" in terms of sides and angles.

Mini Example: A square (n=4) is a regular polygon. An equilateral triangle (n=3) is a regular polygon. A rectangle (unless it's a square) is not a regular polygon because not all sides are equal, though all angles are. A rhombus (unless it's a square) is not regular because not all angles are equal, though all sides are.

Interior Angle

Definition: An Interior Angle of a polygon is an angle formed by two adjacent sides inside the polygon.

Intuition: These are the "inside" angles, where two walls meet within a room.

Key Observations:

  • The sum of interior angles depends on the number of sides.
  • For a regular polygon, all interior angles are equal.
  • Always positive and less than 180°.

Memory Tip: "Interior" = "inside".

Mini Example: In a square, each interior angle is 90°. In an equilateral triangle, each interior angle is 60°.

Exterior Angle

Definition: An Exterior Angle of a polygon is an angle formed by one side of the polygon and the extension of an adjacent side.

Intuition: Imagine walking along the perimeter of the polygon. At each corner, if you turn, the amount you turn is the exterior angle.

Key Observations:

  • The sum of all exterior angles (one at each vertex) of any convex polygon is always 360°.
  • For a regular polygon, all exterior angles are equal.
  • Always positive and less than 180°.

Memory Tip: "Exterior" = "outside", it's the turn you make at a corner.

Mini Example: In a square, each exterior angle is 90°. In an equilateral triangle, each exterior angle is 120°.

Feature Interior Angle (\(I\)) Exterior Angle (\(E\))
Formation Inside the polygon, by two adjacent sides. Outside the polygon, by one side and the extension of an adjacent side.
Relationship Forms a linear pair with the exterior angle at the same vertex. Forms a linear pair with the interior angle at the same vertex.

📏 Rules & Properties

Sum of Interior Angles of a Polygon

Explanation: The total measure of all interior angles of a polygon depends directly on its number of sides.

Reason: Any n-sided polygon can be divided into \((n-2)\) triangles by drawing diagonals from one vertex. Since the sum of angles in a triangle is 180°, the total sum is \((n-2) \times 180^\circ\).

Shortcut: Just remember \((n-2) \times 180\). For \(n=3\), \(1 \times 180 = 180\). For \(n=4\), \(2 \times 180 = 360\).

Exception: None for convex polygons. For non-convex (concave) polygons, the formula still holds, but some interior angles will be reflex angles (>180°).

Example: For a pentagon (n=5), Sum of Interior Angles = \((5-2) \times 180^\circ = 3 \times 180^\circ = 540^\circ\).

Each Interior Angle of a Regular Polygon

Explanation: Since all interior angles of a regular polygon are equal, we can find the measure of a single angle by dividing the total sum by the number of sides.

Reason: Equal angles, equal division of total sum.

Shortcut: Use \(E = 360^\circ/n\), then \(I = 180^\circ - E\). This is often faster for calculations.

Exception: Only applicable to regular polygons.

Example: For a regular hexagon (n=6), Each Interior Angle = \(\frac{(6-2) \times 180^\circ}{6} = \frac{4 \times 180^\circ}{6} = \frac{720^\circ}{6} = 120^\circ\).

Sum of Exterior Angles of a Polygon

Explanation: The total measure of all exterior angles (one at each vertex) of any convex polygon is always constant, irrespective of the number of sides.

Reason: Imagine walking around the perimeter of the polygon. The sum of the turns you make at each vertex eventually brings you back to your starting orientation, completing a full circle (360°).

Shortcut: It's always 360°. No calculation needed for the sum!

Exception: This applies to convex polygons only. For star polygons or self-intersecting polygons, the sum can be different.

Example: For any triangle, quadrilateral, pentagon, or decagon, the sum of their exterior angles is always \(360^\circ\).

Each Exterior Angle of a Regular Polygon

Explanation: For a regular polygon, all exterior angles are equal. Thus, each exterior angle is the sum of exterior angles divided by the number of sides.

Reason: Equal angles, equal division of total sum.

Shortcut: This formula \(360^\circ/n\) is super quick and often used to derive interior angles.

Exception: Only applicable to regular polygons.

Example: For a regular octagon (n=8), Each Exterior Angle = \(\frac{360^\circ}{8} = 45^\circ\).

📐 Formula Box

$$ \text{Sum of Interior Angles} = (n-2) \times 180^\circ $$

Meaning: Total degrees inside any polygon (convex) with 'n' sides.

Variables: \(n\) = Number of sides of the polygon.

When to use: When asked for the total sum of all interior angles. Applicable to both regular and irregular convex polygons.

When NOT to use: To find a single interior angle of an irregular polygon (unless other angles are given). Not for exterior angles.

Memory Trick: "Subtract 2, then multiply by 180." It's like finding how many triangles fit inside.

$$ \text{Each Interior Angle (Regular Polygon)} = \frac{(n-2) \times 180^\circ}{n} $$

Meaning: The measure of one single interior angle in a regular polygon with 'n' sides.

Variables: \(n\) = Number of sides of the regular polygon.

When to use: When asked for the measure of one interior angle of a REGULAR polygon.

When NOT to use: For irregular polygons. For exterior angles.

Memory Trick: Take the sum formula and divide by 'n' because all angles are equal.

$$ \text{Sum of Exterior Angles} = 360^\circ $$

Meaning: The total degrees of all exterior angles (one at each vertex) of ANY convex polygon.

Variables: No variable needed; it's a constant!

When to use: Always, when asked for the total sum of exterior angles of a convex polygon.

When NOT to use: For interior angles. For non-convex polygons without specific context.

Memory Trick: "A full circle turn around the polygon is 360 degrees."

$$ \text{Each Exterior Angle (Regular Polygon)} = \frac{360^\circ}{n} $$

Meaning: The measure of one single exterior angle in a regular polygon with 'n' sides.

Variables: \(n\) = Number of sides of the regular polygon.

When to use: When asked for the measure of one exterior angle of a REGULAR polygon. Often used as a first step to find the interior angle quickly.

When NOT to use: For irregular polygons. For interior angles directly (unless used with \(180^\circ - E\)).

Memory Trick: Total 360° spread evenly among 'n' equal turns.

$$ \text{Relation: } \text{Interior Angle (I)} + \text{Exterior Angle (E)} = 180^\circ $$

Meaning: At any vertex of a polygon, the interior and its corresponding exterior angle form a linear pair.

Variables: \(I\) = Interior Angle, \(E\) = Exterior Angle.

When to use: To find one angle if the other is known. Works for both regular and irregular polygons at any given vertex.

When NOT to use: To find the sum of angles. This is for individual angles at a single vertex.

Memory Trick: "Straight line is 180 degrees."

$$ \text{Number of Sides (n)} = \frac{360^\circ}{\text{Each Exterior Angle}} \quad \text{ (for Regular Polygons)} $$

Meaning: If you know one exterior angle of a regular polygon, you can find the number of sides.

Variables: \(n\) = Number of sides. Each Exterior Angle = \(E\).

When to use: When given an exterior angle and asked to find 'n'.

When NOT to use: If the polygon is irregular. If given an interior angle (first convert to exterior: \(E = 180^\circ - I\)).

Memory Trick: Rearrangement of \(E = 360^\circ/n\).

$$ \text{Number of Sides (n)} = \frac{360^\circ}{180^\circ - \text{Each Interior Angle}} \quad \text{ (for Regular Polygons)} $$

Meaning: If you know one interior angle of a regular polygon, you can find the number of sides.

Variables: \(n\) = Number of sides. Each Interior Angle = \(I\).

When to use: When given an interior angle and asked to find 'n'.

When NOT to use: If the polygon is irregular.

Memory Trick: This is derived from \(n = 360^\circ / E\) and \(E = 180^\circ - I\).

🧠 Solved Examples

Easy

Example 1: Finding Angles from Sides

Find the sum of interior angles and each exterior angle of a regular decagon.

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Identify the number of sides (n).

A decagon has 10 sides, so \(n = 10\).

Step 2: Calculate the sum of interior angles.

Formula: Sum of Interior Angles \( = (n-2) \times 180^\circ \)

\( = (10-2) \times 180^\circ \)

\( = 8 \times 180^\circ \)

\( = 1440^\circ \)

Step 3: Calculate each exterior angle.

Formula: Each Exterior Angle \( = \frac{360^\circ}{n} \)

\( = \frac{360^\circ}{10} \)

\( = 36^\circ \)

Answer: The sum of interior angles is \(1440^\circ\), and each exterior angle is \(36^\circ\).

Medium

Example 2: Finding Sides from Angles

The interior angle of a regular polygon is \(150^\circ\). Find the number of sides of the polygon.

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Find the exterior angle.

We know that Interior Angle + Exterior Angle \( = 180^\circ \).

Exterior Angle \( = 180^\circ - \text{Interior Angle} \)

\( = 180^\circ - 150^\circ \)

\( = 30^\circ \)

Step 2: Calculate the number of sides.

Formula: Number of Sides \( (n) = \frac{360^\circ}{\text{Each Exterior Angle}} \)

\( n = \frac{360^\circ}{30^\circ} \)

\( n = 12 \)

Answer: The polygon has 12 sides (it's a dodecagon).

Hard

Example 3: Ratio of Angles

The ratio of an interior angle to an exterior angle of a regular polygon is 5:1. Find the number of sides of the polygon.

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Set up the ratio.

Let the interior angle be \(5x\) and the exterior angle be \(1x\) (or just \(x\)).

Step 2: Use the relationship between interior and exterior angles.

We know that Interior Angle + Exterior Angle \( = 180^\circ \).

\( 5x + x = 180^\circ \)

\( 6x = 180^\circ \)

\( x = \frac{180^\circ}{6} \)

\( x = 30^\circ \)

Step 3: Determine the exterior angle.

The exterior angle \( = x = 30^\circ \).

Step 4: Calculate the number of sides.

Formula: Number of Sides \( (n) = \frac{360^\circ}{\text{Each Exterior Angle}} \)

\( n = \frac{360^\circ}{30^\circ} \)

\( n = 12 \)

Answer: The polygon has 12 sides.

SSC CGL Tier-I

Example 4: Combined Angle Problem

A regular polygon has its interior angle equal to 3 times its exterior angle. The number of sides of the polygon is:

  1. 9
  2. 12
  3. 8
  4. 10
View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Formulate the given relationship.

Let Interior Angle be \(I\) and Exterior Angle be \(E\).

Given: \(I = 3E\).

Step 2: Use the fundamental relationship \(I + E = 180^\circ\).

Substitute \(I = 3E\) into the equation:

\( 3E + E = 180^\circ \)

\( 4E = 180^\circ \)

\( E = \frac{180^\circ}{4} \)

\( E = 45^\circ \)

Step 3: Calculate the number of sides (n).

Formula: \( n = \frac{360^\circ}{\text{Each Exterior Angle}} \)

\( n = \frac{360^\circ}{45^\circ} \)

\( n = 8 \)

Answer: The polygon has 8 sides. Option C is correct.

⚡ Exam Tricks & Shortcuts

1. Always Start with Exterior Angle (for Regular Polygons)

The formula for each exterior angle \(E = \frac{360^\circ}{n}\) is simpler and often faster to use. If you need the interior angle, just calculate \(E\) first, then \(I = 180^\circ - E\).

Example: Find Interior Angle of a Regular Hexagon (n=6).

Instead of \(I = \frac{(6-2) \times 180}{6} = \frac{720}{6} = 120^\circ\).

Do: \(E = \frac{360}{6} = 60^\circ \implies I = 180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ\). Less mental arithmetic for many!

2. Memorize Common Polygon Angles

Knowing angles for frequently asked polygons saves crucial seconds.

  • Equilateral Triangle (n=3): \(I=60^\circ, E=120^\circ\)
  • Square (n=4): \(I=90^\circ, E=90^\circ\)
  • Regular Pentagon (n=5): \(I=108^\circ, E=72^\circ\)
  • Regular Hexagon (n=6): \(I=120^\circ, E=60^\circ\)
  • Regular Octagon (n=8): \(I=135^\circ, E=45^\circ\)
  • Regular Decagon (n=10): \(I=144^\circ, E=36^\circ\)
  • Regular Dodecagon (n=12): \(I=150^\circ, E=30^\circ\)

3. Ratio Problems: Direct Substitution

When given a ratio of interior to exterior angle, e.g., \(I:E = k:1\), directly use \(I+E=180^\circ\). Then \(kE + E = 180^\circ \implies (k+1)E = 180^\circ\). Solve for E, then find 'n'.

Example: \(I:E = 5:1\). So \(6E = 180^\circ \implies E = 30^\circ\). \(n = 360/30 = 12\).

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Pitfalls

  • Confusing Interior and Exterior Angles: Always remember \(I + E = 180^\circ\) for angles at the same vertex. They are supplementary.
  • Using Sum Formula for Individual Angle: Don't use \((n-2) \times 180^\circ\) to find *one* interior angle for a regular polygon; you must divide by \(n\).
  • Forgetting "Regular": The formulas for "Each Interior/Exterior Angle" (\(\frac{(n-2)180}{n}\) and \(\frac{360}{n}\)) ONLY apply to regular polygons where all angles are equal. The sum formulas \((n-2)180\) and \(360\) apply to any convex polygon.
  • Incorrect 'n' value: Double-check the number of sides for polygons (e.g., heptagon n=7, octagon n=8, nonagon n=9, decagon n=10).
  • Calculation Errors: Simple arithmetic mistakes, especially with multiplication and division by 180 or 360. Practice mental calculations or use rough work carefully.

📌 Summary

  • A Regular Polygon has equal sides and equal angles.
  • Interior Angle (\(I\)): Angle inside the polygon.
  • Exterior Angle (\(E\)): Angle formed by a side and extension of adjacent side.
  • At any vertex: \(I + E = 180^\circ\).
  • Sum of Interior Angles (any convex polygon): \( (n-2) \times 180^\circ \).
  • Each Interior Angle (regular polygon): \( \frac{(n-2) \times 180^\circ}{n} \).
  • Sum of Exterior Angles (any convex polygon): \( 360^\circ \).
  • Each Exterior Angle (regular polygon): \( \frac{360^\circ}{n} \).
  • To find \(n\): \( n = \frac{360^\circ}{E} \).

💡 Key Takeaways

  • The sum of exterior angles for ANY convex polygon is a constant \(360^\circ\). This is the most powerful starting point for many problems.
  • The concept of regularity is crucial: it means all sides AND all angles are equal, allowing us to divide the sum by 'n'.
  • Always leverage the \(I + E = 180^\circ\) relationship to switch between interior and exterior angles.
  • Practice the exterior angle shortcut (\(E = 360/n\)) for efficiency in regular polygon problems.
