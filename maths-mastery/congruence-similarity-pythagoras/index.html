Congruence, Similarity & Pythagoras Theorem

Medium Difficulty 25-30 Mins Read High Exam Weightage Updated 2026

📘 Introduction to Congruence, Similarity & Pythagoras Theorem

This microtopic covers three fundamental concepts in Geometry: Congruence, Similarity, and the Pythagoras Theorem. Mastering these is crucial for solving a wide range of geometric problems.

  • Congruence deals with identical shapes and sizes.
  • Similarity deals with shapes that are scaled versions of each other.
  • Pythagoras Theorem is a cornerstone for right-angled triangles, relating their side lengths.

🎯 Exam Relevance:

  • SSC CGL/CHSL/MTS/CPO/GD: Regularly features 2-4 questions, often combined with other geometry concepts. Direct questions on properties and theorems.
  • Railway (NTPC/Group D): Basic applications of Pythagoras and similar triangles are common.
  • Banking (PO/Clerk - Mains): Indirect application in data sufficiency or quantitative comparison, sometimes in diagram-based questions.
  • CDS/CAPF/State PCS/Police: High importance, frequently testing conceptual understanding and problem-solving skills with multi-step questions.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Identify and apply the criteria for congruent triangles (SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS).
  • Distinguish between congruent and similar figures and understand their properties.
  • Apply the criteria for similar triangles (AAA/AA, SSS-proportional, SAS-proportional) to solve problems.
  • State and apply the Pythagoras Theorem and its converse to find unknown side lengths.
  • Recognize and utilize Pythagorean Triplets and properties of special right triangles.

📝 Prerequisites

  • Basic understanding of triangles: Types (equilateral, isosceles, scalene, right-angled), angles, sides, vertices.
  • Lines and Angles: Parallel lines, transversals, corresponding angles, alternate interior angles.
  • Basic Algebra: Solving linear equations, understanding squares and square roots.
  • Ratios and Proportions: Ability to work with proportional quantities.

💡 Core Concepts

1. Congruence

  • Definition: Two geometric figures are congruent if they have the exact same shape and the exact same size. They can be perfectly superimposed on each other.
  • Symbol: \(\cong\) (e.g., \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR\)).
  • Intuition: Imagine tracing one triangle and being able to perfectly place that tracing over another triangle.
  • Key Observation: All corresponding sides and all corresponding angles of congruent figures are equal.
  • Memory Tip: Think "Carbon Copy" – identical in every aspect.
  • Mini Example: Two identical 5 Rupee coins are congruent.

2. Similarity

  • Definition: Two geometric figures are similar if they have the exact same shape but can have different sizes. One is a scaled version of the other.
  • Symbol: \(\sim\) (e.g., \(\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR\)).
  • Intuition: Think of zooming in or out on an image; the shape remains the same but the size changes.
  • Key Observation: Corresponding angles of similar figures are equal, and corresponding sides are proportional (their ratios are constant).
  • Memory Tip: Think "Scale Model" – same shape, different size.
  • Mini Example: A photograph and its enlargement are similar.

3. Pythagoras Theorem

  • Definition: In a right-angled triangle, the square of the length of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) is equal to the sum of the squares of the lengths of the other two sides (legs or perpendicular and base).
  • Intuition: If you draw squares on each side of a right triangle, the area of the square on the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the areas of the squares on the other two sides.
  • Key Observation: This theorem applies ONLY to right-angled triangles.
  • Memory Tip: "Hypotenuse is the 'boss' side, its square equals the sum of the 'worker' sides' squares."
  • Mini Example: A triangle with sides 3, 4, and 5 units is a right-angled triangle because \(3^2 + 4^2 = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5^2\).

4. Comparison: Congruence vs. Similarity

Feature Congruence (\(\cong\)) Similarity (\(\sim\))
Shape Same Same
Size Same Different (can be same)
Corresponding Angles Equal Equal
Corresponding Sides Equal Proportional
Relationship Congruent figures are always similar. Similar figures are not always congruent.
Example Two identical copies of a book. A map and the actual territory.

📐 Rules & Properties

1. Congruence Criteria for Triangles

Two triangles are congruent if any of the following conditions are met:

  • SSS (Side-Side-Side):
    • Explanation: If all three sides of one triangle are equal to the three corresponding sides of another triangle.
    • Reason: The side lengths uniquely define the triangle's shape and size.
    • Shortcut: Compare side lengths directly.
    • Example: If AB=PQ, BC=QR, AC=PR, then \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR\).
  • SAS (Side-Angle-Side):
    • Explanation: If two sides and the included angle (the angle between those two sides) of one triangle are equal to the corresponding two sides and included angle of another triangle.
    • Reason: The two sides and their included angle fix the triangle uniquely.
    • Shortcut: Look for the angle *between* the two known sides.
    • Exception: SSA (Side-Side-Angle) is NOT a valid congruence criterion.
    • Example: If AB=PQ, \(\angle B = \angle Q\), BC=QR, then \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR\).
  • ASA (Angle-Side-Angle):
    • Explanation: If two angles and the included side (the side between those two angles) of one triangle are equal to the corresponding two angles and included side of another triangle.
    • Reason: Two angles and the included side uniquely determine the triangle.
    • Shortcut: Look for the side *between* the two known angles.
    • Example: If \(\angle B = \angle Q\), BC=QR, \(\angle C = \angle R\), then \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR\).
  • AAS (Angle-Angle-Side):
    • Explanation: If two angles and a non-included side of one triangle are equal to the corresponding two angles and non-included side of another triangle.
    • Reason: This is effectively the same as ASA because if two angles are known, the third angle is also determined (\(180^\circ - \text{sum of two angles}\)).
    • Shortcut: Similar to ASA, but the side is not between the given angles.
    • Example: If \(\angle A = \angle P\), \(\angle B = \angle Q\), AC=PR, then \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR\).
  • RHS (Right-angle-Hypotenuse-Side):
    • Explanation: If in two right-angled triangles, the hypotenuse and one side of one triangle are equal to the hypotenuse and one corresponding side of the other triangle.
    • Reason: This is a special case of SSA that works for right triangles due to the fixed 90-degree angle.
    • Shortcut: Only applicable to right triangles.
    • Example: If \(\angle B = \angle Q = 90^\circ\), AC=PR (hypotenuse), AB=PQ (side), then \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR\).
  • CPCTC (Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent):
    • Explanation: Once two triangles are proven congruent, all their corresponding angles and sides are equal.
    • Reason: By definition of congruence.
    • Shortcut: A powerful tool to prove equality of other parts after establishing congruence.

2. Similarity Criteria for Triangles

Two triangles are similar if any of the following conditions are met:

  • AAA (Angle-Angle-Angle) or AA (Angle-Angle):
    • Explanation: If all three corresponding angles (or just two, since the third will automatically be equal) of two triangles are equal.
    • Reason: Equal angles ensure the same shape, even if size differs.
    • Shortcut: Easiest criterion to check.
    • Example: If \(\angle A = \angle P\), \(\angle B = \angle Q\), \(\angle C = \angle R\), then \(\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR\).
  • SSS (Side-Side-Side - Proportionality):
    • Explanation: If the ratios of all three corresponding sides of two triangles are equal.
    • Reason: Proportional sides maintain the same shape.
    • Shortcut: Check if \(AB/PQ = BC/QR = AC/PR\).
    • Example: If AB=2, BC=3, AC=4 and PQ=4, QR=6, PR=8, then \(2/4 = 3/6 = 4/8 = 1/2\), so \(\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR\).
  • SAS (Side-Angle-Side - Proportionality):
    • Explanation: If two sides of one triangle are proportional to two corresponding sides of another triangle, and the included angle (between those sides) are equal.
    • Reason: Similar to congruent SAS, but with proportional sides.
    • Shortcut: Check ratio of two sides and the included angle.
    • Example: If \(AB/PQ = BC/QR\) and \(\angle B = \angle Q\), then \(\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR\).
  • Properties of Similar Triangles:
    • If \(\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR\), then:
    • Ratio of Sides: \(AB/PQ = BC/QR = AC/PR = k\) (constant ratio of similarity).
    • Ratio of Perimeters: Perimeter(\(\triangle ABC\))/Perimeter(\(\triangle PQR\)) = \(k\).
    • Ratio of Altitudes: \(h_{A}/h_{P} = k\). (Altitude from A to BC, and P to QR).
    • Ratio of Medians: \(m_{A}/m_{P} = k\). (Median from A to BC, and P to QR).
    • Ratio of Angle Bisectors: \(b_{A}/b_{P} = k\). (Angle bisector from A, and P).
    • Ratio of Areas: Area(\(\triangle ABC\))/Area(\(\triangle PQR\)) = \(k^2 = (AB/PQ)^2\).

3. Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT) / Thales Theorem

  • Explanation: If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, then the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.
  • Reason: Creates similar triangles.
  • Shortcut: Use ratios for parallel lines within a triangle.
  • Example: In \(\triangle ABC\), if DE || BC (where D is on AB, E is on AC), then \(AD/DB = AE/EC\).
  • Converse of BPT: If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then the line is parallel to the third side.

4. Converse of Pythagoras Theorem

  • Explanation: If, in a triangle, the square of one side is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides, then the angle opposite the first side is a right angle (\(90^\circ\)).
  • Reason: Directly proves if a triangle is right-angled.
  • Shortcut: Use \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\) to check for a right angle.
  • Example: If sides are 7, 24, 25. \(7^2 + 24^2 = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25^2\). So, it's a right triangle with \(25\) as the hypotenuse.

5. Pythagorean Triplets

  • Explanation: A set of three positive integers \(a, b, c\) such that \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\). They represent the side lengths of a right-angled triangle.
  • Reason: Helps quickly identify right triangles or missing sides.
  • Shortcut: Memorize common triplets and their multiples.
  • Common Examples: (3, 4, 5), (5, 12, 13), (7, 24, 25), (8, 15, 17), (9, 40, 41), (11, 60, 61), (12, 35, 37), (20, 21, 29). Multiples are also triplets, e.g., (6, 8, 10).

6. Special Right Triangles (Side Ratios)

  • 45-45-90 Triangle:
    • Explanation: An isosceles right-angled triangle where the two non-right angles are \(45^\circ\).
    • Side Ratio: Sides are in the ratio \(1 : 1 : \sqrt{2}\) (base : perpendicular : hypotenuse).
    • Shortcut: If legs are \(x\), hypotenuse is \(x\sqrt{2}\).
  • 30-60-90 Triangle:
    • Explanation: A right-angled triangle with angles \(30^\circ, 60^\circ, 90^\circ\).
    • Side Ratio: Sides are in the ratio \(1 : \sqrt{3} : 2\) (side opposite \(30^\circ\) : side opposite \(60^\circ\) : hypotenuse).
    • Shortcut: If side opposite \(30^\circ\) is \(x\), then side opposite \(60^\circ\) is \(x\sqrt{3}\) and hypotenuse is \(2x\).

📏 Formula Box

1. Pythagoras Theorem

$$ a^2 + b^2 = c^2 $$
  • Meaning: In a right-angled triangle, the sum of the squares of the two shorter sides (legs) equals the square of the longest side (hypotenuse).
  • Variables: \(a, b\) are the lengths of the legs, \(c\) is the length of the hypotenuse.
  • When to use: To find a missing side of a right-angled triangle when two sides are known, or to check if a triangle is right-angled.
  • When NOT to use: For non-right-angled triangles.
  • Memory Trick: "A-squared plus B-squared equals C-squared, always for a right triangle!"

2. Area Ratio of Similar Triangles

$$ \frac{\text{Area}(\triangle ABC)}{\text{Area}(\triangle PQR)} = \left(\frac{AB}{PQ}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{BC}{QR}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{AC}{PR}\right)^2 $$
  • Meaning: The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides (or any corresponding linear measurements like altitudes, medians, perimeters).
  • Variables: \(\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR\). AB, BC, AC are sides of \(\triangle ABC\); PQ, QR, PR are corresponding sides of \(\triangle PQR\).
  • When to use: To find the ratio of areas or an unknown area when the triangles are similar and side ratios are known.
  • Memory Trick: "Area is 2D, so square the 1D side ratio!"

3. Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT)

$$ \text{If DE || BC in } \triangle ABC \text{ (D on AB, E on AC), then } \frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC} $$
  • Meaning: A line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides proportionally.
  • Variables: D and E are points on sides AB and AC respectively; DE is parallel to BC.
  • When to use: To find unknown segment lengths when a line parallel to a side intersects the other two sides.
  • When NOT to use: If the line is not parallel to the third side.
  • Memory Trick: "Parallel lines cut proportional segments on the other sides."

🧠 Solved Examples

Easy

Example 1: Identifying Congruent Triangles

Given \(\triangle ABC\) with AB = 5 cm, BC = 6 cm, \(\angle B = 70^\circ\), and \(\triangle PQR\) with PQ = 5 cm, QR = 6 cm, \(\angle Q = 70^\circ\). Are the triangles congruent? If yes, by what criterion?

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Identify given information.

  • For \(\triangle ABC\): AB = 5 cm, BC = 6 cm, \(\angle B = 70^\circ\).
  • For \(\triangle PQR\): PQ = 5 cm, QR = 6 cm, \(\angle Q = 70^\circ\).

Step 2: Compare corresponding parts.

  • AB = PQ = 5 cm (Side)
  • \(\angle B = \angle Q = 70^\circ\) (Angle)
  • BC = QR = 6 cm (Side)

Step 3: Apply congruence criterion.

  • We have two sides and the included angle equal in both triangles.
  • Therefore, \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR\) by the SAS (Side-Angle-Side) congruence criterion.

Conclusion: Yes, the triangles are congruent by SAS.

Medium

Example 2: Using Basic Proportionality Theorem

In \(\triangle PQR\), ST is a line segment such that S is on PQ and T is on PR. If ST || QR, PS = 3 cm, SQ = 6 cm, and PT = 2 cm, find the length of TR.

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Identify given information and theorem.

  • \(\triangle PQR\), with ST || QR.
  • PS = 3 cm, SQ = 6 cm, PT = 2 cm.
  • We need to find TR.
  • Since ST || QR, we can apply the Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT).

Step 2: Apply BPT.

  • According to BPT: \(PS/SQ = PT/TR\).

Step 3: Substitute values and solve for TR.

  • \(3/6 = 2/TR\)
  • \(1/2 = 2/TR\)
  • Cross-multiply: \(1 \times TR = 2 \times 2\)
  • \(TR = 4\) cm.

Conclusion: The length of TR is 4 cm.

Hard

Example 3: Combining Similarity and Pythagoras Theorem

In a right-angled triangle ABC, right-angled at B, AB = 6 cm and BC = 8 cm. A perpendicular BD is drawn from B to AC. Find the length of BD.

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Find the hypotenuse AC using Pythagoras Theorem.

  • In right \(\triangle ABC\), \(AC^2 = AB^2 + BC^2\).
  • \(AC^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 36 + 64 = 100\).
  • \(AC = \sqrt{100} = 10\) cm.
  • (Recognize (6, 8, 10) as a multiple of (3, 4, 5) triplet).

Step 2: Identify similar triangles.

  • When a perpendicular is drawn from the right-angle vertex to the hypotenuse, it creates two smaller triangles similar to the original triangle and to each other.
  • So, \(\triangle ADB \sim \triangle BDC \sim \triangle ABC\).

Step 3: Use the area relationship or similarity ratio to find BD.

  • Method 1: Area approach.
  • Area of \(\triangle ABC = (1/2) \times \text{base} \times \text{height}\).
  • Using AB as base and BC as height: Area = \((1/2) \times 6 \times 8 = 24\) cm\(^2\).
  • Using AC as base and BD as height: Area = \((1/2) \times AC \times BD\).
  • \(24 = (1/2) \times 10 \times BD\).
  • \(24 = 5 \times BD\).
  • \(BD = 24/5 = 4.8\) cm.
  • Method 2: Similarity ratio (e.g., \(\triangle ADB \sim \triangle ABC\)).
  • Corresponding sides: \(AD/AB = DB/BC = AB/AC\).
  • From \(DB/BC = AB/AC\):
  • \(DB/8 = 6/10\).
  • \(DB = (8 \times 6) / 10 = 48/10 = 4.8\) cm.

Conclusion: The length of BD is 4.8 cm.

Exam Level (SSC CGL)

Example 4: Combined Problem on Similar Triangles and Ratios

In \(\triangle ABC\), D and E are points on sides AB and AC respectively, such that DE || BC. If AD:DB = 3:2 and the area of \(\triangle ADE\) is 27 sq cm, find the area of the trapezium DECB.

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Understand the given information and implied relationships.

  • DE || BC, which implies \(\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC\) by AA similarity (\(\angle A\) is common, \(\angle ADE = \angle ABC\) (corresponding angles)).
  • AD:DB = 3:2. This means AD is 3 parts and DB is 2 parts.
  • Therefore, AB = AD + DB = 3 + 2 = 5 parts.

Step 2: Determine the ratio of corresponding sides of similar triangles.

  • The ratio of corresponding sides of \(\triangle ADE\) and \(\triangle ABC\) is \(AD/AB\).
  • \(AD/AB = 3/5\).

Step 3: Use the area ratio property of similar triangles.

  • Area(\(\triangle ADE\))/Area(\(\triangle ABC\)) = \((AD/AB)^2\).
  • Area(\(\triangle ADE\))/Area(\(\triangle ABC\)) = \((3/5)^2 = 9/25\).

Step 4: Calculate the Area of \(\triangle ABC\).

  • Given Area(\(\triangle ADE\)) = 27 sq cm.
  • \(27 / \text{Area}(\triangle ABC) = 9/25\).
  • Area(\(\triangle ABC\)) = \((27 \times 25) / 9 = 3 \times 25 = 75\) sq cm.

Step 5: Calculate the Area of trapezium DECB.

  • Area(trapezium DECB) = Area(\(\triangle ABC\)) - Area(\(\triangle ADE\)).
  • Area(trapezium DECB) = \(75 - 27 = 48\) sq cm.

Conclusion: The area of the trapezium DECB is 48 sq cm.

⚡ Exam Tricks & Shortcuts

1. Memorize Pythagorean Triplets

  • Trick: Instantly recognize common triplets like (3, 4, 5), (5, 12, 13), (7, 24, 25), (8, 15, 17), (20, 21, 29). Also, remember their multiples (e.g., 6, 8, 10; 10, 24, 26).
  • Benefit: Saves significant time in calculating hypotenuse or a leg, especially in right-angled triangles questions.
  • Example: If legs are 15 and 20, you know it's \(5 \times (3,4)\), so hypotenuse must be \(5 \times 5 = 25\). No need for \(15^2 + 20^2\).

2. Special Right Triangle Ratios

  • Trick: For a 45-45-90 triangle, sides are \(x : x : x\sqrt{2}\). For a 30-60-90 triangle, sides are \(x : x\sqrt{3} : 2x\) (opposite \(30^\circ\), opposite \(60^\circ\), hypotenuse respectively).
  • Benefit: Quickly find all sides if one side is known, without using trigonometry.
  • Example: If the hypotenuse of a 30-60-90 triangle is 10 cm, the side opposite \(30^\circ\) is \(10/2 = 5\) cm, and the side opposite \(60^\circ\) is \(5\sqrt{3}\) cm.

3. Directly Apply Similar Triangle Area Ratios

  • Trick: If ratio of sides of similar triangles is \(a:b\), then ratio of areas is \(a^2:b^2\). Conversely, if ratio of areas is \(X:Y\), ratio of sides is \(\sqrt{X}:\sqrt{Y}\).
  • Benefit: Bypasses intermediate calculations when dealing with areas.
  • Example: If Area(\(\triangle PQR\)) = 4 * Area(\(\triangle ABC\)), then \(\text{side}_{PQR} = 2 \times \text{side}_{ABC}\).

4. Shortcut for Perpendicular from Right Angle to Hypotenuse

  • Trick: In a right \(\triangle ABC\), right-angled at B, if BD is perpendicular to AC, then:
    • \(BD = (AB \times BC) / AC\)
    • \(AB^2 = AD \times AC\)
    • \(BC^2 = CD \times AC\)
    • \(BD^2 = AD \times CD\)
  • Benefit: These direct formulas derived from similarity save steps compared to setting up full similarity ratios or area calculations.
  • Example: Using Example 3, \(BD = (6 \times 8) / 10 = 48/10 = 4.8\) cm.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Pitfalls

  • Confusing Congruence and Similarity: Remember, congruence means *identical* (same shape, same size), while similarity means *scaled versions* (same shape, different size). All congruent triangles are similar, but not all similar triangles are congruent.
  • Incorrectly Identifying Corresponding Parts: When comparing triangles, ensure you match corresponding vertices, sides, and angles correctly. For example, if \(\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR\), then AB corresponds to PQ, \(\angle B\) to \(\angle Q\), etc.
  • Applying SAS/ASA/AAS/RHS Incorrectly:
    • For SAS (congruence or similarity), the angle MUST be included between the two sides.
    • For ASA, the side MUST be included between the two angles.
    • RHS applies ONLY to right-angled triangles.
    • SSA is NOT a valid congruence criterion (unless it's RHS).
  • Calculation Errors with Pythagoras Theorem: Double-check squares and square roots. Ensure you always use the hypotenuse as 'c' in \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\).
  • Misapplying BPT: Remember BPT requires the line segment to be parallel to one side. If it's not parallel, the proportionality does not hold.
  • Forgetting to Square Ratios for Area: The ratio of areas of similar figures is the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides, not just the ratio itself.
  • Not Considering All Cases: Sometimes a problem might have two possible solutions for a missing side or angle, especially when working with non-right triangles and trigonometric rules (beyond this microtopic, but good to keep in mind for advanced geometry).

📌 Summary

  • Congruent Figures (\(\cong\)): Same shape, same size. Corresponding angles and sides are equal. Criteria: SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS.
  • Similar Figures (\(\sim\)): Same shape, different size. Corresponding angles are equal, corresponding sides are proportional. Criteria: AAA/AA, SSS (proportional), SAS (proportional).
  • Properties of Similar Triangles: Ratio of sides = Ratio of perimeters/altitudes/medians/angle bisectors. Ratio of areas = (Ratio of sides)\(^2\).
  • Pythagoras Theorem: In a right triangle, \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\). \(c\) is the hypotenuse.
  • Converse of Pythagoras Theorem: If \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\), then the triangle is right-angled at the vertex opposite side \(c\).
  • Pythagorean Triplets: Sets of integers (e.g., 3, 4, 5) satisfying \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\).
  • Special Right Triangles: 45-45-90 (\(x:x:x\sqrt{2}\)) and 30-60-90 (\(x:x\sqrt{3}:2x\)) side ratios.
  • Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT): If a line is parallel to one side of a triangle, it divides the other two sides proportionally.

🚀 Quick Revision Cheatsheet: Congruence, Similarity & Pythagoras

  • Congruence (\(\cong\)): Same Shape & Size. SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS. \(\implies\) CPCTC.
  • Similarity (\(\sim\)): Same Shape, Different Size. AA, SSS (proportional), SAS (proportional).
    • Sides Ratio = Perimeters Ratio = Altitudes Ratio = Medians Ratio.
    • Areas Ratio = (Sides Ratio)\(^2\).
  • Pythagoras Theorem: For Right \(\triangle\), \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\).
    • Converse: If \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\), then it's a right triangle.
    • Triplets: (3,4,5), (5,12,13), (7,24,25), (8,15,17), etc.
    • Special \(\triangle\):
      • 45-45-90: \(x : x : x\sqrt{2}\)
      • 30-60-90: \(x : x\sqrt{3} : 2x\)
  • BPT: If DE || BC in \(\triangle ABC\), then \(AD/DB = AE/EC\).

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • Master the Criteria: Be able to instantly recall and apply the congruence (SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS) and similarity (AA, SSS, SAS) criteria. This is fundamental.
  • Proportionality is Key for Similarity: Understand that similarity is all about proportional sides and equal angles. This extends to perimeters, altitudes, medians, and area (squared ratio).
  • Pythagoras for Right Triangles: This theorem is exclusively for right-angled triangles and provides a direct relationship between side lengths. Memorize common triplets to save time.
  • CPCTC is Powerful: Once congruence is proven, CPCTC lets you deduce equality of any corresponding part, simplifying many proofs and problems.
  • Practice with Diagrams: Most geometry problems are diagram-based. Practice redrawing, marking knowns, and identifying relevant triangles to apply the theorems.
