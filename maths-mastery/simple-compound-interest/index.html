Simple & Compound Interest (Rate, Time, Principal)

Medium Difficulty 25-30 Mins Read High Exam Weightage Updated 2026

📘 Introduction to Simple & Compound Interest

Interest is the cost of borrowing money or the reward for lending it. Understanding how interest is calculated is crucial for financial literacy and for mastering a frequently tested topic in competitive exams.

  • Simple Interest (SI) calculates interest only on the initial principal amount.
  • Compound Interest (CI) calculates interest on the principal amount and also on the accumulated interest from previous periods.
  • This microtopic equips you with the fundamental concepts, formulas, and strategies to confidently solve problems related to Simple and Compound Interest, focusing on determining Rate, Time, and Principal.

🎯 Learning Objectives

Upon completing this module, you will be able to:

  • Define and differentiate between Simple Interest (SI) and Compound Interest (CI).
  • Identify and apply the key components: Principal, Rate, Time, Interest, and Amount.
  • Accurately calculate SI, CI, Amount, Principal, Rate, or Time using various methods.
  • Solve problems involving the difference between SI and CI for different time periods.
  • Master advanced concepts like half-yearly/quarterly compounding and variable rates.

💡 Core Concepts

Grasping these fundamental terms is key to mastering interest problems.

Interest

  • Definition: The extra money paid for using borrowed money, or the income earned from lending money.
  • Intuition: It's the "rent" for money.
  • Key Observation: Always expressed as a percentage of the principal over a period.
  • Memory Tip: Think of interest as the 'change' in your money over time due to lending/borrowing.

Principal (P)

  • Definition: The initial sum of money borrowed or invested.
  • Intuition: The starting amount.
  • Key Observation: This is the base on which interest is calculated.
  • Memory Tip: P for 'Primary' or 'Starting' amount.

Rate of Interest (R)

  • Definition: The percentage at which interest is charged or earned, usually per annum (p.a.).
  • Intuition: How quickly your money grows or how much you pay extra per year.
  • Key Observation: Always expressed as a percentage, e.g., 5% p.a.
  • Memory Tip: R for 'Rate' or 'Ratio' of interest per 100.

Time (T)

  • Definition: The duration for which the money is borrowed or invested.
  • Intuition: How long the money is "out there".
  • Key Observation: Always expressed in years for standard formulas. Convert months/days to years.
  • Memory Tip: T for 'Tenure' or 'Total years'.

Amount (A)

  • Definition: The total money received back at the end of the period. It includes the Principal and the accumulated Interest.
  • Intuition: Principal + Interest = Amount.
  • Key Observation: This is the final value.
  • Memory Tip: A for 'Accumulated' or 'Final' sum.
Basis Simple Interest (SI) Compound Interest (CI)
Calculation Calculated only on the original Principal for the entire period. Calculated on the Principal + accumulated Interest from previous periods.
Growth Linear growth: Interest earned is constant for each period. Exponential growth: Interest earned increases with each successive period.
Interest on Interest No, interest is not charged/earned on previously earned interest. Yes, interest is charged/earned on previously earned interest.
Effective Rate Remains constant. Increases over time.
Formula Intuition Basic multiplication, proportional to time. Successive percentage increase, like population growth.

📏 Rules & Properties

These rules highlight key behaviors and properties of Simple and Compound Interest.

Rule 1: Simple Interest is Constant per Period

  • Explanation: The interest earned in the first year will be the same as in the second year, third year, and so on, as it's always calculated on the initial principal.
  • Reason: SI = $(P \times R \times T)/100$. If P and R are constant, SI is directly proportional to T. For equal T intervals, SI is equal.
  • Shortcut: If SI for 1 year is ₹X, then SI for T years is ₹\(T \times X\). This is very useful for finding SI for multiple years or for finding the interest for a single year when total SI is given.
  • Example: If Simple Interest on ₹1000 at 10% p.a. is ₹100 for the 1st year, it will be ₹100 for the 2nd year and ₹100 for the 3rd year.

Rule 2: Compound Interest Grows Faster than Simple Interest (for T > 1 year)

  • Explanation: For the first year, SI and CI are always equal. However, for periods longer than one year, CI will always be greater than SI because CI earns interest on previous interest, whereas SI does not.
  • Reason: CI's growth is exponential (interest on interest), while SI's growth is linear.
  • Shortcut: Always assume CI > SI for T > 1, unless specific rate manipulation occurs (which is rare). This helps in quickly eliminating options in MCQs.
  • Exception: For T=1 year, SI = CI.
  • Example: For P=₹1000, R=10%, T=2 years: SI = ₹200. CI = ₹210. (CI > SI)

Rule 3: Difference between CI and SI for 2 Years

  • Explanation: The difference arises from the second year's interest on the first year's simple interest.
  • Reason: SI for 2 years = $2 \times (P \times R/100)$. CI for 2 years = $P[(1 + R/100)^2 - 1]$. The difference captures the 'interest on the first year's interest'.
  • Shortcut: Difference ($D_2$) = $P \times (R/100)^2$. This is a direct formula for exam problems.
  • Example: P=₹10,000, R=10%, T=2 years. $D_2 = 10000 \times (10/100)^2 = 10000 \times (1/10)^2 = 10000 \times 1/100 = ₹100$.

Rule 4: Difference between CI and SI for 3 Years

  • Explanation: This difference accounts for interest on interest for multiple periods.
  • Reason: More complex, involving interest on interest from previous periods.
  • Shortcut: Difference ($D_3$) = $P \times (R/100)^2 \times (3 + R/100)$.
  • Memory Tip: Note the term $(3 + R/100)$ after the 2-year difference formula.
  • Example: P=₹10,000, R=10%, T=3 years. $D_3 = 10000 \times (10/100)^2 \times (3 + 10/100) = 10000 \times (1/100) \times (3 + 1/10) = 100 \times (31/10) = ₹310$.

Rule 5: Compounding Frequency Adjustment

  • Explanation: If interest is compounded more frequently than annually (e.g., half-yearly, quarterly), the effective rate and number of periods change.
  • Reason: Interest is added back to the principal more often, leading to interest on interest being calculated more times.
  • Shortcut:
    • Half-yearly (Semi-annually): Rate becomes $R/2$, Time becomes $2T$. (2 compounding periods per year)
    • Quarterly: Rate becomes $R/4$, Time becomes $4T$. (4 compounding periods per year)
    • Monthly: Rate becomes $R/12$, Time becomes $12T$.
  • Memory Tip: Divide Rate by number of periods per year, Multiply Time by number of periods per year.
  • Example: If R=10% p.a. for 2 years compounded half-yearly, then the effective rate is 5% per half-year and the number of periods is 4.

📐 Formula Box

These are the essential formulas you need to solve Simple and Compound Interest problems. Understand when to use each.

Simple Interest (SI)

$$ \text{SI} = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100} $$
  • Meaning: Simple Interest is directly proportional to Principal, Rate, and Time.
  • Variables:
    • P = Principal amount
    • R = Rate of interest per annum (%)
    • T = Time period in years
  • When to use: When interest is calculated only on the initial principal.
  • Memory Trick: PRT/100 – "Please Remember This".

Amount for Simple Interest

$$ A = P + \text{SI} \quad \text{or} \quad A = P \left(1 + \frac{RT}{100}\right) $$
  • Meaning: The total amount repaid or received, including the principal and the simple interest.
  • Variables: A = Amount, P = Principal, R = Rate, T = Time, SI = Simple Interest.
  • When to use: To find the total value at the end of the SI period.
  • Memory Trick: Final Amount = Original Principal + Simple Interest.

Amount for Compound Interest (Annually)

$$ A = P \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^T $$
  • Meaning: The total amount repaid or received when interest is compounded annually.
  • Variables:
    • A = Amount
    • P = Principal amount
    • R = Rate of interest per annum (%)
    • T = Time period in years
  • When to use: When interest is compounded once a year.
  • When NOT to use: If compounding frequency is not annual (e.g., half-yearly, quarterly).
  • Memory Trick: (1 + R/100) acts as a multiplier, applied 'T' times due to compounding.

Compound Interest (CI)

$$ \text{CI} = A - P \quad \text{or} \quad \text{CI} = P \left[\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^T - 1\right] $$
  • Meaning: The total interest earned when interest is compounded.
  • Variables: CI = Compound Interest, A = Amount, P = Principal, R = Rate, T = Time.
  • When to use: To find only the interest component from a compound interest calculation.
  • Memory Trick: Compound Interest is always Amount minus Principal.

Difference between CI and SI for 2 Years ($D_2$)

$$ D_2 = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 $$
  • Meaning: The difference between the compound interest and simple interest for a period of two years.
  • Variables: $D_2$ = Difference, P = Principal, R = Rate.
  • When to use: Directly calculates the difference for a 2-year period.
  • Memory Trick: $P(R/100)^2$ - The square term implies 2 years.

Difference between CI and SI for 3 Years ($D_3$)

$$ D_3 = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 \left(3 + \frac{R}{100}\right) $$
  • Meaning: The difference between the compound interest and simple interest for a period of three years.
  • Variables: $D_3$ = Difference, P = Principal, R = Rate.
  • When to use: Directly calculates the difference for a 3-year period.
  • Memory Trick: Same as 2-year formula, but multiplied by $(3 + R/100)$ for the third year's effect.

Compounding Frequency Adjustments

  • Half-Yearly: (2 compounding periods per year) $$ A = P \left(1 + \frac{R/2}{100}\right)^{2T} \quad \text{or} \quad A = P \left(1 + \frac{R}{200}\right)^{2T} $$
  • Quarterly: (4 compounding periods per year) $$ A = P \left(1 + \frac{R/4}{100}\right)^{4T} \quad \text{or} \quad A = P \left(1 + \frac{R}{400}\right)^{4T} $$
  • Meaning: Adjusts the rate and time based on how often interest is compounded within a year.
  • Variables: A = Amount, P = Principal, R = Annual Rate, T = Time in years.
  • When to use: When compounding is specified as half-yearly, quarterly, etc.
  • Memory Trick: Divide R by the number of compounding periods per year, multiply T by the number of compounding periods per year.

Variable Rates for CI

$$ A = P \left(1 + \frac{R_1}{100}\right) \left(1 + \frac{R_2}{100}\right) \left(1 + \frac{R_3}{100}\right) \dots $$
  • Meaning: Calculates the amount when the rate of interest changes for different years.
  • Variables: A = Amount, P = Principal, $R_1, R_2, R_3 \dots$ = Rates for 1st, 2nd, 3rd year respectively.
  • When to use: When the problem states different interest rates for successive years.
  • Memory Trick: Multiply successive (1 + R/100) factors. Each factor represents the growth for that year.

🧠 Solved Examples

Practice these step-by-step examples to build confidence and apply the formulas and concepts learned.

Easy

Example 1: Calculating Simple Interest

Find the Simple Interest on ₹8,000 at 5% p.a. for 3 years.

View Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Identify the given values:
    • Principal (P) = ₹8,000
    • Rate (R) = 5% p.a.
    • Time (T) = 3 years
  2. Apply the Simple Interest formula: $$ \text{SI} = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100} $$
  3. Substitute the values: $$ \text{SI} = \frac{8000 \times 5 \times 3}{100} $$
  4. Calculate: $$ \text{SI} = \frac{120000}{100} = ₹1,200 $$

Answer: The Simple Interest is ₹1,200.

Medium

Example 2: Calculating Compound Interest and Amount

Calculate the Compound Interest on ₹12,000 at 10% p.a. for 2 years, compounded annually.

View Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Identify the given values:
    • Principal (P) = ₹12,000
    • Rate (R) = 10% p.a.
    • Time (T) = 2 years
    • Compounding: Annually
  2. Apply the Amount for Compound Interest formula: $$ A = P \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^T $$
  3. Substitute the values: $$ A = 12000 \left(1 + \frac{10}{100}\right)^2 $$ $$ A = 12000 \left(1 + \frac{1}{10}\right)^2 $$ $$ A = 12000 \left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^2 $$ $$ A = 12000 \times \frac{121}{100} $$
  4. Calculate the Amount (A): $$ A = 120 \times 121 = ₹14,520 $$
  5. Calculate the Compound Interest (CI): $$ \text{CI} = A - P $$ $$ \text{CI} = 14520 - 12000 = ₹2,520 $$

Answer: The Compound Interest is ₹2,520, and the Amount is ₹14,520.

Hard

Example 3: Finding Principal using CI - SI Difference

The difference between the Compound Interest and Simple Interest on a certain sum for 2 years at 8% p.a. is ₹12.80. Find the Principal sum.

View Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Identify the given values:
    • Difference ($D_2$) = ₹12.80
    • Rate (R) = 8% p.a.
    • Time (T) = 2 years
    • Unknown: Principal (P)
  2. Apply the formula for difference between CI and SI for 2 years: $$ D_2 = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 $$
  3. Substitute the known values: $$ 12.80 = P \left(\frac{8}{100}\right)^2 $$ $$ 12.80 = P \left(\frac{2}{25}\right)^2 $$ $$ 12.80 = P \times \frac{4}{625} $$
  4. Solve for P: $$ P = \frac{12.80 \times 625}{4} $$ $$ P = \frac{1280 \times 625}{400} $$ $$ P = \frac{320 \times 625}{100} $$ $$ P = 3.20 \times 625 $$ $$ P = 2000 $$

Answer: The Principal sum is ₹2,000.

Exam Level

Example 4: Compounding Half-Yearly

Find the Compound Interest on ₹20,000 for 1 year at 10% p.a. if the interest is compounded half-yearly.

View Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Identify the given values:
    • Principal (P) = ₹20,000
    • Annual Rate (R) = 10% p.a.
    • Time (T) = 1 year
    • Compounding: Half-yearly
  2. Adjust Rate and Time for half-yearly compounding:
    • New Rate (R') = $R/2 = 10\% / 2 = 5\%$ per half-year
    • New Time (T') = $T \times 2 = 1 \text{ year} \times 2 = 2$ half-years
  3. Apply the Amount for Compound Interest formula with adjusted values: $$ A = P \left(1 + \frac{R'}{100}\right)^{T'} $$ $$ A = 20000 \left(1 + \frac{5}{100}\right)^2 $$ $$ A = 20000 \left(1 + \frac{1}{20}\right)^2 $$ $$ A = 20000 \left(\frac{21}{20}\right)^2 $$ $$ A = 20000 \times \frac{441}{400} $$
  4. Calculate the Amount (A): $$ A = 50 \times 441 = ₹22,050 $$
  5. Calculate the Compound Interest (CI): $$ \text{CI} = A - P $$ $$ \text{CI} = 22050 - 20000 = ₹2,050 $$

Answer: The Compound Interest is ₹2,050.

⚡ Exam Tricks & Shortcuts

Boost your speed and accuracy with these high-yield methods.

1. Tree Method for Compound Interest

  • Concept: Visualize interest calculation year by year. It clearly shows the interest on principal and interest on previous interest.
  • How to use:
    1. For 1st year: CI = SI = P * R%
    2. For 2nd year: SI = P * R%. Additional interest = (1st year's CI) * R%. Total CI = (2nd year's SI) + (additional interest).
    3. For 3rd year: SI = P * R%. Additional interest = (Total CI till 2nd year) * R%. (More simply, interest on P + interest on 1st year's interest + interest on 2nd year's interest on P + interest on 2nd year's interest on 1st year's interest)
  • Benefit: Excellent for finding CI for 2 or 3 years, and for finding the difference between CI and SI easily.
  • Example: P=₹10,000, R=10%, T=2 years
    • Year 1: Interest on P = 10% of 10000 = ₹1000
    • Year 2: Interest on P = 10% of 10000 = ₹1000
    • Interest on Year 1's interest = 10% of 1000 = ₹100
    • Total CI = 1000 + 1000 + 100 = ₹2100.
    • Total SI = 1000 + 1000 = ₹2000.
    • Difference = ₹100.

2. Effective Rate Method for CI (especially 2 years)

  • Concept: Calculate a single equivalent percentage rate for compound interest over multiple periods.
  • How to use: For two periods (e.g., 2 years, or 2 half-years), the effective rate ($R_{eff}$) is: $$ R_{eff} = R_1 + R_2 + \frac{R_1 R_2}{100} $$ If the rate is the same for both periods ($R_1 = R_2 = R$), then: $$ R_{eff} = 2R + \frac{R^2}{100} $$
  • Benefit: Quickly find CI for 2 periods. CI = $P \times R_{eff} / 100$.
  • Example: P=₹10,000, R=10%, T=2 years. $$ R_{eff} = 2(10) + \frac{10^2}{100} = 20 + \frac{100}{100} = 20 + 1 = 21\% $$ CI = $10000 \times 21 / 100 = ₹2100$.

3. Fraction Method for CI (Rate to Ratio)

  • Concept: Convert the rate percentage into a fraction and use ratios to find the Amount and CI.
  • How to use:
    1. Convert R% to a fraction (e.g., 10% = 1/10).
    2. If the rate is 1/x, then in one year, Principal becomes $x+1$ parts and Amount becomes $x$ parts. The ratio of P:A for one year is $x : (x+1)$.
    3. For T years, the ratio P:A becomes $x^T : (x+1)^T$.
  • Benefit: Avoids large calculations, especially when R is easy to convert to a fraction (e.g., 10%, 20%, 25%, 16 2/3%, 12.5%).
  • Example: P=?, A=₹14,520, R=10%, T=2 years.
    • 10% = 1/10.
    • P:A for 1 year = 10:11.
    • P:A for 2 years = $10^2 : 11^2 = 100:121$.
    • If 121 parts = ₹14,520, then 1 part = $14520/121 = ₹120$.
    • Principal (100 parts) = $100 \times 120 = ₹12,000$.

4. Approximation for CI when R is small (for competitive exams)

  • Concept: For small R and T, $(1 + R/100)^T \approx 1 + TR/100 + T(T-1)/2 \cdot (R/100)^2$.
  • How to use: Useful for quick estimation or when options are far apart. For 2 years, CI is approximately $SI + P(R/100)^2$.
  • Benefit: Can save time by quickly narrowing down options, especially if exact calculation is lengthy.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Pitfalls

Be aware of these frequent errors to avoid losing marks.

  • Incorrect Time Conversion: Always convert the time period to years. If given in months, divide by 12. If in days, divide by 365 (or 366 for leap year, but generally 365 in exams).
  • Misplacing % in Rate: Remember that $R$ in $P \times R \times T / 100$ is a percentage value (e.g., 5 for 5%). In $P(1 + R/100)^T$, $R$ is also the percentage value. Do not write 0.05 for 5% as $R$ in the formula unless you remove the /100.
  • Confusing SI and CI Formulas: The most basic mistake. Double-check which type of interest the question asks for.
  • Ignoring Compounding Frequency: Failing to adjust R and T for half-yearly, quarterly, or other compounding periods. This is a common trap.
  • Calculation Errors: Especially with large numbers or decimals. Practice mental arithmetic and quick multiplication/division. Use fractions where possible (e.g., 1/20 for 5%) to simplify.
  • Finding Amount instead of Interest (or vice versa): Read the question carefully if it asks for the 'Amount' or the 'Interest'.
  • Unit Inconsistency: Ensure Principal, Interest, and Amount are in the same currency units throughout the problem.

📊 Exam Strategy & Weightage

Understand the importance and typical question patterns for various exams.

SSC CGL/CHSL/MTS/CPO/GD

  • Weightage: High (typically 1-3 questions in Tier 1, 2-4 in Tier 2).
  • Pattern: Questions often involve CI-SI difference, finding P/R/T when SI and CI for different periods are given, or half-yearly/quarterly compounding.
  • Difficulty: Moderate to Hard for CGL Tier 2, Easy to Moderate for Tier 1. CHSL/MTS/GD are generally easier.
  • Focus: Master CI-SI difference formulas, fraction method for CI, and effective rates.

Banking (IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI PO/Clerk, RRB)

  • Weightage: High (often 3-5 questions, sometimes part of Data Interpretation or Data Sufficiency).
  • Pattern: Application-based problems, multi-step calculations, comparing SI and CI of different schemes.
  • Difficulty: Moderate to Hard (especially for PO level). Speed and accuracy are critical.
  • Focus: Practice questions with varying rates for different years, half-yearly/quarterly compounding, and approximation techniques.

Railway (NTPC, Group D, ALP)

  • Weightage: Moderate (1-2 questions).
  • Pattern: Usually direct formula application, finding SI/CI or P/R/T. Less complex than SSC/Banking.
  • Difficulty: Easy to Moderate.
  • Focus: Strong command of basic SI/CI formulas and simple algebraic manipulation.

CDS, CAPF, State PCS, Police

  • Weightage: Moderate (1-2 questions in Quant section).
  • Pattern: Can range from direct formula application to slightly tricky conceptual questions. CDS often includes questions where SI & CI are used in combination with other topics.
  • Difficulty: Easy to Moderate for most state exams; Moderate for CDS/CAPF.
  • Focus: Clear understanding of core concepts, standard formulas, and their application.

📌 Summary

  • Interest: Cost of borrowed money or earnings from lent money.
  • Principal (P): Initial sum. Rate (R): % p.a. Time (T): In years. Amount (A): P + Interest.
  • Simple Interest (SI): Interest on principal only. $$ \text{SI} = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100} $$ $$ A = P + \text{SI} $$
  • Compound Interest (CI): Interest on principal + accumulated interest. $$ A = P \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^T $$ $$ \text{CI} = A - P $$
  • Key Difference: SI is linear, CI is exponential (interest on interest).
  • Compounding Frequency: Adjust R and T (e.g., half-yearly: R/2, 2T).
  • Difference CI - SI for 2 years: $D_2 = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2$
  • Difference CI - SI for 3 years: $D_3 = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 \left(3 + \frac{R}{100}\right)$
  • Tricks: Tree method, effective rate, fraction method save time.
