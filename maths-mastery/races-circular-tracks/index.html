Races & Circular Tracks

Medium Difficulty 25-30 Mins Read High Exam Weightage Updated 2026

📘 Introduction to Races & Circular Tracks

This microtopic dives into the dynamics of competitive races and movement on circular paths, crucial for understanding relative motion in complex scenarios. It extends the fundamental concepts of Time, Speed & Distance to practical, multi-entity problems.

  • Exam Relevance: A high-yield topic, frequently appearing in SSC, Railway, Banking, and State PSC exams.
  • Question Types: Expect problems on linear races (A beats B), head starts, and runners on circular tracks (meeting points, relative speeds).
  • Core Skill: Mastering relative speed and LCM concepts is key to solving these questions efficiently.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand the mechanics of linear races, including head starts and 'A beats B' scenarios.
  • Master calculations for relative speed in both same and opposite directions on circular tracks.
  • Determine the time and location for runners to meet for the first time on a circular track.
  • Calculate the time for runners to meet at the starting point on a circular track.
  • Solve problems involving multiple runners on circular tracks using LCM methods.

📝 Prerequisites

  • Time, Speed & Distance Basics: Knowledge of \( \text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}} \), unit conversions (km/hr to m/s, etc.).
  • Relative Speed Fundamentals: Basic understanding of how speeds combine when objects move towards or away from each other.
  • Ratio & Proportion: Ability to work with speed ratios and distances.
  • LCM (Least Common Multiple): Essential for meeting points on circular tracks.

💡 Core Concepts

1. Linear Races

A linear race involves competitors moving along a straight line from a starting point to a finish line.

  • Definition: A contest where participants cover a fixed distance in a straight line.
  • Intuition: Think of a 100-meter dash; one person crosses the finish line, while others are behind.
  • Key Observations:
    • The person with higher speed wins.
    • The winner covers the full distance. Others cover less or take more time.
  • Memory Tip: "Linear = Line". Focus on who covers how much distance/time compared to the winner.
  • Mini Example: In a 100m race, if A beats B by 10m, A covers 100m while B covers 90m in the same time.

2. Head Start

A head start is an advantage given to one competitor, either in distance or time, before the race officially begins for others.

  • Definition: A pre-determined advantage (distance or time) given to a competitor.
  • Intuition: A slower runner might start 10m ahead, or start 5 seconds earlier than a faster runner.
  • Key Observations:
    • A distance head start means the slower person has less distance to cover to the finish line.
    • A time head start means the slower person runs for a certain duration before the faster person starts.
  • Memory Tip: "Head start = Already ahead". Adjust the total distance or time calculation for the disadvantaged person.
  • Mini Example: A gives B a 10m head start in a 100m race. A runs 100m, B needs to run only 90m.

3. Circular Tracks

Circular tracks involve runners moving along a circular path. The key complexity arises from their paths intersecting multiple times.

  • Definition: A race or movement where participants run along a closed circular path.
  • Intuition: Imagine athletes running laps on an athletic track. They can meet at various points.
  • Key Observations:
    • Relative Speed: Crucial for determining meeting times. Add speeds for opposite directions, subtract for same.
    • Meeting Points: Can be for the first time anywhere, or specifically at the starting point.
  • Memory Tip: "Circular = Cyclical". Focus on relative motion and the periodic nature of meeting.
  • Mini Example: Two runners on a 400m track. If they run in opposite directions, they'll meet sooner than if they run in the same direction.

4. Dead Heat/Tie

A dead heat or tie occurs when two or more competitors finish the race at exactly the same time.

  • Definition: Two or more competitors finish the race simultaneously.
  • Intuition: Photo finish scenario – no clear winner between two or more participants.
  • Key Observations:
    • All involved runners cover the same distance in the same amount of time.
    • Their average speeds would be equal for the duration of the race.
  • Memory Tip: "Dead Heat = Equal Finish". Time and distance are identical for those involved.
  • Mini Example: If A and B finish a 100m race in 12 seconds, it's a dead heat between them.

📏 Rules & Properties

Rule 1: A beats B by Distance 'x' meters

  • Explanation: When A finishes the race, B is 'x' meters behind the finish line.
  • Reason: A covers the full track length, while B covers (Track Length - x) meters in the same amount of time.
  • Shortcut: Calculate B's speed or distance based on the time A took to finish.
  • Exception: If A gives B a head start, the effective distance B has to cover changes.
  • Example: In a 100m race, A beats B by 10m. When A runs 100m, B runs 90m. The time taken is identical for both.

Rule 2: A beats B by Time 't' seconds

  • Explanation: When A finishes the race, B reaches the finish line 't' seconds later.
  • Reason: A completes the track in time 'T', while B completes it in time 'T + t'. Both cover the full track length.
  • Shortcut: If you know A's time, you automatically know B's time to finish the race.
  • Exception: Does not apply if one gives the other a time head start.
  • Example: A finishes a 100m race in 10s, beating B by 2s. B finishes the 100m race in 12s.

Rule 3: Meeting for the First Time on a Circular Track (2 Runners)

  • Explanation: Time until two runners meet for the very first time on the track, regardless of location.
  • Reason: Based on their relative speed to cover the track length.
  • Shortcut: Use the direct formula for relative speed.
  • Exception: This is NOT the time they meet at the starting point.
  • Example: Two runners on a 100m track. Speeds 10m/s and 5m/s.
    • Same direction: Relative speed \( (10-5) = 5 \) m/s. Time to meet \( \frac{100}{5} = 20 \)s.
    • Opposite direction: Relative speed \( (10+5) = 15 \) m/s. Time to meet \( \frac{100}{15} = \frac{20}{3} \)s.

Rule 4: Meeting at the Starting Point on a Circular Track (2+ Runners)

  • Explanation: Time until all runners simultaneously arrive back at the starting point together.
  • Reason: Each runner completes full rounds in certain times. They will meet at the start when all have completed an integer number of rounds, and the total time is a multiple of each runner's time per round. This is the definition of LCM.
  • Shortcut: Calculate time for each runner to complete one round, then find their LCM.
  • Exception: This does not tell you if they meet anywhere else on the track before this point.
  • Example: A takes 10s, B takes 15s to complete one round. They meet at the starting point after LCM(10, 15) = 30s.

Rule 5: Number of Distinct Meeting Points (2 Runners)

  • Explanation: The total count of unique locations on the track where two runners will meet before they meet again at the starting point.
  • Reason: Depends on the ratio of their speeds and their direction of movement.
  • Shortcut: Simplify speed ratio \( S_1:S_2 = p:q \) (where p, q are coprime).
    • Same direction: Number of distinct meeting points = \( |p-q| \).
    • Opposite direction: Number of distinct meeting points = \( p+q \).
  • Example: Speeds 10m/s and 15m/s. Ratio \( 10:15 = 2:3 \).
    • Same direction: \( |2-3| = 1 \) distinct meeting point.
    • Opposite direction: \( 2+3 = 5 \) distinct meeting points.

📐 Formula Box

1. Linear Races: A beats B by Distance 'x'

$$ \frac{\text{Distance covered by A}}{\text{Speed of A}} = \frac{\text{Distance covered by B}}{\text{Speed of B}} $$
  • Meaning: The time taken by A to cover the full distance is equal to the time taken by B to cover (Full Distance - x).
  • Variables:
    • A, B = Runners
    • x = Distance by which A beats B
    • Total Distance = Race length
  • When to use: When given how much distance one runner beats another by.
  • When NOT to use: When a time head start is involved, or comparing only finishing times.
  • Memory Trick: "Same Time for Different Distances" (for the winner and loser up to the point winner finishes).

2. Linear Races: A beats B by Time 't'

$$ \text{Time taken by B} = \text{Time taken by A} + t $$ $$ \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Speed of B}} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Speed of A}} + t $$
  • Meaning: B takes 't' seconds more than A to complete the entire race.
  • Variables:
    • A, B = Runners
    • t = Time by which A beats B
    • Total Distance = Race length
  • When to use: When given how much time one runner beats another by for the full race.
  • When NOT to use: When a distance head start is involved, or comparing distances covered.
  • Memory Trick: "Same Distance for Different Times".

3. Circular Tracks: Time to Meet for the First Time (2 Runners)

$$ T_{\text{first meet}} = \frac{\text{Length of track (L)}}{\text{Relative Speed}} $$
  • Meaning: Time until the two runners first cross paths anywhere on the track.
  • Variables:
    • L = Length of the circular track
    • \(S_1, S_2\) = Speeds of runners 1 and 2
    • Relative Speed:
      • Same direction: \( |S_1 - S_2| \)
      • Opposite direction: \( S_1 + S_2 \)
  • When to use: To find the earliest time they encounter each other.
  • When NOT to use: To find when they meet *at the starting point*.
  • Memory Trick: "Track length / Gap or Sum" (depending on direction).

4. Circular Tracks: Time to Meet at the Starting Point (N Runners)

$$ T_{\text{start meet}} = \text{LCM} \left( \frac{L}{S_1}, \frac{L}{S_2}, \dots, \frac{L}{S_N} \right) $$
  • Meaning: The time at which all 'N' runners simultaneously return to the starting point together.
  • Variables:
    • L = Length of the circular track
    • \(S_1, S_2, \dots, S_N\) = Speeds of the N runners
    • \( \frac{L}{S_i} \) = Time taken by runner 'i' to complete one round.
  • When to use: Specifically when asking for meeting *at the starting point*.
  • When NOT to use: To find meeting points anywhere else or for the first time.
  • Memory Trick: "LCM of Individual Lap Times".

5. Circular Tracks: Number of Distinct Meeting Points (2 Runners)

Let the ratio of speeds be \( S_1:S_2 = p:q \), where p and q are coprime integers.

  • Meaning: The total number of unique spots on the track where the two runners will cross paths before meeting at the starting point.
  • Variables:
    • \(S_1, S_2\) = Speeds of runners 1 and 2
    • \(p, q\) = Coprime ratio components of speeds.
  • Formula:
    • If moving in Same Direction: \( |p-q| \)
    • If moving in Opposite Direction: \( p+q \)
  • When to use: To find the count of distinct meeting locations.
  • When NOT to use: To calculate the time of meeting.
  • Memory Trick: "Ratio difference for Same, Ratio sum for Opposite".

🧠 Solved Examples

Easy

Example 1: Linear Race - A beats B by Distance

In a 100-meter race, A runs at 10 m/s and B runs at 8 m/s. By what distance does A beat B?

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Calculate time taken by A to finish the race.

A runs 100m at 10 m/s.

$$ \text{Time}_A = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}_A} = \frac{100 \text{ m}}{10 \text{ m/s}} = 10 \text{ seconds} $$

Step 2: Calculate distance covered by B in the same time.

Since A beats B, B runs for the same duration A took to finish (10 seconds).

$$ \text{Distance}_B = \text{Speed}_B \times \text{Time}_A = 8 \text{ m/s} \times 10 \text{ s} = 80 \text{ meters} $$

Step 3: Calculate the distance by which A beats B.

Distance A beats B by = Total Distance - Distance covered by B

$$ 100 \text{ m} - 80 \text{ m} = 20 \text{ meters} $$

Answer: A beats B by 20 meters.

Medium

Example 2: Circular Track - First Meeting (Opposite Direction)

Two persons A and B start from the same point on a 1200m circular track and run in opposite directions. Their speeds are 20 m/s and 10 m/s respectively. When will they meet for the first time?

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Identify given values.

Track length \( L = 1200 \) m

Speed of A \( S_A = 20 \) m/s

Speed of B \( S_B = 10 \) m/s

Direction: Opposite

Step 2: Calculate their relative speed.

Since they are moving in opposite directions, their relative speed is the sum of their speeds.

$$ \text{Relative Speed} = S_A + S_B = 20 \text{ m/s} + 10 \text{ m/s} = 30 \text{ m/s} $$

Step 3: Calculate the time to meet for the first time.

$$ \text{Time} = \frac{\text{Track Length}}{\text{Relative Speed}} = \frac{1200 \text{ m}}{30 \text{ m/s}} = 40 \text{ seconds} $$

Answer: They will meet for the first time after 40 seconds.

Hard

Example 3: Circular Track - Meeting at Starting Point (3 Runners)

Three runners A, B, and C start running simultaneously from the same point in the same direction around a circular track of 300 meters. Their speeds are 10 m/s, 15 m/s, and 6 m/s respectively. After what time will they meet again at the starting point for the first time?

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Identify given values.

Track length \( L = 300 \) m

Speed of A \( S_A = 10 \) m/s

Speed of B \( S_B = 15 \) m/s

Speed of C \( S_C = 6 \) m/s

Step 2: Calculate the time each runner takes to complete one round.

$$ \text{Time}_A = \frac{L}{S_A} = \frac{300}{10} = 30 \text{ seconds} $$ $$ \text{Time}_B = \frac{L}{S_B} = \frac{300}{15} = 20 \text{ seconds} $$ $$ \text{Time}_C = \frac{L}{S_C} = \frac{300}{6} = 50 \text{ seconds} $$

Step 3: Find the LCM of the individual lap times.

To meet at the starting point, the time elapsed must be a multiple of each runner's lap time.

$$ \text{Time to meet at start} = \text{LCM}(30, 20, 50) $$

Prime factorization:

  • \( 30 = 2 \times 3 \times 5 \)
  • \( 20 = 2^2 \times 5 \)
  • \( 50 = 2 \times 5^2 \)
$$ \text{LCM}(30, 20, 50) = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5^2 = 4 \times 3 \times 25 = 300 \text{ seconds} $$

Answer: They will meet again at the starting point after 300 seconds.

Exam Level

Example 4: Linear Race with Head Start

In a 1 km race, A gives B a head start of 50m and beats him by 10 seconds. If A's speed is 20 m/s, what is B's speed?

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Convert total distance to meters.

Total Race Distance \( = 1 \text{ km} = 1000 \text{ meters} \)

Step 2: Calculate the distance A runs and time taken by A.

A runs the full 1000m.

$$ \text{Time}_A = \frac{\text{Distance}_A}{\text{Speed}_A} = \frac{1000 \text{ m}}{20 \text{ m/s}} = 50 \text{ seconds} $$

Step 3: Calculate the time taken by B.

A beats B by 10 seconds, meaning B took 10 seconds more than A to finish.

$$ \text{Time}_B = \text{Time}_A + 10 \text{ s} = 50 \text{ s} + 10 \text{ s} = 60 \text{ seconds} $$

Step 4: Calculate the distance B runs.

B was given a head start of 50m, so B had to run less than the total distance.

$$ \text{Distance}_B = \text{Total Distance} - \text{Head Start} = 1000 \text{ m} - 50 \text{ m} = 950 \text{ meters} $$

Step 5: Calculate B's speed.

$$ \text{Speed}_B = \frac{\text{Distance}_B}{\text{Time}_B} = \frac{950 \text{ m}}{60 \text{ s}} = \frac{95}{6} \text{ m/s} $$ $$ \text{Speed}_B \approx 15.83 \text{ m/s} $$

Answer: B's speed is \( \frac{95}{6} \) m/s or approximately 15.83 m/s.

Exam Level

Example 5: Circular Track - Number of Distinct Meeting Points

Two runners are running on a 600m circular track. Their speeds are 30 m/s and 20 m/s. How many distinct points will they meet if they run in: a) the same direction, and b) opposite directions?

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Identify given values and find the ratio of speeds.

Speeds \( S_1 = 30 \) m/s, \( S_2 = 20 \) m/s.

Ratio of speeds \( S_1 : S_2 = 30 : 20 = 3 : 2 \).

Here, \( p=3 \) and \( q=2 \) (which are coprime).

a) Same Direction:

Step 2: Apply the formula for same direction.

Number of distinct meeting points = \( |p-q| \)

$$ = |3-2| = 1 $$

Answer a): They will meet at 1 distinct point if running in the same direction.

b) Opposite Direction:

Step 3: Apply the formula for opposite direction.

Number of distinct meeting points = \( p+q \)

$$ = 3+2 = 5 $$

Answer b): They will meet at 5 distinct points if running in opposite directions.

⚡ Exam Tricks & Shortcuts

1. Ratio Method for Linear Races

  • Trick: If time is constant (e.g., in 'A beats B by distance'), then \( \text{Speed} \propto \text{Distance} \). If distance is constant (e.g., in 'A beats B by time'), then \( \text{Speed} \propto \frac{1}{\text{Time}} \).
  • How to use:
    • A runs 100m, B runs 90m in same time. Speed ratio \( S_A:S_B = 100:90 = 10:9 \).
    • A takes 10s, B takes 12s for 100m. Speed ratio \( S_A:S_B = \frac{1}{10}:\frac{1}{12} = 12:10 = 6:5 \).
  • Benefit: Avoids direct TSD calculations, faster for proportional problems.

2. LCM for Circular Tracks (Mental Math)

  • Trick: For meeting at the starting point, quickly find individual lap times and then their LCM.
  • How to use: Track 400m. Speeds 10m/s, 16m/s. Lap times: \( \frac{400}{10}=40 \), \( \frac{400}{16}=25 \). LCM(40, 25) = LCM(\(2^3 \times 5\), \(5^2 \times 2^0\)) = \(2^3 \times 5^2 = 8 \times 25 = 200\) seconds.
  • Benefit: Direct and robust method for multi-runner problems.

3. Relative Speed as "Effective Distance"

  • Trick: For meeting for the first time on a circular track, visualize one person stationary and the other covering the entire track length at their relative speed.
  • How to use: 100m track, speeds 10m/s & 8m/s, same direction. Faster runner gains 2m/s. To cover 100m gap, it takes \( \frac{100}{2} = 50 \)s.
  • Benefit: Provides intuitive understanding and quick calculation for first meeting time.

⚠️ Common Mistakes & Pitfalls

1. Confusing First Meet vs. Meet at Starting Point

  • Mistake: Using relative speed for "meeting at starting point" or LCM for "first meet anywhere".
  • Avoid: Remember that "first meet" uses relative speed to cover track length, while "meet at starting point" uses LCM of individual lap times.

2. Incorrect Relative Speed Application

  • Mistake: Always subtracting speeds (even in opposite directions) or always adding.
  • Avoid:
    • Same Direction: Subtract speeds \( |S_1 - S_2| \).
    • Opposite Direction: Add speeds \( S_1 + S_2 \).

3. Unit Conversion Errors

  • Mistake: Mixing km/hr with m/s without converting, or distances in km with speeds in m/s.
  • Avoid: Always ensure all units (distance, speed, time) are consistent before calculation. Convert to a common unit (e.g., m/s for short tracks/races, km/hr for longer distances) at the beginning of the problem.

4. Misinterpreting Head Starts

  • Mistake: Adding/subtracting head start distance/time incorrectly from the total race parameters.
  • Avoid:
    • Distance Head Start: The person receiving the head start covers (Total Distance - Head Start).
    • Time Head Start: The person receiving the head start runs for (Time taken by other runner + Head Start Time), or starts earlier.

Railway (NTPC/Group D)

  • Weightage: Moderate (1-2 questions)

  • Weightage: Low to Moderate (0-1 question, possibly in Data Interpretation or caselets)

CDS/CAPF/State PCS/Police

  • Weightage: Moderate (1-2 questions)
  • Pattern: Can involve slightly more complex scenarios, sometimes integrating algebraic expressions or multiple variables.
  • Focus: Strong understanding of all formulas, ability to derive relations, and efficient problem-solving for both linear and circular track scenarios.

📌 Summary

  • Linear Races:
    • A beats B by distance 'x': A covers full distance, B covers (total - x) in same time.
    • A beats B by time 't': A finishes in 'T', B finishes in 'T+t', both cover full distance.
    • Head start: Adjust distance or time for the disadvantaged runner.
  • Circular Tracks:
    • First Meeting: \( T = \frac{\text{Track Length}}{\text{Relative Speed}} \). (Opposite: \( S_1+S_2 \), Same: \( |S_1-S_2| \)).
    • Meeting at Starting Point: \( T = \text{LCM} \left( \frac{L}{S_1}, \frac{L}{S_2}, \dots \right) \).
    • Number of Distinct Meeting Points (2 runners, \( S_1:S_2 = p:q \) coprime):
      • Same Direction: \( |p-q| \)
      • Opposite Direction: \( p+q \)
  • Key Tools: Relative Speed, LCM, and Ratio-Proportion concepts.
  • Common Error: Confusing "first meet" with "meet at start".

🚀 Quick Revision: Races & Circular Tracks

  • Linear Races:
    • A beats B by X meters \( \implies \) A runs Full, B runs (Full-X) in same time.
    • A beats B by T seconds \( \implies \) A takes 't', B takes 't+T' for Full distance.
    • Head Start: Adjust distance (Xm ahead) or time (Xs earlier).
  • Circular Tracks:
    • Relative Speed (RS): Same Dir \( = |S_1-S_2| \); Opp Dir \( = S_1+S_2 \).
    • Time to First Meet: \( T_{1st} = \frac{\text{Track Length}}{\text{RS}} \).
    • Time to Meet at Start: \( T_{start} = \text{LCM} \left( \frac{L}{S_1}, \frac{L}{S_2}, \dots \right) \).
    • Number of Distinct Meeting Points (Speeds \( p:q \) coprime):
      • Same Dir: \( |p-q| \)
      • Opp Dir: \( p+q \)
  • Critical: Consistent Units, LCM for start point, Relative Speed for first meet.

🚀 Key Takeaways

  • Relative Speed is Foundational: Always consider the combined or differential speed when objects move towards or away from each other.
  • LCM for Periodicity: Whenever multiple entities need to align at a common point in a cyclical process (like runners meeting at the start), LCM is the go-to tool.
  • Read Carefully: Distinguish between "meeting for the first time" and "meeting at the starting point," as they require different approaches.
  • Ratio Power: Use speed ratios for quick calculations in linear races and to determine the number of meeting points on circular tracks.
  • Unit Consistency: Double-check units before beginning calculations to prevent common errors.
